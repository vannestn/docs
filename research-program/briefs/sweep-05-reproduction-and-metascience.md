## The shape of this space — what the work actually consists of

This is the field of **checking whether AI claims hold up**. It is unusually solo-friendly because the unit of work is often *one claim, one paper, one benchmark* rather than a lab-scale training run, and because credibility (not compute) is the scarce input. (Terms: **reproduction** = re-run the same experiment and see if you get the same result; **replication** = test the same claim by a fresh route/dataset; **meta-science** = studying how the field itself reports and self-corrects.) Six overlapping sub-areas, each with an anchor result I read:

- **Direct reproduction / replication of headline claims.** Take a specific published result and re-run it. Two flavors: (a) *"does it reproduce as stated?"* — e.g. Epoch's [Chinchilla scaling replication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102) (re-fit DeepMind's compute-optimal "scaling law"; finding: the original parametric fit was mis-optimized and its confidence intervals implausibly narrow); (b) *"does X replicate on open models?"* — e.g. the flood of open R1-Zero reproductions (open re-creations of DeepSeek's reasoning-RL recipe — [TinyZero](https://github.com/Jiayi-Pan/TinyZero), [Open-R1](https://github.com/huggingface/open-r1)) and open-model [alignment-faking mini-replications](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6c6c3thcDvHPuuvTv/notes-from-a-mini-replication-of-the-alignment-faking-paper) (does a model strategically fake compliance during training — retested on open models).
- **Forensics of hyped results that fell apart.** Post-hoc debugging of a claim that turned out wrong — [Reflection 70B](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/1832457791010959539) (a model touted as SOTA; finding: its benchmarks were not reproducible), [Sakana AI's "AI CUDA Engineer"](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/21/sakana-walks-back-claims-that-its-ai-can-dramatically-speed-up-model-training/) (claimed huge training speedups; finding: it reward-hacked the eval sandbox — exploited a loophole instead of really speeding things up), the [gzip-beats-BERT](https://kenschutte.com/gzip-knn-paper/) tie-breaking bug (a viral "simple compressor beats deep learning" result undone by a scoring artifact).
- **Benchmark auditing / ground-truth forensics.** Checking that the *measuring stick* is sound: [GSM1k](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332) (a fresh grade-school-math test built to detect contamination/overfitting on the older GSM8k), [MMLU-Redux / "Are We Done with MMLU?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04127) (re-audited the popular MMLU knowledge test; finding: 6.49% of items have label errors), [The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) (audit of Chatbot Arena's crowd-vote ranking; finding: structural bias favoring big labs).
- **Meta-analysis / systematic review across many papers.** Aggregate the literature to test a general claim: [Sprague et al.'s "To CoT or not to CoT"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12183) (synthesized 100+ papers; finding: chain-of-thought — making the model "show its work" — mostly helps math/symbolic tasks only); [Kapoor & Narayanan's leakage survey](https://www.cell.com/patterns/fulltext/S2666-3899(23)00159-9) (surveyed 294 papers across 17 fields for data leakage — train/test overlap that inflates scores).
- **Meta-science / methodology & standards.** Position and standards work about *how the field should report and correct itself*: [REFORMS checklist](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adk3452) (a consensus reporting checklist for ML-based science), [Questionable Practices in ML](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12220) (catalog of 44 QRPs — questionable research practices that quietly inflate results), [Lessons from the Trenches on Reproducible Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782) (why eval numbers don't reproduce, from the lm-eval-harness maintainers), the [Refutations & Critiques track proposal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19882) (a pitch for a formal conference venue that publishes challenges to wrong papers).
- **Living reviews / open lab notebooks.** Continuously-updated artifacts rather than frozen papers: the [HEPML Living Review](https://github.com/iml-wg/HEPML-LivingReview) (an ever-updated bibliography of ML-in-particle-physics), [BetterBench's living benchmark registry](https://betterbench.stanford.edu/), and radical open-development labs like [Marin](http://marin.community/) where every experiment (including failures) is public in real time.

A defining cultural fact: much of the highest-impact work here lives **outside peer review** — on personal blogs (kenschutte.com, gwern.net), on LessWrong/Alignment Forum, on X threads, and in GitHub repos — because the traditional venues have no home for "you were wrong." That is simultaneously the field's biggest gap and its biggest opening for a credible independent.

## Who does this work

### Nonprofits / independent research institutes

- **Epoch AI** — the flagship data-driven-analysis nonprofit. Research nonprofit ([about page](https://epoch.ai/about)) funded largely by **Open Philanthropy** ([2022 general support ~$1.96M](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/grants/epoch-general-support/); a later [general-support grant of ~$6.9M](https://openphilanthropy.org/grants/epoch-ai-general-support-2025)). ~13 people / ~11 FTE per their 2022 impact report (⚠️ larger now). Does compute/data-trend analysis, the Chinchilla replication, and benchmark work (FrontierMath). Hires openly. **Caveat that matters for this field:** Epoch itself got caught in a transparency failure over [undisclosed OpenAI funding of FrontierMath](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/) — a live lesson that credibility infrastructure is fragile.
- **METR** (Model Evaluation & Threat Research) — Berkeley nonprofit, **donation-funded** (largest funder: [The Audacious Project at TED](https://metr.org/about)); explicitly **takes no money from AI companies** (uses free compute credits only). Does rigorous third-party capability/uplift evals and human-subject RCTs (randomized controlled trials — the gold-standard experiment design). The [16-dev productivity study](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) (measured whether AI tools actually speed up experienced developers) was run by ~4 staff and **pays participants ~$150/hr**. Hires; recruits paid participants — a real collaborator on-ramp.
- **EleutherAI** — nonprofit research collective; maintains [`lm-evaluation-harness`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782) (the software that runs a model against a benchmark and scores it), the de-facto standard for reproducible LLM eval. Community-driven; contributions accepted from anyone via GitHub. The [Lessons from the Trenches](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782) paper (what it did: distilled why eval numbers diverge across labs) had ~29 contributors (Biderman, Schoelkopf et al.).
- **The Unjournal** — 501(c)(3) that **commissions and pays for open expert evaluations** of existing research (~$500/evaluation incl. bonuses), funded by EA Infrastructure / Long-Term Future Funds ([about](https://www.unjournal.org/about), [open call for evaluators](https://www.unjournal.org/news/open-call-for-evaluators)). Focus is econ/social-science/global-priorities, but it is a rare *paid* venue for the "evaluate someone else's paper rigorously" skill. Recruits evaluators openly.
- **Apollo Research** — safety-eval nonprofit/lab arguing for a ["Science of Evals"](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/we-need-a-science-of-evals) (making evals trustworthy: are we measuring the right thing; can we trust the result). Explicitly invites outside collaborators.
- **ReScience C / ML Reproducibility Challenge (MLRC)** — volunteer-run editorial venue for replication reports ([ReScience C](http://rescience.github.io/read/); [MLRC editorial repo](https://github.com/ReScience/MLRC)). Began 2018 (Pineau, ICLR); as of [MLRC 2026 it becomes an official NeurIPS track](https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/05/04/mlrc-2026-reproducibility-as-an-official-track-at-neurips/) routed through TMLR (Transactions on Machine Learning Research, an open ML journal). This is the closest thing to a formal, citable home for reproduction work — and it takes volunteer reproducers.

### Academic groups

- **Princeton — Kapoor & Narayanan / "Reproducibility in ML-based science"** ([reproducible.cs.princeton.edu](https://reproducible.cs.princeton.edu/)). Produced the leakage survey (*Patterns* 2023) and the [REFORMS consensus standards](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adk3452) (a 19-author cross-discipline reporting checklist for ML-based science). Academic/grant-funded; the intellectual center of AI meta-science. Authors of the *AI Snake Oil* book/newsletter — a public-facing critique channel.
- **Stanford CRFM / HAI — BetterBench + Marin.** [BetterBench](https://betterbench.stanford.edu/) (46 best-practice criteria scoring benchmarks across their lifecycle, NeurIPS 2024 spotlight) is a living registry. [Marin](http://marin.community/) (Percy Liang) is the "open lab" — preregistered, publicly-logged from-scratch model development where outsiders can propose and run experiments via GitHub. Academic; genuinely open to external contributors.
- **Stanford — Schaeffer / Koyejo group.** Behind ["Are Emergent Abilities a Mirage?"](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ITw9edRDlD) (argued sudden "emergent" jumps are largely an artifact of the metric; NeurIPS 2023 outstanding paper) and the ["Refutations & Critiques track" position paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19882) (calls for a formal venue to publish critiques of wrong papers). Effectively the loudest academic voice for institutionalizing critique.
- **Cohere Labs** — company research arm (Sara Hooker) that co-led [The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) (exposed structural bias in Chatbot Arena's ranking) with AI2/Princeton/Stanford/Waterloo/UW. Company-funded but publishes adversarial-to-industry findings; runs an open science community + scholars program.
- **HEPML Living Review (IML working group)** — academic/community-maintained living review (a continuously-updated bibliography); a template for continuously-updated systematic reviews in a subfield.

### Companies doing (or subject to) this work

- **Artificial Analysis** — for-profit independent benchmarking company (founded 2023; investors incl. [AI Grant, Build Club](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/680302-90); ⚠️ exact round/size not confirmed). Runs the standardized third-party evals that debunked [Reflection 70B](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/1832457791010959539) (an over-hyped model whose scores wouldn't reproduce). Commercial model = trusted neutral scoreboard.
- **Scale AI (SEAL)** — commissioned [GSM1k](https://scale.com/research/llm-performance-grade-school-arithmetic) (a fresh math test to catch benchmark contamination); a company funding contamination audits.
- **Answer.AI** — small applied-research company; its [month-with-Devin evaluation](https://www.answer.ai/posts/2025-01-08-devin.html) (ran 20 real tasks against the hyped "AI software engineer"; only 3/20 succeeded) is a model of honest independent product-claim testing.

### Notable individuals (the independent-blogger tradition)

- **Ken Schutte** — engineer who, solo on [kenschutte.com](https://kenschutte.com/), showed the "gzip beats BERT" numbers came from a top-*k* tie-breaking trick that leaked test labels; flipping the bug turned gzip from best to worst. The canonical solo-blogger takedown.
- **Gwern Branwen** — independent researcher funded by [Patreon/commissions](https://gwern.net/scaling-hypothesis); [gwern.net](https://gwern.net/) is 1,000+ essays of analysis and self-experiments. Proof that a non-affiliated writer can shape the field's thinking.
- **Keller Jordan** — runs the [NanoGPT speedrun](https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt), a public, reproducible efficiency-claim leaderboard (race to train a 124M GPT to a fixed validation loss — now <3 min on 8×H100 GPUs). A living, community-audited efficiency benchmark run by one person.
- **Jiayi Pan** (Berkeley PhD) — led [TinyZero](https://x.com/jiayi_pirate/status/1882839370505621655), the <$30 R1-Zero reproduction on Countdown (recreating the reasoning-RL result on a small arithmetic-puzzle task for under $30).
- **Simon Willison** ([simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Apr/30/criticism-of-the-chatbot-arena/)) and **Sebastian Raschka** ([Ahead of AI](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/)) — analysis bloggers who contextualize and stress-test claims for large audiences.
- **Eric Martínez** (LawAI fellow) — solo-authored [Re-evaluating GPT-4's bar exam performance](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4441311) (re-derived OpenAI's marketing number; finding: the "90th percentile" claim was ~48th percentile vs. actual attorneys).

## Published exemplars (favoring small teams)

1. **[Re-evaluating GPT-4's Bar Exam Performance](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4441311)** — Eric Martínez, **solo**, MIT (2023/2024, published in *Artificial Intelligence and Law*). Reworked OpenAI's headline "90th percentile UBE" claim down to ~48th percentile vs. licensed attorneys (~15th on essays). Landed because it re-derived a single, widely-cited, high-stakes marketing number from public NCBE data — no GPUs required. **The purest solo template.**
2. **["Bad numbers in the gzip-beats-BERT paper?"](https://kenschutte.com/gzip-knn-paper/)** — Ken Schutte, **solo blog** (2023). Found the ACL'23 result depended on a top-2 accuracy / tie-breaking artifact that peeked at the test label. Landed because it was reproducible, code-backed, and overturned a viral "simple method beats deep learning" story within days.
3. **[Chinchilla Scaling: A replication attempt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102)** — Besiroglu, Erdil, Barnett, You (Epoch AI, 4 authors, 2024). Re-extracted DeepMind's data and showed the parametric scaling-law fit (the formula relating model size, data, and loss that guides how to spend compute) was mis-estimated (Huber-loss averaging vs. summing — an error in how the fitting objective was aggregated) with impossibly tight CIs (confidence intervals — the stated uncertainty was far too narrow to be real). Landed because scaling laws guide billion-dollar compute decisions; a small team audited a foundational number.
4. **[Are Emergent Abilities of LLMs a Mirage?](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ITw9edRDlD)** — Schaeffer, Miranda, Koyejo (**3 authors**, Stanford; NeurIPS 2023 Outstanding Paper). Reframed "emergence" (abilities that seem to appear suddenly at scale) as largely an artifact of discontinuous metrics (all-or-nothing scoring that hides smooth underlying gains). Landed because it re-analyzed existing BIG-Bench/GPT-3 data — a metric critique, not new training — and won a best-paper award for it.
5. **[To CoT or not to CoT?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12183)** — Sprague et al. (UT Austin/JHU/Princeton; ICLR 2025). Meta-analysis of 100+ papers + re-runs on 20 datasets → chain-of-thought (prompting the model to reason step-by-step) helps mainly on math/symbolic tasks. Landed because it disciplined an over-generalized folk belief with aggregate evidence.
6. **[Leakage and the Reproducibility Crisis in ML-based Science](https://www.cell.com/patterns/fulltext/S2666-3899(23)00159-9)** — Kapoor & Narayanan (**2 authors**, Princeton; *Patterns* 2023). A taxonomy of 8 leakage types (ways train/test data overlap and inflate results) documented across 294 papers in 17 fields. Landed as the citation-anchor for "ML-based science has a systemic validity problem," spawning the REFORMS standards.
7. **[Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20783)** — Sea AI Lab (Liu et al., 2025). Showed the celebrated "aha moment" self-reflection already exists in *base* models before any RL, and exposed a length bias in GRPO (the reinforcement-learning algorithm behind R1 was rewarding longer answers regardless of correctness; fix: Dr. GRPO). Landed as a fast, code-released ([sail-sg/understand-r1-zero](https://github.com/sail-sg/understand-r1-zero)) correction to the dominant reasoning-RL narrative.
8. **[Thoughts On A Month With Devin](https://www.answer.ai/posts/2025-01-08-devin.html)** — Answer.AI (⚠️ author attribution not re-verified). A small team ran 20 real tasks against a heavily-hyped "AI software engineer" and got 3 successes. Landed because it was concrete, task-by-task, and independent of the vendor's benchmarks.

*Adjacent institutional exemplars worth reading:* [The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879), [GSM1k](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332), [MMLU-Redux](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04127), [Questionable Practices in ML](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12220), [BetterBench](https://betterbench.stanford.edu/), [alignment-faking mini-replication](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6c6c3thcDvHPuuvTv/notes-from-a-mini-replication-of-the-alignment-faking-paper).

## Where the gaps are (named, grounded in what I read)

- **No high-status home for refutations.** The [Refutations & Critiques track position paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19882) (Schaeffer, Donoho et al., 2025) argues explicitly that "misleading, incorrect, flawed or perhaps even fraudulent studies" get accepted and *highlighted* at ML conferences with no reputable venue to challenge them — they illustrate with a critique of a 2025 ICLR Oral (a top-billed conference talk). MLRC-as-NeurIPS-track (2026) is the first structural fix and is brand new; the gap is still wide open in 2026.
- **Efficiency & inference claims are under-audited.** Reproduction energy concentrates on *capability/benchmark* claims. "Runs 10–100× faster," "X% cheaper inference," quantization-quality tradeoffs (quantization = shrinking a model to lower-precision numbers, which can quietly cost accuracy) — the [Sakana CUDA episode](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/21/sakana-walks-back-claims-that-its-ai-can-dramatically-speed-up-model-training/) (a walked-back speedup claim) shows how easily these are gamed, yet there is no standing infrastructure for auditing them beyond Keller Jordan's speedrun. A clear whitespace given the profile's XGBoost/pipeline strengths.
- **Contamination detection without weights/data access.** Surveys ([Benchmark Data Contamination survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04244v1)) note n-gram overlap (checking for shared word-sequences between test items and training text) is unreliable and rephrasing defeats it; methods like Oren et al. detect contamination *without* pretraining-data access, but this is unsettled and mostly demonstrated, not operationalized as a routine audit anyone can run.
- **Living systematic reviews are rare outside physics.** HEPML has a mature [living review](https://github.com/iml-wg/HEPML-LivingReview); core LLM topics (reasoning-RL; RAG — retrieval-augmented generation, letting a model look up documents; agents; LLM-as-judge — using one model to grade another's outputs) mostly lack a continuously-updated, community-maintained equivalent. BetterBench is the closest analog and is narrow (benchmark quality only).
- **"Does X replicate on open models?" is done ad hoc.** The R1-Zero wave and alignment-faking replications show huge appetite, but they are scattered blog posts / repos with inconsistent rigor. No standard protocol, preregistration, or aggregation. The [alignment-faking mini-replication notes](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6c6c3thcDvHPuuvTv/notes-from-a-mini-replication-of-the-alignment-faking-paper) and the [Anthropic follow-up](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/alignment-faking-revisited/) (only 3/16 models faked) show how much the answer depends on setup — exactly where methodology work pays off.
- **Conflict-of-interest / independence infrastructure is immature.** The [FrontierMath–OpenAI disclosure failure](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/) (an eval org criticized for not revealing lab funding) and [Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) both show that even "independent" evaluators have entangled incentives (funding, private test access, data-sharing asymmetries). There is demand for provably-independent, holdout-set-based verification (scoring on secret questions kept back so they can't be gamed or leaked) — a credibility niche, not a compute niche.
- **Reporting-standard adoption is weak.** [REFORMS](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adk3452) and the [Questionable Practices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12220) catalog of 44 QRPs (questionable research practices) exist, but the limitation both flag is enforcement/uptake. Empirical work measuring *whether* papers comply, and building tooling to check compliance, is largely undone.

## What we still don't know (for our ledger)

- **Does the independent-blogger path actually convert to "real researcher" recognition, or only to audience?** Gwern/Schutte/Willison have reach; unclear how reliably a blog-first reputation opens grants, co-authorships, or hiring vs. staying a respected-but-outside voice. ⚠️ inferred, not established.
- **Funding paths for a *solo* replicator.** Confirmed paid venues: The Unjournal (~$500/eval), METR (paid participants), Anthropic Economic Futures grants (seen in Sweep 04). **Not verified:** whether Open Phil / EA funds, or any grantmaker, funds *individual* reproduction/meta-science projects at the <$50k scale, and what track record they require. Needs a direct dig into current open calls.
- **Artificial Analysis funding/size** — only PitchBook-snippet level confirmed; round size, revenue model, and whether it takes external contributors are unverified.
- **Legal/reputational exposure of "forensics of hyped results."** Reflection 70B drew fraud accusations; Sakana walked back claims. Unknown what norms/risks attach to an independent publishing a "this claim is wrong" post about a funded startup or lab. Worth understanding before doing it.
- **How much compute a credible open-model replication really needs.** TinyZero says <$30 for a toy task; full Open-R1-scale reproductions are far larger. The boundary of "reproducible on a laptop + <$500 GPU" for 2026-era claims is unmapped and should be tested empirically, claim by claim.
- **Whether MLRC-as-NeurIPS-track will accept solo/unaffiliated reproducers** and how the TMLR-gating (routing submissions through the TMLR journal's review first) changes the barrier. New as of 2026; process unverified from the inside.
- **Meta-analysis tooling for LLM papers.** Whether existing systematic-review tooling handles the pace/heterogeneity of LLM benchmarks, or whether new infrastructure is needed, is open.

## Fit notes

This space aligns tightly with the profile's declared strengths: **evaluation methodology** (a published LLM-as-judge / RAG-hallucination study — using one model to grade another, and detecting when a retrieval-augmented system makes things up — is directly on-topic for benchmark auditing, contamination detection, and "science of evals" work), **classical ML / data pipelines** (meta-analysis, leakage detection, and reproducibility statistics are XGBoost-and-pandas problems, not frontier-training problems), and **LLM tuning** (needed for "does X replicate on open models?" reproductions like the R1-Zero wave). The scarce input here is credibility and rigor rather than compute, so a laptop + <$500 GPU + frontier APIs + Claude-as-force-multiplier covers a large fraction of viable projects — the exemplars that landed hardest (Martínez, Schutte, Schaeffer) used re-analysis of public data, not new training. It is also the most nonprofit/credibility-shaped of the sweeps: the entire value proposition is *independence*, which is easier to claim as an unaffiliated solo than inside a lab whose model is being audited.
