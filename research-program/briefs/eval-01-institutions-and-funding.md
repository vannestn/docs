# AI Evaluation & Benchmarking: The Institutional Landscape (mid-2026)

Brief 01 in the evaluation-field series. All claims below come from pages opened during research (early July 2026) unless marked **⚠️ unverified**. "READ" = seen on a source page; "inferred" flagged where applicable.

> **How to read this brief.** This maps *who does AI evaluation and who pays for it* — the institutions, companies, and funders, not the methods (that's brief 04). Where the text names a paper, org, or technical term, a plain-language gloss follows in parentheses or an em-dash so you can grasp it without opening the link.

## The shape of this space — what the work actually consists of

The field has stratified into distinguishable sub-areas, each with different institutions attached:

1. **Dangerous-capability / safety evaluations** — pre-deployment testing of frontier models for autonomy, cyber-offense, bio/chem uplift (how much the model raises a novice's ability to cause harm), scheming (the model covertly pursuing goals its operators didn't intend). Players: [METR](https://metr.org/about), [Apollo Research](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/apollo-update-may-2026/), [Palisade](https://palisaderesearch.org/about), [UK AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/evals-bounty), [US CAISI/NIST](https://www.nist.gov/caisi).
2. **Capability benchmarks & leaderboards** — static and live rankings of general capability (a benchmark = a fixed test set; a leaderboard = the public ranking of models on it). Players: [Epoch AI (FrontierMath — expert-written hard-math test)](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath), [LMArena/Arena (crowd head-to-head model voting)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMArena), [Scale Labs/SEAL (HLE = "Humanity's Last Exam", SWE-Bench Pro = real software-engineering tasks)](https://scale.com/blog/scales-next-era-building-for-2026), [Berkeley BFCL (tool/function-calling benchmark)](https://sky.cs.berkeley.edu/project/berkeley-function-calling-leaderboard/), [CAIS (HLE; CAIS = Center for AI Safety)](https://agi.safe.ai/).
3. **Evaluation infrastructure / harnesses** — the software everyone runs evals on (a harness loads a model, feeds it the test, and scores the answers): [EleutherAI lm-eval-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness), [UK AISI Inspect](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/) (200+ pre-built evals), [HF lighteval](https://github.com/huggingface/lighteval), [Stanford HELM](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm) (now maintenance-mode = still available but no longer actively developed), [Princeton HAL harness](https://github.com/princeton-pli/hal-harness).
4. **Evaluation science / meta-evaluation** — treating evals themselves as the object of study: validity (does the test measure what it claims), statistics, cost, reproducibility. Players: [EvalEval Coalition](https://evalevalai.com/), Princeton ([HAL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977) — the Holistic Agent Leaderboard, on cost-controlled reproducible agent evals), the [NeurIPS Evaluations & Datasets track](https://blog.neurips.cc/2026/03/23/introducing-the-evaluations-datasets-track-at-neurips-2026/) (renamed from Datasets & Benchmarks for 2026 explicitly because "evaluation itself becomes an object of scientific study"), position papers like [Toward an Evaluation Science for Generative AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05336) (argues eval should borrow safety-engineering rigor from aviation/pharma).
5. **Standards & assurance** — consortium/government-grade certification (turning eval practices into official, auditable requirements): [MLCommons AILuminate + Global Assurance Program](https://mlcommons.org/2026/02/ailuminate-global-assurance/) (a standardized safety benchmark plus a certification program), [NIST's International Network consensus on automated-evaluation practices (Feb 2026)](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/international-network-advanced-ai-measurement-evaluation-and-science), [EU AI Office external-evaluator qualification work](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/events/call-participants-workshop-qualification-requirements-external-evaluators-gpai-models-systemic-risk) (defining who is allowed to audit frontier models).
6. **Demonstrations-as-evidence** — empirical demos of concerning behavior aimed at policymakers/public rather than leaderboards (Palisade's shutdown-resistance work — showing a model resisting being turned off — and CTF work, i.e. capture-the-flag hacking challenges used to test cyber-offense skill).

A structural shift READ across multiple sources: evaluation is professionalizing and bifurcating (splitting into two tiers) — VC-scale money (venture-capital-sized funding) at the leaderboard end (LMArena at $1.7B), government institutionalization in the middle (CAISI, AISI, EU AI Office), and an under-resourced science/commons layer (HELM in maintenance mode, EvalEval arguing [eval costs are the new bottleneck](https://huggingface.co/blog/evaleval/eval-costs-bottleneck) — that the price of running evals now limits who can do them).

## Who does this work

### Safety-eval nonprofits

**[METR](https://metr.org/about)** — nonprofit; "develop scientific methods to assess catastrophic risks stemming from AI systems' autonomous capabilities." Funding (READ on about page): philanthropic grants from The Audacious Project (~$17M of a [$38M "Canary" project with RAND](https://metr.org/blog/2024-10-09-new-support-through-the-audacious-project/)), Sijbrandij Foundation, Pew Charitable Trusts, Schmidt Sciences, Packard Foundation, individual donors (Dylan Field, Geoff Ralston, Steve Newman), pooled funds via Longview Philanthropy; plus a **technical assistance contract with the European AI Office**. Explicitly takes **no money from AI companies** (uses free compute credits only). Size: ~40+ staff on the about page. Getting in: [careers page](https://metr.org/careers); the famous **task bounty ($300/professional-hour, >$100k paid out) is paused indefinitely** ([task dev guide](https://taskdev.metr.org/bounty/)), but METR states bounties were a hiring pipeline and good submissions could fast-track applications.

**[Apollo Research](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/)** — began fiscally sponsored by Rethink Priorities (501c3 = US tax-exempt nonprofit), **converted to a Public Benefit Corporation in Jan 2026** (PBC = a for-profit legally bound to a stated mission) ([announcement](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/apollo-research-is-becoming-a-pbc/)) explicitly to grow faster and diversify funding. Focus shifted from scheming *evals* (tests for whether models scheme) to "**Science of Scheming**" (studying the phenomenon itself, not just measuring it), plus agent monitoring and governance ([May 2026 update](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/apollo-update-may-2026/)). Offices: London + SF (SF growing 3→10+ in 2026), DC office opened June 2026. Hiring rounds expected Q3–Q4 2026 across research, policy, national-security roles ([careers](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/careers/)). Earlier grant funding visible on [Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/apollo-research-scale-up-interpretability--behavioral-model-evals-research). Total headcount: **⚠️ unverified**.

**[Palisade Research](https://palisaderesearch.org/about)** — small nonprofit (9 staff listed on about page), founded 2023 by Jeffrey Ladish (ex-Anthropic infosec). Mission: help institutions avoid "permanent disempowerment by strategic AI agents"; known for shutdown-resistance, chess-hacking (models cheating at chess), and GPT-5-in-CTF demos (a frontier model attempting capture-the-flag hacking challenges) covered by Time/WSJ/BBC. Funding: donations; [SFF matches donations 1:1 up to $1.1M](https://palisaderesearch.org/blog/ai-control-palisade-2026) (SFF = Survival and Flourishing Fund, a philanthropic funder); had **~7 months runway as of Dec 2025** (cash to operate before running out) — a live example of how thin nonprofit eval funding is. Hires via a public Gusto job board; takes donations via Open Collective/every.org.

### Companies

Note the split: **[LMSYS Org](https://www.lmsys.org/about/) continues separately** as an academic open-research collective (SGLang, RouteLLM — open-source model-serving and routing tools; Berkeley/UCSD/CMU). Involvement: company jobs; historically open datasets and public battle data (the head-to-head vote records) (**⚠️ unverified how much data remains open post-commercialization**).

**[Scale AI — SEAL → Scale Labs](https://scale.com/blog/scales-next-era-building-for-2026)** — corporate eval arm (SEAL = Scale's Safety, Evaluations and Alignment Lab). SEAL (founded 2023) expanded into "Scale Labs" in **March 2026**; publishes frontier leaderboards ([HLE with CAIS](https://scale.com/research/humanitys-last-exam), SWE-Bench Pro, MCP Atlas — a benchmark for tool-use via the Model Context Protocol); claims 450+ evaluations across 50+ model releases. Context: Meta bought ~49% for $14.3B (2025); founder Alexandr Wang left for Meta Superintelligence Labs; [~200 FTE layoffs followed](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-scale-ai-layoffs/) (FTE = full-time-equivalent staff). Hires SEAL research scientists ([example posting](https://scale.com/careers/4529091005)). **[Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/docs/leaderboards/en/index)** — company (the main hub for hosting open models and datasets); eval work includes **[lighteval](https://github.com/huggingface/lighteval)** (its eval toolkit), the **Open LLM Leaderboard — now archived** (frozen, no longer updated) ([2024–2025 archive collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/OpenEvals/archived-open-llm-leaderboard-2024-2025)), hosting of hundreds of community leaderboards, and **hosting/co-leading the EvalEval Coalition** with [Every Eval Ever results shown on model pages](https://huggingface.co/blog/eee-community-evals) (a shared schema so any model's eval scores appear in one place). Involvement: open-source PRs, community leaderboards, EvalEval participation.

### Government

**[UK AI Security Institute (AISI)](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/)** — UK government (DSIT = Dept for Science, Innovation and Technology). Built **[Inspect](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/)**, the open-source eval framework (with Meridian Labs; 200+ pre-built evals), and **[Inspect Evals](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/inspect-evals)**, a community repo (a shared code repository anyone can contribute evals to) maintained with **[Arcadia Impact](https://www.arcadiaimpact.org/about)** (via its AI Safety Engineering Taskforce) and the Vector Institute — from May 2026 community submissions go through a [register process](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_evals) (claim a benchmark first via GitHub issue + arXiv link + code, to avoid duplicate work). Ran an **[evals bounty](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/evals-bounty)** (paid open call to build evals): up to £200k, £2k compute at stage 2, £100–200/development-hour (≈£3k–15k per accepted task) — **⚠️ unverified whether a 2026 round is currently open** (the announced deadline seen was Nov 30 of the launch year). Also publishes an [Autonomous Systems Evaluation Standard](https://ukgovernmentbeis.github.io/as-evaluation-standard/) (an agreed way to test agent autonomy). Headcount: **⚠️ unverified**.

**[US CAISI (NIST)](https://www.nist.gov/caisi)** — Center for AI Standards and Innovation (Commerce/NIST — NIST is the US national metrology/standards agency; successor to US AISI). 40+ completed model assessments incl. unreleased models; classified evals via interagency TRAINS Taskforce (a cross-agency group running national-security-sensitive tests); **pre-deployment testing agreements with OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Microsoft, xAI** — the labs give CAISI access to test models before public release ([May 2026](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2026/05/05/advancing-ai-evaluation-with-the-center-for-ai-standards-us-and-innovation-and-the-ai-security-institute-uk/)); published [DeepSeek V4 Pro evaluation](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/05/caisi-evaluation-deepseek-v4-pro); launched the [AI Agent Standards Initiative (Feb 2026)](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-nist-ai-agent-standards-federal-framework/); [MOU with GSA for federal-procurement eval science](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/03/caisi-signs-mou-gsa-boost-ai-evaluation-science-federal-procurement-through) (MOU = memorandum of understanding, a cooperation agreement; GSA = General Services Administration, which handles federal buying). Convenes the [International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation, and Science](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/international-network-advanced-ai-measurement-evaluation-and-science) (consensus practices for automated evaluations — agreed norms multiple governments sign onto, Feb 2026). Involvement: federal hiring; standards working groups.

**[EU AI Office](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)** — regulator. The [GPAI Code of Practice](https://code-of-practice.ai/) (GPAI = general-purpose AI; a voluntary rulebook for showing AI Act compliance) obliges signatories to give "sufficient numbers of independent external evaluators" complimentary (free) access to their most advanced models. Currently defining **qualification and independence requirements for external evaluators** (who is allowed to audit, and how to be seen as independent) ([workshop ~15 July 2026](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/events/call-participants-workshop-qualification-requirements-external-evaluators-gpai-models-systemic-risk)) and will launch a **call to expand EU evaluation capacity, operational by 2027**. Contracts out technical work (METR holds a technical-assistance contract). This is creating a *regulated market* for third-party evaluators — a structural change worth tracking.

### Consortium

**[MLCommons](https://mlcommons.org/ailuminate/)** — industry/academic consortium (member-funded; the benchmark org behind MLPerf, the standard hardware-speed benchmark). Its AI Risk & Reliability working group ships **AILuminate** (12-hazard safety benchmark = a test spanning 12 categories of harm, [v1.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05731)) and in Feb 2026 launched the **[AILuminate Global Assurance Program](https://mlcommons.org/2026/02/ailuminate-global-assurance/)** (private non-saturated benchmarks — kept secret so models can't be gamed to a ceiling — integrated into pre-release workflows; backed by KPMG, Google, Microsoft, Qualcomm). Involvement: **working groups are open to join** — a low-barrier way to sit at the standards table. Member count: **⚠️ unverified**.

### Academic groups

**[Stanford CRFM / HELM](https://crfm.stanford.edu/helm/)** — academic (Percy Liang's center; CRFM = Center for Research on Foundation Models). HELM (Holistic Evaluation of Language Models) pioneered holistic multi-metric evaluation (scoring models on many axes at once — accuracy, robustness, bias, cost — not a single number) and spawned domain variants (MedHELM, Finance, Safety, [Arabic partnership](https://www.wamda.com/2026/01/arabicai-partners-stanford-launch-holistic-benchmark-arabic-ai-models)). **HELM entered maintenance mode June 1, 2026** (READ in the [repo README](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm)) — a major signal about academic eval-infrastructure sustainability (flagship academic tooling struggling to stay funded/staffed). Key individual: Yifan Mai (HELM lead maintainer, also an EvalEval author).

**[Princeton — HAL / Kapoor & Narayanan](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/)** — Princeton Language and Intelligence; Sayash Kapoor & Arvind Narayanan (authors of *AI Snake Oil*, a book/blog debunking AI hype) + Benedikt Stroebl. The [Holistic Agent Leaderboard (ICLR 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977) (HAL; ICLR = a top ML conference) makes **cost-controlled, reproducible agent evaluation** (ranking agents by capability *per dollar*, with runs anyone can repeat) the default, plus a [reliability dashboard](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/reliability/) (consistency = same answer on repeats, robustness = holds under rephrasing, abstention = knowing when to decline — not just accuracy). Small-team academic work with outsized field influence. Involvement: open-source [harness](https://github.com/princeton-pli/hal-harness).

**[UC Berkeley Sky Computing Lab](https://sky.cs.berkeley.edu/project/berkeley-function-calling-leaderboard/)** — the [Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html) (Gorilla project; Shishir Patil, advised by Stoica/Gonzalez) became the standard tool-use benchmark (testing whether a model correctly calls external functions/APIs) from a grad-student project. Also the origin of LMSYS/LMArena. Demonstrates the grad-lab → industry-standard pipeline.

**[EvalEval Coalition](https://evalevalai.com/)** — a researcher community on "evaluating evaluations" (studying whether the evals themselves are any good), hosted by **Hugging Face, University of Edinburgh, and EleutherAI** ([site](https://evaleval.github.io/)). Key individuals: Avijit Ghosh, Leshem Choshen, Yifan Mai, Georgia Channing.

## Published exemplars (small-team-weighted)

1. **[Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)** — Evan Miller, **solo author** (Anthropic), Nov 2024. (What it did:) treats an eval as a statistical experiment; gives formulas for CIs (confidence intervals — the ± on a score), paired model comparisons (compare two models on the *same* questions to cancel question-difficulty noise), and power analysis (how many questions you need to detect a real gap). Landed because the entire field was reporting point scores with no statistics; now routinely cited in eval methodology work. Proof that a single methodologically-sharp person can move field norms.
2. **[Holistic Agent Leaderboard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977)** — Stroebl, Kapoor, Narayanan (3-person core, Princeton), ICLR 2026. (What it did:) ranked AI agents by cost-controlled, reproducible evals across 21,730 rollouts (individual agent task-attempts); [live leaderboard + reliability dashboard](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/). Landed because agent leaderboards ignored cost and variance; HAL made both non-optional.
3. **[Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14499v1)** — METR, Mar 2025 (updated [Time Horizon 1.1, Jan 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/)). (What it did:) introduced the **50%-task-completion time horizon** — the length of task (in human hours) a model can finish half the time — and found it doubling ~every 7 months. Arguably the single most-cited eval result for AGI timelines; even has [its own Epoch tracking page](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/metr-time-horizons). Shows a *metric design* contribution (inventing a better yardstick) can outweigh any individual benchmark.
4. **[The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879)** — Cohere Labs + AI2/Princeton/Stanford/Waterloo/UW, Apr 2025. (What it did:) a 68-page audit of Chatbot Arena finding undisclosed private variant testing (labs quietly trying many model versions and keeping only the best score — 27 Meta variants pre-Llama-4), data-access asymmetry (~20% of battle data each to OpenAI/Google, far more than others got), and overfitting effects (models tuned to the leaderboard rather than genuinely improving). Forced [a public LMArena response](https://arena.ai/blog/our-response/) and policy changes. Exemplar of *auditing the evaluators* — no model training required.
5. **[FrontierMath](https://epoch.ai/frontiermath)** — Epoch AI (small core team + contracted mathematicians). (What it did:) commissioned expert-written, unpublished math problems (kept off the web so models can't have memorized them); became the reference hard-math benchmark. Also a cautionary tale twice over: the [OpenAI funding-disclosure controversy](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/) and a [June 2026 update fixing errors in 42% of problems](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2) (a large chunk of the benchmark turned out to be wrong).
6. **[Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard](https://gorilla.cs.berkeley.edu/leaderboard.html)** — grad-student-led (Shishir Patil et al., Sky Lab). (What it did:) built the reference test for whether models correctly call external tools/APIs; grew from a course-project-scale effort (Feb 2024) to the industry-standard tool-use benchmark, now [a journal-track paper](https://openreview.net/forum?id=2GmDdhBdDk).
7. **[Every Eval Ever](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14516)** — EvalEval Coalition, Feb 2026. (What it did:) defined a unifying schema (common format) + community repository making eval results traceable/reproducible; results surfaced [directly on HF model pages](https://huggingface.co/blog/eee-community-evals). Exemplar of coalition-style infrastructure work with open co-authorship.
8. **[Toward an Evaluation Science for Generative AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05336)** — Weidinger, Raji, Wallach, Mitchell, et al. (10 authors across DeepMind/Berkeley/MSR/HF/Stanford), 2025. (What it argues:) a position paper (an agenda-setting argument, not new experiments) — static benchmarks have validity problems, audits don't scale, so the field should borrow from aerospace/pharma safety engineering. The intellectual frame most 2026 eval-science work cites.

## Where the gaps are (named, sourced)

- **Cost is now the binding constraint, and it concentrates power.** The [EvalEval cost analysis (Apr 2026)](https://huggingface.co/blog/evaleval/eval-costs-bottleneck) documents $40k for one HAL sweep (running the full agent leaderboard once), ~$9.5k per PaperBench run (a benchmark where models try to reproduce ML papers), 33× cost spreads on identical tasks, and argues "whoever can pay for the evaluation gets to write the leaderboard." Named calls-to-action: standardized artifact sharing, single-vs-repeated-run reliability measurement (does re-running change the ranking?), cost-aware Pareto leaderboards (rankings that trade off capability against price), and **benchmark compression** (shrinking a test to a small representative subset) **for agent evals** (static benchmarks compress 100–200×; agent benchmarks only 2–3.5×) — each is a tractable research problem.
- **Statistical malpractice is still the norm.** [Miller 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) shows the basics were missing; follow-ons like [How to Correctly Report LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.21140) (Nov 2025) (finding: judge-based evals — where one model grades another's output — routinely omit calibrated error reporting) show judge-based evals in particular lack error bars. Directly adjacent to the researcher's published LLM-as-judge work.
- **Validity/benchmark quality control.** FrontierMath's [42%-of-problems error correction (June 2026)](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2) happened *after* the benchmark had been shaping frontier-lab narratives — the field has no routine independent error-auditing of benchmarks. The [Inspect Evals maintenance paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06893) (what it documents: keeping a community eval repo correct is hard, thankless labor) shows the same problem from the repo-maintainer side.
- **Leaderboard governance.** [The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) named specific mechanisms (private variant testing, sampling asymmetry, silent retraction). LMArena [disputed parts](https://arena.ai/blog/our-response/), but its commercialization ($1.7B valuation) keeps the referee-with-revenue conflict live. Independent audit of eval platforms remains nearly unoccupied.
- **Funder independence and disclosure norms.** Epoch/OpenAI (FrontierMath), Scale/Meta (49% stake), and Apollo's nonprofit→PBC conversion all show the independence question is unsettled; METR's "no AI-company money" stance is the exception, and even METR notes independent funding was "crucial" ([about page](https://metr.org/about)). No standard disclosure regime exists yet — Evaluation Cards ([EvalEval](https://huggingface.co/blog/evaleval/evaluation-cards-launch)) is an early attempt.
- **Sustainability of the academic commons** (the shared, non-commercial tooling everyone relies on)**.** [HELM entered maintenance mode June 1, 2026](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm); HF's Open LLM Leaderboard is archived; METR's task bounty is paused. ## Fit notes

The researcher's published LLM-as-a-judge / RAG-hallucination evaluation study sits squarely in the "evaluation science" sub-area that the NeurIPS 2026 track rename, the EvalEval coalition, and the Weidinger et al. position paper all formally institutionalized this year. The named community gaps that require no training compute — judge-evaluation statistics, benchmark error auditing, cost/reliability analysis of existing rollout data, meta-analysis via Every Eval Ever's open schema — match a laptop + ≤$500/study + frontier-API budget, and both Anthropic and OpenAI credit programs subsidize exactly this class of work. Open on-ramps that convert directly into track record exist at every tier: harness/Inspect PRs, EvalEval shared-task co-authorship, AISI bounties, LTFF-scale grants ($20k–80k for 3–12 months). Institutional demand for *independent* third-party evaluators is being created by regulation (EU qualification requirements, CAISI agreements) while the nonprofit/commons layer (HELM, Open LLM Leaderboard, METR bounty) is visibly under-resourced — facts recorded here without ranking against the researcher's other candidate fields.
