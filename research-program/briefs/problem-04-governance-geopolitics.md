# Problem 04 — AI Governance, Regulation & Inter-Government Competition

*Researched July 2026. Grounded in the internal map docs [12 · Geopolitics & policy](../../ai-research-map/12-politics-capital-and-business-models/geopolitics-and-policy.md) and [08 · Safety institutes & governance](../../ai-research-map/08-evaluation-and-governance/safety-institutes-and-governance.md) (both verified against primary sources), plus fresh web research. Claims read directly from a source are linked; anything inferred is labeled.*

## The problem, concretely

The governance machinery being built around frontier AI is **racing a faster capability frontier and nobody can yet measure whether it works**. The [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026) (Bengio + 100+ experts) puts "capability advancing faster than safeguards" as its central finding; UK AISI measures the 80%-reliability autonomous-cyber task horizon [doubling every ~4.7 months](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/how-fast-is-autonomous-ai-cyber-capability-advancing). The measurable sub-problems:

- **Voluntary safety frameworks are weak and quietly weakenable.** SaferAI's rating study ([arXiv:2512.01166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166)) scores 12 providers' frameworks 8%–34% (median 18%) against a 54% "peer ceiling" — best practice exists somewhere but no one adopts it all; 9/12 providers score 0% on identifying *unknown* risks; xAI weakened commitments Feb→Aug 2025 with no rationale. Whether frameworks predict behavior is unmeasured.
- **Binding regulation is arriving with no established measurement layer.** EU AI Act GPAI enforcement powers (fines to 3% global turnover) go live **2 Aug 2026** — the [Digital Omnibus agreed May–June 2026](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions/) delayed *high-risk-system* obligations to Dec 2027/Aug 2028 but [kept the Aug 2026 GPAI/transparency dates live](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/05/07/artificial-intelligence-council-and-parliament-agree-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/). Translating the Act's legal language into runnable benchmarks is embryonic (see COMPL-AI below — models score ~50% on some mapped requirements and some requirements have *no* benchmark at all).
- **US state law is now the de facto US frontier regime, unevaluated.** California [SB 53](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/10/california-sb-53-frontier-ai-law-what-it-does) (Sep 2025) and New York's [RAISE Act](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2026/02/ai-state-law-new-york-raise-act-california-sb53) (Dec 2025) both hinge on 10²⁶-FLOP / $500M-revenue thresholds and mandated transparency reports; nobody yet systematically scores the quality/completeness of the disclosures they compel. Federal preemption remains a live industry push ([Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2026/02/ai-state-law-new-york-raise-act-california-sb53)).
- **Export controls demonstrably shape research but leak at unknown scale.** Controls pushed Chinese labs toward efficiency work (map doc, verified); yet Epoch's April 2026 estimate puts **290k–1.6M H100-equivalents smuggled into China through 2025 (median 660k, ~⅓ of China's AI compute)** — a 5.5× uncertainty band ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling)). BIS already treats model weights >10²⁶ ops as export-controlled ([BIS framework](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/biden-harris-administration-announces-regulatory-framework-responsible-diffusion-advanced-artificial)).
- **Government evaluation capacity exists but its validity is contested.** US CAISI has run 40+ pre-deployment evals; the 10-member [International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/international-network-advanced-ai-measurement-evaluation-and-science) runs joint agent-testing exercises — yet RepliBench's own authors document over/under-estimation cut-both-ways problems and eval-aware sandbagging ([arXiv:2504.18565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18565)), and Epoch argues biorisk evals [measure proxy knowledge, not end-to-end risk](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/do-the-biorisk-evaluations-of-ai-labs-actually-measure-the-risk-of-developing-bioweapons).
- **Treaty/verification research is pre-paradigmatic.** The main works are case-study catalogs of what *might* work (nuclear/CWC analogies), not demonstrations that anything *does* (see exemplars).
- **Procurement is becoming a red-line battleground.** The DoD–Anthropic dispute (supply-chain-risk designation, First Amendment injunction, Mar–Apr 2026; map doc, verified: [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/03/26/business/anthropic-pentagon-injunction-supply-chain-risk)) shows deployment rules being litigated case-by-case with no framework.

## The research field around it

Sub-areas, with methods and maturity:

| Sub-area | What it looks like | Methods | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical AI governance (TAIG)** | Tools/measurements that make governance enforceable | ML experiments, benchmarks, hardware analysis | Agenda-stage: [Open Problems in TAIG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14981) (Reuel, Bucknall + ~30 coauthors) catalogs open problems across data/compute/model/deployment; searchable at [taig.stanford.edu](https://taig.stanford.edu/) |
| **Compute governance** | Using chips/datacenters as the governable chokepoint | Policy analysis + hardware feasibility studies | Most-developed thread; canonical paper [Computing Power and the Governance of AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797) (Sastry, Heim, Belfield, Anderljung, Brundage et al., 2024) |
| **Export-control empirics** | Measuring leakage, stockpiling, smuggling | OSINT, indictment mining, Monte Carlo estimation | Young but quantitative: [Epoch smuggling estimate](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling), [IAPS/Grunewald](https://www.iaps.ai/research/tag/Erich+Grunewald), [CNAS smuggling reports](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/countering-ai-chip-smuggling-has-become-a-national-security-priority) |
| **Regulatory design & its evaluation** | EU AI Act implementation, US state laws, threshold design | Legal→technical translation, scorecards, compliance benchmarks | Very early: [COMPL-AI](https://github.com/compl-ai/compl-ai) is the first Act-to-benchmark mapping; state-law analysis is mostly law-firm commentary ([FPF comparison](https://fpf.org/blog/the-raise-act-vs-sb-53-a-tale-of-two-frontier-ai-laws/)) |
| **Safety-framework watch** | Rating and tracking lab commitments | Rubric scoring, longitudinal document tracking | Functioning cottage industry: [METR Common Elements](https://metr.org/blog/2025-12-09-common-elements-of-frontier-ai-safety-policies/) (12 companies tracked, updated Dec 2025), [SaferAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166), [FLI AI Safety Index](https://futureoflife.org/ai-safety-index-winter-2025/) |
| **Treaty & verification research** | What an international AI agreement could verify | Historical case studies (nuclear/CWC/export control), mechanism design | Pre-paradigmatic: [Wasil et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16074), [Baker et al. six-layer framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15916); hardware-enabled mechanisms mostly working papers ([CNAS](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/technology-to-secure-the-ai-chip-supply-chain-a-primer)) |
| **Safety-institute ecosystem studies** | What AISIs do, joint testing, institutional design | Comparative institutional analysis, participation in joint exercises | Institutes operational (UK AISI, CAISI, EU AI Office, [10-member Network](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/international-network-advanced-ai-measurement-evaluation-and-science)); *research about* them is thin |
| **Dangerous-capability eval validity** | Do the evals justify the governance decisions built on them? | Construct-validity analysis, replication | Named-problem stage: RepliBench self-critique, [Epoch biorisk critique](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/do-the-biorisk-evaluations-of-ai-labs-actually-measure-the-risk-of-developing-bioweapons) |

The field's distinctive feature (read from the [ICML position paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06987), Reuel et al.): a documented **divergence between what policies assume is technically measurable and what actually is** — both the EU AI Act and (former) EO 14110 mandate systemic-risk evaluation capabilities that don't robustly exist.

## Published exemplars

1. **[Diversion and resale: estimating compute smuggling to China](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling)** — Isabel Juniewicz, Epoch, Apr 2026. **Single author.** Two independent estimation strategies (DOJ indictment mining + gray-market vendor observation) combined via Monte Carlo → 290k–1.6M H100e (median 660k). Mattered because it turned an anecdote-driven policy fight into a quantified, criticizable estimate. Pure laptop work: public documents + probabilistic modeling.
2. **[Open Problems in Technical AI Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14981)** — Reuel, Bucknall et al., 2024; searchable problem repo at [taig.stanford.edu](https://taig.stanford.edu/). The field's job board: a taxonomy of concrete technical problems (data/compute/model/deployment) explicitly written "for technical researchers or research funders looking to contribute."
3. **[COMPL-AI](https://github.com/compl-ai/compl-ai)** — ETH Zurich/INSAIT/LatticeFlow, Oct 2024 ([announcement](https://insait.ai/compl-ai/), [ETH story](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2024/10/how-law-abiding-is-ai-eth-researchers-put-it-to-the-test.html)). First technical interpretation of the EU AI Act as 27 runnable benchmarks; found leading models ~50% on cybersecurity/fairness requirements. Mattered as the existence proof that "regulation → benchmark" is a research genre; open-source and accepting contributions.
4. **[Verification methods for international AI agreements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16074)** — Wasil, Reed, Miller, Barnett, 2024. **Four authors, no lab.** Ten verification methods in three access classes, grounded in nuclear/CWC/export-control case studies. Mattered by giving treaty talk a concrete menu; extended by Baker et al.'s [six-layer framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15916) (2025).
5. **[SaferAI risk-management ratings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166)** — small nonprofit team, Dec 2025. 65 weighted criteria × 12 providers = 780 scored pairs; median 18% vs 54% peer ceiling; caught xAI's silent weakening. Mattered because a tiny org built the accountability instrument the frameworks themselves lack — and it's methodologically an *evaluation-design* paper.
6. **[Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety Policies](https://metr.org/blog/2025-12-09-common-elements-of-frontier-ai-safety-policies/)** — METR, updated Dec 2025. Longitudinal comparative tracking of 12 companies' safety policies (thresholds, security, halting conditions). Mattered as the reference document policymakers cite for "what industry practice actually is."
7. **[Computing Power and the Governance of AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797)** — Sastry, Heim et al., 2024. Established compute as *the* governable node (visibility/allocation/enforcement); the intellectual foundation under FLOP thresholds in the EU Act, SB 53, and BIS rules.
8. **[How banned AI chips end up in China](https://blog.peterwildeford.com/p/how-banned-ai-chips-end-up-in-china)** — Erich Grunewald (IAPS), essay-form OSINT synthesis; with his [IAPS report estimating ~100k+ smuggled GPUs](https://www.iaps.ai/research/tag/Erich+Grunewald) and CNAS collaborations. Mattered as proof that one persistent researcher doing open-source investigation can set the empirical terms of an export-control debate.

## Where the gaps are

- **Regulation-to-measurement translation is one paper deep.** COMPL-AI covers the Act's GPAI-adjacent principles with 27 existing benchmarks and reports that some requirements have weak or missing benchmarks entirely ([ETH](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2024/10/how-law-abiding-is-ai-eth-researchers-put-it-to-the-test.html)); nothing equivalent exists for the [GPAI Code of Practice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-code-practice) obligations that become enforceable Aug 2 2026, or for SB 53/RAISE transparency reports.
- **Nobody scores the disclosures that SB 53 and the RAISE Act compel.** The laws mandate frameworks + transparency reports ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/californias-sb-53-the-first-frontier-ai-law-explained/)); SaferAI/FLI/METR score *voluntary* frameworks, not statutory filings — a statute-anchored scorecard is an open niche (inferred from absence in all opened sources).
- **Eval construct validity is a named-but-unworked problem.** Epoch's [biorisk critique](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/do-the-biorisk-evaluations-of-ai-labs-actually-measure-the-risk-of-developing-bioweapons) and RepliBench's own caveats ([arXiv:2504.18565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18565) — component tasks overestimate, lax security underestimates, judge models unreliable, sandbagging observed) are critiques; systematic validity studies of governance-cited evals don't exist yet.
- **FLOP thresholds are eroding and the erosion is unquantified in policy terms.** The map doc (verified) cites Epoch projecting >200 models above 10²⁶ FLOP by 2030; SB 53, RAISE, EU systemic-risk tier, and the BIS weight rule all pin to 10²⁶ — no published analysis models regulatory-coverage decay across these four instruments jointly (inferred from absence).
- **Verification research lacks any empirical demonstration.** Wasil et al. and Baker et al. are case-study/design papers; no published prototype shows e.g. that a training run can be verified adversarially (the papers themselves frame this as future work — [arXiv:2408.16074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16074)).
- **Smuggling estimates span 5.5×.** Epoch's own 90% CI is 290k–1.6M H100e ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling)) — driven by unknown detection rates (10–80%); better data pipelines directly narrow a number policymakers actively use.
- **The technical-talent gap in governance institutions is itself documented.** The ICML position paper ([Reuel et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06987)) surveys EU/US/China policy documents and finds "significant divergence between current technical solutions and those necessary for enacting proposed policy actions," and calls for more technical expertise *inside* governing institutions — i.e., the field's own literature says people who can run evals are scarce in policy work.
- **Research *about* the safety institutes is thin.** The institutes publish evals; almost no independent work audits their methods, reproducibility, or the joint-testing exercises of the [International Network](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/international-network-advanced-ai-measurement-evaluation-and-science) (inferred from absence across opened sources).

## What a solo researcher could do here

Concrete study shapes matching the profile (eval methodology, LLM-as-judge experience, data pipelines, laptop + APIs + ≤$500 GPU). Facts and precedents only — no ranking:

1. **Statute-anchored disclosure scorecard.** Build a rubric from SB 53/RAISE statutory requirements, then score every published frontier-developer framework and transparency report with an LLM-assisted coding pipeline + human validation (inter-rater reliability reported). Direct precedent: SaferAI's 780-pair rubric study ([arXiv:2512.01166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166)) was done by a small nonprofit; METR's [Common Elements](https://metr.org/common-elements) shows the document-tracking format. Cost: API calls only.
2. **GPAI Code of Practice → benchmark mapping.** Extend [COMPL-AI](https://github.com/compl-ai/compl-ai) (open-source, accepts contributions) to the Code of Practice commitments enforceable from Aug 2026; run the suite on 3–5 open-weight models on a rented GPU. The original project itself demonstrated the genre with 27 repurposed benchmarks.
3. **Construct-validity audit of a governance-cited eval.** Take one eval class that appears in transparency reports or AISI publications (e.g., cyber-uplift or the RepliBench task families) and test its judge reliability — exactly the LLM-as-a-judge validity methodology from the published RAG-hallucination study, aimed at RepliBench's own admitted weakness (Claude 3.5 Haiku judges "may misjudge complex human interaction," [arXiv:2504.18565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18565)).
4. **Threshold-erosion model.** Use Epoch's public model database to quantify how many models cross 10²⁶ FLOP (and equivalent-capability-below-threshold models) through 2028 under efficiency trends, and translate into "regulatory coverage decay" for SB 53 / RAISE / EU systemic tier / BIS ECCN 4E091 jointly. Classical data-pipeline + forecasting work; the map doc (verified) confirms this is a flagged-but-unworked concern.
5. **Smuggling-estimate replication/extension.** Re-implement Juniewicz's [Monte Carlo pipeline](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling) from the described method, add post-2025 indictments and trade data, and publish sensitivity analysis narrowing (or honestly widening) the 290k–1.6M band. Single-author precedent already exists at Epoch.
6. **Safety-institute eval reproducibility study.** Attempt to reproduce a published AISI/CAISI evaluation result on open-weight models and document what's reproducible from public artifacts — feeding the documented gap that no independent audits of institute methodology exist. Funding fit: [UK AISI Challenge Fund](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/grants) explicitly funds external academic/nonprofit researchers at £50k–£200k.

## What we still don't know

- Whether framework/disclosure scores **predict** anything (incidents, behavior change) — no outcome-linkage study found; the whole scorecard genre could be measuring paperwork quality. ⚠️ unverified either way.
- How the EU AI Office will actually operationalize GPAI enforcement after Aug 2 2026 — which evals, which third parties, what evidentiary standard (no opened source specifies).
- Whether Coefficient Giving (ex-Open Phil) will fund **individuals without org affiliation** at small scale in the next RFP round — the closed RFP said individuals were eligible at $200k–$2M/yr; micro-grant availability is unclear.
- Whether independent researchers can get the **data** for several of these studies (statutory filings are public, but eval transcripts/institute artifacts may not be) — untested assumption in study shapes 3 and 6.
- The real politics-side half-life of any 2026 analysis: preemption fights, the omnibus, and the DoD–Anthropic litigation are all live; findings may date in months. (Inferred risk, not sourced.)
- Whether the field rewards part-time contributors as it did in 2024–25 (Ball, Grunewald arcs) now that it is professionalizing — no direct evidence either way.

## Fit notes

The domain's scarcest input — per its own literature ([Reuel et al., ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06987)) — is people who can run measurements, which is the profile's core strength (evaluation methodology, LLM-as-judge publication, data pipelines). Nearly all high-leverage work here is laptop + API scale: document scoring, Monte Carlo estimation, benchmark mapping — the $500/study GPU budget is only needed for open-weight model runs (study shapes 2, 6). The fellowship pipeline (IAPS remote-compatible $15k; RAND CAST explicitly part-time-possible; GovAI/ERA seasonal) is structurally compatible with a part-time researcher, and the Ball/Grunewald arcs show independent published analysis is a recognized entry route. The published RAG-hallucination-judge study maps directly onto the field's open construct-validity problem — the same methodology pointed at governance-cited evals instead of RAG systems.
