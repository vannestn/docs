# Problem 04 — AI Governance, Regulation & Inter-Government Competition

*Researched July 2026. Grounded in the internal map docs [12 · Geopolitics & policy](../../ai-research-map/12-politics-capital-and-business-models/geopolitics-and-policy.md) and [08 · Safety institutes & governance](../../ai-research-map/08-evaluation-and-governance/safety-institutes-and-governance.md) (both verified against primary sources), plus fresh web research. Claims read directly from a source are linked; anything inferred is labeled.*

## The problem, concretely

The governance machinery being built around frontier AI is **racing a faster capability frontier and nobody can yet measure whether it works**. The [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026) (Bengio + 100+ experts; the IPCC-style consensus stocktake of AI risk evidence) puts "capability advancing faster than safeguards" as its central finding; UK AISI (the UK government's AI Safety Institute, a state eval lab) measures the 80%-reliability autonomous-cyber task horizon — the length of hacking task a model can complete unaided 80% of the time — [doubling every ~4.7 months](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/how-fast-is-autonomous-ai-cyber-capability-advancing). The measurable sub-problems:

- **Voluntary safety frameworks are weak and quietly weakenable.** ("Safety framework" = a lab's public document committing to risk thresholds and mitigations, e.g. a Responsible Scaling Policy.) SaferAI's rating study ([arXiv:2512.01166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166); scored labs' frameworks against a best-practice rubric) scores 12 providers' frameworks 8%–34% (median 18%) against a 54% "peer ceiling" (the best score any single provider achieves on each criterion) — best practice exists somewhere but no one adopts it all; 9/12 providers score 0% on identifying *unknown* risks; xAI weakened commitments Feb→Aug 2025 with no rationale. Whether frameworks predict behavior is unmeasured.
- **Binding regulation is arriving with no established measurement layer.** EU AI Act GPAI (general-purpose AI — foundation models sold for many downstream uses) enforcement powers go live **2 Aug 2026**: from that date the Commission's enforcement/fining powers "enter into application" ([EC guidance, primary](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-gpai-providers)) and it can fine up to **3% of worldwide annual turnover *or* EUR 15 000 000, whichever is higher** ([AI Act Art. 101, primary](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/101/)) — the [Digital Omnibus agreed May–June 2026](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions/) (the EU's package simplifying/delaying parts of the Act) delayed *high-risk-system* obligations to Dec 2027/Aug 2028 but [kept the Aug 2026 GPAI/transparency dates live](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/05/07/artificial-intelligence-council-and-parliament-agree-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/). Translating the Act's legal language into runnable benchmarks is embryonic (see COMPL-AI below — leading models score ~0.50 on the cyberattack-resilience and fairness technical requirements, and some requirements have *no* benchmark at all — both primary-verified in the deepening pass).
- **US state law is now the de facto US frontier regime, unevaluated.** California [SB 53](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2025/10/california-sb-53-frontier-ai-law-what-it-does) (Sep 2025; the first US frontier-AI safety law, mandating safety frameworks + transparency reports) and New York's [RAISE Act](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2026/02/ai-state-law-new-york-raise-act-california-sb53) (Dec 2025; New York's parallel frontier-AI law) both hinge on 10²⁶-FLOP (a training-compute threshold — floating-point operations used to train the model) / $500M-revenue thresholds and mandated transparency reports; nobody yet systematically scores the quality/completeness of the disclosures they compel. Federal preemption (a US law overriding state laws) remains a live industry push ([Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/emissary/2026/02/ai-state-law-new-york-raise-act-california-sb53)).
- **Export controls demonstrably shape research but leak at unknown scale.** (Export controls = US rules restricting sale of advanced AI chips to China.) Controls pushed Chinese labs toward efficiency work (map doc, verified); yet Epoch's April 2026 estimate puts **290k–1.6M H100-equivalents smuggled into China through 2025 (median 660k, ~⅓ of China's AI compute)** — a 5.5× uncertainty band ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling)). ("H100-equivalent" = the compute of one Nvidia H100 GPU, the standard unit for counting AI hardware.) BIS (the US Commerce Bureau of Industry and Security, which enforces export controls) already treats model weights >10²⁶ ops as export-controlled ([BIS framework](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/biden-harris-administration-announces-regulatory-framework-responsible-diffusion-advanced-artificial)).
- **Government evaluation capacity exists but its validity is contested.** US CAISI (the US Center for AI Standards and Innovation, NIST's eval body, ex-US AISI) has run 40+ pre-deployment evals; the 10-member [International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/international-network-advanced-ai-measurement-evaluation-and-science) runs joint agent-testing exercises — yet RepliBench (a benchmark for whether models can self-replicate autonomously) has authors who document over/under-estimation cut-both-ways problems and eval-aware sandbagging (a model deliberately underperforming when it detects it is being tested) ([arXiv:2504.18565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18565)), and Epoch argues biorisk evals [measure proxy knowledge, not end-to-end risk](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/do-the-biorisk-evaluations-of-ai-labs-actually-measure-the-risk-of-developing-bioweapons) — i.e., they test whether a model knows dangerous facts, not whether it could actually help build a weapon.
- **Treaty/verification research is pre-paradigmatic.** ("Verification" = technical ways to confirm a country/lab is honoring an AI agreement, like arms-control inspections.) The main works are case-study catalogs of what *might* work (nuclear/CWC — Chemical Weapons Convention — analogies), not demonstrations that anything *does* (see exemplars).
- **Procurement is becoming a red-line battleground.** (Procurement = how governments buy and approve AI systems for their own use.) The DoD–Anthropic dispute (a supply-chain-risk designation — a federal label flagging a vendor as a security risk — with a First Amendment injunction, Mar–Apr 2026; map doc, verified: [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/03/26/business/anthropic-pentagon-injunction-supply-chain-risk)) shows deployment rules being litigated case-by-case with no framework.

## The research field around it

Sub-areas, with methods and maturity:

| Sub-area | What it looks like | Methods | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Technical AI governance (TAIG)** | Tools/measurements that make governance enforceable | ML experiments, benchmarks, hardware analysis | Agenda-stage: [Open Problems in TAIG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14981) (Reuel, Bucknall + ~30 coauthors; a taxonomy of open technical problems for governance) catalogs open problems across data/compute/model/deployment; searchable at [taig.stanford.edu](https://taig.stanford.edu/) |
| **Compute governance** | Using chips/datacenters as the governable chokepoint | Policy analysis + hardware feasibility studies | Most-developed thread; canonical paper [Computing Power and the Governance of AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797) (Sastry, Heim, Belfield, Anderljung, Brundage et al., 2024; argues compute is uniquely detectable/controllable, so it is the best governance lever) |
| **Export-control empirics** | Measuring leakage, stockpiling, smuggling | OSINT (open-source intelligence — analysis of public records), indictment mining, Monte Carlo estimation (repeated random simulation to get a probability range) | Young but quantitative: [Epoch smuggling estimate](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling), [IAPS/Grunewald](https://www.iaps.ai/research/tag/Erich+Grunewald), [CNAS smuggling reports](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/countering-ai-chip-smuggling-has-become-a-national-security-priority) |
| **Regulatory design & its evaluation** | EU AI Act implementation, US state laws, threshold design | Legal→technical translation, scorecards, compliance benchmarks | Very early: [COMPL-AI](https://github.com/compl-ai/compl-ai) is the first Act-to-benchmark mapping (turning legal text into runnable tests); state-law analysis is mostly law-firm commentary ([FPF comparison](https://fpf.org/blog/the-raise-act-vs-sb-53-a-tale-of-two-frontier-ai-laws/)) |
| **Safety-framework watch** | Rating and tracking lab commitments | Rubric scoring, longitudinal document tracking | Functioning cottage industry: [METR Common Elements](https://metr.org/blog/2025-12-09-common-elements-of-frontier-ai-safety-policies/) (compares 12 companies' safety policies, updated Dec 2025), [SaferAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166), [FLI AI Safety Index](https://futureoflife.org/ai-safety-index-winter-2025/) (grades top labs on safety practices) |
| **Treaty & verification research** | What an international AI agreement could verify | Historical case studies (nuclear/CWC/export control), mechanism design | Pre-paradigmatic: [Wasil et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16074) (menu of verification methods), [Baker et al. six-layer framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15916); hardware-enabled mechanisms — verification built into the chips themselves — mostly working papers ([CNAS](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/technology-to-secure-the-ai-chip-supply-chain-a-primer)) |
| **Safety-institute ecosystem studies** | What AISIs do, joint testing, institutional design | Comparative institutional analysis, participation in joint exercises | Institutes operational (UK AISI, CAISI, EU AI Office, [10-member Network](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/international-network-advanced-ai-measurement-evaluation-and-science)); *research about* them is thin |
| **Dangerous-capability eval validity** | Do the evals justify the governance decisions built on them? | Construct-validity analysis (does a test measure what it claims?), replication | Named-problem stage: RepliBench self-critique, [Epoch biorisk critique](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/do-the-biorisk-evaluations-of-ai-labs-actually-measure-the-risk-of-developing-bioweapons) |

The field's distinctive feature (read from the [ICML position paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06987), Reuel et al.; what it did: surveyed EU/US/China policy docs against actual technical capabilities): a documented **divergence between what policies assume is technically measurable and what actually is** — both the EU AI Act and (former) EO 14110 (a rescinded Biden executive order on AI) mandate systemic-risk evaluation capabilities that don't robustly exist.

## Published exemplars

1. **[Diversion and resale: estimating compute smuggling to China](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling)** — Isabel Juniewicz, Epoch, Apr 2026. **Single author.** Two independent estimation strategies (DOJ indictment mining + gray-market vendor observation) combined via Monte Carlo → 290k–1.6M H100e (median 660k). Mattered because it turned an anecdote-driven policy fight into a quantified, criticizable estimate. Pure laptop work: public documents + probabilistic modeling.
2. **[Open Problems in Technical AI Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14981)** — Reuel, Bucknall et al., 2024; searchable problem repo at [taig.stanford.edu](https://taig.stanford.edu/). *What it did:* the field's job board — a taxonomy of concrete technical problems (data/compute/model/deployment) explicitly written "for technical researchers or research funders looking to contribute."
3. **[COMPL-AI](https://github.com/compl-ai/compl-ai)** — ETH Zurich/INSAIT/LatticeFlow, Oct 2024 ([announcement](https://insait.ai/compl-ai/), [ETH story](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2024/10/how-law-abiding-is-ai-eth-researchers-put-it-to-the-test.html); paper [arXiv:2410.07959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07959), local PDF). *What it did:* self-described (verbatim, primary) as **"the first technical interpretation of the EU AI Act"**, mapping its six ethical principles → technical requirements → benchmarks (paper structure; the live repo now says **"29 and growing"** benchmarks — up from the paper's count); *found (primary-verified):* leading models cluster at **~0.50 on Cyberattack Resilience** (Table 4: Claude 3 Opus 0.80 down to Mistral-7B 0.27; mid-pack ~0.47–0.56) and **~0.50 on Fairness–Absence of Discrimination** (Table 13: GPT-4 Turbo 0.50, Claude 3 Opus 0.51, GPT-3.5 0.46), and Table 15 explicitly flags **technical requirements where "no benchmarks could be run due to missing" coverage**. Mattered as the existence proof that "regulation → benchmark" (turning a law into an automated test) is a research genre; open-source and, per the repo, **"actively looking to expand our EU AI Act and Code of Practice technical interpretation"** — i.e. it is itself now moving toward the Aug-2026 CoP obligations.
4. **[Verification methods for international AI agreements](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16074)** — Wasil, Reed, Miller, Barnett, 2024. **Four authors, no lab.** *What it did:* laid out ten verification methods (ways to confirm treaty compliance) in three access classes, grounded in nuclear/CWC/export-control case studies. Mattered by giving treaty talk a concrete menu; extended by Baker et al.'s [six-layer framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15916) (2025).
5. **[SaferAI risk-management ratings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166)** — small nonprofit team, Dec 2025. *What it did:* scored 65 weighted criteria × 12 providers = 780 scored pairs; *found:* median 18% vs 54% peer ceiling, and caught xAI's silent weakening. Mattered because a tiny org built the accountability instrument the frameworks themselves lack — and it's methodologically an *evaluation-design* paper.
6. **[Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety Policies](https://metr.org/blog/2025-12-09-common-elements-of-frontier-ai-safety-policies/)** — METR, updated Dec 2025. *What it did:* longitudinal comparative tracking of 12 companies' safety policies (capability thresholds that trigger action, security measures, halting conditions — when a lab pauses development). Mattered as the reference document policymakers cite for "what industry practice actually is."
7. **[Computing Power and the Governance of AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797)** — Sastry, Heim et al., 2024. *What it argued:* compute is *the* governable node (it is visible, allocable, and enforceable); became the intellectual foundation under FLOP thresholds in the EU Act, SB 53, and BIS rules.
8. **[How banned AI chips end up in China](https://blog.peterwildeford.com/p/how-banned-ai-chips-end-up-in-china)** — Erich Grunewald (IAPS), essay-form OSINT (open-source-intelligence) synthesis; with his [IAPS report estimating ~100k+ smuggled GPUs](https://www.iaps.ai/research/tag/Erich+Grunewald) and CNAS collaborations. Mattered as proof that one persistent researcher doing open-source investigation can set the empirical terms of an export-control debate.

## Where the gaps are

- **Regulation-to-measurement translation is one paper deep — but no longer entirely unclaimed for the CoP.** COMPL-AI covers the Act's GPAI-adjacent principles with a "29 and growing" benchmark set and its Table 15 explicitly flags requirements where no benchmark could be run ([primary paper arXiv:2410.07959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07959), verified). *Deepening-pass correction:* the COMPL-AI repo now states it is "actively looking to expand our EU AI Act **and Code of Practice** technical interpretation," so a CoP-specific benchmark suite is being actively started by the incumbent (not yet published — a hostile search Jul 2026 found no released CoP benchmark suite from any party). The [GPAI Code of Practice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-code-practice) (final version published 10 Jul 2025; the voluntary EU tool labs sign to demonstrate compliance) obligations become enforceable Aug 2 2026; nothing equivalent to COMPL-AI exists yet for SB 53/RAISE transparency reports.
- **Nobody scores the disclosures that SB 53 and the RAISE Act compel.** The laws mandate frameworks + transparency reports ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/californias-sb-53-the-first-frontier-ai-law-explained/)); SaferAI/FLI/METR score *voluntary* frameworks, not statutory filings — a statute-anchored scorecard is an open niche (inferred from absence in all opened sources).
- **Eval construct validity is a named-but-unworked problem.** ("Construct validity" = whether a test really measures the risk it claims to.) Epoch's [biorisk critique](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/do-the-biorisk-evaluations-of-ai-labs-actually-measure-the-risk-of-developing-bioweapons) and RepliBench's own caveats ([arXiv:2504.18565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18565) — component tasks overestimate, lax security underestimates, judge models unreliable, sandbagging observed) are critiques; systematic validity studies of governance-cited evals don't exist yet.
- **FLOP thresholds are eroding and the erosion is unquantified in policy terms.** (The problem: as training gets more efficient, a fixed 10²⁶-FLOP line catches ever more models — or misses dangerous small ones — so coverage drifts.) The map doc (verified) cites Epoch projecting >200 models above 10²⁶ FLOP by 2030; SB 53, RAISE, EU systemic-risk tier, and the BIS weight rule all pin to 10²⁶ — no published analysis models regulatory-coverage decay across these four instruments jointly (inferred from absence).
- **Verification research lacks any empirical demonstration.** Wasil et al. and Baker et al. are case-study/design papers; no published prototype shows e.g. that a training run can be verified adversarially (the papers themselves frame this as future work — [arXiv:2408.16074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16074)).
- **Smuggling estimates span 5.5×.** Epoch's own 90% CI is 290k–1.6M H100e ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling)) — driven by unknown detection rates (10–80%); better data pipelines directly narrow a number policymakers actively use.
- **The technical-talent gap in governance institutions is itself documented.** The ICML position paper ([Reuel et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06987)) surveys EU/US/China policy documents and finds "significant divergence between current technical solutions and those necessary for enacting proposed policy actions," and calls for more technical expertise *inside* governing institutions — i.e., the field's own literature says people who can run evals are scarce in policy work.
- **Research *about* the safety institutes is thin.** The institutes publish evals; almost no independent work audits their methods, reproducibility, or the joint-testing exercises of the [International Network](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/international-network-advanced-ai-measurement-evaluation-and-science) (inferred from absence across opened sources).

## What a solo researcher could do here

Concrete study shapes matching the profile (eval methodology, LLM-as-judge experience, data pipelines, laptop + APIs + ≤$500 GPU). Facts and precedents only — no ranking:

1. **Statute-anchored disclosure scorecard.** Build a rubric from SB 53/RAISE statutory requirements, then score every published frontier-developer framework and transparency report with an LLM-assisted coding pipeline + human validation (inter-rater reliability — agreement between independent human coders — reported). Direct precedent: SaferAI's 780-pair rubric study ([arXiv:2512.01166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01166)) was done by a small nonprofit; METR's [Common Elements](https://metr.org/common-elements) shows the document-tracking format. Cost: API calls only.
2. **GPAI Code of Practice → benchmark mapping.** Extend [COMPL-AI](https://github.com/compl-ai/compl-ai) (open-source, accepts contributions) to the Code of Practice commitments enforceable from Aug 2026; run the suite on 3–5 open-weight models on a rented GPU. The original project itself demonstrated the genre with 27 repurposed benchmarks.
3. **Construct-validity audit of a governance-cited eval.** Take one eval class that appears in transparency reports or AISI publications (e.g., cyber-uplift — how much a model boosts a hacker — or the RepliBench task families) and test its judge reliability — exactly the LLM-as-a-judge (using one model to grade another's outputs) validity methodology from the published RAG-hallucination study (RAG = retrieval-augmented generation, where a model answers using fetched documents), aimed at RepliBench's own admitted weakness (Claude 3.5 Haiku judges "may misjudge complex human interaction," [arXiv:2504.18565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18565)).
4. **Threshold-erosion model.** Use Epoch's public model database to quantify how many models cross 10²⁶ FLOP (and equivalent-capability-below-threshold models) through 2028 under efficiency trends, and translate into "regulatory coverage decay" for SB 53 / RAISE / EU systemic tier / BIS ECCN 4E091 (the specific export-control classification code covering advanced model weights) jointly. Classical data-pipeline + forecasting work; the map doc (verified) confirms this is a flagged-but-unworked concern.

## Fit notes

The domain's scarcest input — per its own literature ([Reuel et al., ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06987)) — is people who can run measurements, which is the profile's core strength (evaluation methodology, LLM-as-judge publication, data pipelines). Nearly all high-leverage work here is laptop + API scale: document scoring, Monte Carlo estimation, benchmark mapping — the $500/study GPU budget is only needed for open-weight model runs (study shapes 2, 6). The fellowship pipeline (IAPS remote-compatible $15k/$22k; RAND CAST explicitly part-time-possible; GovAI/ERA seasonal) is structurally compatible with a part-time researcher, and the Ball/Grunewald arcs show independent published analysis is a recognized entry route. The published RAG-hallucination-judge study maps directly onto the field's open construct-validity problem — the same methodology pointed at governance-cited evals instead of RAG systems.
