# Career + impact paths through evaluation work — documented cases (mid-2026)

**Scope:** concrete, sourced stories of people and teams who established themselves via benchmarks/evals; how eval work converts into lab roles, nonprofits, and companies. Compiled 2026-07; every claim traces to a page opened during research unless flagged ⚠️.

---

## The shape of this space

Eval work in 2026 splits into six sub-areas, each with a distinct conversion path from artifact → career:

1. **Benchmark construction** — building the test itself (a *benchmark* is a fixed set of tasks used to score and compare models): SWE-bench (fixing real software bugs), MMLU/MATH (broad knowledge / competition math), FrontierMath (research-level math), ARC-AGI (abstract visual reasoning puzzles), Vending-Bench (running a simulated business over many steps). Conversion: labs adopt your benchmark → you become the reference point → hires, faculty offers, or a foundation forms around it.
2. **Live evaluation platforms** — continuous, community- or crowd-fed leaderboards (*leaderboard* = a public ranking of models by score, updated as new models arrive): [Chatbot Arena → LMArena](https://research.contrary.com/company/lmarena), [HF Open LLM Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard/discussions/1135), [Artificial Analysis](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/680302-90). Conversion: traffic + lab dependence → venture funding or org budget.
3. **Frontier-safety / dangerous-capability evals** — third-party auditing of frontier models (*dangerous-capability eval* = testing whether a model can do something harmful, e.g. help build a weapon or act autonomously): [METR](https://metr.org/about), [Apollo Research](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/FG6icLPKizEaWHex5/announcing-apollo-research), [UK AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/careers), lab preparedness teams. Conversion: philanthropic grants → nonprofit; or government hiring.
4. **Eval criticism & meta-science** — studying whether evals measure anything ([Kapoor & Narayanan](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691249131/ai-snake-oil), [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990), [The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879), [Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). Conversion: academic reputation, policy influence, book/newsletter audiences.
5. **Eval infrastructure & tooling** — harnesses and observability products (an *eval harness* is the software that runs a model against a benchmark and scores it; *observability* = tooling to log/trace/inspect what a deployed model actually does): [lm-evaluation-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness), AISI's Inspect, [LangSmith](https://www.langchain.com/blog/series-b), [Braintrust](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-braintrust/), [Langfuse](https://langfuse.com/blog/announcing-our-seed-round), [Patronus AI](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/patronus-ai-lands-50m-to-build-digital-worlds-that-stress-test-ai-agents/). Conversion: open-source adoption → venture-funded company or acquisition.
6. **Data/trend analysis on top of evals** — measuring the trajectory of AI itself ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/about/careers)). Conversion: grant-funded research org with remote analyst roles.

The recurring mechanism across all six: **ship a public artifact that labs come to depend on** (benchmark, leaderboard, harness, or critique), then the institutional form (hire / nonprofit / startup) follows the dependence.

## Who does this work

### Nonprofits / grant-funded orgs

- **[METR](https://metr.org/about)** — dangerous-capability + autonomy evals on frontier models (*autonomy eval* = can the model complete long, multi-step tasks on its own without a human in the loop). Founded by **Beth Barnes**, ex-OpenAI alignment researcher who left in 2022 to start ARC Evals inside the Alignment Research Center; [spun out Dec 2023 as an independent 501(c)(3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METR). Funded by Open Philanthropy, Survival and Flourishing Fund, and a [$220k Longview Philanthropy grant](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/charities/arc-evals); **explicitly takes no funding from AI companies** (uses free compute credits). Hiring: [paid take-home tasks + paid 1–2 day work trials in Berkeley](https://metr.org/hiring); historically ran a **[task-development bounty](https://taskdev.metr.org/bounty/)** paying $300/hour-of-professional-task-time (>$100k paid out) and [explicitly used bounties to find and fast-track hires](https://metr.org/blog/2023-12-16-bounty-diverse-hard-tasks-for-llm-agents/) — bounty currently closed to new submissions.
- **[Apollo Research](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/FG6icLPKizEaWHex5/announcing-apollo-research)** (London) — scheming/deception evals + interpretability (*scheming eval* = does the model pursue hidden goals or deceive its operators; *interpretability* = techniques to see inside a model and explain why it produced an output); positions itself as an external auditor of AGI labs. Founded 2023 by **Marius Hobbhahn** (CEO, [TIME100 AI 2025](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/team/)); launched [fiscally sponsored by Rethink Priorities](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/ysC6crBKhDBGZfob3/announcing-apollo-research) with a stated first-year funding gap of ~$1.4M and a [Manifund appeal](https://manifund.org/projects/apollo-research-scale-up-interpretability--behavioral-model-evals-research) — i.e., it bootstrapped on EA-adjacent philanthropy, not venture money. Size: ⚠️ unverified (team page lists staff but I did not count a dated roster).
- **[Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/about/careers)** — data/trends org (also builds benchmarks: FrontierMath, SWE-bench Verified tracking). Founded April 2022 by **[Jaime Sevilla](https://time.com/6985850/jaime-sevilla-epoch-ai/)**, who was an Open Philanthropy contractor immediately before; [~75% of funding from Open Philanthropy, ~$21.7M in grants](https://grokipedia.com/page/Epoch_AI) (⚠️ figures from a third-party wiki; verify against Open Phil grant database). **Most relevant hiring model in this brief:** fully remote, hires internationally, and has hired [part-time associate data analysts as contractors at 5–15 hrs/week](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8mxvFzzW7HBLfBj7W/epoch-ai-is-hiring-for-multiple-roles) to extract data from ML papers; [current openings](https://epoch.ai/about/careers) include Researcher (Benchmarks/Evaluations), Software Engineer (Benchmarking), contract data scientists, plus a standing "Expression of Interest" form.
- **[ARC Prize Foundation](https://arcprize.org/about)** — nonprofit formed Jan 2025 around Chollet's ARC-AGI benchmark; board = **François Chollet** (also co-founder of the Ndea lab) and **Mike Knoop** (Zapier co-founder), president = **Greg Kamradt**, an [ex-Salesforce engineering director who ran an AI product studio](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/08/ai-researcher-francois-chollet-is-co-founding-a-nonprofit-to-build-benchmarks-for-agi/) — i.e., a non-academic who converted community eval work into leading a foundation. Open participation via Kaggle competitions.
- **EleutherAI** — nonprofit research lab; maintains [lm-evaluation-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness), the de-facto open-source eval framework (backend of HF's leaderboard). Open-source contribution is the on-ramp; maintainer **Hailey Schoelkopf** built her research career largely on it ([her site](https://haileyschoelkopf.github.io/) lists eval rigor as a core interest). ⚠️ I could not verify reports that she moved to a frontier lab; her site still says Research Scientist, EleutherAI.

## Published exemplars (small-team / solo weighted)

1. **[SWE-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770)** — Jimenez, Yang + 4, Princeton/UChicago; two of the leads were students. Why it landed: real GitHub issues with executable tests, released exactly as coding agents took off; became the industry-standard coding-agent metric, spawning [SWE-bench Verified](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/) (OpenAI, 500 human-validated tasks). Lifecycle lesson: Verified was [reportedly deprecated by OpenAI Feb 2026 over test flaws and contamination](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/swe-bench_verified) (*contamination* = benchmark questions leaked into a model's training data, so a high score reflects memorization, not skill) ⚠️ (wiki source — verify against an OpenAI statement).
2. **[Chatbot Arena](https://research.contrary.com/company/lmarena)** — a handful of Berkeley grad students; 3.5M+ preference votes across 400+ models by Sept 2025 (users pick which of two anonymous model replies is better; votes are aggregated into a ranking). Why it landed: it answered "which model is actually better for people" when static benchmarks saturated (*saturated* = top models all near the max score, so the test no longer separates them); every major lab began citing Arena Elo (an *Elo* rating, borrowed from chess, ranks players/models from pairwise win/loss records). Converted into a $600M company. (The same LMSYS group's MT-Bench / "LLM-as-a-judge" paper — using one strong model to grade another's answers — underpins the methodology; ⚠️ arXiv 2306.05685, from memory, not re-opened this round.)
3. **[AidanBench](https://github.com/aidanmclaughlin/AidanBench)** — 3 people, no institution; measures novelty/creativity on open-ended questions; [accepted at NeurIPS 2024 (workshop)](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/106567). Why it landed: deliberately measured what big benchmarks didn't ("big model smell"), showed weak correlation with standard benchmarks, and was aggressively socialized on X. Author now at OpenAI.
4. **[Vending-Bench](https://grokipedia.com/page/Vending-Bench)** — 2-founder startup (Andon Labs). Why it landed: first widely-noticed long-horizon agent-coherence eval (running a simulated vending business); Anthropic ran the physical version in its own office as [Project Vend](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1), making Andon a named research partner of a frontier lab.
5. **[AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)** — Kapoor, Stroebl, Siegel, Nadgir, Narayanan (5 authors, Princeton). Why it landed: showed agent benchmarks ignore cost, encourage needless complexity, and mislead on accuracy gains; introduced cost-accuracy joint optimization (score an agent on accuracy *and* dollar/compute cost together, not accuracy alone); grew into the [Holistic Agent Leaderboard (HAL)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11977) infrastructure.
6. **[RewardBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13787)** — Lambert et al., Ai2. Why it landed: literally the first evaluation for reward models (a *reward model* scores how good a response is and is the signal used to fine-tune LLMs via RLHF); created a subfield (now [RewardBench 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01937)) and anchored the author's public-writing-driven career.
7. **[Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)** — **solo author** (Evan Miller, Anthropic). Why it landed: pointed out that evals are experiments yet ignore experiment-analysis statistics; showed *clustered standard errors* (the honest ± when questions come in related groups, e.g. several questions off one passage) can be 3× the naive error bars — so many "A beats B" gaps aren't statistically real. Now the standard citation for eval statistics.
8. **[BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990)** — two Stanford student leads + collaborators; NeurIPS 2024 spotlight. Why it landed: assessed 24 benchmarks against 46 best practices and found most don't report statistical significance or support replication; turned "benchmarks are broken" from vibes into a rubric with a [living repository](https://betterbench.stanford.edu/).

## Where the gaps are

Named gaps, each grounded in a specific source read this round:

- **No science of evals.** Apollo's ["We Need a Science of Evals"](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/science/we-need-a-science-of-evals/) argues eval results are not yet resistant to scrutiny (minor prompt changes flip results) and **explicitly invites external collaborators, including individual contributors**, to build the field; their companion ["The Evals Gap"](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/the-evals-gap/) argues capability evals are not keeping pace with model progress. Their own [research note](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/research-note-our-scheming-precursor-evals-had-limited-predictive-power-for-our-in-context-scheming-evals/) that precursor evals failed to predict scheming evals is a rare published negative result — the predictive-validity question (does a cheap early-warning eval actually forecast the real thing?) is wide open.
- **Statistical malpractice is the norm.** [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990): most of 24 assessed benchmarks report no statistical significance and are hard to replicate. [Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640): the eval literature ignores experiment planning; clustered SEs 3× naive. Concrete method work here is cheap in compute and undersupplied.
- **Leaderboard governance / data asymmetry.** [The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) (Cohere + Stanford/Princeton/MIT/Ai2/UW; what it did: audited who gets how much Chatbot Arena data): private pre-release testing and asymmetric sampling (some labs' models shown to far more voters than others — OpenAI+Google got ~40% of data; 83 open models got ~30%); training on Arena data yields up to 112% relative gains *on the Arena's own distribution* (i.e., you can game the board without getting genuinely better). [Cohere Labs' head called leaderboard unreliability a "crisis"](https://betakit.com/cohere-labs-head-calls-unreliable-ai-leaderboard-rankings-a-crisis-in-the-field/).
- **Funder conflicts and access secrecy in benchmark orgs.** The FrontierMath episode: [OpenAI funded the benchmark and had access to problems/solutions, disclosed only at o3 launch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/); Epoch's own lead mathematician [could not independently verify OpenAI's reported score](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/); [community post-mortems](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8ZgLYwBmB3vLavjKE/some-lessons-from-the-openai-frontiermath-debacle) drew explicit governance lessons. Independent verification of lab-reported scores is an unfilled niche (METR's no-lab-funding stance is the counter-model).
- **Agent evals lack cost, realism, and infrastructure.** [AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502) (accuracy-only evals mislead); [HAL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11977) frames agent-eval infrastructure as "missing"; Vending-Bench exists precisely because long-horizon coherence had no benchmark ([Grokipedia summary](https://grokipedia.com/page/Vending-Bench) ⚠️ tertiary source).
- **Benchmark lifecycle management.** The two most-used community leaderboards both died of saturation/obsolescence: [Open LLM Leaderboard retired](https://huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard/discussions/1135) ("could encourage people to optimize for irrelevant directions"); SWE-bench Verified reportedly deprecated Feb 2026 (⚠️ [wiki source](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/swe-bench_verified)). Nobody owns "benchmark maintenance, contamination auditing, and retirement" as a discipline.

## What we still don't know

### Survivorship-bias caveats (read before imitating anyone above)

- Every case here is a winner; there is **no denominator**. Hundreds of benchmarks are published per year (BetterBench sampled from a large population) and almost none change careers. Nothing I read quantifies the base rate.
- The biggest wins had **institutional tailwinds**: SWE-bench (Princeton NLP + timing of the coding-agent boom), Arena (Berkeley/LMSYS + Stoica's founder network), MMLU (Berkeley), FrontierMath (Epoch's existing lab relationships). Hendrycks and Chollet had standing *before* their benchmarks became famous. Solo counter-examples exist (AidanBench, Artificial Analysis, Andon Labs) but all paired the artifact with relentless public distribution.
- Eval fame carries **reputational tail risk**: FrontierMath's disclosure scandal and Arena's Leaderboard Illusion critique both landed on the eval builders, not just the labs.

## Fit notes

The researcher's published strength — LLM-as-a-judge methodology for hallucination detection — sits squarely in the two gaps this field itself names as most undersupplied: eval statistics/reliability ([Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640), [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990)) and judge/agent eval validity ([AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)); notably, the Arena team's own methodology rests on LLM-as-judge. The budget profile matches the exemplars: AidanBench, Vending-Bench, error-bars, and the Kapoor agent-eval line were all API-budget projects by 1–5 people, not GPU-cluster projects. Documented part-time/remote on-ramps that fit a solo, part-time schedule exist right now: Epoch's contract analyst/data-scientist roles and expression-of-interest, AISI's Inspect open-source surface, HF community leaderboards, ARC Prize Kaggle competitions, and (historically) METR bounties. Apollo has publicly invited individual contributors to "science of evals" collaboration, and Anthropic/AISI post eval-specific roles — all three routes (hired, nonprofit, startup) have multiple documented recent precedents in this space.
