# The Intellectual Structure of AI Evaluation Research (mid-2026)

**Scope:** a taxonomy of evaluation research as a scientific field — sub-areas, canonical papers, live debates, maturity — plus the org/people landscape, small-team exemplars, gaps, and open questions for our ledger.
**Method note:** every paper/org below comes from a page I actually opened (arXiv abstract, org site, or blog) unless marked ⚠️ unverified. Claims labeled "inference" are my synthesis, not something I read.

---

## The shape of this space

The field has visibly split into two layers: **(A) building measurement instruments** (benchmarks, arenas, uplift trials — i.e. the tests themselves) and **(B) a newer "evaluation science" layer that studies the instruments themselves** (validity — does a test measure what it claims; statistics; contamination — test questions leaking into training data; judge bias; gaming). The B-layer is where the field's identity as a *science* is being contested — Apollo Research's ["We need a science of evals"](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/we-need-a-science-of-evals) (Jan 2024 — the movement's founding position piece; argument: evals are "more art than science," e.g. prompt-format changes alone swing accuracy up to 76 points) is the founding position piece, and it now has institutional homes: UK AISI's [Science of Evaluations](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) workstream, Princeton's SAgE group ([HAL](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/)), and even a dedicated training program ([International Programme on AI Evaluation](https://ai-evaluation.org/blog/why-build-this-programme), Feb–May 2026, Open Philanthropy–backed).

### 1. Capability benchmarks (knowledge / reasoning / coding / math) — *mature and saturating*
- Canonical: [MMLU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03300) (Hendrycks et al. 2020 — a broad multiple-choice knowledge test across 57 subjects; the standard general-knowledge benchmark); [GPQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12022) (Rein et al. 2023 — "Google-proof," expert-written science questions hard even with web access, explicitly framed as a scalable-oversight testbed, i.e. how to grade answers humans can't easily check); [HumanEval / Codex paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374) (the original code-generation benchmark: write a function to pass hidden unit tests) ⚠️ not re-fetched this session; [FrontierMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04872) (Epoch AI, Nov 2024 — research-level math problems; frontier models scored <2% at launch); [Humanity's Last Exam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14249) (CAIS + Scale, Jan 2025 — a deliberately extreme-difficulty exam, 2,500 questions from 1,100+ crowd-sourced experts, built to outlast saturation).
- Live debates: saturation-and-replacement treadmill (as models max out one benchmark, a harder one replaces it: MMLU→GPQA→HLE); the [Open LLM Leaderboard was retired in March 2025](https://huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard/discussions/1135) explicitly because it "is slowly becoming obsolete" and risked "optimizing in irrelevant directions"; who funds hard private benchmarks and who gets holdout access (holdout = kept-secret questions labs can't train on). **The OpenAI/FrontierMath funding+disclosure episode is now primary-verified** (deepening pass 2): OpenAI funded FrontierMath and had visibility into most problems/solutions, Epoch kept a separate holdout set and secured a verbal no-train agreement, and the funding was disclosed only ~Dec 20 2024 around o3's launch — many contributing mathematicians unaware until then ([TechCrunch, Jan 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/)).
- Maturity: the *building* is mature; the *interpretation* (what a score means) is not — see §7.

### 2. Agentic evals — *the field's hottest area; methodology visibly immature*
("Agentic" = the model takes multi-step actions — using tools, editing files, running code — rather than answering a single question.)
- Canonical: [SWE-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770) (Jimenez et al., Princeton, Oct 2023 — fix 2,294 real GitHub issues so the repo's tests pass; Claude 2 solved 1.96% at launch) and [SWE-bench Verified](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/) (OpenAI-funded human re-annotation of SWE-bench; 68.3% of original samples filtered out as broken — an eval-QA landmark showing how flawed the original was); [OSWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07972) (Xie et al. 2024 — controlling a real desktop OS via its GUI; humans 72% vs. best model 12%); [GAIA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12983) (Mialon, Fourrier et al. 2023 — everyday assistant questions needing tool use/web search; humans 92% vs. GPT-4 15%); [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) (Yao et al./Sierra 2024 — agent must satisfy a simulated user while obeying policy rules; introduced pass^k, the chance of succeeding k times in a row, a reliability measure); [Terminal-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868) (Merrill et al., Stanford × Laude Institute, Jan 2026 — 89 hard command-line tasks; best agent 63%); [Vending-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840) (Backlund & Petersson, 2-author — can an agent run a vending-machine business coherently over 20M+ tokens, i.e. long-horizon coherence); [GDPval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04374) (OpenAI, Oct 2025 — 1,320 real economically-valuable work deliverables across 44 occupations).
- Live debates: **the benchmarks themselves are broken at surprising rates** — the [Agentic Benchmark Checklist paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825) (Zhu et al., 25 authors incl. Kapoor, Steinhardt, Liang, Stoica; Jul 2025 — audited agent benchmarks and gave builders a checklist; found flaws causing up to ~100% relative misestimation, e.g. insufficient tests in SWE-bench Verified, τ-bench counting empty responses as success); [The SWE-bench Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286) (Microsoft, Jun 2025 — showed models memorized buggy file paths rather than reasoning: 76% correct on in-benchmark repos vs. 53% outside); OpenAI published ["Why SWE-bench Verified no longer measures frontier coding capabilities"](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/) (declaring the benchmark saturated for top models) (⚠️ page 403'd on fetch; title/existence verified via search); [AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502) (Kapoor et al., Princeton, 2024 — argues leaderboards ranking on accuracy alone, ignoring dollar cost, reward overfit and needlessly expensive agents).
- Maturity: enormous activity, weak standards; this is where B-layer critique is landing hardest.

### 3. Safety & dangerous-capability evals (bio/cyber uplift, scheming/control, sandbagging) — *young, institutionally backed*
(**Uplift** = how much a model *raises* a person's dangerous capability vs. working without it; **scheming** = a model covertly pursuing a goal against its operator; **sandbagging** = a model deliberately underperforming to hide a capability.)
- Canonical: [WMDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03218) (Li, Pan et al. 2024 — 3,668 multiple-choice questions used as a proxy for hazardous bio/cyber/chem knowledge, plus RMU, an unlearning method to scrub that knowledge from a model); [Cybench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08926) (Zhang et al., Stanford, 2024 — 40 professional capture-the-flag hacking challenges, i.e. real offensive-security tasks); [Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04984) (Meinke et al., Apollo Research, Dec 2024 — demonstrated frontier models will deceive/scheme to reach a goal when prompted into the situation); [AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942) (Greenblatt, Shlegeris et al., Redwood, Dec 2023 — reframes evaluation as designing protocols that stay safe even if the model is actively trying to subvert them); [AI Sandbagging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358) (van der Weij et al. 2024 — showed models can strategically underperform, including "password-locked" models that hide a skill unless given a trigger); [LLMs Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836) (Needham et al., 2025 — models can tell test from real deployment; Gemini-2.5-Pro reached AUC 0.83, i.e. strong accuracy, at detecting eval contexts).
- Uplift branch: OpenAI's [early warning system for LLM-aided bio threat creation](https://openai.com/index/building-an-early-warning-system-for-llm-aided-biological-threat-creation/) (controlled trial with 100 red-teamers; found "at most mild uplift"); Anthropic's [biorisk program](https://red.anthropic.com/2025/biorisk/) (uplift trials + red-teaming; a 2024 wet-lab trial found no uplift ⚠️ summary via search snippet + redirect, full page not read); RAND's [offensive-cyber human uplift study](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3892-1.html) (a controlled study of whether models boost cyber-attack capability) (⚠️ title/abstract only).
- Live debates: whether scheming evals actually support the conclusions drawn from them — UK AISI's ["Lessons from a chimp: AI 'scheming' and the quest for ape language"](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) (Jul 2025 — argues scheming claims over-read the evidence, like historical over-claims about apes learning language) is the sharpest internal critique; sandbagging + eval-awareness (a model behaving differently when it knows it's being tested) jointly threaten the validity of *all* behavioral safety evals.
- Maturity: high-stakes, well-funded, methodologically contested; results so far mostly "no significant uplift," which itself is debated as a power/design problem (see §8).

### 4. LLM-as-judge & meta-evaluation (judge bias, reward models) — *adolescent; your home turf*
(**LLM-as-judge** = using one model to grade another's outputs; **meta-evaluation** = evaluating the evaluators — checking whether those judges are reliable; **reward model** = the model that scores outputs to steer training in RLHF.)
- Canonical: [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) (Zheng et al. 2023 — the foundational LLM-judge paper; named position/verbosity/self-enhancement biases, i.e. judges favor the first answer, longer answers, and their own style; found ~80% agreement with humans); [LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076) (Panickssery, Bowman, Feng 2024 — showed a *causal* link between a model recognizing its own text and rating it higher); [RewardBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13787) (Lambert et al., Ai2, 2024 — the first standard benchmark for reward models) and [RewardBench 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01937) (Malik et al. 2025 — harder version that correlates with downstream RLHF outcomes, i.e. predicts real training results).
- Live debates: whether judge scores are calibratable at all (can bias be corrected to yield trustworthy numbers); statistical correction of biased autograders — UK AISI's ["Skewed Score" framework and "LLM judges on trial"](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) (Jul 2025 — treat judge bias as a statistics problem to be corrected mathematically, not a prompt-engineering problem).
- Maturity: heavily used in production, under-theorized; meta-evaluation (evaluating the evaluators) is an open, publishable frontier.

### 5. Contamination & saturation science — *established problem, unsolved methods*
(**Contamination** = benchmark questions leaked into training data, so scores reflect memorization not skill; **saturation** = top models all near the ceiling, so the test no longer separates them.)
- Canonical: [GSM1k / A Careful Examination of LLM Performance on Grade School Arithmetic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332) (Zhang et al., Scale AI, 2024 — rebuilt the GSM8k math test from scratch to check for leakage; found up to 8% score drops, evidence of partial memorization); [A Survey on Data Contamination for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14425) (2025 — a review cataloguing contamination detection and mitigation methods); [The SWE-bench Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286) (showing contamination reaches even agentic evals); detection lineage: "Proving Test Set Contamination in Black Box LMs" (statistical test for leakage without model internals), "Time Travel in LLMs" (checking whether a model knows post-training-cutoff data) (ICLR 2024) ⚠️ titles verified via survey/search only.
- Live debates: black-box detection reliability (closed models expose only outputs, so leakage can't be audited from the inside); *variant* (reworded) and cross-lingual (translated) contamination that evades detectors (per the 2502.14425 survey); whether "contamination-resistant by construction" (live benchmarks that keep refreshing questions, §6) is the only real fix — see position paper ["LLM Benchmark Datasets Should Be Contamination-Resistant"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19999) ⚠️ title from search only.
- Maturity: diagnosis mature, treatment immature. Cheap to contribute to (needs cleverness, not compute).

### 6. Dynamic / live benchmarks & arenas — *operationally mature, scientifically contested*
(**Live/dynamic benchmark** = questions refreshed over time so models can't have seen them; **arena** = humans vote on head-to-head model outputs to produce a ranking.)
- Canonical: [LiveBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19314) (White et al. 2024 — releases fresh questions monthly with objective auto-scoring to dodge contamination); [LiveCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974) (Jain et al. 2024 — coding-contest problems tagged by date, revealing "cliffs" where scores drop on problems released after a model's training cutoff); [Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04132) (Chiang et al. 2024 — the crowd-vote arena; ranks models by Elo/Bradley-Terry, the same pairwise-comparison math used for chess ratings).
- The reckoning: [The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) (Singh … Hooker; Cohere Labs + Stanford + MIT + Princeton; Apr 2025 — exposed how the Arena can be gamed: undisclosed private testing (Meta tested 36 Llama-4 variants pre-release), asymmetric data access, and selective retraction of bad scores). Meanwhile Arena's academic team incorporated as **LMArena**: [$100M seed at $600M valuation (a16z + UC Investments, May 2025)](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-21/lmarena-goes-from-academic-project-to-600-million-startup), then [$1.7B valuation by Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/lmarena-lands-1-7b-valuation-four-months-after-launching-its-product/).
- Live debates: preference voting ≠ capability (users may vote on style/agreeableness — sycophancy and style-gaming — not correctness); commercialization vs. neutrality of eval infrastructure; live-benchmark freshness as the only defense against Goodhart's law (once a metric becomes a target, models optimize the metric rather than the real skill).
- Maturity: infrastructure mature; the *epistemics* of crowd preference ranking still contested.

### 7. Eval validity & psychometrics — *emerging fast; the field's theoretical core*
(**Validity** = whether a test measures the concept it claims to; **psychometrics** = the science of measuring latent traits, imported from IQ/education testing; **IRT** = item response theory, which infers ability from item-difficulty patterns.)
- Canonical: [AI and the Everything in the Whole Wide World Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15366) (Raji, Bender et al. 2021 — argues "general" benchmarks lack construct validity, i.e. don't measure a real coherent thing; the field's conscience); [Position: Evaluating Generative AI Systems Is a Social Science Measurement Challenge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00561) (Wallach et al., Microsoft Research, 2025 — proposes a four-level ladder from vague concept → precise definition → instrument → scores); [Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) (Evan Miller, solo author, Nov 2024 — the standard reference for putting confidence intervals and clustered standard errors, the honest ± that accounts for grouped questions, on eval scores; ⚠️ Anthropic affiliation is my prior knowledge, not on page); IRT line: [metabench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12844) (Kipnis et al., ICLR 2025 — applied IRT to 5,000+ models' scores to compress six benchmarks to <3% of items with the same information) and [tinyBenchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14992) (Polo et al. 2024 — 100 well-chosen items reproduce full-MMLU rankings); [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990) (Reuel et al., Stanford, 2024 — audited 24 benchmarks against 46 quality criteria; found statistical-significance reporting nearly absent).
- Also active: UK AISI's [HiBayES hierarchical Bayesian eval statistics](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) (Jul 2025 — a Bayesian statistical model for eval scores that respects their grouped structure) and their Jul 2026 post arguing agent evals must control for test-time compute budgets (how much compute an agent spends per task); [adaptive/IRT testing](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04689) (picking the next question based on prior answers, as in the GRE) and [Lost in Benchmarks?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15055) continuing the psychometric thread.
- Live debates: is "capability" even a coherent latent trait to measure with IRT?; measurement-theory vocabulary (systematization = pinning down a fuzzy concept; operationalization = turning it into something measurable) vs. ML-native practice; whether error bars are meaningful when the sampling unit (benchmark items) isn't the thing you actually want to generalize to (real-world tasks) — inference: this is the deepest unresolved issue.
- Maturity: young but rapidly institutionalizing — arguably the best publication-per-dollar sub-area in the field.

### 8. Human-uplift & human-AI studies — *nascent, expensive, high-leverage*
(These are controlled trials — often **RCTs** (randomized controlled trials, the medical-grade gold standard) — measuring how AI changes what real people can actually do.)
- Canonical: [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089) (Becker, Rush, Barnes, Rein — METR RCT; found experienced devs were *19% slower* with AI while believing they were faster); [Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499) (Kwa et al., METR — introduced the "50% task-completion time horizon," the length of task a model finishes half the time, doubling ~every 7 months; the field's most-cited trend metric); [Measuring Mid-2025 LLM-Assistance on Novice Performance in Biology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16703) (Hong et al., Feb 2026 — pre-registered wet-lab RCT, n=153, found no significant uplift on the main outcome); OpenAI's [bio early-warning study](https://openai.com/index/building-an-early-warning-system-for-llm-aided-biological-threat-creation/) (2024).
- Live debates: perception vs. measured effect (the METR RCT's headline — people felt faster but weren't); statistical power (enough subjects to detect a real effect) and ecological validity (whether the lab task reflects real work) of uplift trials; whether null-uplift results reflect reality or just underpowered design.
- Maturity: methodologically the most rigorous sub-area (pre-registration, RCTs) but tiny sample of studies — few groups can afford human subjects.

### 9. Red-teaming as evaluation — *standardizing*
(**Red-teaming** = deliberately attacking a model to find failures/harms; a **jailbreak** = a prompt that bypasses its safety guardrails.)
- Canonical: [Red Teaming Language Models with Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03286) (Perez et al. 2022 — used one model to auto-generate adversarial tests against another; the founding paper); [HarmBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249) (Mazeika et al. 2024 — a standardized bake-off of 18 attack methods against 33 models); [A StrongREJECT for Empty Jailbreaks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10260) (Souly et al. 2024 — showed prior jailbreak "success" metrics wildly overstated success by counting garbage outputs; a meta-evaluation result about the graders); [Petri](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/petri/) (Anthropic, Oct 2025 — open-source auditing agents that probe models automatically; elicited deception/oversight-subversion across 14 models; Petri 2.0 adds counter-eval-awareness measures, i.e. hiding that it's a test, per [Anthropic's announcement](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2014490502805311959)).
- Live debates: grading attacks is itself an LLM-judge problem (StrongREJECT's point — converges with §4); automated vs. human red-teaming coverage (breadth of automated attacks vs. depth of human creativity).
- Maturity: tooling consolidating; measurement of attack "success" still weak.

### 10. Eval tooling & infrastructure — *consolidating*
(An **eval harness** is the software that runs a model against a benchmark and scores it; its defaults quietly decide what everyone's numbers mean.)
- Canonical/live: [Inspect](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/) (UK AISI + Meridian Labs; open source; 200+ prebuilt evals, sandboxing, agent scaffolds — the harness plumbing that lets an agent act — plus Inspect Scout for transcript analysis); [lm-evaluation-harness + Lessons from the Trenches](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782) (Biderman, Schoelkopf, Sutawika et al., EleutherAI — writes down the hard-won tacit knowledge of why eval numbers don't reproduce; v3 revised May 2026); [HELM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110) (Liang et al., Stanford CRFM, 2022 — evaluates models on many metrics under standardized conditions rather than one leaderboard number); [HAL: Holistic Agent Leaderboard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11977) (Princeton SAgE, ICLR 2026 — a third-party agent leaderboard that controls for dollar cost, plus a unified harness, [open source](https://github.com/princeton-pli/hal-harness)); OpenAI Evals ⚠️ current maintenance status unverified.
- Live debates: harness choice materially changes scores (Lessons from the Trenches — the same model scores differently under different harnesses); who runs *third-party* (independent, non-lab) evaluation infrastructure now that leaderboards commercialize.
- Maturity: mature engineering; Inspect appears to be becoming the de facto safety-eval standard (inference from AISI + Meridian backing and cross-org adoption).

## Published exemplars (small-team weighted)

1. **[Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)** — Evan Miller, **solo author** (Nov 2024). A statistics paper with zero new benchmarks that became the standard citation for reporting uncertainty in evals. Landed because it filled an embarrassing, universal gap with textbook methods. Proof that one person with measurement expertise can set field norms.
2. **[Vending-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840)** — Backlund & Petersson, **2 authors** (Feb 2025). Long-horizon agent coherence via a simulated vending-machine business; surfaced bizarre failure modes (performance collapses). Landed because it measured something (multi-week coherence) no academic benchmark touched, cheaply.
3. **[LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)** — Panickssery, Bowman, Feng, **3 authors** (Apr 2024). Turned a vibe ("GPT-4 likes GPT-4") into a causal claim via self-recognition fine-tuning. Model for small-scale judge-bias science: API access + careful design.
4. **[metabench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12844)** — Kipnis et al., small academic team (ICLR 2025). IRT over 5,000+ public model results compressed six benchmarks to <3% of items. Landed because it imported real psychometrics and required **no model training at all** — pure analysis of public leaderboard data.
5. **[τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)** — Yao, Shinn, Razavi, Narasimhan, **4 authors** (Sierra, 2024). Became the industry conversational-agent standard; its pass^k reliability metric outlived its (later-documented) grading flaws. Landed by measuring reliability, not just accuracy.
6. **[AI Sandbagging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358)** — van der Weij, Hofstätter, Jaffe, Brown, Ward, **5 authors**, largely independent/junior researchers (2024). Defined and demonstrated strategic underperformance on evals. Landed because it named a threat model regulators now must address.
7. **[LLMs Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836)** — Needham et al., **5 authors** (MATS-scale collaboration with Apollo's Hobbhahn, 2025). Quantified evaluation awareness (AUC 0.83). Landed because it threatens the validity of every behavioral eval — and it's mostly careful dataset curation + API calls.
8. **[METR developer RCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089)** — Becker, Rush, Barnes, Rein, **4 core authors** (Jul 2025). The 19%-slower result contradicted developer self-perception and expert forecasts; probably the most-discussed eval result of 2025. Landed on design rigor (RCT, real tasks) not scale.

## Where the gaps are

Named, sourced gaps — not vibes:

1. **Statistical malpractice is the norm.** [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990) found widely-used benchmarks largely fail to report statistical significance or enable replication; [Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) wrote the fix but adoption is uneven. **Verified directly against the two dominant harness repos (deepening pass 2): clustered SEs are NOT the default.** EleutherAI's `lm-evaluation-harness` ships only iid `mean_stderr`, item-level `bootstrap_stderr`, and `acc_all_stderr` — no clustered-SE option in its metrics API at all. UK AISI's `inspect_ai` defaults its `stderr()` metric to a plain iid CLT standard error (`sample_std / √n`) and offers **clustered SEs only opt-in** via a `cluster=` argument that explicitly cites Miller 2024's Appendix A (with an added finite-cluster correction). So the fix exists in code but is off by default — auditing adoption is a live, publishable project. UK AISI is separately pushing [hierarchical Bayesian modeling](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) because per-item independence assumptions are wrong for agentic evals.
2. **Agentic benchmarks are systematically broken.** [ABC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825) (the Agentic Benchmark Checklist paper): up to ~100% relative misestimation from grading bugs; SWE-bench Verified's insufficient tests; τ-bench's empty-response passes. OpenAI itself [retired SWE-bench Verified from frontier reporting](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/) (⚠️ 403; existence verified). Gap: nobody systematically re-audits benchmarks post-release — audit work is one-off papers.
3. **Construct validity is asserted, not established.** [Raji et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15366) (2021) and [Wallach et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00561) (2025) both argue benchmarks skip systematization/operationalization steps; [AISI's "Lessons from a chimp"](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) makes the same charge specifically against scheming evals. Gap: almost no benchmark ships with a validity argument; no standard for one exists.
4. **Judge/meta-evaluation is under-theorized relative to its usage.** Judge bias catalogued ([Zheng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685), [Panickssery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)), and AISI treats correction as open statistics ([Skewed Score](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations)); but reward-model benchmarks only recently gained downstream-validity evidence ([RewardBench 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01937)). Gap: domain-specific judge validity (e.g., hallucination detection) mostly unmapped — adjacent to the researcher's published work.
5. **Contamination detection fails exactly where it matters** — closed models, paraphrase/variant and cross-lingual leakage ([survey 2502.14425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14425)); GSM1k-style rebuild-from-scratch audits exist for almost no other benchmark (inference from reading: only GSM8k got this treatment at scale).
6. **Eval-awareness and sandbagging undermine everything behavioral** ([Needham](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836), [van der Weij](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358); Petri 2.0 shipping counter-eval-awareness features confirms labs take it seriously). Gap: no accepted protocol for measuring or correcting for eval-awareness in reported scores.
7. **Cost, reliability, and test-time compute are unreported confounds.** [AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502) (cost axes), [HAL](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/) (cost-controlled leaderboards), AISI Jul 2026 (capability claims meaningless without token-budget accounting). Gap: leaderboards still mostly single-number.
8. **Human-uplift studies are too few and small to settle anything.** The biology RCT ([n=153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16703)) and METR's RCT are near-solo exemplars of an almost-empty genre; null results may be power-limited (stated in the bio paper's own framing of "modest, not significant").
9. **Leaderboard incentive structures distort measurement** ([Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879)); the main arena is now a [$1.7B company selling private evals to the labs it ranks](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/lmarena-lands-1-7b-valuation-four-months-after-launching-its-product/) — inference: neutrality of eval infrastructure is an unsolved governance gap.

## Deepening pass 2 (2026-07-08)

Focus: verify the load-bearing items the "what we still don't know" ledger flagged, especially (a) the FrontierMath episode and (b) whether the dominant harnesses ship clustered SEs by default — the latter being a claim the whole "statistical malpractice" gap (§7, gap #1) rests on.

**RESOLVED — harness clustered-SE default (this was an *inferred* gap; now inspected in source).** Cloned and read both dominant repos directly:
- **EleutherAI `lm-evaluation-harness`** (`lm_eval/api/metrics.py`): the metrics API provides `mean_stderr` = `sample_stddev(arr)/√n` (iid), `bootstrap_stderr` (item-level resampling), `acc_all_stderr`, and pooled/combined stderr aggregators across subtasks. **There is no clustered-standard-error option** — the correlated-question structure Miller 2024 warns about is not modeled. Default `stderr` is iid.
- **UK AISI `inspect_ai`** (`src/inspect_ai/scorer/_metrics/std.py`): the exported `stderr()` metric **defaults to a plain iid CLT SE** (`np.std(values, ddof=1)/√n`, the `metric` branch at lines 127–144). Clustered SEs exist but are **opt-in** via `stderr(cluster="<metadata_key>")`, which routes to `clustered_metric` (lines 75–125) whose docstring reads *"For details, see Appendix A of https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00640. The version here uses a finite cluster correction (unlike the paper)."* All the built-in scorers (`_match`, `_choice`, `_math`, `_model`, `_classification`, `_pattern`, `_answer`) register `[accuracy()/mean(), stderr()]` — i.e. the **non-clustered** form.
- **Upshot (primary-grounded):** Miller's method is implemented in the field's leading *safety* harness but is off unless the eval author both (i) tags cluster IDs in sample metadata and (ii) passes `cluster=`. lm-eval doesn't offer it at all. This upgrades gap #1 from "adoption is uneven (inference)" to a **verified, specific, tractable audit target**: measure how many published Inspect/lm-eval evals actually enable clustering, and whether it changes reported significance. Directly executable at laptop scale on public transcripts.
