# The Intellectual Structure of AI Evaluation Research (mid-2026)

**Scope:** a taxonomy of evaluation research as a scientific field — sub-areas, canonical papers, live debates, maturity — plus the org/people landscape, small-team exemplars, gaps, and open questions for our ledger.
**Method note:** every paper/org below comes from a page I actually opened (arXiv abstract, org site, or blog) unless marked ⚠️ unverified. Claims labeled "inference" are my synthesis, not something I read.

---

## The shape of this space

The field has visibly split into two layers: **(A) building measurement instruments** (benchmarks, arenas, uplift trials) and **(B) a newer "evaluation science" layer that studies the instruments themselves** (validity, statistics, contamination, judge bias, gaming). The B-layer is where the field's identity as a *science* is being contested — Apollo Research's ["We need a science of evals"](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/we-need-a-science-of-evals) (Jan 2024) is the movement's founding position piece ("more art than science"; prompt-format changes alone swing accuracy up to 76 points), and it now has institutional homes: UK AISI's [Science of Evaluations](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) workstream, Princeton's SAgE group ([HAL](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/)), and even a dedicated training program ([International Programme on AI Evaluation](https://ai-evaluation.org/blog/why-build-this-programme), Feb–May 2026, Open Philanthropy–backed).

### 1. Capability benchmarks (knowledge / reasoning / coding / math) — *mature and saturating*
- Canonical: [MMLU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03300) (Hendrycks et al. 2020, 57 subjects); [GPQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12022) (Rein et al. 2023 — "Google-proof," expert-written, explicitly framed as a scalable-oversight testbed); [HumanEval / Codex paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03374) ⚠️ not re-fetched this session; [FrontierMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04872) (Epoch AI, Nov 2024 — frontier models <2% at launch); [Humanity's Last Exam](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14249) (CAIS + Scale, Jan 2025, 2,500 questions, 1,100+ crowd-sourced authors).
- Live debates: saturation-and-replacement treadmill (MMLU→GPQA→HLE); the [Open LLM Leaderboard was retired in March 2025](https://huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard/discussions/1135) explicitly because it "is slowly becoming obsolete" and risked "optimizing in irrelevant directions"; who funds hard private benchmarks and who gets holdout access (⚠️ unverified this session: the reported OpenAI funding of FrontierMath and its disclosure controversy — verify before citing).
- Maturity: the *building* is mature; the *interpretation* (what a score means) is not — see §7.

### 2. Agentic evals — *the field's hottest area; methodology visibly immature*
- Canonical: [SWE-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770) (Jimenez et al., Princeton, Oct 2023; 2,294 GitHub issues; Claude 2 solved 1.96% at launch) and [SWE-bench Verified](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/) (OpenAI-funded human re-annotation; 68.3% of original samples filtered out as broken — an eval-QA landmark); [OSWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07972) (Xie et al. 2024, real-OS GUI tasks, humans 72% vs. best model 12%); [GAIA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12983) (Mialon, Fourrier et al. 2023, tool-use assistant questions, humans 92% vs. GPT-4 15%); [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) (Yao et al./Sierra 2024, simulated-user + policy compliance, introduced pass^k reliability); [Terminal-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868) (Merrill et al., Stanford × Laude Institute, Jan 2026; 89 hard CLI tasks; best agent 63%); [Vending-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840) (Backlund & Petersson, 2-author, long-horizon coherence over 20M+ tokens); [GDPval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04374) (OpenAI, Oct 2025; 1,320 real occupational deliverables across 44 occupations).
- Live debates: **the benchmarks themselves are broken at surprising rates** — the [Agentic Benchmark Checklist paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825) (Zhu et al., 25 authors incl. Kapoor, Steinhardt, Liang, Stoica; Jul 2025) found flaws causing up to ~100% relative misestimation (insufficient tests in SWE-bench Verified; τ-bench counting empty responses as success); [The SWE-bench Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286) (Microsoft, Jun 2025) showed memorization of buggy file paths (76% in-benchmark vs. 53% outside); OpenAI published ["Why SWE-bench Verified no longer measures frontier coding capabilities"](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/) (⚠️ page 403'd on fetch; title/existence verified via search); [AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502) (Kapoor et al., Princeton, 2024) argues accuracy-only leaderboards without cost axes drive overfit, needlessly expensive agents.
- Maturity: enormous activity, weak standards; this is where B-layer critique is landing hardest.

### 3. Safety & dangerous-capability evals (bio/cyber uplift, scheming/control, sandbagging) — *young, institutionally backed*
- Canonical: [WMDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03218) (Li, Pan et al. 2024; 3,668 MCQs proxying hazardous bio/cyber/chem knowledge + RMU unlearning); [Cybench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08926) (Zhang et al., Stanford, 2024; 40 professional CTFs); [Frontier Models are Capable of In-context Scheming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04984) (Meinke et al., Apollo Research, Dec 2024); [AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942) (Greenblatt, Shlegeris et al., Redwood, Dec 2023 — reframes evaluation as adversarial protocol design against an intentionally subversive model); [AI Sandbagging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358) (van der Weij et al. 2024 — models can strategically underperform, incl. password-locked); [LLMs Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836) (Needham et al., 2025; Gemini-2.5-Pro AUC 0.83 at detecting eval contexts).
- Uplift branch: OpenAI's [early warning system for LLM-aided bio threat creation](https://openai.com/index/building-an-early-warning-system-for-llm-aided-biological-threat-creation/) (100 red-teamers, "at most mild uplift"); Anthropic's [biorisk program](https://red.anthropic.com/2025/biorisk/) (uplift trials + red-teaming; 2024 wet-lab trial found no uplift ⚠️ summary via search snippet + redirect, full page not read); RAND's [offensive-cyber human uplift study](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3892-1.html) (⚠️ title/abstract only).
- Live debates: whether scheming evals support the inferences drawn from them — UK AISI's ["Lessons from a chimp: AI 'scheming' and the quest for ape language"](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) (Jul 2025) is the sharpest internal critique; sandbagging + eval-awareness jointly threaten the validity of *all* behavioral safety evals.
- Maturity: high-stakes, well-funded, methodologically contested; results so far mostly "no significant uplift," which itself is debated as a power/design problem (see §8).

### 4. LLM-as-judge & meta-evaluation (judge bias, reward models) — *adolescent; your home turf*
- Canonical: [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685) (Zheng et al. 2023 — named position/verbosity/self-enhancement biases; ~80% human agreement); [LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076) (Panickssery, Bowman, Feng 2024 — causal link between self-recognition and self-preference); [RewardBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13787) (Lambert et al., Ai2, 2024) and [RewardBench 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01937) (Malik et al. 2025 — harder, correlates with downstream RLHF outcomes).
- Live debates: whether judge scores are calibratable at all; statistical correction of biased autograders — UK AISI's ["Skewed Score" framework and "LLM judges on trial"](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) (Jul 2025) treat judge bias as a statistics problem, not a prompt-engineering problem.
- Maturity: heavily used in production, under-theorized; meta-evaluation (evaluating the evaluators) is an open, publishable frontier.

### 5. Contamination & saturation science — *established problem, unsolved methods*
- Canonical: [GSM1k / A Careful Examination of LLM Performance on Grade School Arithmetic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332) (Zhang et al., Scale AI, 2024 — rebuilt GSM8k from scratch; up to 8% drops; evidence of partial memorization); [A Survey on Data Contamination for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14425) (2025); [The SWE-bench Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286) (contamination reaches agentic evals); detection lineage: "Proving Test Set Contamination in Black Box LMs," "Time Travel in LLMs" (ICLR 2024) ⚠️ titles verified via survey/search only.
- Live debates: black-box detection reliability (closed models can't be audited); *variant* and cross-lingual contamination evading detectors (per the 2502.14425 survey); whether "contamination-resistant by construction" (live benchmarks, §6) is the only real fix — see position paper ["LLM Benchmark Datasets Should Be Contamination-Resistant"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19999) ⚠️ title from search only.
- Maturity: diagnosis mature, treatment immature. Cheap to contribute to (needs cleverness, not compute).

### 6. Dynamic / live benchmarks & arenas — *operationally mature, scientifically contested*
- Canonical: [LiveBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19314) (White et al. 2024 — monthly fresh questions, objective scoring); [LiveCodeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974) (Jain et al. 2024 — time-windowed contest problems, shows post-cutoff performance cliffs); [Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04132) (Chiang et al. 2024 — crowdsourced pairwise Elo/BT ranking).
- The reckoning: [The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) (Singh … Hooker; Cohere Labs + Stanford + MIT + Princeton; Apr 2025) documented undisclosed private testing (Meta tested 36 Llama-4 variants pre-release), asymmetric data access, and selective retraction on the Arena. Meanwhile Arena's academic team incorporated as **LMArena**: [$100M seed at $600M valuation (a16z + UC Investments, May 2025)](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-05-21/lmarena-goes-from-academic-project-to-600-million-startup), then [$1.7B valuation by Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/lmarena-lands-1-7b-valuation-four-months-after-launching-its-product/).
- Live debates: preference voting ≠ capability (sycophancy/style gaming); commercialization vs. neutrality of eval infrastructure; live-benchmark freshness as the only anti-Goodhart defense.
- Maturity: infrastructure mature; the *epistemics* of crowd preference ranking still contested.

### 7. Eval validity & psychometrics — *emerging fast; the field's theoretical core*
- Canonical: [AI and the Everything in the Whole Wide World Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15366) (Raji, Bender et al. 2021 — construct-validity critique of "general" benchmarks; the field's conscience); [Position: Evaluating Generative AI Systems Is a Social Science Measurement Challenge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00561) (Wallach et al., Microsoft Research, 2025 — four-level measurement framework); [Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) (Evan Miller, solo author, Nov 2024 — the standard reference for CIs/clustered SEs on evals; ⚠️ Anthropic affiliation is my prior knowledge, not on page); IRT line: [metabench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12844) (Kipnis et al., ICLR 2025 — IRT on 5,000+ models compresses six benchmarks to <3% of items) and [tinyBenchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14992) (Polo et al. 2024 — 100 curated items ≈ full MMLU); [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990) (Reuel et al., Stanford, 2024 — 46-criterion audit of 24 benchmarks; statistical significance reporting nearly absent).
- Also active: UK AISI's [HiBayES hierarchical Bayesian eval statistics](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) (Jul 2025) and their Jul 2026 post arguing agent evals must control for test-time compute budgets; [adaptive/IRT testing](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.04689) and [Lost in Benchmarks?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15055) continuing the psychometric thread.
- Live debates: is "capability" even a coherent latent trait for IRT?; measurement-theory vocabulary (systematization/operationalization) vs. ML-native practice; whether error bars are meaningful when the sampling unit (benchmark items) isn't the inference target (real-world tasks) — inference: this is the deepest unresolved issue.
- Maturity: young but rapidly institutionalizing — arguably the best publication-per-dollar sub-area in the field.

### 8. Human-uplift & human-AI studies — *nascent, expensive, high-leverage*
- Canonical: [Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089) (Becker, Rush, Barnes, Rein — METR RCT; devs *19% slower* with AI while believing they were faster); [Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499) (Kwa et al., METR — "50% task-completion time horizon" doubling ~every 7 months; the field's most-cited trend metric); [Measuring Mid-2025 LLM-Assistance on Novice Performance in Biology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16703) (Hong et al., Feb 2026 — pre-registered wet-lab RCT, n=153, no significant uplift on primary endpoint); OpenAI's [bio early-warning study](https://openai.com/index/building-an-early-warning-system-for-llm-aided-biological-threat-creation/) (2024).
- Live debates: perception vs. measured effect (METR RCT's headline); statistical power and ecological validity of uplift trials; whether null-uplift results reflect reality or design limits.
- Maturity: methodologically the most rigorous sub-area (pre-registration, RCTs) but tiny sample of studies — few groups can afford human subjects.

### 9. Red-teaming as evaluation — *standardizing*
- Canonical: [Red Teaming Language Models with Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03286) (Perez et al. 2022 — automated adversarial test generation, the founding paper); [HarmBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249) (Mazeika et al. 2024 — standardized comparison of 18 attack methods × 33 models); [A StrongREJECT for Empty Jailbreaks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10260) (Souly et al. 2024 — showed prior jailbreak metrics wildly overstate success; a meta-evaluation result); [Petri](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/petri/) (Anthropic, Oct 2025 — open-source automated auditing agents; elicited deception/oversight-subversion across 14 models; Petri 2.0 adds counter-eval-awareness measures per [Anthropic's announcement](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2014490502805311959)).
- Live debates: grading attacks is itself an LLM-judge problem (StrongREJECT's point — converges with §4); automated vs. human red-teaming coverage.
- Maturity: tooling consolidating; measurement of attack "success" still weak.

### 10. Eval tooling & infrastructure — *consolidating*
- Canonical/live: [Inspect](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/) (UK AISI + Meridian Labs; open source; 200+ prebuilt evals, sandboxing, agent scaffolds, Inspect Scout for transcript analysis); [lm-evaluation-harness + Lessons from the Trenches](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782) (Biderman, Schoelkopf, Sutawika et al., EleutherAI — the codified tacit knowledge of reproducible evals; v3 revised May 2026); [HELM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110) (Liang et al., Stanford CRFM, 2022 — multi-metric, standardized-conditions evaluation); [HAL: Holistic Agent Leaderboard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11977) (Princeton SAgE, ICLR 2026 — cost-controlled third-party agent leaderboard + unified harness, [open source](https://github.com/princeton-pli/hal-harness)); OpenAI Evals ⚠️ current maintenance status unverified.
- Live debates: harness choice materially changes scores (Lessons from the Trenches); who runs *third-party* evaluation infrastructure now that leaderboards commercialize.
- Maturity: mature engineering; Inspect appears to be becoming the de facto safety-eval standard (inference from AISI + Meridian backing and cross-org adoption).

## Published exemplars (small-team weighted)

1. **[Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)** — Evan Miller, **solo author** (Nov 2024). A statistics paper with zero new benchmarks that became the standard citation for reporting uncertainty in evals. Landed because it filled an embarrassing, universal gap with textbook methods. Proof that one person with measurement expertise can set field norms.
2. **[Vending-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15840)** — Backlund & Petersson, **2 authors** (Feb 2025). Long-horizon agent coherence via a simulated vending-machine business; surfaced bizarre failure modes (performance collapses). Landed because it measured something (multi-week coherence) no academic benchmark touched, cheaply.
3. **[LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)** — Panickssery, Bowman, Feng, **3 authors** (Apr 2024). Turned a vibe ("GPT-4 likes GPT-4") into a causal claim via self-recognition fine-tuning. Model for small-scale judge-bias science: API access + careful design.
4. **[metabench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12844)** — Kipnis et al., small academic team (ICLR 2025). IRT over 5,000+ public model results compressed six benchmarks to <3% of items. Landed because it imported real psychometrics and required **no model training at all** — pure analysis of public leaderboard data.
5. **[τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)** — Yao, Shinn, Razavi, Narasimhan, **4 authors** (Sierra, 2024). Became the industry conversational-agent standard; its pass^k reliability metric outlived its (later-documented) grading flaws. Landed by measuring reliability, not just accuracy.
6. **[AI Sandbagging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358)** — van der Weij, Hofstätter, Jaffe, Brown, Ward, **5 authors**, largely independent/junior researchers (2024). Defined and demonstrated strategic underperformance on evals. Landed because it named a threat model regulators now must address.
7. **[LLMs Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836)** — Needham et al., **5 authors** (MATS-scale collaboration with Apollo's Hobbhahn, 2025). Quantified evaluation awareness (AUC 0.83). Landed because it threatens the validity of every behavioral eval — and it's mostly careful dataset curation + API calls.
8. **[METR developer RCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089)** — Becker, Rush, Barnes, Rein, **4 core authors** (Jul 2025). The 19%-slower result contradicted developer self-perception and expert forecasts; probably the most-discussed eval result of 2025. Landed on design rigor (RCT, real tasks) not scale.

## Where the gaps are

Named, sourced gaps — not vibes:

1. **Statistical malpractice is the norm.** [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990) found widely-used benchmarks largely fail to report statistical significance or enable replication; [Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) wrote the fix but adoption is uneven (inference: auditing adoption itself is an open project). UK AISI is pushing [hierarchical Bayesian modeling](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) because per-item independence assumptions are wrong for agentic evals.
2. **Agentic benchmarks are systematically broken.** [ABC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825): up to ~100% relative misestimation from grading bugs; SWE-bench Verified's insufficient tests; τ-bench's empty-response passes. OpenAI itself [retired SWE-bench Verified from frontier reporting](https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/) (⚠️ 403; existence verified). Gap: nobody systematically re-audits benchmarks post-release — audit work is one-off papers.
3. **Construct validity is asserted, not established.** [Raji et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.15366) (2021) and [Wallach et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00561) (2025) both argue benchmarks skip systematization/operationalization steps; [AISI's "Lessons from a chimp"](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations) makes the same charge specifically against scheming evals. Gap: almost no benchmark ships with a validity argument; no standard for one exists.
4. **Judge/meta-evaluation is under-theorized relative to its usage.** Judge bias catalogued ([Zheng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685), [Panickssery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)), and AISI treats correction as open statistics ([Skewed Score](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/category/science-of-evaluations)); but reward-model benchmarks only recently gained downstream-validity evidence ([RewardBench 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01937)). Gap: domain-specific judge validity (e.g., hallucination detection) mostly unmapped — adjacent to the researcher's published work.
5. **Contamination detection fails exactly where it matters** — closed models, paraphrase/variant and cross-lingual leakage ([survey 2502.14425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14425)); GSM1k-style rebuild-from-scratch audits exist for almost no other benchmark (inference from reading: only GSM8k got this treatment at scale).
6. **Eval-awareness and sandbagging undermine everything behavioral** ([Needham](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836), [van der Weij](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07358); Petri 2.0 shipping counter-eval-awareness features confirms labs take it seriously). Gap: no accepted protocol for measuring or correcting for eval-awareness in reported scores.
7. **Cost, reliability, and test-time compute are unreported confounds.** [AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502) (cost axes), [HAL](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/) (cost-controlled leaderboards), AISI Jul 2026 (capability claims meaningless without token-budget accounting). Gap: leaderboards still mostly single-number.
8. **Human-uplift studies are too few and small to settle anything.** The biology RCT ([n=153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16703)) and METR's RCT are near-solo exemplars of an almost-empty genre; null results may be power-limited (stated in the bio paper's own framing of "modest, not significant").
9. **Leaderboard incentive structures distort measurement** ([Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879)); the main arena is now a [$1.7B company selling private evals to the labs it ranks](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/lmarena-lands-1-7b-valuation-four-months-after-launching-its-product/) — inference: neutrality of eval infrastructure is an unsolved governance gap.
