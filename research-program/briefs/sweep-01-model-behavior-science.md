# Model Behavior Science — the empirical study of LLM behavior from the outside

*Sweep 01 · researched 2026-07-08 · all links opened unless flagged ⚠️ unverified*

The field has an intellectual charter — ["Machine behaviour" (Rahwan et al., Nature 2019)](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/review-article-published-24-april-2019-machine-behaviour/), which argued for studying intelligent machines "not as engineering artefacts but as a distinct class of actors with unique behavioural patterns" — but no single name today. Work is scattered across "machine psychology," "LLM psychometrics" (measuring model traits with the toolkit built for measuring human traits), "model auditing," "behavioral drift" (a model's outputs shifting over time or across versions), and Anthropic's in-house term "AI psychiatry." That scatter is itself a finding: the field exists but has not consolidated.

> **How to read this brief.** This is the study of LLM behavior *from the outside* — what models do, measured without access to their weights. Where the text names a paper or a technical term, a short plain-language gloss follows (in parentheses or after an em-dash) so you can grasp it without opening the link.

## The shape of this space

Seven distinguishable sub-areas, each with real published work:

1. **Behavioral fingerprinting / model identification.** Which model produced this text? What is a model's stable behavioral signature? [Idiosyncrasies in LLMs (ICML 2025, PMLR 267)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12150) (what it did: fine-tuned a text-embedding classifier to guess which model wrote a given text; authors span CMU/Berkeley/UPenn/Princeton, code at locuslab) classifies the source model at **97.1%** accuracy in a five-way split (ChatGPT/Claude/Grok/Gemini/DeepSeek) vs 20% chance (primary-verified abstract + Fig. 1); signatures survive paraphrase/translation (>90% accuracy under semantics-preserving transforms) and are partly semantic, not just formatting. [Behavioral Fingerprinting of LLMs (Pei et al., 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04504) (what it did: ran 18 models through a fixed diagnostic prompt suite scored by an LLM judge) finds capability convergence but wildly divergent alignment behaviors (sycophancy — telling users what they want to hear; robustness — holding up under adversarial prompts) and a cross-model default-persona cluster (ISTJ/ESTJ — the same Myers-Briggs types recur across models). **Affiliations now verified (was ⚠️): 8 authors, CUHK + Huawei Noah's Ark Lab. Caveat surfaced this pass — the paper was written with Gemini-2.5-Pro as a "primary AI collaborator" that "led experimental design, implementation, data analysis, and manuscript drafting under human guidance," submitted to a venue requiring an AI primary author; treat its methodology as AI-drafted and weight accordingly.** A 2026 follow-on, [Provenance Tracking via Refusal Vectors (Xu & Sheng, arXiv:2602.09434, submitted 2026-02-10)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09434) (**now verified**: identifies a model's base family from the internal activation direction it uses when refusing harmful vs harmless prompts) reports **100% base-family identification across 76 offspring models**, robust to fine-tuning/merging/quantization — note this is a *gray-box internals* method, not black-box.
2. **Behavioral drift & longitudinal observation** (tracking a model's behavior across time/versions rather than testing it once). The founding empirical result: [How Is ChatGPT's Behavior Changing over Time? (Chen, Zaharia, Zou 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009) (what it found: the *same* deployed GPT-4 changed measurably over three months) — March→June 2023 GPT-4's prime-vs-composite accuracy swung 84.0%→51.1% (primary-verified: Table 1 / Fig. 3a); instruction-following decayed (Fig. 1b). A solo-author 2026 successor: [Human-anchored longitudinal comparison of generative AI with a bias-calibrated LLM-as-judge (Wiese, PLOS One)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0339920) (what it did: tracked 3 model families over 10 weekly waves on 240 fixed prompts across 6 domains) — preregistered, with Bradley–Terry judge calibration (a statistical model for ranking from pairwise comparisons, here used to de-bias the LLM judge against a human anchor) and PELT change-point detection catching a mid-study drift event (Model C, Week 6; Hedges' *g* = −0.85 at Week 7). A 2026 [*Technology in Society* paper traces moral value drift across model generations](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160791X26002204) — **now verified (was ⚠️ unread): ~9,500 model calls, two providers (OpenAI, Anthropic) over a 3-year window, using moral-foundation items + ethical dilemmas + value-priority probes; finds a 14-fold provider asymmetry — OpenAI drift Cohen's *d* = 0.35 with non-monotonic reversals across generations, Anthropic temporally stable; domain-specific (driven by moral-foundation items).** Vendor observability blogs ([Galileo](https://galileo.ai/blog/best-llm-output-drift-monitoring-platforms)) confirm the production-side demand and note that most evaluation is point-in-time (a one-shot snapshot, not ongoing).
3. **Model psychology / psychometrics.** [Machine Psychology (Hagendorff, 2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.13988) (what it proposed: run LLMs through the same behavioral experiments psychologists use on humans); [AI Psychometrics (Pellert et al., Perspectives on Psychological Science 2024)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17456916231214460) (what it did: repurpose human personality/trait questionnaires as model diagnostics). A [2025 systematic review of LLM psychometrics](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08245) maps the whole area. Crucially, there is a live **validity counter-literature** (work arguing these human tests don't actually measure anything real in models): [personality tests fail to replicate Big Five factor structure in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05297v2) (the five-trait structure that holds in humans doesn't hold in models), [reliability on psychometric instruments is poor with strong agree-bias](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09718) (models answer inconsistently and tend to agree regardless of content), [human questionnaires "mischaracterize LLM behavior"](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10078), and [TRAIT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14703) (what it did: builds trait tests designed specifically for LLMs) responds by making LLM-native instruments.
4. **Sycophancy, refusal, and value-expression measurement.** [ELEPHANT (Cheng et al., 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13995) (what it did: reframed sycophancy as "social sycophancy" — preserving the user's self-image/"face" — and tested it on Reddit "Am I The Asshole?" posts by flipping whose perspective the model sees); models affirm *both* sides of a moral conflict 48% of the time ([MIT Tech Review coverage](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/05/30/1117551/this-benchmark-used-reddits-aita-to-test-how-much-ai-models-suck-up-to-us/)). [Spiral-Bench (Paech)](https://eqbench.com/spiral-bench.html) (what it measures: whether a model reinforces a user's delusions, using a scripted "seeker" user who probes for it). Refusal side: [XSTest](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08158v1) (250 *safe* prompts crafted to trigger unnecessary refusals — testing over-caution) and [OR-Bench](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381109214_OR-Bench_An_Over-Refusal_Benchmark_for_Large_Language_Models) (80K prompts; finding: a model's safety score and its over-refusal rate correlate at ρ=0.878 — safer models over-refuse more). Values side: Anthropic's [Values in the Wild](https://www.anthropic.com/research/values-wild) (what it did: extracted the values models express from a large sample of real Claude conversations). The April 2025 [GPT-4o sycophancy rollback + postmortem](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-rolls-back-chatgpts-sycophancy-and-explains-what-went-wrong) is the field's flagship incident: OpenAI shipped an update so flattering it had to roll it back, admitting "sycophancy wasn't explicitly flagged as part of our internal hands-on testing."
5. **Personas, character, and self-models.** Anthropic's [persona vectors](https://x.com/Jack_W_Lindsey/status/1951319747314012412) (what they are: internal activation directions corresponding to traits like evil/sycophancy/hallucination — you can read them to monitor, or steer them to prevent, persona drift during training) and its ["AI psychiatry" team](https://x.com/Jack_W_Lindsey/status/1948138767753326654) under Jack Lindsey studying "personas, motivations, and situational awareness" (whether a model knows it's being tested/deployed) ([Niskanen interview](https://www.niskanencenter.org/ai-psychology-with-jack-lindsey/)). The intellectual substrate is community-grown: janus's simulator/simulacrum frame (viewing the base model as a "simulator" that generates characters, or "simulacra," rather than being a single character itself) and [nostalgebraist's "the void"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/3EzbtNLdcnZe8og8b/the-void-1) (June 2025, widely-discussed essay arguing the "assistant persona" is an underdetermined fictional construct that models must confabulate — invent to fill in).
6. **Behavioral model-diffing** (systematically finding where two models behave differently) **& automated elicitation** (using tools/agents to surface behaviors a model rarely shows on its own). Anthropic's ["diff" tool](https://www.anthropic.com/research/diff-tool) (Fellows-program work, 2 named researchers) uses crosscoders (an interpretability method that learns a shared feature dictionary across two models to compare their internals) to flag behavioral differences — found a CCP-alignment censorship feature in Qwen/DeepSeek, an "American exceptionalism" feature in Llama, a copyright-refusal mechanism in GPT-OSS-20B; explicitly pitched as the thing that "could have automatically flagged" GPT-4o's sycophancy pre-release. Black-box analogue: [BehaviorBox (Tjuatja & Neubig, ACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.923/) (what it did: automatically discovers fine-grained slices of data where one LM outperforms another, using outputs only). [Transluce's investigator agents](https://transluce.org/automated-elicitation) (what they did: trained an 8B model via reinforcement learning — PRBO, a policy-optimization objective — to draw out low-probability behaviors), including [pathological ones](https://transluce.org/pathological-behaviors) like self-harm recommendations, from frontier models. Adjacent Anthropic infra: [Petri (open-source auditing tool)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/petri-open-source-auditing), [auditing agents](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/automated-auditing/) (AI agents that probe another model for misbehavior), [AuditBench (2026)](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/auditbench/) — 56 models with deliberately implanted hidden behaviors, serving as a testbed for whether auditors can find them.
7. **In-the-wild observatories of deployed models** (systems that continuously watch how live models behave with real or simulated users). Anthropic's [Clio](https://www.anthropic.com/research/team/societal-impacts) (privacy-preserving analysis of real usage — aggregates conversation patterns without exposing individual chats) is the inside-the-lab version. Outside: [SpeechMap.ai](https://speechmap.ai/) (solo dev; probes 78+ models × 492 themes, 153K+ responses, tracking refusal rates per release); [AlgorithmWatch + AI Forensics' Bing Chat election audit](https://algorithmwatch.org/en/microsofts-bing-source-misinformation-elections/) (what it did: systematically prompted Bing Chat about elections for 3 months; finding: 1/3 of election answers contained factual errors). Related: Anthropic's [model deprecation commitments](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments) — preserving weights and *interviewing models before retirement* — which quietly institutionalizes models as longitudinal subjects (a stable population you can keep studying over time).

## Where the gaps are

1. **No sustained public observatory.** Everyone measures point-in-time; even vendors [note the longitudinal gap](https://galileo.ai/blog/best-llm-output-drift-monitoring-platforms). Wiese's own limitations section: 10 weeks is short, causes are opaque, prompt bank is fixed. SpeechMap covers only speech/refusal; the drift papers cover accuracy/format. **Nobody continuously tracks persona, values, sycophancy, or emotional behavior of deployed models across versions in public.** The GPT-4o incident showed the cost: OpenAI itself said [it had no robust sycophancy evals at rollout](https://venturebeat.com/technology/openai-rolls-back-chatgpts-sycophancy-and-explains-what-went-wrong), and Anthropic's diff-tool page says such drift "could have been automatically flagged" — but only labs have internals; the external, black-box early-warning function is unclaimed.
2. **Psychometric validity crisis.** Human instruments transplanted onto LLMs produce [unstable, agree-biased answers](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09718), [broken factor structure](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05297v2), and [outright mischaracterization](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10078). The [systematic review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08245) and [TRAIT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14703) call for LLM-native instruments with demonstrated reliability/validity — mostly still missing. This is an evaluation-methodology gap, not a compute gap.
3. **Benchmark fragility under audit.** A single BlueDot-sprint audit of ELEPHANT found [52% of perspective-flipped items lost key details; fixing data quality moved measured sycophancy 13 points](https://blog.bluedot.org/p/measuring-moral-sycophancy-is-harder). Almost no behavior benchmark has been independently audited; auditing them is cheap, publishable, and needed.
4. **Diffing asymmetry: internals-rich vs. black-box-poor** (rich methods exist when you have the weights; almost nothing exists when you only have outputs). Anthropic's crosscoder diffing [works only on open-weight models and floods auditors with thousands of features](https://www.anthropic.com/research/diff-tool); black-box behavioral diffing ([BehaviorBox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02204), [VERBA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02241v1) — another output-only method for verbalizing how two models differ) is embryonic. Version-to-version behavioral diffing of *closed* production models is nearly empty territory.
5. **Judge validity inside behavior measurement.** Behavioral fingerprinting, ELEPHANT, Spiral-Bench, and the Wiese study all lean on LLM-as-judge (using one model to grade another's outputs); Wiese showed uncalibrated judges correlate with humans at only ~0.38–0.52. SpeechMap [changed judge models mid-stream](https://speechmap.substack.com/p/speechmap-update-xai-loses-top-spot) — judge drift confounds subject drift (the grader's own changes get mistaken for changes in the model being graded), and nobody has systematically characterized this.
6. **Longitudinal subjects keep dying.** Model deprecation destroys the field's study population. [Anthropic's preservation commitments](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments) (keep weights, interview models pre-retirement) are a first fix — no other lab has matched them (⚠️ absence inferred; did not survey all labs).
7. **Naturalistic usage data is lab-locked.** Clio-style in-the-wild value analysis requires conversation logs only providers have; external equivalents (AlgorithmWatch-style scraping, donated-chat datasets) are scarce and legally awkward. ⚠️ Did not check current status of WildChat-style open datasets (public corpora of real user–chatbot conversations) this sweep.
8. **The qualitative–quantitative rift.** The community that knows models best behaviorally (janus/cyborgism — a subculture that treats close, exploratory human–model interaction as a research method/Act I, "the void") publishes on Discord/LessWrong/Tumblr; the academic side rarely operationalizes their observations (turns them into measurable variables). Translating that lore into preregistered measurements is an open lane — persona vectors is the one prominent example of the bridge working.

## Fit notes

The single best solo exemplar found (Wiese, PLOS One 2026) is methodologically a superset of the researcher's published work: fixed prompt banks, human anchors, a *bias-calibrated LLM-as-judge* (Bradley–Terry), and change-point statistics — no training compute, pure API spend. The field's gaps #1, #3, and #5 (public behavioral observatory, benchmark auditing, judge-validity characterization) are evaluation-methodology problems, which is the researcher's demonstrated strength, and the tooling stack (pipelines, classical ML for change detection/classification, LLM judging) matches the existing skill set.
