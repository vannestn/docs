# Problem 11 — Agent Architectures & Orchestration

*How agents are wired: memory, context management, multi-agent topology, environments, and the reliability engineering that makes (or fails to make) any of it dependable. Grounded in the internal map docs [Agents & Tool Use](../../ai-research-map/01-foundation-models-and-capabilities/agents-and-tool-use.md), [RAG & Memory Systems](../../ai-research-map/11-emerging-application-subfields/rag-and-memory-systems.md), and [Multi-Agent Systems](../../ai-research-map/11-emerging-application-subfields/multi-agent-systems.md), then web-verified beyond them.*

---

## The problem, concretely

Agents fail for **architectural** reasons, not just model reasons — and the field mostly can't measure which is which.

- **Error compounding over horizons.** Small per-step error rates compound across long runs (map: core open gap). [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) (Sierra) made this measurable with **pass^k** ("all k trials succeed"): GPT-4o drops to **~25% pass^8** on τ-retail — a ~60% relative fall from its pass@1 — meaning the same agent solves the same class of task 8 times in a row only a quarter of the time ([Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/benchmarking-ai-agents)). Anthropic model cards now report pass^k.
- **Most multi-agent failures are design failures.** [MAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657) (UC Berkeley) hand-annotated 1,600+ traces across 7 frameworks into 14 failure modes: **~42% specification/system-design issues, ~37% inter-agent misalignment, ~21% weak task verification** — i.e., orchestration problems that bigger models won't fix (map-verified).
- **Context is a consumable that degrades.** Chroma's [Context Rot](https://research.trychroma.com/context-rot) report (18 models incl. GPT-4.1, Claude 4, Gemini 2.5) shows performance is non-uniform in input length even on simple tasks; [Context-Folding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11967) shows a 36B model with learned 32K-context management matches 100B+ agents — but gains plateau past ~320K tokens (map-verified).
- **Cost is unmanaged.** Anthropic's orchestrator-worker system beats single-agent by 90.2% on breadth research at **~15× token cost** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)); [AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502) (Princeton) found needlessly complex agents where simple baselines matched accuracy at **~50× lower cost**.
- **Memory claims don't replicate.** The Mem0-vs-Zep LOCOMO fight ([Zep's rebuttal](https://blog.getzep.com/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/)) shows vendors disagree by 10+ points because configuration, judge, and dataset choices aren't pinned down — and Zep's own post concedes a **full-context baseline (~73%) beat Mem0 (~68%)** on the benchmark both vendors market against. [PersistBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01146) (map-verified) finds frontier models fail cross-domain memory-leakage checks at a median 53% and sycophancy checks at 97%.
- **The measuring instruments are broken.** The [Agentic Benchmark Checklist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825) audited 10 popular agentic benchmarks: 7 violated task validity, 7 outcome validity, all 10 had reporting flaws — errors up to 100% relative (e.g., τ-bench counted empty responses as successes).

**Measurable sub-problems:** reliability-vs-horizon curves (pass^k, decay); failure-mode distributions per architecture (MAST-style); what compaction/summarization destroys; memory read/write policy value vs. full-context baseline; topology (single vs. orchestrator-worker) under token-matched budgets; benchmark task/outcome validity rates.

## The research field around it

Six sub-areas, roughly ordered by maturity of the *science* (not the engineering):

1. **Agent evaluation & reliability methodology** — most mature as science. τ-bench pass^k ([arXiv:2406.12045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)); Princeton's cost-controlled evaluation agenda ([AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)) and the [HAL harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977) (ICLR 2026: 21,730 rollouts, 9 models × 9 benchmarks, ~$40K, three-way model/scaffold/benchmark analysis; found *higher reasoning effort reduced accuracy in most runs*); the [ABC checklist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825); early "reliability science" framings ([Beyond pass@1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29231), 3 authors — decay curves, variance amplification, meltdown onset); METR's time-horizon metric (map-verified; [metr.org](https://metr.org)).
2. **Multi-agent topologies & failure modes** — active and contested. MAST taxonomy + [MAST-Data + validated LLM-as-a-Judge annotation pipeline](https://github.com/multi-agent-systems-failure-taxonomy/MAST) (Cohen's κ 0.88). The live architectural dispute: Anthropic's orchestrator-worker win ([blog](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)) vs. Cognition's ["Don't Build Multi-Agents"](https://cognition.com/blog/dont-build-multi-agents) (Walden Yan: fragmented context → conflicting decisions; keep writes single-threaded). Token-matched studies are new and cut against multi-agent: [Tran & Kiela 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460) (2 authors) show single agents match/exceed MAS on multi-hop reasoning under equal thinking-token budgets, and flag API-side budget-measurement artifacts that inflated MAS advantages.
3. **Context management ("context engineering")** — doctrine ahead of evidence. Anthropic's [engineering guide](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) names the levers (compaction, structured note-taking, sub-agent isolation, just-in-time retrieval); trained approaches exist — [Context-Folding/FoldGRPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11967) (branch/return tools + process rewards; map-verified) and [ACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618) (Stanford/SambaNova/Berkeley — evolving "playbook" contexts via delta updates, +10.6% AppWorld, names *brevity bias* and *context collapse* as failure modes). Independent ablations of these levers are scarce.
4. **Agent memory systems** — early, no agreed architecture, eval crisis. [MemGPT/Letta](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560) (OS-style memory tiers, model edits its own memory), [Mem0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413), [A-Mem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110) (NeurIPS 2025, Zettelkasten-style linked memory), subtask-level memory keyed to functional phase ([arXiv:2602.21611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21611), map-verified: +4.7pp SWE-bench, +8.7pp on >28-step tasks), graph memories (MAGMA/GAM, map-verified as 2026-fresh), [sleep-time compute](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171) (Letta + Berkeley: offline memory consolidation between sessions). Benchmarks: LOCOMO (contested), PersistBench (forgetting/leakage).
5. **Agent-environment design** — infrastructure science. ServiceNow's [BrowserGym ecosystem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05467) + [AgentLab](https://github.com/ServiceNow/AgentLab) (unified obs/action spaces across MiniWoB/WebArena/WorkArena; explicitly motivated by fragmented, non-comparable evals); [Terminal-Bench](https://www.tbench.ai/) (Stanford + Laude Institute; 2.0 has 89 tasks with ~3 reviewer-hours auditing each; [Harbor](https://github.com/harbor-framework/terminal-bench) rewrite for containerized rollouts); OSWorld (map-verified, execution-based checkers).
6. **Orchestration frameworks** — mostly engineering churn, thin science. Evidence of churn: Microsoft moved AutoGen to maintenance in favor of [Microsoft Agent Framework](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/agent-framework/migration-guide/from-autogen/) while the community forked it as AG2; OpenAI's experimental Swarm was superseded by its Agents SDK. The *scientific* residue is framework-agnostic: automated agent design — [ADAS/Meta Agent Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08435) (Hu, Lu, Clune: a meta-agent programs new agents in code; discovered agents transfer across domains/models) and [AFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10762) (ICLR 2025 oral: MCTS over code-represented workflows; weaker models pushed past stronger ones on cost-effectiveness Pareto front).

**What's open science vs. vendor churn:** the durable questions are architecture-independent — *when does decomposition beat a single context; what does compaction lose; what should be written to memory and when; how to measure reliability* — and they survive any framework's deprecation. Framework comparisons ("LangGraph vs. CrewAI") are churn: they answer questions the next release invalidates. The MAST authors, Princeton HAL, and Tran & Kiela all deliberately position at the framework-agnostic layer.

Funding: standard academic grants; Princeton's agent-eval line runs through a university center (CITP) — see [release announcement](https://citp.princeton.edu/news/2025/sage-team-princeton-releases-holistic-agent-leaderboard).
- **Nonprofits/institutes.** METR (autonomy time-horizons, nonprofit); [Laude Institute](https://www.tbench.ai/) (co-leads and funds Terminal-Bench with Stanford).
- **Company teams publishing openly.** Anthropic (multi-agent system + context-engineering posts), Sierra (τ-bench), ServiceNow Research (BrowserGym/AgentLab/WorkArena), Chroma (Context Rot, with a [replication toolkit](https://github.com/chroma-core/context-rot)), Cognition (position essays from production experience), Letta (Berkeley spinout, publishes papers), ByteDance Seed/Moonshot/Meta FAIR (trained-agent papers, map-verified). Caveat: memory vendors (Mem0, Zep) publish benchmark claims with direct commercial conflicts — the LOCOMO dispute is the cautionary tale.
- **Solo/small.** Paul Gauthier built [aider](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/) and its polyglot benchmark solo — his leaderboard became a field-wide reference (Epoch AI [tracks it](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/aider-polyglot)). ## Published exemplars

1. **[Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail? (MAST)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)** — Berkeley. First empirically grounded failure taxonomy (14 modes, 3 classes, 1,600+ annotated traces, κ=0.88, validated LLM-judge annotator). Mattered because it converted "multi-agent systems feel flaky" into a measurable distribution — and showed most failures are design, not model capability. Method is replicable by one person with API access.
2. **[AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)** — Princeton, 5 authors. Showed agent benchmarks ignore cost and reproducibility; simple baselines matched SOTA agents at ~50× lower cost. Set the methodological agenda that HAL later industrialized.
3. **[τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)** — Sierra (small industry research team). Introduced simulated-user interaction plus **pass^k**; the single most-adopted reliability statistic for agents (now in Anthropic model cards).
4. **[ADAS: Automated Design of Agentic Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08435)** — Hu, Lu, Clune (3 authors). Meta Agent Search: an agent that programs better agents in code; discoveries transferred across domains and models. Founded the "agent architecture as a search space" line (AFlow etc.) — the scientific alternative to hand-designed orchestration.
5. **[Context Rot](https://research.trychroma.com/context-rot)** — Chroma (small industry lab; report + [replication toolkit](https://github.com/chroma-core/context-rot)). Behavioral, API-only study of 18 models showing non-uniform context use. Exemplar of the exact study shape available to outsiders: no training, careful task design, big influence on context-engineering practice.
6. **[Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems... Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460)** — Tran & Kiela, 2 authors. Token-matched single-vs-multi comparison + information-theoretic argument (Data Processing Inequality); found API budget-measurement artifacts inflating MAS results. Two people reframed a debate the vendors had run on vibes.
7. **[A-Mem: Agentic Memory for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110)** — 6 authors, NeurIPS 2025, code open. Zettelkasten-style dynamic memory linking with memory evolution — a concrete, testable alternative to extraction-pipeline memory (Mem0) and OS-style memory (MemGPT).
8. **[Aider polyglot leaderboard](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/)** — Paul Gauthier, solo. A solo-built agent *and* evaluation (225 Exercism tasks, 6 languages, cost + edit-format metrics) that frontier labs get measured against. Existence proof that one person can own a reference instrument.

## Where the gaps are

- **Benchmark validity is the meta-gap.** [ABC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825): 7/10 agentic benchmarks violate task validity, 7/10 outcome validity, 10/10 have reporting flaws — up to 100% relative error. Nobody systematically re-audits after fixes, and new benchmarks keep shipping without the checklist.
- **No neutral memory evaluation exists.** LOCOMO is simultaneously the industry-standard memory benchmark and demonstrably broken: contested configs, missing ground truth in a whole category, conversations short enough (16–26K tokens) that full-context beats the memory systems ([Zep's own analysis](https://blog.getzep.com/lies-damn-lies-statistics-is-mem0-really-sota-in-agent-memory/)). ⚠️ unverified (secondary essays, e.g. [this audit write-up](https://essays.bloo-mind.ai/posts/2026-05-20-mem-eval/)): claims that ~6.4% of the answer key is wrong and the LLM judge accepts 63% of intentionally wrong answers — I did not open the underlying audit. PersistBench covers forgetting/leakage but not read/write-policy value.
- **The single-vs-multi-agent question is unresolved under controls.** Anthropic reports +90.2% on breadth research at 15× tokens; Cognition says don't do it; Tran & Kiela show single wins token-matched on multi-hop reasoning. No study yet spans task *types* (breadth-first vs. depth-first vs. write-heavy) under matched budgets — the map's Context-Folding entry even notes parallel branching gave *zero* gain on a depth-first benchmark.
- **Compaction is doctrine without ablation.** Anthropic's [guide](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents) prescribes compaction/note-taking/sub-agent isolation, and Claude Opus 4.5 ships context compaction (map-verified), but there is no published independent measurement of *what information compaction destroys* or when each lever dominates.
- **Reliability statistics are immature.** pass^k assumes independent trials; decay-curve/variance frameworks ([Beyond pass@1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29231)) are brand-new and unadopted; HAL found reasoning effort *reducing* accuracy in most runs — unexplained.
- **Failure taxonomies lag the architectures.** MAST was built from 2024–25 frameworks (AutoGen-era); whether its 42/37/21 distribution holds for 2026 orchestrator-worker and single-threaded-compaction scaffolds is untested.

## What a solo researcher could do here

Study shapes matching the profile (API-behavioral, evaluation-methodology-heavy, ≤$500/study). Facts and precedents, not a ranking:

1. **MAST-style failure annotation on a modern scaffold.** Apply/extend the open [MAST pipeline](https://github.com/multi-agent-systems-failure-taxonomy/MAST) (taxonomy + validated LLM-judge) to traces from a 2026 scaffold class it never saw (e.g., Claude Agent SDK orchestrator-worker runs, or compaction-based single agents). Precedent: MAST itself was trace annotation + judge validation — the profile's published LLM-as-a-judge work is the same method. Cost: API rollouts + judging, well under $500 for a few hundred traces.
2. **A neutral, pre-registered memory-system audit.** Re-run Mem0, Zep, Letta, and A-Mem plus a full-context baseline and a plain notes-file baseline under pinned configs on LOCOMO + PersistBench; separately *validate the judge* (the known weak link). Precedent: the [GitHub-issue audits](https://github.com/getzep/zep-papers/issues/5) show demand; nobody has published a conflict-free version. Directly reuses the profile's RAG-hallucination judge methodology.
3. **Token-matched topology ablation across task types.** Extend [Tran & Kiela's](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460) design from multi-hop QA to breadth-first (research/wide-search) vs. depth-first (τ-bench/Terminal-Bench subsets) tasks, using the open [HAL harness](https://github.com/princeton-pli/hal-harness) and cost-controlled reporting per [Kapoor et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502) HAL's $40K bought 21,730 rollouts across 81 model×benchmark cells; a 2-topology × 2-model × 1-benchmark slice fits a few hundred dollars.
4. **Compaction-loss study (Context-Rot for agents).** Plant verifiable facts/constraints early in long agent runs; measure post-compaction recall and downstream task effects across compaction strategies (built-in compaction vs. structured notes vs. sub-agent summaries per the [Anthropic guide](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)). Precedent: Chroma's [Context Rot](https://research.trychroma.com/context-rot) — a small industry team, pure API behavioral design, field-wide influence; its [toolkit](https://github.com/chroma-core/context-rot) is open.
5. **Reliability decay curves on open scaffolds.** Measure pass^k, run-to-run variance, and failure-onset as a function of task length for 2–3 open scaffolds (mini-SWE-agent, aider, a Claude Agent SDK baseline), testing whether pass^k's independence assumption holds. Precedents: τ-bench's pass^k ([arXiv:2406.12045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045)) and the 3-author [Beyond pass@1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29231) framework awaiting empirical adoption.
6. **Benchmark validity audit as a contribution.** Apply the [ABC checklist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825) to 2–3 post-2025 agentic benchmarks (e.g., Terminal-Bench 2.0, memory benchmarks) and publish fix-verified re-scores. Precedent: ABC itself, plus the Princeton CORE-Bench/HAL line; zero GPUs required, pure evaluation science.

## What we still don't know

- Whether multi-agent decomposition beats a token-matched single agent on *any* task family other than breadth-first research — and where the crossover sits.
- What compaction destroys, and whether "what to forget" can be specified better than heuristically (PersistBench shows the cost of getting it wrong).
- Whether MAST's failure distribution (design ≫ capability) holds for 2026 scaffolds, and whether taxonomy-guided fixes actually shift the distribution (no published intervention study found).
- Whether *any* memory system beats a full-context baseline once conversations exceed the context window — the only regime where memory is necessary rather than convenient (LOCOMO never tests it).
- The right reliability statistic for agents: pass^k independence, variance sources (model sampling vs. environment nondeterminism vs. scaffold), and how HAL's "more reasoning effort → lower accuracy" result generalizes. ⚠️ that result is from the paper's own summary; I did not reproduce its analysis.
- Whether automated agent design (ADAS/AFlow) discoveries survive contamination controls and transfer to held-out benchmark suites at 2026 model scale.
- The economics: no published model of when a 15× token premium pays for itself in task value.

## Fit notes

The domain's most acute open problems are *evaluation-methodology* problems — judge validation (MAST's annotator, LOCOMO's broken judge), cost-controlled comparison (Kapoor et al.), and reliability statistics (pass^k) — which is the profile's published strength (LLM-as-a-judge study on RAG hallucination detection). Every study shape above is behavioral/architectural work on open scaffolds via frontier APIs: no training runs, no GPU rental required, within a ≤$500 API budget by HAL's published cost arithmetic. The data-pipeline/XGBoost background applies directly to trace-corpus analysis (MAST-Data is 1,600+ annotated traces; HAL logs tens of thousands of rollouts). Heavy Claude usage aligns with the fact that several primary sources (multi-agent system, context-engineering doctrine, Claude Agent SDK compaction) are Anthropic-published and openly scaffoldable.
