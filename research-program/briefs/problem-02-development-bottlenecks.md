# Problem 02 — Model-Development Bottlenecks: measuring what actually limits AI progress

*Brief for the research program · researched 2026-07-08 · grounded in map docs [data-centric-ai](../../ai-research-map/07-data-systems-hardware/data-centric-ai.md) and [scaling-laws-and-theory](../../ai-research-map/02-architectures-and-training/scaling-laws-and-theory.md), then web-verified.*

---

## The problem, concretely

Frontier AI progress depends on a small number of scaling inputs, and each has a suspected limit. The domain here is not *fixing* the limits — it is **measuring, forecasting, and auditing them**, which almost nobody does independently. The measurable sub-problems:

1. **The data wall** (running out of human-written text to train on). Epoch AI estimates the effective stock of human text at **~300T tokens (90% CI 100T–1,000T)**, exhausted by compute-optimal training (the loss-minimizing balance of model size vs. data) ~2028, and as early as ~2025–27 under overtraining (deliberately using more data than compute-optimal) ([Villalobos et al. / Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/will-we-run-out-of-data-limits-of-llm-scaling-based-on-human-generated-data) — estimated the usable text supply and projected when training would exhaust it; also an [ICML 2024 position paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3692070.3694094)). Ilya Sutskever's NeurIPS 2024 framing — data as "the fossil fuel of AI," "pre-training as we know it will end" — made this mainstream ([coverage](https://www.machine.news/ilya-sutskever-peak-data-ai-openai/)). Synthetic data (model-generated training text) routes around it only in *verifiable* domains (math/code); training on model output risks **model collapse** — loss of distribution tails, i.e. rare cases vanish as models learn from their own outputs ([Shumailov et al., Nature 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y) — showed recursive training on generated data degrades models) — though collapse is avoided when real data *accumulates* rather than being replaced ([Gerstgrasser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413) — showed adding synthetic data on top of real data, rather than substituting, prevents collapse).
2. **Compute scaling limits.** Epoch's flagship analysis finds ~2e29-FLOP runs (total arithmetic operations in a training run) feasible by 2030, with **power the first binding constraint** (electricity supply hits first), then chip-manufacturing capacity, then data, then the "latency wall" (the physical limit on how fast signals move between chips) ([Can AI scaling continue through 2030?](https://epoch.ai/blog/can-ai-scaling-continue-through-2030) — projected which resource caps out first as training scales; companion [power-demand forecast](https://epoch.ai/blog/power-demands-of-frontier-ai-training)). Power-as-first-binding is independently corroborated by [RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3572-1.html) and [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/electricity-supply-bottleneck-us-ai-dominance) (neither citing Epoch for the claim); the *ordering of the downstream* constraints remains Epoch-only. Background trend: training compute ~5×/year, algorithmic efficiency (same capability for less compute) ~3×/year ([Epoch trends](https://epoch.ai/trends)).
3. **RL-scaling bottlenecks** (limits on reinforcement learning, now the main lever for reasoning models). RLVR = RL with verifiable rewards — training on tasks (math/code) where an answer can be automatically checked right or wrong. (a) **Entropy collapse** — in RLVR, policy entropy H (a measure of how varied/exploratory the model's outputs are) crashes early and performance obeys an empirical exchange law **R = −a·e^H + b** (reward R trades off against remaining entropy), so the ceiling is set before training finishes ([Cui et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617) — identified and formalized the entropy-vs-reward tradeoff); follow-ups **refine, not refute,** the mechanism by localizing the cause in positive-advantage tokens (the tokens that get reinforced) ([arXiv:2511.05993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05993) — "tokens with positive advantages are the primary drivers of entropy collapse," consistent with Cui's "high-probability action with high advantage would reduce policy entropy"; primary-verified pass 2) and clipping asymmetries (uneven treatment of up- vs down-weighted updates) ([arXiv:2509.26114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26114)). (b) **Whether RLVR adds capability at all** — RLVR-trained models beat base models at pass@1 (first-try success) but *lose* at large-k pass@k (success within k tries), suggesting RL sharpens sampling rather than expanding the reasoning boundary ([Yue et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837) — found RL narrows but doesn't grow what the model can solve; primary-verified pass 2); **directly contested** by prolonged-RL work ([ProRL / Liu et al., NVIDIA 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24864) — RL-trained models "consistently outperform base models across a wide range of pass@k evaluations, including scenarios where base models fail entirely regardless of the number of attempts"; primary-verified pass 2). The two papers contradict each other head-on on the same metric (pass@k at large k); the debate is **unresolved as of 2026**, with a two-stage reconciliation attempt ([arXiv:2510.04028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04028)) arguing both are phase-dependent. (c) **Environment scarcity** — verifiable RL environments (checkable task simulators for RL training) are called "the key bottleneck to the next wave of AI progress"; labs buy them privately while open supply is thin ([Prime Intellect Environments Hub](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/environments); [Mechanize's "GPT-3 moment for RL"](https://www.mechanize.work/blog/the-upcoming-gpt-3-moment-for-rl/)). RL compute-performance itself follows a **sigmoid** (S-curve that flattens out), **not a power law** (steady straight-line gains on a log plot) ([ScaleRL, Meta, 400k+ GPU-hours](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13786) — first systematic measurement of how RL performance scales with compute). The **direction** — RL post-training scaling is *bounded/saturating and predictable from small runs*, unlike pretraining — is now independently corroborated by a non-Meta study ([Tan et al. 2026, USTC/Shanghai AI Lab/Oxford, Qwen2.5 0.5B–72B, 63 RL runs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25300) — RL learning-efficiency k(N) follows a **saturation trend** k(N)=K_max/(1+N₀/N) with model scale, and RL gains are extrapolable from small models; primary-verified pass 3). **Caveat:** the two disagree on functional form — ScaleRL argues a sigmoid is *more robust than a power law* for the reward-vs-compute curve, while Tan et al. fit an *approximately log-linear power law* on the compute axis (test loss L=1−PassRate); so the **saturation/diminishing-returns direction is multi-source, but ScaleRL's specific sigmoid form is not independently replicated** — Tan et al. in fact prefer a power law there.
4. **Training instabilities** (large runs suddenly blowing up mid-training). Loss spikes (sudden jumps in training error) and divergences appear at scales where reproduction is unaffordable, so evidence is anecdotal and proprietary; small-scale proxies (high learning rate) can reproduce attention-logit growth and output-logit divergence (two known failure signatures where internal activation values explode) ([Wortsman et al., ICLR 2024 oral](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) — showed big-model instabilities can be triggered cheaply at small scale by cranking the learning rate); newer work builds module-level monitors that fire thousands of steps before divergence, but validates via fault injection (deliberately breaking things), not real incidents ([arXiv:2606.28116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28116) — built early-warning instability detectors).
5. **Evaluation as a bottleneck.** Benchmarks saturate (top models all cluster near the ceiling, so the test can't tell them apart — frontier models cluster >88–90%, GPQA Diamond's (a hard expert-level science QA benchmark) usable range consumed in ~2 years — [systematic saturation study](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16763v1)), leaderboards are gameable ([The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) — audited a major leaderboard and found it manipulable), and the community lacks trusted independent measurement — Karpathy's "evaluation crisis" framing ([analysis](https://acingai.com/articles/llm-evaluation-crisis)). If you can't measure capability, you can't measure the other bottlenecks either.

**What's at stake:** hundreds of billions in capex, compute-governance thresholds, and AGI-timeline forecasts all lean on a handful of measurements (Epoch's token stock, METR's time horizons, saturating benchmarks) produced by very few, sometimes conflicted, teams.

## The research field around it

- **Bottleneck forecasting / trend measurement** (mature, tiny field): Epoch AI's data-stock, compute, power, and efficiency trend lines ([epoch.ai/trends](https://epoch.ai/trends)); METR's **time-horizon metric** (how long a task a model can reliably finish, as a proxy for capability) — task length completable at 50% reliability doubles ~every 7 months, ~4 months in 2024–25 ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/) — introduced the metric and measured its doubling rate, [cross-domain follow-up](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-14-how-does-time-horizon-vary-across-domains/) — checked whether it holds outside software tasks); bottleneck-interaction models coupling compute slowdowns to capability timelines ([Whitfill, Snodin & Becker 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19492) — modeled how slower compute growth pushes back capability forecasts).
- **Scaling-law science and its forensics** (mature empirics, weak theory). Scaling laws = fitted equations predicting how error falls as model size, data, and compute grow. Chinchilla lineage (the DeepMind result on the compute-optimal data-to-parameter ratio); Epoch's replication that found the original Chinchilla fit's constants implausible ([Besiroglu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102) — re-fit the published Chinchilla data and showed the reported numbers don't hold up, [blog](https://epoch.ai/blog/chinchilla-scaling-a-replication-attempt)); the two-author reconciliation of the Kaplan-vs-Chinchilla exponent discrepancy (the two foundational scaling papers disagreed on the key exponent) ([Pearce & Song](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12907) — traced the disagreement to a parameter-counting difference). Map doc's verdict stands: laws predict *loss*, not capabilities.
- **Data-wall and synthetic-data-limits research** (active, contested): the Shumailov/Gerstgrasser/Borji collapse debate — does self-training destroy models, and under what conditions ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y) — the original collapse result / [accumulation rebuttal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413) — collapse avoided if real data is kept / [solo statistical note](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12954) — reframes the claim statistically); routing-around work — squeezing more from limited data (REWIRE recycling, i.e. rewriting low-quality web text into usable data, [arXiv:2506.04689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04689); fastText-scale curation, i.e. cheap classifier-based filtering of massive corpora, [Ultra-FineWeb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05427)) — see map doc for depth.
- **RL-scaling measurement** (young, lab-dominated but no longer single-source on direction): ScaleRL's sigmoid framework (fitting an S-curve to how RL performance grows with compute) is the largest systematic study and cost 400k+ GPU-hours inside Meta ([arXiv:2510.13786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13786)); an independent academic study reaches the same bounded-scaling conclusion at far lower cost but via a power-law-plus-saturation form rather than a sigmoid ([Tan et al. 2026, arXiv:2509.25300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25300)); entropy-collapse mechanism work is mostly Chinese academic labs ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617) — the founding entropy-collapse paper, and successors); individual analysts synthesize causes (e.g. [Jean Kaddour's blog](https://www.jeankaddour.com/blog/entropy_collapse) — a solo synthesis of entropy-collapse findings). Environment supply is a *market claim*, not yet a measured quantity ([Environments Hub](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/environments)).
- **Training-instability science** (immature): small-scale proxies ([Wortsman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) — reproduces big-model instabilities cheaply), preemptive monitors ([arXiv:2606.28116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28116) — early-warning detectors); community efficiency baselines like the **modded-nanoGPT speedrun** (an open competition to train a small GPT-2 to a target loss as fast as possible: 45 min → under 90 s on 8×H100) double as an open ledger of what training improvements actually work ([repo](https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt), [progress analysis](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/j3gp8tebQiFJqzBgg/how-the-nanogpt-speedrun-wr-dropped-by-20-in-3-months)).
- **Evaluation-integrity research** (growing fast): leaderboard audits ([Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) — exposed manipulation of a major leaderboard), saturation studies ([arXiv:2602.16763](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16763v1) — measured how fast benchmarks stop discriminating), Epoch's [Benchmarking Hub](https://epoch.ai/blog/introducing-benchmarks-dashboard) running standardized independent evals (Inspect = UK AISI's open eval-running framework; UK AISI-funded — [about](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/about)).

## Published exemplars

1. **[Chinchilla Scaling: A replication attempt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102)** — Besiroglu, Erdil, Barnett, You (4 people, Epoch). Re-extracted data *from the original paper's figures*, showed the reported constants imply ~70 tokens/param (not 20) and the confidence intervals would need ~600k experiments. Proof that the field's canonical law went un-audited for two years, and that auditing needs a laptop, not a cluster.
2. **[Reconciling Kaplan and Chinchilla Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12907)** — Pearce & Song (2 authors, TMLR). Resolved the field's biggest empirical contradiction (C^0.73 vs C^0.50) as mostly a non-embedding-parameter counting artifact. A two-person paper that fixed how everyone runs scaling studies.
3. **[A Note on Shumailov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12954)** — Borji (solo). A KDE-based (kernel density estimation — a standard way to estimate a distribution's shape) statistical reframing of a Nature paper's headline collapse claim. Shows a solo commentary can shape a high-stakes debate.
4. **[Will we run out of data?](https://epoch.ai/publications/will-we-run-out-of-data-limits-of-llm-scaling-based-on-human-generated-data)** — Villalobos et al. (Epoch, small team; ICML position paper). The 300T-token / ~2028 estimate that anchors *every* data-wall discussion — and is still essentially the only quantitative estimate.
5. **[Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/)** — METR. Invented the time-horizon metric (doubling ~7 months); arguably the most-cited capability-trend measurement outside Epoch, produced by a nonprofit.
6. **[The Entropy Mechanism of RL for Reasoning LMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617)** — Cui et al. (Tsinghua-led). Named entropy collapse, gave the R = −a·e^H + b exchange law and Clip-Cov/KL-Cov fixes (two proposed tweaks to the RL update that slow the entropy crash); defined the RL-bottleneck research agenda now being contested by a dozen follow-ups.
7. **[The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879)** — Singh et al. (Cohere Labs + academics, NeurIPS 2025). Audited Chatbot Arena (the crowd-voted head-to-head model ranking) from outside: 27 private Meta variants pre-Llama-4, selective disclosure, 112% ArenaHard win-rate gain from training on Arena-like data. Template for adversarial eval-infrastructure audits.
8. **[Forecasting AI Time Horizon Under Compute Slowdowns](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19492)** — Whitfill, Snodin & Becker (3 authors ⚠️ affiliations unverified). Couples the compute bottleneck to the capability trend: under realistic compute growth, 1-month/80%-reliability horizons (tasks a model can do reliably over a month of human-equivalent work) arrive ~7 years later than naive extrapolation. Rare example of *bottleneck-interaction* modeling.

## Where the gaps are

- **RL-scaling measurement has a partial independent counterpart, but ScaleRL's specific sigmoid form is unreplicated.** The largest systematic RL compute-scaling study is still Meta's 400k-GPU-hour [ScaleRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13786), but it is **no longer the only** one: an independent academic study ([Tan et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25300), Qwen2.5 0.5B–72B, VeRL, 63 runs) reaches the same *higher-level* conclusion — RL post-training scaling is bounded/saturating and extrapolable from small models — at a budget far below Meta's. **However**, Tan et al. fit a *power law* on the compute axis and locate saturation in *model scale* (k(N) plateau), whereas ScaleRL argues a *sigmoid* is more robust for *reward-vs-compute*; so ScaleRL's exact sigmoid coefficients remain independently unreplicated (pass 3). The entropy exchange law's coefficients ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617)) have still not been tested for generality across model families by a neutral party (inferred from absence in searches, ⚠️) — the shape-1 telemetry study below remains the open opportunity.
- **Nobody measures the RL-environment stock.** Epoch measured the token stock; there is no equivalent census of verifiable environments — count, quality, reward-hackability, growth rate — despite "environments are the bottleneck" being a funding thesis for [Prime Intellect](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/environments) and [Mechanize](https://www.mechanize.work/blog/the-upcoming-gpt-3-moment-for-rl/) (both parties with a commercial stake in the claim).
- **The data wall's central number has no independent *replication* — but its magnitude is independently corroborated.** Epoch's 300T central estimate has no direct re-fit by a neutral party; however, an independent bottom-up analysis arrives at ~60–160T tokens of high-quality *public* English/multilingual text ([Educating Silicon 2024](https://www.educatingsilicon.com/2024/05/09/how-much-llm-training-data-is-there-in-the-limit/) — original per-source calculation, not citing Epoch's stock figure; verified pass 2), which overlaps Epoch's 90% CI (100T–1,000T) and sits within an order of magnitude of the 300T center. So the *order of magnitude* is now multi-source; the *specific 300T point estimate and 2028 exhaustion date* remain Epoch-only. No public *longitudinal* (tracked-over-time) measurement exists of how much of fresh web crawl is already synthetic — the model-collapse debate runs on toy recursive setups ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y) vs [accumulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413)) rather than measured contamination.
- **Training-instability evidence is proprietary.** [Wortsman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) note reproduction is unaffordable; the [monitors paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28116) validates on injected faults. There is no public incident dataset of real loss spikes across open training runs.
- **Eval measurement is both the tool and a bottleneck, and its independence is fragile:** the leading independent benchmarker took undisclosed money from the lab it benchmarks ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/)); saturation dynamics were only recently studied systematically ([arXiv:2602.16763](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16763v1)).
- **Laws predict loss, not capabilities** (map doc, [scaling-laws-and-theory](../../ai-research-map/02-architectures-and-training/scaling-laws-and-theory.md)): emergence forecasting remains open, which undermines every bottleneck→capability translation.

## What a solo researcher could do here

*(Study shapes with precedents; facts only, no ranking.)*

1. **Cross-family entropy-collapse telemetry.** Fit R = −a·e^H + b across 3–5 open models (0.5–3B) × 2–3 RLVR recipes; test whether Clip-Cov/KL-Cov generalize; release the training telemetry. Cui et al.'s law is from one group; small-scale RLVR runs fit a ≤$500 rented-GPU budget. Precedent for the value of small-scale replication: [Wortsman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) showed instabilities reproduce at small scale + high LR.
2. **An "Epoch-style" census of RL environments.** Enumerate the [Environments Hub](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/environments) and verifiers ecosystem; score environments for verifiability and reward-hackability (how easily a model can game the reward without doing the task) using programmatic checks plus LLM-as-a-judge (using a language model to grade outputs — the researcher's published method); publish stock/growth/quality numbers where currently only vendor claims exist.
3. **A synthetic-contamination index for the open web.** Train a cheap classifier (fastText/XGBoost-class — lightweight text classifiers that run on CPUs, not GPUs; precedent: [Ultra-FineWeb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05427) filtered 15T tokens on ~1,000 CPU-hours) over successive Common Crawl snapshots (the public periodic web-scrape corpus) to estimate the AI-generated share over time — the missing empirical input to the model-collapse debate.
4. **Scaling-law forensics.** Re-extract and re-fit published curves (ScaleRL's sigmoids, Apple's [distillation law](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08606) — a scaling curve for how well a small model can be trained to imitate a larger one, RL-entropy fits) the way [Besiroglu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102) and [Borji](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12954) did — laptop-only, high-leverage, an established genre with 1–4-author precedents.
5. **Benchmark-lifetime measurement.** Quantify saturation velocity (usable-range consumption per year, GPQA-style) across [Epoch's Benchmarking Hub](https://epoch.ai/blog/introducing-benchmarks-dashboard) public data; forecast when current benchmarks stop discriminating — extends the [saturation study](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16763v1) with the researcher's eval-methodology background.
6. **Open training-instability incident dataset.** Mine public artifacts (OLMo logs, [modded-nanogpt](https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt) history, open HF runs) for spike/divergence events; annotate and test whether small-scale predictors (LR-sensitivity, attention-logit growth) flag them — the real-incident validation the [monitors paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28116) lacks.

## What we still don't know

- Whether the entropy-performance exchange law holds across model families and scales (single-group finding; follow-ups *refine* the mechanism toward positive-advantage tokens rather than refute it — [arXiv:2511.05993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05993), verified pass 2; but no neutral cross-family test of the R = −a·e^H + b coefficients themselves).
- **[STRUCTURAL — open empirical question] Whether RLVR can expand the capability boundary at all.** [Yue et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837) (verified pass 2) says base models win at large pass@k; [ProRL/Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24864) (verified pass 2) says RL-trained models win even where base models fail entirely. The two contradict head-on and the field itself calls the debate "unresolved" as of 2026 (reconciliation attempt: [arXiv:2510.04028](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04028)). This is a genuine open question, not a citation gap — it cannot be closed by verification.
- The actual synthetic share of fresh web text — no verified public longitudinal number found.
- Whether Epoch has materially revised the estimate for the reasoning/RL era: as of pass 2, the current public figure remains ~300T with full utilization projected **2026–2032** (80% CI), earlier under overtraining (verified against Epoch's page pass 2) — no RL-era-specific revision published. Open nuance: MoE models need ~6× more tokens/param than dense at high sparsity, which analysts argue pulls the effective wall *earlier* (~2028–29) for large MoE runs ([Nesov, LessWrong 2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/XiMRyQcEyKCryST8T/slowdown-after-2028-compute-rlvr-uncertainty-moe-data-wall)) — not yet reflected in Epoch's headline number.
- The true size, quality, and growth rate of the RL-environment supply; lab spending figures are journalistic (⚠️).
- Whether small-scale instability proxies predict frontier-scale spikes (open question in [Wortsman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322)).
- Whether the *exact ordering* of Epoch's [2030 constraints](https://epoch.ai/blog/can-ai-scaling-continue-through-2030) (power → chips → data → latency) still holds given 2025–26 buildouts. **Power-as-first-binding is now corroborated by multiple independent primaries** ([RAND RRA3572-1, Pilz/Mahmood/Heim 2025](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3572-1.html) — grid capacity "already posing challenges," verified pass 2; and [CSIS 2025, McGeady et al.](https://www.csis.org/analysis/electricity-supply-bottleneck-us-ai-dominance) — "electricity supply is the most acutely binding constraint on expanded U.S. computational capacity," synthesized from LBNL/EIA/grid-operator data, not Epoch; verified pass 3), so the *direction* is now three-source-corroborated; the precise ranking of the *downstream* constraints (chips → data → latency) remains Epoch-only.
- Whitfill/Snodin/Becker affiliations and independence (⚠️ unverified).

## Fit notes

The field's flagship results were produced by 1–4-person teams doing data re-extraction, curve fitting, and classifier-based measurement — the profile's classical-ML/pipeline strengths map directly onto shapes 3–5, and none require more than a laptop plus occasional API calls. The published LLM-as-a-judge evaluation work transfers directly to environment auditing (shape 2) and benchmark-lifetime work (shape 5), both of which are evaluation-methodology problems at heart. The ≤$500/study GPU budget covers sub-3B RLVR telemetry runs (shape 1) but rules out ScaleRL-style multi-thousand-GPU-hour studies, keeping the researcher on the measurement/audit side rather than the recipe-development side.
