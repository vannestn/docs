# Problem 02 — Model-Development Bottlenecks: measuring what actually limits AI progress

*Brief for the research program · researched 2026-07-08 · grounded in map docs [data-centric-ai](../../ai-research-map/07-data-systems-hardware/data-centric-ai.md) and [scaling-laws-and-theory](../../ai-research-map/02-architectures-and-training/scaling-laws-and-theory.md), then web-verified.*

---

## The problem, concretely

Frontier AI progress depends on a small number of scaling inputs, and each has a suspected limit. The domain here is not *fixing* the limits — it is **measuring, forecasting, and auditing them**, which almost nobody does independently. The measurable sub-problems:

1. **The data wall.** Epoch AI estimates the effective stock of human text at **~300T tokens (90% CI 100T–1,000T)**, exhausted by compute-optimal training ~2028, and as early as ~2025–27 under overtraining ([Villalobos et al. / Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/will-we-run-out-of-data-limits-of-llm-scaling-based-on-human-generated-data), also an [ICML 2024 position paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3692070.3694094)). Ilya Sutskever's NeurIPS 2024 framing — data as "the fossil fuel of AI," "pre-training as we know it will end" — made this mainstream ([coverage](https://www.machine.news/ilya-sutskever-peak-data-ai-openai/)). Synthetic data routes around it only in *verifiable* domains (math/code); training on model output risks **model collapse** — loss of distribution tails ([Shumailov et al., Nature 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y)) — though collapse is avoided when real data *accumulates* rather than being replaced ([Gerstgrasser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413)).
2. **Compute scaling limits.** Epoch's flagship analysis finds ~2e29-FLOP runs feasible by 2030, with **power the first binding constraint**, then chip-manufacturing capacity, then data, then the "latency wall" ([Can AI scaling continue through 2030?](https://epoch.ai/blog/can-ai-scaling-continue-through-2030); companion [power-demand forecast](https://epoch.ai/blog/power-demands-of-frontier-ai-training)). Background trend: training compute ~5×/year, algorithmic efficiency ~3×/year ([Epoch trends](https://epoch.ai/trends)).
3. **RL-scaling bottlenecks.** (a) **Entropy collapse** — in RLVR, policy entropy H crashes early and performance obeys an empirical exchange law **R = −a·e^H + b**, so the ceiling is set before training finishes ([Cui et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617)); follow-ups localize the cause in positive-advantage tokens ([arXiv:2511.05993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05993)) and clipping asymmetries ([arXiv:2509.26114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26114)). (b) **Whether RLVR adds capability at all** — RLVR-trained models beat base models at pass@1 but *lose* at large-k pass@k, suggesting RL sharpens sampling rather than expanding the reasoning boundary ([Yue et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837)); contested by later prolonged-RL work (⚠️ counter-evidence, e.g. ProRL, not verified in this pass). (c) **Environment scarcity** — verifiable RL environments are called "the key bottleneck to the next wave of AI progress"; labs buy them privately while open supply is thin ([Prime Intellect Environments Hub](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/environments); [Mechanize's "GPT-3 moment for RL"](https://www.mechanize.work/blog/the-upcoming-gpt-3-moment-for-rl/)). RL compute-performance itself follows a **sigmoid, not a power law** ([ScaleRL, Meta, 400k+ GPU-hours](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13786)).
4. **Training instabilities.** Loss spikes and divergences appear at scales where reproduction is unaffordable, so evidence is anecdotal and proprietary; small-scale proxies (high learning rate) can reproduce attention-logit growth and output-logit divergence ([Wortsman et al., ICLR 2024 oral](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322)); newer work builds module-level monitors that fire thousands of steps before divergence, but validates via fault injection, not real incidents ([arXiv:2606.28116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28116)).
5. **Evaluation as a bottleneck.** Benchmarks saturate (frontier models cluster >88–90%, GPQA Diamond's usable range consumed in ~2 years — [systematic saturation study](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16763v1)), leaderboards are gameable ([The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879)), and the community lacks trusted independent measurement — Karpathy's "evaluation crisis" framing ([analysis](https://acingai.com/articles/llm-evaluation-crisis)). If you can't measure capability, you can't measure the other bottlenecks either.

**What's at stake:** hundreds of billions in capex, compute-governance thresholds, and AGI-timeline forecasts all lean on a handful of measurements (Epoch's token stock, METR's time horizons, saturating benchmarks) produced by very few, sometimes conflicted, teams.

## The research field around it

- **Bottleneck forecasting / trend measurement** (mature, tiny field): Epoch AI's data-stock, compute, power, and efficiency trend lines ([epoch.ai/trends](https://epoch.ai/trends)); METR's **time-horizon metric** — task length completable at 50% reliability doubles ~every 7 months, ~4 months in 2024–25 ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/), [cross-domain follow-up](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-14-how-does-time-horizon-vary-across-domains/)); bottleneck-interaction models coupling compute slowdowns to capability timelines ([Whitfill, Snodin & Becker 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19492)).
- **Scaling-law science and its forensics** (mature empirics, weak theory): Chinchilla lineage; Epoch's replication that found the original Chinchilla fit's constants implausible ([Besiroglu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102), [blog](https://epoch.ai/blog/chinchilla-scaling-a-replication-attempt)); the two-author reconciliation of the Kaplan-vs-Chinchilla exponent discrepancy ([Pearce & Song](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12907)). Map doc's verdict stands: laws predict *loss*, not capabilities.
- **Data-wall and synthetic-data-limits research** (active, contested): the Shumailov/Gerstgrasser/Borji collapse debate ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y) / [accumulation rebuttal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413) / [solo statistical note](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12954)); routing-around work (REWIRE recycling, [arXiv:2506.04689](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04689); fastText-scale curation, [Ultra-FineWeb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05427)) — see map doc for depth.
- **RL-scaling measurement** (young, lab-dominated): ScaleRL's sigmoid framework is the first systematic study and cost 400k+ GPU-hours inside Meta ([arXiv:2510.13786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13786)); entropy-collapse mechanism work is mostly Chinese academic labs ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617) and successors); individual analysts synthesize causes (e.g. [Jean Kaddour's blog](https://www.jeankaddour.com/blog/entropy_collapse)). Environment supply is a *market claim*, not yet a measured quantity ([Environments Hub](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/environments)).
- **Training-instability science** (immature): small-scale proxies ([Wortsman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322)), preemptive monitors ([arXiv:2606.28116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28116)); community efficiency baselines like the **modded-nanoGPT speedrun** (GPT-2-level loss: 45 min → under 90 s on 8×H100) double as an open ledger of what training improvements actually work ([repo](https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt), [progress analysis](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/j3gp8tebQiFJqzBgg/how-the-nanogpt-speedrun-wr-dropped-by-20-in-3-months)).
- **Evaluation-integrity research** (growing fast): leaderboard audits ([Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879)), saturation studies ([arXiv:2602.16763](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16763v1)), Epoch's [Benchmarking Hub](https://epoch.ai/blog/introducing-benchmarks-dashboard) running standardized independent evals (Inspect-based, UK AISI-funded — [about](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/about)).

## Who does this work

- **[Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai)** — the anchor institution; nonprofit, ~funded by Open Philanthropy (now Coefficient Giving; [$1.96M 2022 grant](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/grants/epoch-general-support/), later [$6.9M general support](https://openphilanthropy.org/grants/epoch-ai-general-support-2025)), fiscally sponsored by Rethink Priorities, plus lab/government contracts disclosed at [epoch.ai/our-funding](https://epoch.ai/our-funding). Note the independence stress test: OpenAI funded FrontierMath and disclosure came late ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/)).
- **[METR](https://metr.org)** — nonprofit evaluator; time-horizon trend work is the de-facto capability-growth yardstick.
- **Academic scaling-law groups** — small and scattered: Pearce & Song (TMLR reconciliation); Stanford's Koyejo/Schaeffer cluster (model-collapse rebuttal, Leaderboard Illusion co-authors); the Tsinghua RLVR-entropy cluster (Cui et al.).
- **Companies whose business *is* the bottleneck** — [Prime Intellect](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/environments) (open environment supply, VC-funded startup) and [Mechanize](https://www.mechanize.work/blog/the-upcoming-gpt-3-moment-for-rl/) (founded by ex-Epoch staff Besiroglu/Erdil/Barnett — the Chinchilla-replication authors — to build RL environments commercially; [EA Forum note](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/HqKnreqC3EFF9YcEs/untitled-draft-siuu)). Meta/Apple/DeepMind do the biggest measurement studies (ScaleRL, distillation laws) but only publish what suits them.
- **Individuals** — this field is unusually open to solo analysts: Ali Borji's single-author collapse note ([arXiv:2410.12954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12954)); Jean Kaddour's [entropy-collapse synthesis](https://www.jeankaddour.com/blog/entropy_collapse); Keller Jordan's speedrun community; [AI Digest's time-horizon explainer](https://theaidigest.org/time-horizons).
- **How outsiders contribute:** re-extraction/re-fitting of published curves (no GPUs needed), open telemetry at small scale, community benchmarks (speedrun records are posted "on arXiv or X"), Environments Hub contributions, and Epoch-style public dashboards. Funding models: Coefficient Giving/UK AISI grants (Epoch), nonprofit donations (METR), VC (Prime Intellect, Mechanize), academia, and unfunded solo work.

## Published exemplars

1. **[Chinchilla Scaling: A replication attempt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102)** — Besiroglu, Erdil, Barnett, You (4 people, Epoch). Re-extracted data *from the original paper's figures*, showed the reported constants imply ~70 tokens/param (not 20) and the confidence intervals would need ~600k experiments. Proof that the field's canonical law went un-audited for two years, and that auditing needs a laptop, not a cluster.
2. **[Reconciling Kaplan and Chinchilla Scaling Laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12907)** — Pearce & Song (2 authors, TMLR). Resolved the field's biggest empirical contradiction (C^0.73 vs C^0.50) as mostly a non-embedding-parameter counting artifact. A two-person paper that fixed how everyone runs scaling studies.
3. **[A Note on Shumailov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12954)** — Borji (solo). A KDE-based statistical reframing of a Nature paper's headline claim. Shows a solo commentary can shape a high-stakes debate.
4. **[Will we run out of data?](https://epoch.ai/publications/will-we-run-out-of-data-limits-of-llm-scaling-based-on-human-generated-data)** — Villalobos et al. (Epoch, small team; ICML position paper). The 300T-token / ~2028 estimate that anchors *every* data-wall discussion — and is still essentially the only quantitative estimate.
5. **[Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/)** — METR. Invented the time-horizon metric (doubling ~7 months); arguably the most-cited capability-trend measurement outside Epoch, produced by a nonprofit.
6. **[The Entropy Mechanism of RL for Reasoning LMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617)** — Cui et al. (Tsinghua-led). Named entropy collapse, gave the R = −a·e^H + b exchange law and Clip-Cov/KL-Cov fixes; defined the RL-bottleneck research agenda now being contested by a dozen follow-ups.
7. **[The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879)** — Singh et al. (Cohere Labs + academics, NeurIPS 2025). Audited Chatbot Arena from outside: 27 private Meta variants pre-Llama-4, selective disclosure, 112% ArenaHard win-rate gain from training on Arena-like data. Template for adversarial eval-infrastructure audits.
8. **[Forecasting AI Time Horizon Under Compute Slowdowns](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.19492)** — Whitfill, Snodin & Becker (3 authors ⚠️ affiliations unverified). Couples the compute bottleneck to the capability trend: under realistic compute growth, 1-month/80%-reliability horizons arrive ~7 years later than naive extrapolation. Rare example of *bottleneck-interaction* modeling.

## Where the gaps are

- **RL-scaling measurement has no independent counterpart.** The only systematic RL compute-scaling study is Meta's 400k-GPU-hour [ScaleRL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13786); its sigmoid fits have not been independently replicated at small scale, and the entropy exchange law's coefficients ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617)) have not been tested for generality across model families by a neutral party (inferred from absence in searches, ⚠️).
- **Nobody measures the RL-environment stock.** Epoch measured the token stock; there is no equivalent census of verifiable environments — count, quality, reward-hackability, growth rate — despite "environments are the bottleneck" being a funding thesis for [Prime Intellect](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/environments) and [Mechanize](https://www.mechanize.work/blog/the-upcoming-gpt-3-moment-for-rl/) (both parties with a commercial stake in the claim).
- **The data wall is a single-source estimate.** Epoch's 300T/2028 numbers anchor the field with no independent replication; and no public *longitudinal* measurement exists of how much of fresh web crawl is already synthetic — the model-collapse debate runs on toy recursive setups ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y) vs [accumulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413)) rather than measured contamination.
- **Training-instability evidence is proprietary.** [Wortsman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) note reproduction is unaffordable; the [monitors paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28116) validates on injected faults. There is no public incident dataset of real loss spikes across open training runs.
- **Eval measurement is both the tool and a bottleneck, and its independence is fragile:** the leading independent benchmarker took undisclosed money from the lab it benchmarks ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/)); saturation dynamics were only recently studied systematically ([arXiv:2602.16763](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16763v1)).
- **Laws predict loss, not capabilities** (map doc, [scaling-laws-and-theory](../../ai-research-map/02-architectures-and-training/scaling-laws-and-theory.md)): emergence forecasting remains open, which undermines every bottleneck→capability translation.

## What a solo researcher could do here

*(Study shapes with precedents; facts only, no ranking.)*

1. **Cross-family entropy-collapse telemetry.** Fit R = −a·e^H + b across 3–5 open models (0.5–3B) × 2–3 RLVR recipes; test whether Clip-Cov/KL-Cov generalize; release the training telemetry. Cui et al.'s law is from one group; small-scale RLVR runs fit a ≤$500 rented-GPU budget. Precedent for the value of small-scale replication: [Wortsman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) showed instabilities reproduce at small scale + high LR.
2. **An "Epoch-style" census of RL environments.** Enumerate the [Environments Hub](https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/environments) and verifiers ecosystem; score environments for verifiability and reward-hackability using programmatic checks plus LLM-as-a-judge (the researcher's published method); publish stock/growth/quality numbers where currently only vendor claims exist.
3. **A synthetic-contamination index for the open web.** Train a cheap classifier (fastText/XGBoost-class — precedent: [Ultra-FineWeb](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05427) filtered 15T tokens on ~1,000 CPU-hours) over successive Common Crawl snapshots to estimate the AI-generated share over time — the missing empirical input to the model-collapse debate.
4. **Scaling-law forensics.** Re-extract and re-fit published curves (ScaleRL's sigmoids, Apple's [distillation law](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08606), RL-entropy fits) the way [Besiroglu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10102) and [Borji](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12954) did — laptop-only, high-leverage, an established genre with 1–4-author precedents.
5. **Benchmark-lifetime measurement.** Quantify saturation velocity (usable-range consumption per year, GPQA-style) across [Epoch's Benchmarking Hub](https://epoch.ai/blog/introducing-benchmarks-dashboard) public data; forecast when current benchmarks stop discriminating — extends the [saturation study](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16763v1) with the researcher's eval-methodology background.
6. **Open training-instability incident dataset.** Mine public artifacts (OLMo logs, [modded-nanogpt](https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt) history, open HF runs) for spike/divergence events; annotate and test whether small-scale predictors (LR-sensitivity, attention-logit growth) flag them — the real-incident validation the [monitors paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28116) lacks.

## What we still don't know

- Whether the entropy-performance exchange law holds across model families and scales (single-group finding; follow-ups dispute the mechanism — [arXiv:2511.05993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05993)).
- Whether RLVR can expand the capability boundary at all ([Yue et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837) says no at large pass@k; ⚠️ prolonged-RL counter-claims unverified this pass).
- The actual synthetic share of fresh web text — no verified public longitudinal number found.
- Whether Epoch's 300T/2028 data-wall estimate has been materially revised for the reasoning/RL era (⚠️ not checked for a 2025–26 update).
- The true size, quality, and growth rate of the RL-environment supply; lab spending figures are journalistic (⚠️).
- Whether small-scale instability proxies predict frontier-scale spikes (open question in [Wortsman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322)).
- Whether the power-first constraint ordering from Epoch's [2030 analysis](https://epoch.ai/blog/can-ai-scaling-continue-through-2030) (Aug 2024) still holds given 2025–26 datacenter buildouts (⚠️ not re-verified).
- Whitfill/Snodin/Becker affiliations and independence (⚠️ unverified).

## Fit notes

The field's flagship results were produced by 1–4-person teams doing data re-extraction, curve fitting, and classifier-based measurement — the profile's classical-ML/pipeline strengths map directly onto shapes 3–5, and none require more than a laptop plus occasional API calls. The published LLM-as-a-judge evaluation work transfers directly to environment auditing (shape 2) and benchmark-lifetime work (shape 5), both of which are evaluation-methodology problems at heart. The ≤$500/study GPU budget covers sub-3B RLVR telemetry runs (shape 1) but rules out ScaleRL-style multi-thousand-GPU-hour studies, keeping the researcher on the measurement/audit side rather than the recipe-development side. All three career paths stay open: this is Epoch/METR's nonprofit lane, Prime Intellect/Mechanize's startup lane, and an active academic publishing lane (TMLR, NeurIPS D&B, ICLR).
