## Why inequality is a lens, not a mechanism

"AI and labor" asks *does AI destroy jobs*. "AI and wealth inequality" asks a bigger, messier question: **when AI creates value, who ends up owning it?** That value moves through many channels at once — wages, prices, profits, equity, compute rents, IP, taxes, and cross-border flows — and the same technology can compress inequality on one channel while widening it on another. The single most important structural fact in this brief is that the two best-measured channels **point in opposite directions**:

- **Usage is diffusing *down* the income gradient** (equalizing) — *but this is the brief's weakest-corroborated headline claim, and an independent primary contradicts it at the individual-income level (see corroboration §):* within the US, Claude usage inequality *measured across states* is *falling* — the top-5 states' share of per-capita usage dropped from 30% to 24% (Aug 2025–Feb 2026), projecting rough per-capita parity in 5–9 years ([Anthropic March 2026 report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)). **⚠️ Corroboration caveat:** this is a *state-level per-capita geographic* convergence. An independent household-browsing panel finds the *individual income-tier* adoption gap **widening**, not converging — a "widening generative AI divide … with little signs of convergence" ([McElheran et al., "Household Impact of Generative AI," arXiv:2603.03144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03144), Comscore panel 2021–2024, VERIFIED-PRIMARY this pass). The equalizing framing therefore holds *geographically* but is **contested at the income-tier level** — see the corroboration section.
- **Ownership is concentrating *up* the wealth gradient** (unequalizing): the top 1% held **50.2% of US corporate equities and mutual-fund shares** as of 2026 Q1, up from **42.8% in 1989 Q3** (Fed Distributional Financial Accounts — DFA, the Fed's quarterly series splitting national wealth totals across percentiles; **now verified-primary** — computed directly from the [Fed DFA data download](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/dataviz/download/zips/dfa.zip), June 18 2026 release; top-1% = "TopPt1"+"RemainingTop1"). **This is now multi-source corroborated by an *independent methodology*:** the capitalization method (Saez-Zucman / Smith-Zidar-Zwick), which builds top-wealth shares from *tax data* rather than the DFA's SCF-survey allocation, independently finds the top-1% *net-worth* share rising to ~33.7% (2016) — the same rising-concentration trend from a non-DFA instrument; and [Edward Wolff / Levy Institute](https://www.levyinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/wp_502.pdf), using SCF microdata with his *own* wealth series, finds the top 10% holding a record **89% of individually-held corporate equities & mutual-fund shares (end 2021)** and the top-1% wealth share nearly doubling mid-1970s→2013 (see corroboration §). And Anthropic's co-founders became worth **$15.5B each on ~1.6% stakes** after the $965B round (up from a $380B valuation four months prior) ([Forbes verified-primary](https://www.forbes.com/sites/richardnieva/2026/05/29/anthropics-cofounders-worth/); [Bloomberg's $8B/each headline](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-29/anthropic-co-founders-worth-8-billion-each-after-funding-round) reflects a lower stake/valuation basis).

*Nobody has published a dataset that joins these two realized curves.* But the decoupling is no longer just hand-wave: a calibrated general-equilibrium model ([IMF WP/25/68](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2025/068/article-A001-en.xml), verified below) predicts AI *lowers* the wage Gini by 1.73 p.p. while *raising* the wealth Gini by 7.18 p.p. Whether the equalizing tool-access effect can outrun the unequalizing ownership effect *in realized data* is the central open question, and it is a **measurement** question before it is an economic one. That is the thread running through this brief.

**Reading the table:** the mechanisms that *widen* inequality are numerous and mostly ESTABLISHED-as-phenomena but THEORIZED-as-AI-attribution. The mechanisms that *narrow* it are fewer, and the two strongest (A6 augmentation dividend, P4 UBI) share the same fatal gap — the equalizing effect is measured in *productivity or a pilot*, never in *realized economy-wide pay or wealth*. That asymmetry is the honest headline.

## The mechanisms, in depth

### Labor channels (L) — the value split at the point of production
See [problem-05](problem-05-labor-economics.md) for displacement/augmentation, exposure indices, and the Canaries–Denmark null contradiction. The *distributional* extensions:

- **The augmentation/automation split flips by deployment context (L1).** (Augmentation = AI assists a worker; automation = AI does the task instead.) On consumer Claude.ai, augmentation ~57% vs automation ~43% late 2025; but on the enterprise **API, automation dominates 77% vs 12% augmentation** ([AEI Sept 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report) — Anthropic Economic Index, which classifies how Claude conversations map to occupational tasks; confirmed via search of the report). Automation-mode use is the labor→capital transfer; augmentation-mode is the worker-retaining one. The sign of AI's inequality effect therefore depends on *where* AI is deployed, and the enterprise side — where the money is — leans automation. **CONTESTED**; effect on inequality unmeasured.
- **Past automation explains 50–70% of the 1980–2016 rise in between-group wage inequality (L2)** ([Acemoglu-Restrepo, Econometrica 2022](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-10/Tasks%20Automation%20and%20the%20Rise%20in%20US%20Wage%20Inequality.pdf)) — but that is robotics/software, not genAI, and the genAI "leveling" RCTs point the *other* way (A6). Which dominates economy-wide: unmeasured.
- **Data-labor value capture (L5).** Kenyan labelers took home **$1.32–$2/hr** while OpenAI paid Sama (the outsourcing subcontractor) **$12.50/hr** — a 6–9× markup ([TIME 2023](https://time.com/6247678/openai-chatgpt-kenya-workers/); investigation into ChatGPT's content-moderation labor); across 15 platforms Oxford Fairwork found none scored above "bare minimum," only 2 guarantee minimum wage, none pays a living wage ([Fairwork AI](https://fair.work/en/fw/fairwork-ai/) — an academic project scoring platforms against fair-work principles). Exploitation is **ESTABLISHED**; the *share of model value* captured vs. paid out is unquantified.

### Capital & ownership channels (C) — who owns the assets
This is the cluster where the *direction* is least ambiguous (all widening) and the *AI-specific magnitude* is least measured.

- **Labor share fell ~63%→~59% (2000–2022)** ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/employment-and-growth/a-new-look-at-the-declining-labor-share-of-income-in-the-united-states); the 2000 figure is verified against BLS — nonfarm-business labor share was **62.8% in 2000 Q4**, falling below 60% by 2005 and to a ~56% trough in 2011 ([BLS series PRS85006173](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PRS85006173))); [Karabarbounis-Neiman](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19136/w19136.pdf) (the canonical global labor-share study) attribute ~half the *global* decline to cheaper investment goods substituting for labor. **The decline predates AI** — isolating AI's marginal contribution is the field's single biggest gap (C1).
- **Equity concentration is real and rising (C2), now verified-primary:** top 1% **50.2%** of equities (2026 Q1) vs **42.8%** (1989 Q3); bottom 50% **1.1%** (2026 Q1) — computed directly from Fed DFA microdata (see deepening pass). Top-1% *net-worth* share: **22.8% (1989) → 31.6% (2026 Q1)**. **Important nuance the earlier draft missed:** the top-1% *equity* share actually peaked ~52% in 2019–2020 and has been roughly flat-to-slightly-declining since (~50%), even as the top-1% *net-worth* share kept climbing — so the "equity rally = concentration engine" story is real over the long run but is *not* currently accelerating on the equity-share channel specifically. Because AI gains arrive first as equity, an AI market rally is mechanically a wealth-concentration engine. **Update (deepening pass 2):** the AI-contribution claim is now **CONTESTED, not THEORIZED** — a realized-data panel ([Skare et al. 2024, *Technology in Society*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0160791X24002677); US/EU/Japan 1995–2020) finds AI capital-stock accumulation *significantly and positively* associated with wealth inequality, and two calibrated models (IMF WP/25/68 GE; [Liu-Liang, *Heliyon* 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405844025003238) DSGE) agree AI concentrates wealth — though they *disagree on the wage channel* (IMF: wage Gini falls; Skare et al.: income and wealth inequality co-move). Still, **no study decomposes the *top-1% equity-share series specifically* into AI vs. non-AI firms** (they regress an aggregate inequality index on an AI-capital proxy, and Skare et al.'s proxy is pre-2020, not the genAI wave) — which is exactly opening #4.
- **Compute-as-capital (C3):** Nvidia holds ~80–86% of AI accelerators (the specialized chips that run AI) — but at **~73–75% gross margins, not ~85–88%** (⚠️ *corrected*: the earlier draft conflated market share with margin). Verified-primary from Nvidia's [Q3 FY2026 8-K (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581025000228/q3fy26pr.htm): GAAP gross margin 73.4% / non-GAAP 73.6% (guidance ~75%), on record quarterly revenue of **$57.0B** with **$51.2B** from Data Center (up 66% YoY). Four hyperscalers (the largest cloud providers) guide ~$725B 2026 capex (capital spending) ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/big-techs-ai-spending-plans-reach-725-billion)). The "compute landlord" collects rent regardless of who wins the app layer — a ~74% gross margin on $200B+/yr in Data Center sales *is* a rent, but the *net* rent size and who ultimately bears it is unmeasured.
- **IP appropriation (C5):** documented deals exist (News Corp–OpenAI ~$250M/5yr; Shutterstock $104M — content-licensing agreements) but these are negotiated prices, not measures of *uncompensated* transfer. No economy-wide creator→firm transfer estimate exists.

### Access & distribution channels (A) — who gets the gains
The pivotal, most solo-tractable cluster, because it contains the field's **central measurement hole**.

- **Surplus capture is essentially unmeasured (A1).** The flagship RCT ([Brynjolfsson-Li-Raymond, QJE 2025](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658) — the call-center field study, 5,172 agents, +15% issues/hour, novices gain most) *explicitly states its data do not allow observation of wages*. Dozens of productivity RCTs, near-zero wage measurement — so whether a 15–40% productivity jump reaches workers, prices, or profits is **unknown from the studies themselves**. This is the highest-leverage gap in the whole brief.
- **The augmentation dividend is the one strong equalizing force (A6)** — but "equalizing productivity ≠ equalizing income" until pay incidence is measured (same hole as A1).
- **Geographic concentration (A4):** Bay Area captured **60% ($126B) of 2025 global AI VC** (venture-capital funding) and **91% of generative-AI unicorn market cap** ([theaieconomy](https://theaieconomy.substack.com/p/ai-vc-2025-bay-area-concentration)). Datacenter cost incidence (who bears the cost) is the sharpest live controversy: [Bahar-Wright synthetic control (~770 facilities)](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/new-evidence-on-data-center-employment-effects/) (synthetic control = a statistical stand-in for the untreated comparison) finds +4–5% local employment but naive comparisons overstate jobs 3×; largest datacenters employ **<150 permanent workers, sometimes 25** ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/insights/us-data-center-growth-impacts)); PJM (the mid-Atlantic power grid) power costs rose $2.2B→$14.7B in one year, but attribution splits 50% (Amazon/E3) vs 63% (IEEFA) depending on method ([Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/studies-say-data-centers-dont-raise-rates-does-it-matter/)). Genuinely **CONTESTED** and funder-entangled.
- **AI-skill premium 56%→62% (A5)** ([PwC 2026 Barometer](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-jobs-barometer.html)) — but correlational, vendor-sourced, and blind to displaced workers (survivorship — only survivors are counted). Notably, *degree* requirements for AI-exposed jobs are *falling*, hinting the gate is shifting from credential to skill.

### Global channels (G) — the between-country axis
- **Between-country usage inequality is *rising* even as within-US falls:** the top-20 countries went from 45%→48% of population-adjusted usage (Aug 2025–Feb 2026) ([Anthropic March 2026 report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report)); AI Usage Index (usage relative to population share) Israel 7.0×, US 3.6× vs India 0.27×, Nigeria 0.2×; **+1% GDP/capita → +0.7% usage** ([AEI Sept 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report)). **This between-country gap is now robustly multi-source corroborated by non-Anthropic primaries:** the [World Bank AI Atlas (data360)](https://data360.worldbank.org/en/atlas/artificial-intelligence/) finds ChatGPT visits per internet user **~50× higher in high-income than low-income countries (May 2025)** on Semrush traffic data (a different vendor, different dataset); OpenAI's own usage study reports the same high-income overrepresentation ([Chatterji-Cunningham-Deming et al., "How People Use ChatGPT," NBER 2025](https://forklightning.substack.com/p/how-people-use-chatgpt)); and an independent academic panel finds Global-North gen-AI adoption **growing ~2× as fast** as the Global South and a ~2× language gap ([Kapoor et al., "How Early Adopters Used Generative AI Worldwide," arXiv:2605.30685](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30685)). Advanced GPUs are concentrated in ~33 countries ([Oxford, AIES 2024](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/31683)).
- **Ladder-closing has measured precedent for robots** — trade-partner robotization reduced Brazil's manufacturing employment share via the export-demand channel ([World Dev 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305750X23001675) — found partner-country robots hurt Brazilian manufacturing jobs) — but whether *AI specifically* closes the ladder is THEORIZED. CGD cautions the developing world's 800M-job gap "was here" before AI ([CGD](https://www.cgdev.org/blog/developing-worlds-jobs-crisis-was-here-ai)).

### Policy channels (P) — the response, and its evidence
This is the least-mapped and most advocacy-contaminated literature. Two anchors have real empirical weight:
- **Korea's 2017 automation-credit cut (3%→1% for large firms) is the field's one natural experiment (P2):** it reduced automation investment, *raised* employment, and *reduced wage inequality* (slower top-half wage growth) with a positive fiscal externality ([Kang-Lee-Quach, SSRN 2024](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5005128)) — a genuine quasi-experiment (a real-world policy change used like a controlled trial), though a single-country credit reduction, not a clean robot tax.
- **UBI is the strongest-evidenced redistribution mechanism, and the evidence is split by design (P4).** RCT (partial equilibrium — measures the pilot group only, not economy-wide ripple effects): OpenResearch/Vivalt et al., $1,000/mo × 3yr × 1,000 people → **−3.9pp employment, −1.3 to 1.4 hrs/week, ~$1,800/yr fall in non-transfer income; extra time went to leisure** ([NBER w32719](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32719); confirmed via [OpenResearch findings](https://www.openresearchlab.org/findings/nber-working-paper-employment)). Quasi-experiment (general equilibrium — captures economy-wide effects): the *permanent, universal* Alaska dividend shows **no employment effect, +1.8pp part-time** ([Jones-Marinescu, NBER w24312](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24312/w24312.pdf)). The RCT-vs-Alaska tension — an RCT structurally *cannot* capture the macro-demand offset a universal program would create — is the key open policy question.
- **Windfall clauses / data dividends are ADVOCACY-ONLY (P7):** as of 2025 no major lab has adopted a formal windfall clause (a pre-commitment to share extreme AI profits) ([Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/4061/)); OpenAI's own "hollowing out the tax base" warning cites no evidence ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/06/openais-vision-for-the-ai-economy-public-wealth-funds-robot-taxes-and-a-four-day-work-week/)).

### Measurement channels (M) — can we even see it?
This cluster is why the whole brief carries so many THEORIZED tags.
- **Top-end inequality is estimated by *distributing* macro aggregates onto micro shares (M1)** — WID's DINA and the Fed's DFA both allocate national totals to percentiles using survey/tax data (DINA = distributional national accounts, WID's method for splitting national income across the population) ([WID](https://wid.world/methodology/); [Fed DFA](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/efa/efa-distributional-financial-accounts.htm)). Any AI signal must survive interpolation and only refreshes when the triennial SCF microdata does (SCF = Survey of Consumer Finances, the Fed's every-3-years household-wealth survey). The DFA gives quarterly top-1% net-worth shares (**31.6% in 2026 Q1**, verified-primary — see deepening) back to 1989 — the instrument is solid, but **no opened source isolates an AI component within it.**
- **Capital-share and within-labor channels live on different instruments with different lags (M2)** — so no single series captures "AI's effect on inequality." [Chen-Meng](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.05565) model the trap directly: AI can *compress* wage dispersion while *asset ownership* concentrates, so watching wage-Gini alone (Gini = a 0–1 inequality index) can show "improvement" while total inequality worsens. **The IMF's calibrated GE model now puts numbers on exactly this trap** ([WP/25/68](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2025/068/article-A001-en.xml)): under AI, wage Gini −1.73 p.p. but wealth Gini +7.18 p.p. — i.e., a researcher watching wages alone would report AI is *reducing* inequality while total (wealth) inequality worsens by ~4× the wage improvement. This is the formal justification for the decoupling-dashboard study (opening #1).
- **Attribution is structurally hard (M3):** AI is a general-purpose technology deployed everywhere at once — no untreated control group — and 2022–2026 bundles COVID recovery, the [§174 R&D-capitalization tax change](https://www.lbke.fr/fondateurs/eric-burel/ai-impact-employment-and-peer-review) (a 2022 US rule forcing firms to spread R&D deductions over years — a live confound for the junior-software decline), the tech-hiring correction, and rate hikes. The K-shaped (some groups rise, others fall) 2025–26 economy mimics or masks an AI signal.
- **Usage telemetry (aggregate usage data) is a leading indicator, not an outcome, and the *classifier* is a two-lab oligopoly (M4)** — only Anthropic (open, CC-BY on [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex) — Hugging Face, the model/dataset hub) and OpenAI (internal) publish the *conversation→O*NET* classifier (O*NET = the US occupational-task database; the classifier maps chats to job tasks), which is vendor-built and self-audited. *Corroboration note:* the coarser *geographic-usage-inequality* signal is **not** lab-locked — independent third-party traffic panels (World Bank/Semrush, Comscore household-browsing, OpenRouter token studies) reproduce the between-country concentration and the within-US household divide without lab data. It is the *task-level augmentation/automation labels*, not the raw usage geography, that remain the single-vendor bottleneck.

## How much do we actually know

**Established (as phenomena, with realized data):**
- Equity/wealth ownership is extremely concentrated and the concentration is rising (Fed DFA, verified-primary: top 1% = 50.2% of equities & 31.6% of net worth, 2026 Q1) — **now multi-methodology corroborated**: the capitalization method from tax data (Saez-Zucman / Smith-Zidar-Zwick) and Edward Wolff's independent SCF-based series (top 10% = 89% of individually-held equities, end 2021) reach the same rising-concentration conclusion via non-DFA instruments. ✔ (multi-source primary)
- Data-labor wages are poverty-level with 6–9× subcontractor markups (TIME, Fairwork). ✔
- AI VC, talent, and compute are geographically concentrated (Bay Area, ~33-country GPU map). ✔
- Between-country usage inequality is rising (**multi-source: Anthropic + World Bank/Semrush + OpenAI + arXiv:2605.30685**). ✔ Within-US *geographic* (state-level) usage inequality is falling per Anthropic — but the within-US *income-tier* adoption gap is **widening** per an independent Comscore panel ([arXiv:2603.03144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03144)); the "equalizing" reading holds geographically, contested by income tier. ⚠️ (single-org on the equalizing side, contradicted at income level)
- Past automation drove a large share of historical wage inequality (Acemoglu-Restrepo). ✔ (analogy, not AI)
- The AI-skill wage premium exists (PwC). ✔ (correlational)
- UBI labor-supply effects: −3.9pp employment in an RCT, ~zero under Alaska's permanent universal design. ✔ (both clean)
- Korea's automation-credit cut raised employment and reduced wage inequality. ✔ (single natural experiment)

**Theorized / contested (mechanism plausible, AI-specific magnitude unmeasured):**
- Every "AI's marginal contribution to X" claim: to labor share (C1), to compute rents (C3), to the tax base (P3). No counterfactual "no-AI" world has been built. **This is the field's defining weakness.** *(Exception noted in deepening pass 2: the AI→wealth-inequality attribution (C2) is now **CONTESTED, not THEORIZED** — [Skare et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0160791X24002677) supplies a realized-data panel estimate and two models (IMF, Liu-Liang) corroborate the direction — but they disagree on the wage channel and none covers the 2022+ genAI wave or decomposes the top-1% equity share, so the counterfactual for the *modern* AI era is still unbuilt.)*
- Surplus capture (A1) — the pay-incidence RCT does not exist.
- Datacenter cost/rate incidence (A4) — 50% vs 63% depending on method.
- Entry-level ladder collapse (L3) — Canaries vs. Yale vs. §174 confound, unadjudicated.
- Creator→firm IP transfer (C5) — no denominator, mid-litigation.
- "AI colonialism" framing (G3) — wages established, framing and value-wedge magnitude contested.

**What limits the research itself:**
- **Data locked in labs.** The best usage data sit inside the firms that own the equity — a conflict of interest specific to the capital-side questions. Only Anthropic open-sources.
- **Attribution has no control group.** A GPT deployed everywhere, amid COVID/§174/rate confounds, cannot be cleanly separated with current designs.
- **Timescale mismatch.** Usage telemetry is real-time; DFA is quarterly-but-interpolated-from-triennial-SCF; IRS/WID tax microdata lags 2–3+ years and is access-gated. *The cause is observable years before the effect.*
- **Funding slant runs both directions.** Optimistic numbers (PwC 56–62% premium) come from consultancies selling AI transformation; some displacement papers carry founder conflicts (Workhelix); datacenter studies are commissioned by Amazon/E3 vs. IEEFA; the main external money (Anthropic Economic Futures) is vendor-shaped. "AI-washing" — blaming AI for cost-driven layoffs — contaminates both directions ([Fortune on Yale](https://fortune.com/2026/02/02/ai-labor-market-yale-budget-lab-ai-washing/)).
- **Politicization.** "AI colonialism" is contested on ideological lines; IMF/OECD framing is member-state-shaped ("prepare and benefit"); IP is mid-litigation.

## What we still don't know (for the ledger)

- **The single biggest gap: nobody has decomposed AI's marginal contribution** to the top-1% equity share, the labor-share decline, or the tax base. Every such claim is THEORIZED because no counterfactual "no-AI" baseline exists.
- **Surplus capture is unmeasured:** productivity RCTs almost never observe wages, so who captures the AI productivity dividend (workers/consumers/owners) is unknown from the studies themselves.
- **Do the two best-measured curves actually decouple?** Usage-inequality is falling (within-US) while ownership-inequality rises — but no dataset joins **the specific two operational curves** (Anthropic usage-AUI Gini vs Fed DFA ownership Gini) to test whether the equalizing effect can outrun the unequalizing one. *(Deepening pass 2 re-scoped this: a realized-data literature attributing AI to wealth inequality now **does** exist — [Skare et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0160791X24002677) — but it uses a pre-2020 AI-capital proxy and does not join the usage-telemetry curve; the specific two-curve join is confirmed still unpublished by hostile search. **This is the structural blocker capping the row at Confidence 4.**)*
- **Is the AI-skill premium (56–62%) skill or selection?** Confounded by self-selection of high-ability workers; no clean causal estimate.
- **Datacenter cost incidence:** 50% vs 63% of PJM increases depending on method; unresolved. *(Deepening pass 2: split now attributed — 63% = Monitoring Analytics, PJM's independent market monitor; ~50% = E3, commissioned by the Data Center Coalition (industry). The higher, ratepayer-facing figure is the independent one; still CONTESTED, but provenance is no longer opaque.)*
- **Data-labor value-capture wedge:** wages established, but the *share of model value* paid to Global-South labelers vs. captured by capital is unquantified.
- **UBI at scale:** every RCT is partial-equilibrium by construction; Alaska is the only permanent-universal case and it's one state. The macro-demand offset that matters for a *universal* AI-funded program is unmeasured.
- **Is the Anthropic classifier valid as a measurement instrument?** The augmentation/automation labels underpinning every distributional inference are vendor-built. *(Deepening pass 2 sharpened this: an independent open-source reconstruction of the AEI-style pipeline on public data now exists ([Somerstep et al., arXiv:2606.26118, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26118)), and a replication found less education / more protective-service usage than Anthropic's index — so the gap is no longer "no external audit" but "**no head-to-head robustness audit of Anthropic's own automation/augmentation labels**" via alternative judge models/prompts. That specific audit — opening #3 — is still unbuilt and now better-motivated.)*
- **Timescale:** we can observe the *cause* (usage, capex, equity) years before the *effect* (tax-microdata top shares) — so any near-term "AI isn't worsening inequality" claim is premature by construction.

### The decoupling now has a formal model — the single biggest evidentiary upgrade this pass

Sourced and read a paper the earlier draft **mis-cited** (it listed IMF WP/25/68 under L6 "within-firm wage effects"). WP/25/68 is actually **Rockall, Tavares & Pizzinelli, "AI Adoption and Inequality" (IMF, April 2025)** — a calibrated general-equilibrium, task-based, heterogeneous-household model (an adaptation of Moll–Rachel–Restrepo 2022 "Uneven Growth," calibrated to the UK). Read in full via the [IMF eLibrary XML](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2025/068/article-A001-en.xml) (IMF PDF/landing endpoints 403 non-browser fetches; eLibrary XML returns 200; full text saved locally to `papers/program-pdfs/imf-wp2025-068-ai-adoption-inequality.xml.html`). Headline quantified findings (VERIFIED-PRIMARY, quoted from the paper):
- Under AI (occupational AI-exposure → task displacement), the model predicts the **wage Gini falls 1.73 p.p.** (low-income wages rise on aggregate productivity; high-income wages fall on displacement) **while the wealth Gini rises 7.18 p.p.** (displaced high earners are complementary with AI and better positioned to capture higher capital returns).
- Contrast: calibrated to *routine-biased automation* instead, **both** rise (wage +2.05 p.p., wealth +6.89 p.p.). AI's distinctive signature is the *sign flip on wages* combined with a *larger* wealth-concentration effect.
- **The firm's AI-adoption choice amplifies the wealth channel:** when firms endogenously choose how much AI to adopt, cost savings from automating *high-wage* tasks drive higher adoption, making the wealth-inequality effect "particularly pronounced." Models that ignore the adoption margin *understate* the equity-efficiency trade-off.

Why this matters: the brief's central thesis — "usage/wage compression can mask ownership/wealth concentration" — moves from THEORIZED-hand-wave to **THEORIZED-with-a-calibrated-GE-model-and-magnitudes**. It does *not* close the empirical gap (nobody has joined the two *realized* curves), but it (a) supplies a falsifiable magnitude for the decoupling-dashboard study (opening #1) to test against, and (b) independently grades mechanisms C1/C2 as ESTABLISHED-mechanism via the Moll–Rachel–Restrepo spine (automation raises returns to wealth → concentration). I moved the citation from L6 to C1/C2/M2 accordingly.

### The persuasion paper (EXTRA FOCUS) — a new, honestly-bounded political-economy channel

Read [Hackenburg et al., "The Levers of Political Persuasion with Conversational AI"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13919) (Science 2025; N=76,977; 19 LLMs; 707 political issues; UK AI Security Institute / Oxford / LSE / Stanford / MIT). Its relevance to *wealth inequality* is the **influence/power-concentration channel** — an axis the brief did not previously cover. Key findings, and the honest read for this brief:
- Persuasive power comes overwhelmingly from **post-training and prompting (up to +51% and +27%)**, **not from raw model scale** (only **+1.59 pp per order-of-magnitude** of compute; +1.83 pp for chat-tuned models). A small open-source Llama-3.1-8B with reward-model persuasion training reached the persuasiveness of the much larger GPT-4o (~6→9 pp).
- The dominant lever is **information density** (packing verifiable-sounding claims): it explains ~44% of persuasion variance overall, ~75% among developer-post-trained models. Personalization/microtargeting was **small** (+0.43 pp, never >1 pp) — contradicting the popular "AI will microtarget us into submission" fear.
- Strikingly, the methods that increased persuasion **also decreased factual accuracy** (more persuasive ⇒ more false claims).

**Honest implication for the inequality brief (THEORIZED, new):** naive intuition says "the actor with the most compute monopolizes persuasive/political power, compounding wealth-into-influence." This paper *cuts against* the strong form of that story — because persuasion is **not primarily a returns-to-scale capability**, it is cheaply reproducible via post-training on commodity open models. So the *influence-concentration* channel is **more diffusible** than the *compute/ownership-concentration* channel (C3): the thing that concentrates (frontier compute, equity) is *not* the thing that most drives persuasive power. This is a genuinely non-obvious, empirically-grounded nuance — I add it as a **theorized political-economy channel** rather than a widening mechanism, because it is a capability finding, not a measured distributional outcome. It belongs in this brief precisely because it *disciplines* an intuitive-but-wrong "power concentrates with compute" claim.
