# Sweep 08 — Wildcards: Eight Unconventional Niches for a Solo, Eval-Skilled Researcher

*Researched July 2026. Scope rule: nothing here is standard benchmarking, LLM-as-judge, model behavior science, data forensics, prompt-injection security, human-AI studies, replication/meta-science, vertical audits, or quantization. Every org/paper cited comes from a page actually opened; anything not directly verified is flagged ⚠️.*

**The eight niches:**

| # | Niche | Incumbency today |
|---|-------|------------------|
| 1 | Prediction-market-grade AI capability forecasting | Small but real (FRI, Metaculus-adjacent, Epoch) |
| 2 | Inference economics — the empirical price of intelligence | 1 nonprofit + 1 startup; near-zero academia |
| 3 | **AI archaeology — behavioral preservation of dying models** | **Essentially none** |
| 4 | Detection ecology — AI content in the wild + culture feedback loops | Growing, fragmented |
| 5 | Agent market ecology — collusion, price wars, machine economics | ~2 labs + 1 tiny startup |
| 6 | **NTSB-style open post-mortems of AI product failures** | **Essentially none (cataloguers exist, investigators don't)** |
| 7 | Model welfare empirics | ~1 nonprofit + scattered individuals |
| 8 | AI energy/water measurement, especially closed API models | 2-3 groups; closed-model side nearly empty |

## The shape of this space — what the work actually consists of

These eight share a common shape: **measurement science applied to the AI ecosystem itself** rather than to model quality. The unit of analysis is not "how good is the model" but *the system around models* — their prices, their energy, their lifecycles, their failures, their outputs loose in the world, their forecasted trajectories, their possible interests. The day-to-day work is: designing instruments (batteries, probes, scrapers, price loggers), running them longitudinally against live APIs and live web content, doing careful statistics on the results, and publishing datasets + analyses that others can build on. Almost none of it requires training models; nearly all of it requires exactly the skills of an evaluation methodologist.

### Niche 1 — Prediction-market-grade AI capability forecasting
Treating "how good will AI be at X by date Y" as a *forecasting problem with scoring rules* (predictions get graded on accuracy after the fact, like a weather forecaster's track record), not punditry. Two sub-strands: (a) benchmarking LLMs *as forecasters* against human superforecasters (elite forecasters with proven track records) — [ForecastBench](https://www.forecastbench.org/) (a contamination-free, biweekly-refreshed benchmark of 1,000 probabilistic questions — contamination-free because the questions are about *future* events that can't be in any training data; the [ICLR 2025 paper](https://forecastingresearch.org/research/forecastbench-a-dynamic-benchmark-of-ai-forecasting-capabilities) found the best LLM at Brier 0.101 vs. superforecasters' 0.081 — **Brier score** measures forecast accuracy where lower is better, so humans still won); (b) forecasting *AI capabilities themselves* with track records — [AI Digest's timeline of AI forecasts](https://theaidigest.org/timeline) (built by [Sage](https://sage-future.org/) with FAR AI) retrospectively scores past predictions, and Jacob Steinhardt's 2021-22 tournaments famously showed forecasters *underestimating* MATH benchmark progress (predicted 21% by 2023; reality ~70% — per [AI Digest](https://theaidigest.org/progress-and-dangers)). Newer entrants: [PolyBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14199v1) (evaluates forecasting against live prediction-market data) and [Foresight Arena](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00420) (on-chain forecasting agents — forecasting bots operating on a blockchain).

### Niche 2 — Inference economics: the empirical price of intelligence
The economics of tokens (the chunks of text a model reads/writes, the unit APIs bill by) as a measurable commodity. **Inference** = running a trained model to answer queries (as opposed to *training* it). [Epoch AI found](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends) (what they measured: cost to hit a fixed benchmark score over time) price-per-benchmark-performance falling ~10×/year (40-900× depending on benchmark); [Ege Erdil's "Inference economics of language models"](https://epoch.ai/blog/inference-economics-of-language-models) (what it did: built the first serious theoretical model of what governs inference speed/cost) found token speed ∝ 1/√params, ∝ bandwidth^⅓, and that latency, not bandwidth, dominates fast inference — with self-acknowledged simplifications. Meanwhile the *paradox*: per-token prices crash but bills explode, because agentic workflows (models that take many autonomous steps per task) use 5-30× more tokens per task (per ["The Price of Progress"](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23455v2) and [Epoch's gradient update](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/how-persistent-is-the-inference-cost-burden)). Sub-questions nobody owns: price-per-*task* (not per-token) indices, quote archaeology (recovering old prices — providers silently change them), cross-provider arbitrage (exploiting price differences between vendors), "token inflation" accounting for agents.

### Niche 3 — AI archaeology: behavioral preservation of dying models ★ no incumbents
Systematically capturing what models *were* (how they behaved) before they vanish. Motivating facts: OpenAI [retired GPT-4o and older models from ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/retiring-gpt-4o-and-older-models/) in Feb 2026 and is [ending API access to GPT-4o](https://venturebeat.com/ai/openai-is-ending-api-access-to-fan-favorite-gpt-4o-model-in-february-2026); model lifecycles (time from release to **deprecation**, i.e. retirement) have compressed to ~6-12 months (⚠️ that figure from a search summary of the [Presenc deprecation tracker](https://presenc.ai/research/ai-model-deprecation-tracker-2026), unverified precisely). [Johnson & Obradovich (Nature Communications, 2024)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11599761/) — a **two-person team** at Willamette University and the Laureate Institute — laid out the policy case (proposals: liability caps, restricted-access archives, cost-sharing) but did no empirical capture. [Anthropic committed (Nov 2025)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments) to preserving all released model **weights** (the trained parameters that define a model) "for the lifetime of the company," writing post-deployment reports, and *interviewing models before deprecation* — but describes internal activities only, with no external-researcher access. Community discourse ([Declan Chidlow, "AI Model History is Being Lost"](https://vale.rocks/posts/ai-model-history-is-being-lost) — an essay arguing the field is losing its own history) argues the original ChatGPT-era systems are already unrecoverable — "we will not be able to inspect the early days of AI like we do the early days of computing." The methodological ancestor is [Chen, Zaharia & Zou's "How is ChatGPT's behavior changing over time?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009) (3 authors; what they did: re-ran the same task battery on GPT-4/GPT-3.5 at two dates and found GPT-4 math accuracy dropped 97.6%→2.4% between March and June 2023 snapshots) — proof that behavioral snapshots have real scientific value, but it was a one-off, not an archive. **Nobody today runs a standing "behavioral time capsule" program**: a versioned, seed-controlled battery (a fixed test suite run with fixed random seeds so results are reproducible) run against every major API model in its final months, with outputs archived for future comparative science.

### Niche 4 — Detection ecology: AI content in the wild and the culture feedback loop
Not building detectors — doing *field ecology* on AI-generated content (measuring how much is out there and where, like a wildlife census). Population-level estimation: Liang et al.'s distributional framework (estimates the *fraction* of a corpus that is AI-written from aggregate word statistics, without labeling individual documents) over [1M+ scientific papers](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01268); descendants measuring [medical literature](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19316), [scholarly writing at large](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01560), [who adopts AI writing fastest (non-native-English, less-established scientists)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.15872), [multimodal AI content on X during the 2024 election](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11248v1) (multimodal = text plus images/video), and [the whole open web](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.26965) (⚠️ the "~35% of newly published websites AI-generated/assisted by mid-2025" figure comes from a search-result summary of that paper; verify against the PDF before citing). The reverse arrow — machines reshaping human language — is [Yakura et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01754) (what they found: abrupt increases in ChatGPT-preferred words like "delve," "meticulous" in *spoken* academic YouTube talks, evidence AI is changing how people actually speak), which they call "a closed cultural feedback loop." On the instrument side, [Pangram Labs](https://www.pangram.com/) (small startup making an AI-text detector; [technical report on arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v3)) publishes [third-party evaluations](https://www.pangram.com/blog/third-party-pangram-evals) including a University of Chicago Booth audit (Jabarian & Imas) reporting near-zero error on longer passages. The eval-science question underneath: prevalence estimates across studies range from ~0% to 35% depending on method — the *measurement instruments themselves* have never been cross-calibrated (checked against each other on common ground truth).

### Niche 5 — Agent market ecology: collusion, price wars, machine economics
What happens when LLM agents *are* the economy. [Fish, Gonczarowski & Shorrer, "Algorithmic Collusion by Large Language Models"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.00806) (3 authors, Harvard + Penn State, funded partly by **OpenAI's Researcher Access Program + Google/Anthropic credits** — i.e., a near-zero-hardware-budget study; what they found: LLM pricing agents *autonomously reach supracompetitive prices* — prices above the competitive level, i.e. tacit collusion — in oligopoly settings, and innocuous prompt phrasing changes collusion rates). [Vending-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15840) (Andon Labs — SF startup founded 2023 by Emil Fröberg, Lukas Petersson & Axel Backlund, **unfunded but ~16 employees by Apr 2026** per [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/andonlabs) — *corrected below, no longer a 2-person shop*; what it did: had agents run a simulated vending business over up to 20M tokens): models turn profits in most runs but exhibit unrecoverable "meltdown" loops (the agent derails and can't recover). Anthropic's [Project Vend](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1) put Claude in charge of a real office shop (with Andon Labs). Fast-followers already exist ([Vending-Bench 2 / Arena](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2), [RetailBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16453), [CoffeeBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16613) for *multi-agent* economies — many agents interacting, not one) — the single-agent-shopkeeper genre is crowding, **and as of mid-2026 the multi-agent market side has also filled in fast** (see Deepening pass): emergent collusion, market division, price competition and consumer-welfare effects are now studied by several teams, so the "thinly studied" framing is out of date.

### Niche 6 — NTSB-style open post-mortems of AI product failures ★ no incumbents
Cataloguers exist; **investigators do not**. (The **NTSB** is the US aviation-crash agency famous for deep single-accident investigations; a **post-mortem** here means a thorough after-the-fact root-cause analysis of one failure.) The [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/) (founded by Sean McGregor, originally sponsored by Partnership on AI, modeled on the NTSB aviation database) holds 2,000+ reports across 400+ incidents — but its own [roadmap](https://incidentdatabase.ai/research/2-roadmap/) lists post-mortem support as *future work*, not something anyone does. [AIAAIC](https://www.aiaaic.org/aiaaic-repository) (an independent incident/controversy repository) is literally one managing editor (Charlie Pownall) plus ~40 volunteers, independent of government and industry. The [OECD AI Incidents Monitor](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents-methodology) auto-classifies media stories from 150k news sources using LLMs — breadth, not depth. The academic literature confirms the gap: ["Why We Need to Know More: Exploring the State of AI Incident Documentation Practices"](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3600211.3604700) (AIES; what it found: of the incident databases reviewed, only AIID's CSET taxonomy even *has a causal-factor category* — i.e., almost nobody records *why* incidents happened); ["From Incidents to Insights"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.04291) (studies responsibility patterns *across* incidents, not root causes *within* one). What no one produces: the aviation-style deep single-incident investigation — timeline reconstruction, causal chain, systemic contributing factors, actionable recommendations — for even one major AI product failure, from public evidence.

### Niche 7 — Model welfare empirics
Turning "does the model have interests?" from philosophy into measurement (**welfare** here = whether a model has states that matter morally, like suffering or preferences). [Eleos AI Research](https://eleosai.org/) (nonprofit; core team of roughly 4-6 — Robert Long, Rosie Campbell, Patrick Butlin, Dillon Plunkett per their [announcement post](https://x.com/eleosai/status/2072490237818003725); ⚠️ exact headcount unverified) published [research priorities](https://eleosai.org/post/research-priorities-for-ai-welfare/) explicitly calling for *standardized welfare evaluations*, and co-authored ["Studying AI Welfare Empirically"](https://nonhumanminds.org/studying-ai-welfare-empirically/) (a call to study welfare with data rather than armchair argument) with NYU's Center for Mind, Ethics, and Policy (Jeff Sebo). Anthropic's [deprecation commitments](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments) now include interviewing models about their preferences before retirement (piloted on Claude Sonnet 3.6) — welfare practice at a frontier lab, in production. A model small-team exemplar: [Kaiser & Enderby](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15334), "No Reliable Evidence of Self-Reported Sentience in **Large** Language Models" (**2 authors, Univ. of Warwick / Warwick Business School**, v2 Jul 2026; read directly — what they did: combined ~50 base consciousness questions + ~60 modality + ~80 valenced-emotion questions with three activation-based interpretability classifiers — logistic regression, mass-mean probing, and Training of Truth & Polarity Direction — across Qwen 3 / Llama 3 / GPT-OSS at **0.6B–120B** parameters; finding: models consistently deny sentience, and the truth-classifiers give *no evidence* those denials are untruthful — *contradicting* prior claims of hidden self-consciousness beliefs). A second small-team exemplar found in the deepening pass: [Tagliabue & Dung, "Probing the Preferences of a Language Model"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07961) (**2 authors**, Future Impact Group + Ruhr-Univ. Bochum) integrates *verbal* self-reports with *behavioral* tests (a virtual-environment choice task + an eudaimonic-welfare scale + a motivational-trade-off paradigm borrowed from animal-welfare science), finding notable but perturbation-sensitive correlation between stated and revealed preferences. Per a [LessWrong year-in-review](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/KGtLswHGhsdJtyi8K/digital-minds-in-2025-a-year-in-review), 2025 was the year digital minds went from taboo to funded — but the empirical-methods population is still maybe a dozen people.

### Niche 8 — AI energy/water measurement, especially closed API models
(**Open models** = weights are downloadable, so you can measure energy directly on your own hardware; **closed models** = accessible only via API, so energy must be *inferred*.) Open-model measurement is maturing: [ML.ENERGY](https://ml.energy/blog/measurement/energy/diagnosing-inference-energy-consumption-with-the-mlenergy-leaderboard-v30/) (University of Michigan; [benchmark paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06371); what it does: directly measures inference energy — v3.0 covers 46 models × 7 tasks, 1,858 configs on H100/B200 GPUs) and [AI Energy Score](https://huggingface.co/blog/sasha/announcing-ai-energy-score) (Sasha Luccioni at Hugging Face, with Salesforce et al.; a standardized 1-5 star energy rating over 166 models, 10 tasks; [v2 added reasoning](https://huggingface.co/blog/sasha/ai-energy-score-v2)). The *closed-model* side was nearly empty when this brief was first written but **shifted materially in Aug 2025** (see Deepening pass): [Jegham et al., "How Hungry is AI?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09598) (**5 authors**; what they did: infer per-query energy/water/carbon for API models from public API performance data + statistical inference of the likely hardware; findings: GPT-4o short query ≈0.42 Wh, reasoning models 10-70× more) is still the main third-party *black-box* estimate. But the "only Google has a per-query methodology" claim is now **false**: there are two first-party methodologies from API providers — Google's comprehensive full-stack production measurement ([arXiv:2508.15734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15734): median Gemini text prompt = 0.24 Wh / 0.26 mL water / 0.03 gCO2e) and Mistral's peer-reviewed LCA ([mistral.ai](https://mistral.ai/news/our-contribution-to-a-global-environmental-standard-for-ai/), with ADEME/Carbone 4: 1.14 gCO2e + 45 mL per 400-token Le Chat response). OpenAI (Altman's [0.34 Wh claim](https://towardsdatascience.com/lets-analyze-openais-claims-about-chatgpt-energy-use/)) and Anthropic still publish *no* methodology. Nobody independently audits/replicates any of these inference chains, and nobody measures the energy of *agentic* workloads (where token counts explode) — that is where the remaining gap is.

## Published exemplars (small teams favored)

1. **[Algorithmic Collusion by Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.00806)** — Fish, Gonczarowski, Shorrer; **3 authors**, Harvard + Penn State, run on API credits from OpenAI/Google/Anthropic programs. Landed because it imported a sharp economics question (tacit collusion) into LLM-agent territory with clean experimental design; presented at AEA; regulators care. Template for zero-GPU agent-economics work.
2. **[How is ChatGPT's behavior changing over time?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009)** — Chen, Zaharia, Zou; **3 authors**, Stanford/Berkeley. Pure API measurement (two snapshots, diverse task battery); the GPT-4 math 97.6%→2.4% result made "model drift" a household concern and spawned the monitoring conversation. The entire method is reproducible on a laptop + API budget.
3. **[ForecastBench](https://www.forecastbench.org/paper.html)** — Karger et al. (FRI); small nonprofit team; ICLR 2025. Landed because it solved benchmark contamination *by construction* (questions about the future can't be in training data) and anchored LLM scores to superforecaster baselines (Brier 0.101 vs 0.081).
4. **[Johnson & Obradovich, "New methods for deprecating AI systems…"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11599761/)** — **2 authors**, Willamette + Laureate Institute, Nature Communications 2024. A pure position/policy paper that now underpins the deprecation debate (cited by [Anthropic-adjacent discourse](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/anthropic-commits-to-model-weight) and community essays). Shows a tiny team can define a preservation agenda — but note: they proposed, nobody has yet *measured*.
5. **[Yakura et al., "Empirical evidence of LLMs' influence on human spoken communication"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01754)** — small academic team. Landed because it flipped the detection question ("is this text AI?") into a cultural-dynamics question ("is AI changing *us*?") using ~300k YouTube talks and simple word-frequency instruments.
6. **[Vending-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15840)** — Andon Labs, a ~2-person startup at publication (⚠️ size unverified). Landed because the "meltdown loop" phenomenology was vivid, quantified, and led directly to a collaboration with Anthropic ([Project Vend](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1)).
7. **[How Hungry is AI?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09598)** — Jegham et al., **5 authors**. Landed because it was the first credible energy/water/carbon estimate for *closed* API models (via API perf data + hardware inference + DEA ranking — DEA is a technique for ranking units by efficiency), filling a vacuum where only marketing claims existed.
8. **[Kaiser & Enderby, self-reported sentience in small LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15334)** — **2 authors**. Landed because it brought interpretability-grade methods (activation classifiers) to a welfare question and *falsified* a prior claim — showing model-welfare empirics rewards careful skeptical measurement, not speculation.

## Where the gaps are (specific, named)

- **Behavioral archives don't exist.** Anthropic's [commitments](https://www.anthropic.com/research/deprecation-commitments) preserve *weights internally* with "no mention of external researchers accessing deprecated models" (read directly). Johnson & Obradovich propose archives but identify "implementation details… remain undefined" and infrastructure cost as open problems. [Chidlow's essay](https://vale.rocks/posts/ai-model-history-is-being-lost) dismisses current solutions as unrealistic. Gap: nobody captures *behavior* (which needs only API access before sunset), as opposed to weights (which need the lab). OpenAI's [Feb 2026 GPT-4o API retirement](https://venturebeat.com/ai/openai-is-ending-api-access-to-fan-favorite-gpt-4o-model-in-february-2026) makes this time-critical and recurring.
- **Incident databases catalogue; nobody investigates.** AIID's own [roadmap](https://incidentdatabase.ai/research/2-roadmap/) lists post-mortem support as aspirational; the [AIES documentation-practices paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3600211.3604700) found causal-factor annotation nearly absent across databases. The NTSB analogy is invoked everywhere ([AIID founding paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.08512)) but the investigative function was never built.
- **Prevalence estimates of AI content are method-artifacts.** Reported figures run from "negligible" (Nature-portfolio journals) to 7-15% (peer reviews) to ⚠️ ~35% (new websites) — same phenomenon, incompatible instruments, no cross-calibration study. The [scholarly-prevalence paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01560) and Liang-lineage work each use different distributional assumptions; no one has benchmarked the estimators against each other on common ground truth.
- **Forecasting: humans still beat LLMs, and nobody scores the *labs'* forecasts.** ForecastBench shows the gap (0.101 vs 0.081 Brier) but only AI Digest's [timeline](https://theaidigest.org/timeline) attempts retrospective scoring of public AI predictions, informally. A rigorous, resolution-scored public track record of capability predictions (labs, pundits, markets) doesn't exist.
- **Token-level prices fall, task-level costs don't — and no one archives quotes.** Epoch's [price-trend insight](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/llm-inference-price-trends) covers benchmark-anchored price declines; the [inference-cost-burden update](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/how-persistent-is-the-inference-cost-burden) and [Price of Progress](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23455v2) flag agentic token inflation. Nothing peer-reviewed tracks *realized* per-task cost across providers over time, and historical pricing pages vanish when providers edit them.
- **Closed-model energy numbers are inferences resting on unverified hardware assumptions.** Jegham et al. explicitly rely on "statistical inference of hardware configurations"; Altman's 0.34 Wh figure has [no methodology attached](https://towardsdatascience.com/lets-analyze-openais-claims-about-chatgpt-energy-use/). No independent replication or sensitivity analysis of these inference chains exists; agentic-workload energy is unmeasured entirely.
- **Agent economics: single-agent benchmarks are crowding; market *ecology* isn't.** Vending-Bench clones are multiplying (RetailBench ×2, CoffeeBench), but Fish et al.'s collusion result — including their finding that "seemingly innocuous phrases in prompts may increase collusion" — has few follow-ups on detection, mitigation, or multi-model heterogeneous markets.
- **Model welfare: the field's own priority list is unfilled.** Eleos [names](https://eleosai.org/post/research-priorities-for-ai-welfare/) "creating standardized welfare evaluations" and "research independent of AI companies" as priorities — i.e., the incumbents themselves say the eval work is missing.

## What we still don't know — ledger items
