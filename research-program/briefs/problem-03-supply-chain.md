# Problem 03 — The AI Supply Chain: Chokepoints, Export Controls, Smuggling, and Compute Tracking

## The problem, concretely

The entire AI stack rests on an extraordinarily concentrated physical supply chain, and the biggest policy bets of the decade (US export controls, compute-threshold regulation) assume we can measure flows through it. We mostly can't.

- **Chokepoint concentration.** One Dutch firm (ASML) makes all EUV lithography machines (extreme-ultraviolet — the light source needed to etch the finest chip features); TSMC dominates leading-edge logic (the processor chips that do the computing); three firms (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) make all HBM (high-bandwidth memory — the stacked memory chips that feed data to AI accelerators fast enough) — and 2026 HBM4 supply is fully sold out ([internal map, verified](../../ai-research-map/07-data-systems-hardware/hardware-and-neuromorphic.md); [CSET "The Semiconductor Supply Chain"](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/the-semiconductor-supply-chain/) — mapped every input to advanced chips and named the chokepoints). HBM, not logic, is the current binding component — including for China: CXMT's (a Chinese memory maker) HBM output is reportedly sufficient for only ~250–300k Ascend 910Cs/year (Huawei's flagship AI chip) (⚠️ from [CFR](https://www.cfr.org/articles/chinas-ai-chip-deficit-why-huawei-cant-catch-nvidia-and-us-export-controls-should-remain) search snippet, page not opened).
- **Export-control effects are real but contested.** Chris Miller's assessment (author of *Chip War*; page read): controls **did** cripple China's chip *production* (Huawei ~200k AI chips in 2025 vs ~1M chips China legally imported; SMIC — China's top foundry — stuck without EUV) and China's *global* AI-infrastructure market share, but **did not** produce a big model-capability gap ([AI Frontiers, Miller](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/us-chip-export-controls-china-ai)). ITIF (a tech-policy think tank) argues controls backfired — hurt US firms while helping Huawei ([ITIF "Backfire"](https://itif.org/publications/2025/10/27/backfire-export-controls-helped-huawei-and-hurt-us-firms/), ⚠️ snippet only). Nobody has done rigorous causal measurement.
- **Smuggling estimates span an order of magnitude.** CNAS (finding: ~10k–several hundred thousand chips smuggled in 2024, median ~140k) ([CNAS](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/countering-ai-chip-smuggling-has-become-a-national-security-priority)). Epoch (finding: 660k H100-equivalents — i.e. chips scaled to Nvidia's H100 as a common unit — cumulative through 2025, 90% CI 290k–1.6M, possibly ~⅓ of China's AI compute) ([Epoch, page read](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling)). The CI (confidence interval — the plausible range around the estimate) width is dominated by one unknown: what fraction of smuggling ever surfaces in indictments/reporting (Epoch assumes 10–80% detection, median 25%).
- **Compute tracking is nascent.** Regulation keys off training FLOP (floating-point operations — the total arithmetic used to train a model, a proxy for its scale) (BIS 10²⁶ threshold — the US Commerce Bureau of Industry and Security's reporting trigger; EU systemic-risk tier — internal map), but public knowledge of who has what compute comes from a handful of young datasets ([Epoch GPU Clusters](https://epoch.ai/data/gpu-clusters) — an open dataset of 500+ clusters; [Frontier Data Centers Hub](https://epoch.ai/blog/introducing-the-frontier-data-centers-hub/) — tracks 13 US sites ≈ 15% of global H100e; page read).
- **Datacenters are gated by power and equipment, not chips.** IEA projects data-center electricity ~doubling to ~945 TWh by 2030 (internal map); RAND projects AI data centers needing ~327 GW globally by 2030 vs 88 GW total in 2022, with single training runs at 8 GW by 2030 ([RAND RRA3572-1](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3572-1.html), ⚠️ figures from search snippets, PDF blocked 403). Grid interconnection waits (the years-long queue to connect a new datacenter to the electrical grid) of 4–7 years and transformer lead times stretching to ~5 years are widely reported but mostly in commercial gray literature — trade-press and vendor material, not peer-reviewed (⚠️ e.g. [Build.inc](https://build.inc/insights/data-center-transformer-procurement-2026), [DCK](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/gridlocked-how-power-constraints-are-shaping-the-future-of-data-centers) — snippets, not opened).

**Measurable sub-problems:** (1) how many controlled chips reach China per year and via which routes; (2) what compute each country/company actually operates; (3) whether export controls changed Chinese training compute, model quality, or prices; (4) whether hardware location-verification is technically sound; (5) how fast datacenter capacity can physically grow given power/equipment constraints.

## The research field around it

Sub-areas, roughly by maturity:

1. **Supply-chain mapping** (most mature). CSET's 2021 trilogy defined the chokepoint framework ([The Semiconductor Supply Chain](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/the-semiconductor-supply-chain/) — mapped every input and named the chokepoints; [Preserving the Chokepoints](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/preserving-the-chokepoints/) — how to keep the US lead at those points; [Securing Semiconductor Supply Chains](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/securing-semiconductor-supply-chains/) — resilience against disruption) and became the intellectual basis of US export-control policy. Now maintained as an open dataset: [ETO Supply Chain Explorer](https://eto.tech/tool-docs/chipexplorer/) (5 CSV tables, company-level market shares, updated 2024 data).
2. **Export-control design and effects evaluation.** Mostly qualitative policy analysis (CSET, CSIS, CFR, ITIF, Chatham House); the "did it work" question is argued with point estimates, not causal designs ([Miller/AI Frontiers](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/us-chip-export-controls-china-ai); [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/deepseek-huawei-export-controls-and-future-us-china-ai-race) — reads DeepSeek/Huawei as evidence on the US-China AI race). Known control gaps get named fast — e.g. the integrated-HBM loophole (HBM stacks attached to dummy processors to evade rules that only cover raw memory stacks, ⚠️ snippet: [AI Frontiers HBM piece](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/high-bandwidth-memory-critical-gaps-us-export-controls)).
3. **Smuggling/diversion estimation** (young; ~3–4 serious quantitative attempts). Method: catalog public evidence (DOJ indictments, WSJ/NYT/FT/The Information investigations, gray-market vendor observations), then probabilistic modeling over detection rates ([IAPS/Grunewald 2023](https://www.iaps.ai/research/ai-chip-smuggling-into-china) — first public quantification of routes and volumes; [CNAS](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/countering-ai-chip-smuggling-has-become-a-national-security-priority) — framed smuggling as a national-security priority; [Epoch 2026, read](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling) — the 660k H100e estimate; [BISI](https://bisi.org.uk/reports/ai-chip-smuggling-the-limits-of-us-export-controls) — on the limits of enforcement).
4. **Compute tracking / datasets.** [Trends in AI Supercomputers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16026) (Pilz, Sanders, Rahman, Heim; what they did: built a 500-system dataset; findings: performance doubling every 9 months, US ≈75% of tracked performance vs China 15%). Epoch's [Frontier Data Centers Hub](https://epoch.ai/blog/introducing-the-frontier-data-centers-hub/) (read) independently verifies buildouts via Sentinel satellite imagery (the EU's free Earth-observation satellites), permits, aerial photos, cooling-infrastructure identification; finds ~$44B/GW capital cost and multiple >1 GW facilities in 2026. [SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/datacenter-model) does the commercial version: 5,000+ facilities via property records, FOIA (Freedom of Information Act records requests), power records, satellite imagery, plus a CNN (convolutional neural network — an image-recognition model) to automate imagery analysis (⚠️ snippet).
5. **Hardware-enabled governance / verification.** CNAS [Secure, Governable Chips](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/secure-governable-chips) (2024) proposed on-chip mechanisms (governance logic built into the chip itself); ping-time location verification (inferring a chip's physical location from network round-trip delays) is now in draft law — the [Chip Security Act (H.R. 3447)](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/3447/text) passed House Foreign Affairs 42-0 in March 2026, mandating location-verification mechanisms on covered chips within 180 days of enactment (not yet enacted; security critiques exist, ⚠️ snippet: [Center for Cybersecurity Policy](https://www.centerforcybersecuritypolicy.org/insights-and-research/congress-proposed-chip-security-act-threatens-to-create-new-cyber-vulnerabilities-in-us-semiconductors)). Related academic thread: flexHEG (flexible hardware-enabled governance — reprogrammable on-chip controls) and treaty-verification papers ([arXiv 2506.15100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15100), [arXiv 2505.03742](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.03742)).
6. **Datacenter power/construction constraints.** IFP's [Compute in America](https://ifp.org/compute-in-america/) series (Fist & Datta) on 5 GW clusters and behind-the-meter generation (on-site power that bypasses the public grid); RAND's [AI's Power Requirements](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA3572-1.html); [CSIS AI Power Surge](https://www.csis.org/analysis/ai-power-surge-growth-scenarios-genai-datacenters-through-2030) — growth scenarios for datacenter electricity demand through 2030.
7. **Conceptual compute governance** (framework layer). [Computing Power and the Governance of AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797) (read; argues: compute is governable because it is detectable, excludable, quantifiable, and supply-concentrated; warns of privacy/centralization risks). [Open Problems in Technical AI Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14981) — taxonomizes open technical problems, compute verification among them.

**OSINT method toolkit used across the field** (OSINT = open-source intelligence — analysis built only from publicly available data): satellite imagery (free Copernicus Sentinel + commercial), building/environmental permits, FOIA requests, property records, utility interconnection filings, corporate disclosures, customs/trade data, DOJ indictments and court filings, gray-market marketplace listings, investigative-press triangulation, probabilistic (Monte Carlo/hierarchical — repeated random simulation, with parameters layered by uncertainty) modeling over sparse evidence.

## Published exemplars

1. **Khan, Mann & Peterson, [The Semiconductor Supply Chain](https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/the-semiconductor-supply-chain/) (CSET, 2021)** — 3 authors mapped every input to advanced chips and named the chokepoints (EUV, ArF immersion, photoresists); became the blueprint for the Oct-2022 controls. Proof that careful desk research can set national policy.
2. **Grunewald, [AI Chip Smuggling into China: Potential Paths, Quantities, and Countermeasures](https://www.iaps.ai/research/ai-chip-smuggling-into-china) (IAPS, 2023)** — essentially solo; first public quantification of smuggling paths and volumes, identifying third-country routes (Malaysia, Singapore, UAE...). Created a sub-literature.
3. **Juniewicz, [Diversion and Resale: Estimating Compute Smuggling to China](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling) (Epoch, 2026)** — single-author probabilistic model combining a diversion-side estimate (chips redirected from legitimate buyers — indictments + assumed detection rates) with a resale-side gray-market model (chips reappearing on unofficial markets); headline 660k H100e. The methodology section doubles as a how-to for evidence-sparse estimation.
4. **Pilz, Sanders, Rahman & Heim, [Trends in AI Supercomputers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16026) (2025)** — 4 authors, 500-system dataset; the empirical reference for compute distribution (US 75% / China 15%) and growth (performance 2× per 9 months; 2030 lead system projected at 2M chips, $200B, 9 GW).
5. **Sastry, Heim, Belfield et al., [Computing Power and the Governance of AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797) (2024)** — the field-defining framework paper (argues compute is governable because it is detectable, excludable, quantifiable, and supply-concentrated); large author list, but it is the citation anchor every smaller work hangs on.
6. **Aarne, Fist & Withers, [Secure, Governable Chips](https://www.cnas.org/publications/reports/secure-governable-chips) (CNAS, 2024)** — 3 authors; proposed on-chip governance mechanisms including location attestation (a chip cryptographically proving where it is); two years later that idea is in a House-passed bill ([H.R. 3447](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/3447/text)).
7. **Miller, [How US Export Controls Have (and Haven't) Curbed Chinese AI](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/us-chip-export-controls-china-ai) (AI Frontiers, read)** — solo essay that is the clearest public effects-evaluation: controls worked on production and global market share, not on model capability; names four decision-relevant unknowns.
8. **Epoch, [Frontier Data Centers Hub](https://epoch.ai/blog/introducing-the-frontier-data-centers-hub/) (2025, read)** — small team; satellite imagery + permits + aerial photography to independently verify 13 US frontier datacenters (~2.5M H100e); found ~$44B/GW and 1–3.6-year paths to 1 GW. A replicable OSINT method statement.

## Where the gaps are

- **Order-of-magnitude smuggling uncertainty, driven by one parameter.** Epoch's CI (290k–1.6M H100e) mostly reflects the assumed 10–80% detection rate; CNAS's 2024 median (~140k chips/yr) and Epoch's cumulative figure aren't reconciled ([Epoch, read](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling); [CNAS](https://www.cnas.org/press/press-release/new-paper-warns-ai-chip-smuggling-to-china-may-undermine-u-s-national-security-interests)). No one has systematically mined trade/customs data to constrain it.
- **No causal evaluation of export controls.** The effects debate (Miller vs ITIF vs CSIS) runs on narrative + point estimates; nobody has published a quasi-experimental design (a study that mimics a controlled experiment using observational data — e.g., difference-in-differences, which compares before/after change in an affected group against an unaffected one, on Chinese training compute, GPU rental prices, or model-quality trends around control dates) using Epoch's open model/compute data.
- **Compute tracking coverage.** Epoch's hub covers 13 US sites ≈15% of global H100e and states expansion plans (read); non-US buildouts (Gulf states, China, SEA) have no equivalent public tracker — only SemiAnalysis's paywalled model claims global coverage (⚠️ snippet).
- **Verification mechanisms are unaudited.** The Chip Security Act would mandate location verification 180 days post-enactment, but public technical analysis of spoofability/key-extraction is thin, and critics claim it introduces vulnerabilities ([Center for Cybersecurity Policy](https://www.centerforcybersecuritypolicy.org/insights-and-research/congress-proposed-chip-security-act-threatens-to-create-new-cyber-vulnerabilities-in-us-semiconductors), ⚠️ snippet). [Open Problems in TAIG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14981) lists compute verification as open.
- **HBM control loopholes.** The integrated-vs-raw-HBM gap (⚠️ [AI Frontiers](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/high-bandwidth-memory-critical-gaps-us-export-controls), snippet) and CXMT capacity limits are tracked mainly by one commercial shop ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/huawei-ascend-production-ramp)).
- **Datacenter equipment bottlenecks lack rigorous public research.** Transformer/switchgear lead times and interconnection queues appear only in vendor and industry-press material (⚠️ snippets above); nobody has built an open dataset the way Epoch did for facilities.
- **FLOP-threshold erosion.** Internal map (verified): Epoch projects >200 models above 10²⁶ FLOP by 2030 — so as efficiency rises, a fixed compute cutoff catches ever more models; the quantitative "what should replace thresholds" question is open.
- **Chinese utilization is a black box.** Miller repeats reports that "up to 80% of China's AI chips" sit unused — secondhand and unverified even in his own framing ([AI Frontiers](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/us-chip-export-controls-china-ai)).

## What a solo researcher could do here

Concrete study shapes (facts + precedents; no ranking):

1. **Adversarial audit / sensitivity re-analysis of the smuggling estimates.** Rebuild the CNAS and Epoch models (methods are public) in an open Monte Carlo notebook (repeated random simulation to propagate uncertainty); map which assumptions (detection rate, diversion-reached-China probability, vendor-overlap) drive the divergence; publish a reconciliation. Precedent: Juniewicz's report is single-author and invites exactly this ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/publications/chip-smuggling)). Cost: laptop-only.
2. **Extend datacenter OSINT to a non-US region.** Epoch's method statement (Sentinel imagery + permits + cooling-infrastructure identification) is replicable with free imagery ([FDC Hub](https://epoch.ai/blog/introducing-the-frontier-data-centers-hub/)); they state expansion plans, so a well-documented Gulf/SEA/China tracker is either a contribution to them or a standalone dataset. Classical-ML angle: SemiAnalysis trains a CNN (image classifier) for exactly this (⚠️ snippet), and a small labeled-imagery classifier fits a <$500 budget.
3. **Trade-data anomaly detection on transshipment routes.** Grunewald's third-country route list (Malaysia, Singapore, UAE, Taiwan...) is a hypothesis set; customs/trade statistics for server/GPU HS codes (Harmonized System codes — the standardized numbers customs uses to classify traded goods) through those hubs have not been systematically mined in the public literature. XGBoost-style anomaly detection (a gradient-boosted decision-tree method for flagging outliers) on open trade data is a direct profile match.
4. **LLM-pipeline case database of diversion evidence.** Epoch's diversion estimate rests on *manually* cataloged indictments and press reports (read). An LLM-extraction pipeline (with published judge-style validation of extraction accuracy — an LLM scoring another LLM's output, the researcher's existing specialty) over DOJ filings, BIS enforcement actions, and news corpora would produce a living, citable dataset the whole sub-field currently lacks.
5. **Quasi-experimental export-control effects study.** Combine Epoch's open model/training-compute databases ([epoch.ai/data](https://epoch.ai/data)) with control-regime dates to test measurable outcomes (Chinese frontier-model compute growth, model-quality benchmarks, chip prices) against the Miller/ITIF/CSIS qualitative claims. Nothing published does this with an explicit identification strategy (a stated argument for why the observed change is caused by the controls, not confounders).
6. **Technical evaluation of location-verification claims.** Simulate ping-time-based location attestation (accuracy vs network topology, spoofing strategies) and benchmark against the Chip Security Act's requirements; the flexHEG/verification arXiv thread ([2506.15100](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15100), [2505.03742](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.03742)) shows this is publishable without hardware access.

## What we still don't know

- True smuggled volume and its trend (CIs span 5×; detection rate unmeasured).
- Whether the March-2026 arrest wave (Supermicro co-founder case, $170M server case — ⚠️ [BISI](https://bisi.org.uk/reports/ai-chip-smuggling-the-limits-of-us-export-controls) via snippet) reflects better enforcement or larger smuggling.
- China's real 2025–27 domestic chip output (Commerce Secretary Lutnick's 200k vs analyst 400k–1M ranges — ⚠️ CFR snippet) and CXMT HBM capacity.
- Utilization of installed Chinese compute ("80% unused" is unverified).
- Whether H.R. 3447 becomes law, and whether mandated location verification is spoof-resistant in practice.
- Whether FLOP thresholds remain administrable as efficiency improves (internal map: >200 models >10²⁶ by 2030).
- Non-US frontier buildout totals (no public tracker outside the US).
- Whether transformer/interconnection lead-time figures from industry sources survive rigorous measurement.
- Where RAND's compute team stands post-Heim (his profile says "led," past tense — ⚠️ inferred, not confirmed).

## Fit notes

The field's dominant methods — public-document data pipelines, probabilistic estimation with explicit uncertainty, and dataset curation — run on a laptop and free data; no GPU spend is required for any of the study shapes above. The researcher's published evaluation-methodology work (LLM-as-a-judge validation) maps directly onto the field's two weakest points: unaudited estimates and manual evidence cataloging. Classical-ML skills (XGBoost, anomaly detection) match trade-data and satellite-imagery work that Epoch and SemiAnalysis have only begun to automate. Funding-wise, Open Philanthropy names compute governance as relatively neglected and funds individuals and small orgs ([RFP](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/request-for-proposals-ai-governance/)); nonprofit (IAPS/Epoch-style), commercial-newsletter (SemiAnalysis-style), and hired (think-tank) paths all demonstrably exist.
