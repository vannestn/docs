# Human-AI Interaction & Sociotechnical Field Studies — solo-viability sweep

*Sweep 04 · researched 2026-07-08. Sources are pages actually opened or search-result summaries; anything read only via a search snippet or abstract is flagged. ⚠️ = unverified.*

## The shape of this space

The field is "empirical studies of what AI actually does to the humans using it." It sits at the intersection of HCI (CHI/CSCW/IUI/FAccT/AIES venues), labor economics (NBER/QJE), medicine (JAMA/Lancet), psychology, and lab-internal societal-impacts teams. The dominant *methods* are: randomized experiments (lab, online, and field RCTs), large surveys, platform log analysis, and observational before/after studies. The dominant *finding pattern* circa 2024–2026 is divergence: big effects in controlled micro studies, murkier or null effects in the field and at macro scale.

Main sub-areas, each with an anchor result I read about from primary or near-primary sources:

- **Reliance / over-reliance / appropriate reliance.** The oldest and most crowded thread. Microsoft's AETHER [Overreliance on AI: Literature Review](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/overreliance-on-ai-literature-review/) (Passi & Vorvoreanu, 2022, ~60 papers) plus the 2024 [GenAI appropriate-reliance synthesis](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/GenAI_AppropriateReliance_Published2024-3-21.pdf) define the vocabulary; a key documented result is that explanations can *increase* overreliance and many mitigations backfire. Foundational intervention work: [Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos 2021 "To Trust or to Think"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692) — cognitive forcing functions reduce overreliance at a cost to user preference.
- **Deskilling and cognitive effects.** The [Lancet Gastro & Hepatology colonoscopy study](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langas/article/PIIS2468-12532500133-5/abstract) (ACCEPT trial, Poland, Aug 2025): endoscopists' *unassisted* adenoma detection fell 28%→22% after 3 months of AI-assisted practice — widely called the first real-world clinical deskilling evidence ([STAT coverage](https://www.statnews.com/2025/08/12/ai-deskilling-doctors-colonoscopy-study-lancet/)). On knowledge work: MSR/CMU's CHI 2025 survey of 319 knowledge workers ([Lee, Sarkar, Tankelevitch et al.](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3706598.3713778)) — higher confidence in GenAI predicts *less* enacted critical thinking; higher self-confidence predicts more. MIT Media Lab's EEG study ["Your Brain on ChatGPT"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08872) (Kosmyna et al., 54 students, "cognitive debt") got enormous attention and also a published [methodological comment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00856) urging more conservative interpretation.
- **AI companions and parasocial effects.** OpenAI × MIT Media Lab two-part study ([platform analysis of ~4M conversations + 4-week RCT with 981 participants](https://www.media.mit.edu/posts/openai-mit-research-collaboration-affective-use-and-emotional-wellbeing-in-ChatGPT/), [OpenAI page](https://openai.com/index/affective-use-study/)): heavy daily use correlated with loneliness/dependence, but the *experimental* conditions showed no significant effects. Small-team academic side: [Guingrich & Graziano's 21-day longitudinal RCT of Replika use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19515) (Princeton, AIES); [Maples et al.'s survey of 1,006 student Replika users](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-023-00047-6) (npj Mental Health Research — 3% reported the app halted suicidal ideation; note the published [criticism-and-reply exchange](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-024-00083-w)). Teens: Common Sense Media's [Talk, Trust, and Trade-Offs](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/talk-trust-and-trade-offs-how-and-why-teens-use-ai-companions) (n=1,060; 72% of US teens have tried an AI companion).
- **Workplace / productivity field studies.** The canon: [Noy & Zhang, Science 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586) (453 professionals, writing tasks, −40% time, quality compression); [Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, QJE 2025](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658) (5,172 call-center agents, +15% productivity, novices gain most); [Dell'Acqua et al. "Jagged Technological Frontier"](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321) (758 BCG consultants — better inside the frontier, 19pp *worse* outside it); [METR's dev RCT](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/) (16 experienced OSS devs, 246 tasks: 19% *slower* with AI while believing they were 20% faster). Macro counterweight: [Humlum & Vestergaard](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BFI_WP_2025-56-1.pdf) link Danish adoption surveys (n≈100k, later 25k) to register data — precise nulls on earnings/hours two years post-ChatGPT.
- **AI in education.** [Bastani et al., PNAS 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) (~1,000 Turkish high-schoolers): unguarded GPT-4 access boosted practice performance but *hurt* subsequent unassisted exam performance; a "GPT Tutor" with guardrails mitigated the harm. This is the anchor for "crutch vs. scaffold" design questions.
- **Professional behavior (clinicians / lawyers / developers).** [Goh et al., JAMA Netw Open 2024](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/large-language-model-influence-diagnostic-reasoning-randomized-clinical-trial): physicians + GPT-4 ≈ physicians alone, while GPT-4 *alone* beat both by 16pp — the human-AI integration is the bottleneck. Lawyers: [Choi, Monahan & Schwarcz](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4626276) (60 law students, GPT-4: big speed gains, uneven quality gains, largest for weakest participants). Developers: METR above; also GitHub/MSR Copilot RCTs (⚠️ Peng et al. details not re-verified this sweep).
- **Human uplift studies (safety framing).** Controlled trials of whether models make novices more capable of harm: Anthropic ran text-based bio-uplift trials for Claude Opus 4 with biodefense experts and novices ([Frontier Red Team progress post](https://www.anthropic.com/news/strategic-warning-for-ai-risk-progress-and-insights-from-our-frontier-red-team)); OpenAI's [GPT-4 bio early-warning study](https://openai.com/index/building-an-early-warning-system-for-llm-aided-biological-threat-creation/). [Epoch's critique](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/do-the-biorisk-evaluations-of-ai-labs-actually-measure-the-risk-of-developing-bioweapons) questions whether these evals measure real-world risk. Methodologically these are human-subject RCTs — the same skill set as the rest of this field, applied to safety.
- **Log/telemetry analysis of real usage.** Anthropic's [Clio + Economic Index](https://www.anthropic.com/economic-index) (privacy-preserving classification of ~1M+ Claude conversations, now longitudinal reports through [June 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-june-2026-report)); OpenAI/Harvard's [How People Use ChatGPT](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255) (NBER WP 34255 — 73% of mid-2025 messages non-work). Outside labs, the open substitute is [WildChat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01470) (1M consented ChatGPT conversations, 68 languages, AI2 license).
- **AI-mediated communication.** Mor Naaman's Cornell Tech line ([Social Technologies Lab](https://s.tech.cornell.edu/)): smart replies make conversations more positive/efficient but people penalize suspected AI use ([Cornell Chronicle on Hohenstein et al., Sci Reports 2023](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2023/04/study-uncovers-social-cost-using-ai-conversations)); agenda paper: [Hancock, Naaman & Levy, JCMC 2020](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/25/1/89/5714020).
- **Benchmark-vs-human-outcome gap studies.** [Bean et al. (Oxford), "Clinical knowledge in LLMs does not translate to human interactions"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18919): 1,298 participants self-triaging medical scenarios — LLMs alone scored 94.9% on condition ID, but *humans using those LLMs* did no better than controls. This sub-genre (eval-score ≠ human-uplift) is arguably the field's most direct bridge to evaluation methodology.

## Who does this work

**Company lab teams**
- **Anthropic — Societal Impacts + Economic Research** ([team page](https://www.anthropic.com/research/team/societal-impacts), read). Technical team studying real-world use: Clio, Economic Index, "What 81,000 people want from AI" (Mar 2026), agent-autonomy measurement, and an automated qualitative-interviewing tool ("Anthropic Interviewer," Dec 2025, deployed across 1,250 professionals). Company-funded; size not published (⚠️ likely single-digit-to-low-tens). Hires via careers portal. Crucially for outsiders: the [Economic Futures Program](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-the-anthropic-economic-futures-program) offers **$10k–$50k research grants, rolling applications, plus API credits** for empirical work on AI's economic effects (⚠️ eligibility for unaffiliated independents not confirmed on the page I could open).
- **Microsoft Research** — the densest academic-style group: AETHER overreliance reviews (Passi, Vorvoreanu), Tools for Thought / metacognition (Tankelevitch, Sarkar, Rintel, Drosos), the [AICE program](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/collaboration/ai-cognition-and-the-economy-aice/) (funded external network on GenAI × cognition × economy), and the annual [New Future of Work Report](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/new-future-of-work-report-2025/) (2025 edition synthesizes the field; notes shift from "thinking by doing" to "choosing from outputs"). Company-funded, large lab; collaborates heavily with CMU/academia; AICE has taken external grantees (⚠️ current open calls unverified).
- **OpenAI — Economic Research** — the [NBER usage paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34255) with Harvard's David Deming; also the MIT Media Lab affective-use collaboration. Company-funded; collaborates with academics on privacy-preserving internal data.

**Nonprofits**

The dev-productivity RCT was run by **four staff** (Becker, Rush, Barnes, Rein) and they explicitly recruit open-source developers as paid participants ($150/hr); they are [iterating the design in 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/). The most direct "small nonprofit doing rigorous human studies" exemplar.

- **Epoch AI** — nonprofit; critiques uplift-evaluation methodology from the outside ([gradient update](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/do-the-biorisk-evaluations-of-ai-labs-actually-measure-the-risk-of-developing-bioweapons)).

## Published exemplars (small-team-first)

1. **[Noy & Zhang, "Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative AI," Science 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586)** — **two MIT econ PhD students**; preregistered online experiment, 453 professionals via a panel, incentivized writing tasks. Landed because it was the first credible causal estimate of ChatGPT productivity effects and hit a Science paper with essentially a survey-platform budget. Proof that the online-experiment form factor can carry top-venue results.
2. **[METR, "Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity"](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09089)) — **4 authors**, 16 devs, 246 real issues, screen recordings, ~$150/hr participant pay. Landed because the RCT result (19% slower) contradicted both participant perception (+20%) and expert forecasts — a perception-vs-reality result nobody could get from surveys. Study cost is plausibly ~$75k in participant payments alone (⚠️ my arithmetic, not stated), i.e., above solo budget at this scale but the design is shrinkable.
3. **[Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos, "To Trust or to Think," CSCW 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692)** — **3 authors** (incl. one undergrad-level collaborator ⚠️ inferred). Crowdworker experiment showing cognitive forcing functions cut overreliance where explanations fail. Landed because it reframed overreliance as a *dual-process cognition* problem and became the reference intervention; the entire study is runnable on Prolific-scale infrastructure.
4. **[Guingrich & Graziano, "A Longitudinal RCT of Companion Chatbot Use," AIES 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19515)** — **2 authors** (PhD student + advisor). 21 days × 10 min/day Replika vs. word-games control; anthropomorphism mediates social-health effects. Landed because true randomized *longitudinal* companion data is almost nonexistent; shows a solo-scale team can do it with a consumer app and survey instruments.
5. **[Maples et al., "Loneliness and suicide mitigation for students using GPT3-enabled chatbots," npj Mental Health Research 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-023-00047-6)** — small Stanford team; survey of 1,006 student Replika users. Landed (and got heavily covered) for the 3%-halted-suicidal-ideation finding; also instructive for its [published critique](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-024-00083-w) about framing and app-marketing omissions — a live lesson in how self-selected-user surveys get contested.
6. **[Lee et al., "The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking," CHI 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3706598.3713778)** — MSR/CMU, 7 authors but the method is one survey (319 knowledge workers, 936 real examples). Landed because it moved deskilling talk from speculation to measured self-report with a clean predictive structure (confidence-in-AI ↓ critical thinking; self-confidence ↑ it). The instrument-design is fully replicable by one person.
7. **[Bastani et al., "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning," PNAS 2025](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)** — 6 authors, field experiment with ~1,000 students in a Turkish high school. Landed because it's the cleanest "AI as crutch" causal result *and* shows a design fix (GPT Tutor guardrails). Field-partnership-dependent — the school access is the scarce resource, not the analysis.
8. **[Bean et al., "Clinical knowledge in LLMs does not translate to human interactions," 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18919)** — Oxford; 1,298-participant controlled study. Landed because it directly demonstrated that benchmark performance (94.9%) does not survive contact with real users (≤34.5%, no better than control) — the single most important result for anyone coming from evaluation methodology.

(Big-team contrast points, for calibration: [Brynjolfsson et al. QJE](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658) needed a Fortune-500 data partnership; [Dell'Acqua et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321) needed BCG and 9 authors; the [OpenAI/MIT affective-use RCT](https://openai.com/index/affective-use-study/) needed platform data access.)

## Where the gaps are

- **Ecological validity of reliance studies.** Surveys of the human-AI decision-making literature note reliance on crowdworkers standing in for domain experts, simulated/Wizard-of-Oz AI, and dataset-availability-driven task selection ([Towards a Science of Human-AI Decision Making survey](https://paperium.net/article/en/4801/towards-a-science-of-human-ai-decision-making-a-survey-of-empirical-studies); [Frontiers taxonomy of interaction patterns](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2024.1521066/full); [Steyvers & Kumar, "Three Challenges for AI-Assisted Decision-Making"](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/17456916231181102)) — I read these via abstracts/summaries, not full texts ⚠️. Gap: reliance findings validated on *real tasks with real stakes and real practitioners* are rare (Goh et al. and the Lancet study are the exceptions, and both are medicine).
- **Perception ≠ reality; self-report dominates.** METR's headline is that developers' own estimates were directionally wrong. Yet most workplace evidence (Humlum's surveys, MSR's critical-thinking survey, Economic Index user studies) is self-report or usage-classification. Gap named by METR's own limitations discussion: objective outcome measurement outside coding is scarce.
- **Micro-macro reconciliation.** 15–40% micro productivity gains (Noy & Zhang; Brynjolfsson; Dell'Acqua) vs. [precise nulls on Danish earnings/hours](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/BFI_WP_2025-56-1.pdf) two years in. Nobody I read has a validated account of where the gains go (task mix? rent capture? measurement?). Explicit open question in Humlum & Vestergaard's framing ("still waters, rapid currents").
- **Longitudinal deskilling evidence outside medicine.** The colonoscopy study is observational, 3-month, one country; the accompanying [Lancet comment](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/langas/article/PIIS2468-1253(25)00164-5/abstract) explicitly asks whether this generalizes. For knowledge work we have surveys and a 54-person EEG study (already [contested](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00856)) — no multi-month randomized skill-retention study of, e.g., developers or analysts. The [Tools for Thought CHI 2025 synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21036) (56 participants, 10 authors across MSR/Harvard/CMU etc.) names measurement approaches for cognition effects as an open need.
- **Companion effects: self-selection and no outcome tracking.** Maples et al. is a survey of existing users; Common Sense is prevalence-only; the one platform-scale RCT (OpenAI/MIT) found null *causal* effects at 4 weeks while correlations were alarming — leaving the causal question open at longer horizons. The [OII Lancet CAH piece](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/oxford-researchers-outline-recommendations-for-studying-the-impact-of-artificial-intelligence-on-young-peoples-mental-health/) argues AI research must avoid social-media research's decade of underpowered, framework-free studies — i.e., the field *knows* it lacks shared frameworks and industry data access.
- **Benchmark-to-human transfer.** Bean et al. shows model benchmark scores don't predict human-in-the-loop outcomes; Goh et al. shows humans+AI underperform AI alone. Almost no one systematically maps *which* eval scores predict *which* human outcomes — a measurement-science gap, not a data-access gap.
- **Log data asymmetry.** Clio and the OpenAI NBER study are internal-data monopolies; outsiders get WildChat (consented but self-selected, ended May 2024). Gap: fresh, consented, longitudinal usage corpora — and methods for collecting them cheaply (data donation).
- **Credibility/fraud exposure.** The [Toner-Rodgers affair](https://the-decoder.com/mit-says-a-high-profile-ai-productivity-study-used-data-that-cannot-be-trusted/) — a solo student's fabricated AI-productivity field study that fooled Acemoglu/Autor and the press before MIT [disavowed it](https://science.slashdot.org/story/25/05/16/213210/mit-asks-arxiv-to-take-down-preprint-paper-on-ai-and-scientific-discovery) — shows both the field's appetite for these results and its weak verification norms. Preregistration + open data are differentiators.

**Solo-method reality check (methods a solo researcher can actually run):** Prolific/panel experiments (Prolific mandates consent flow, min pay £6/$8/hr, [IRB-application support docs](https://researcher-help.prolific.com/en/articles/449628-irb-applications-and-ethics-approval) — the help page does not state a hard IRB requirement ⚠️); WildChat-style log analyses (no human-subjects issues if using released data under license); survey-instrument studies; N-of-many self-experiments and diary studies; re-analysis/meta-analysis of published effects. **Ethics honestly:** unaffiliated researchers have no home IRB; options are commercial IRBs ([Solutions IRB](https://www.solutionsirb.com/overcoming-irb-challenges-as-an-independent-researcher/) and similar, fees from a few hundred to several thousand USD, ⚠️ exact pricing unverified) or partnering with an institution-based co-author who serves as PI. Anything touching minors, patients, suicidality, or deception is effectively off-limits solo; adult crowdworker studies on work tasks are the realistic lane. Budget arithmetic (⚠️ mine): $500 ≈ 100–150 Prolific participants × 15 min at ~$12/hr + fees — enough for a pilot or a tightly-designed replication, not a Noy-Zhang-scale study.

Need to open the [program page](https://www.anthropic.com/economic-futures/program) and check terms. Same for MSR AICE: any *current* open call?
- **Commercial IRB actual cost/turnaround** for a single Prolific study — get real quotes (Solutions IRB, Advarra, WCG). Also: whether target journals (PNAS, npj MHR, CHI) accept commercial-IRB approval from independents in practice.
- **Whether Prolific enforces ethics review** for researchers without institutions — the help page is ambiguous; test empirically or ask support.
- **Team sizes** of Anthropic Societal Impacts, MSR groups — not published; my "small" characterizations are inference ⚠️.
- **Copilot RCT literature** (Peng et al. 2023; Cui et al. enterprise field experiments) — surfaced but not verified this sweep; needed for a complete developer-behavior picture.
- **Replication status** of pre-2024 overreliance findings under 2026 agentic/chat paradigms — the CFF literature predates conversational agents; the [2026 CFF-for-AI-writing follow-up](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18033) suggests the community is re-testing, unread ⚠️.
- **The contested claims**: strength of the "Your Brain on ChatGPT" conclusions post-comment; the Maples/luddite exchange resolution; whether the Lancet deskilling effect replicates elsewhere (an [ACG commentary](https://gi.org/journals-publications/ebgi/zhou_sep2025/) exists, unread ⚠️).
- **Data-donation tooling** — is there an existing consented-browser-extension pipeline (post-WildChat) a solo researcher could reuse rather than build? Not investigated.
- **Assumption to test:** that a solo researcher can get a benchmark-vs-human-transfer study (Bean-style, scaled down) through a commercial IRB and onto arXiv + a CHI/IUI venue for <$500 + fees. Nothing I read contradicts it; nothing confirms it.

## Fit notes

The researcher's published LLM-as-a-judge / RAG-hallucination evaluation work maps directly onto this field's most active measurement gap: Bean et al.'s benchmark-vs-human-transfer result and Goh et al.'s human+AI integration failure are, structurally, evaluation-methodology problems run with human participants. Data-pipeline and classical-ML skills apply to WildChat-scale log analysis and to building Clio-style open classifiers, which require no human-subjects approval. The <=$500/study budget matches Prolific pilot/replication scale (roughly 100–150 participants) and the two named external funding channels (Anthropic Economic Futures $10k–$50k; MSR AICE) fund exactly this genre. Precedent exists for two-person, top-venue work in this space (Noy & Zhang; Guingrich & Graziano; Choi & Schwarcz), with IRB access via a commercial board or an affiliated co-author being the main structural dependency.
