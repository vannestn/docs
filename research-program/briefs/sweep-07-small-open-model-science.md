# Small/Open-Model Science: Empirical Behavior Research on Open Weights and Modest GPUs

*Sweep 07 — researched 2026-07. Sources are pages actually opened via web search/fetch; anything not directly verified is flagged ⚠️.*

**One-line definition:** treating open-weight models themselves as the object of empirical study — how quantization (shrinking a model by storing its weights in fewer bits, e.g. 16-bit → 4-bit), fine-tuning (further training a base model on a narrow dataset to specialize it), distillation (training a small "student" model to mimic a larger "teacher"), scale, and deployment change model *behavior* — plus the meta-science of tracking the open-weights ecosystem itself. Nearly all of it runs on open checkpoints (downloadable model weights) and consumer/rented GPUs.

> **How to read this brief.** Where the text names a paper or a technical term, a plain-language gloss follows in parentheses or an em-dash so you can grasp the gist without opening the link. Nothing is dumbed down; the glosses just save you a click.

## The shape of this space

Six recognizable sub-areas, each with its own literature and community:

1. **Quantization behavior science** (not method invention — *measuring what quantization does* to model behavior). Key results: aggregate accuracy hides massive answer "flips" between a model and its quantized version even when benchmark scores match ([Accuracy is Not All You Need, MSR India](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09141) — showed that at equal accuracy the compressed model still changes many individual answers, so a matched score hides real behavior change); PTQ/QAT (post-training quantization = compress after training; quantization-aware training = train with compression in the loop) can degrade safety alignment, with results for PTQ still described as "inconclusive" ([Q-resafe, Chen et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20251v1) — verified primary (deepening pass 2): systematically audits safety across mainstream quant methods, and — surveying Egashira et al.'s PGD attack on AWQ/GPTQ — notes quantization can be deliberately exploited to induce malicious behavior; Q-resafe's own contribution is a *safety-patching* framework that re-aligns quantized models; [Preserving Fairness and Safety in Quantized LLMs Through Critical Weight Protection, Al Hakim, Wicaksono & Koto, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12033v1) — Universitas Indonesia + MBZUAI; verified primary (deepening pass 2): states PTQ-safety findings "report inconsistent results," that "larger models exhibit more consistent behavior across quantization methods, whereas smaller models show greater divergence in downstream safety performance," and that safety deterioration is "especially pronounced in non-English settings"); interpretability-style probing of quantized internals (opening up *why* the weights behave differently, not just measuring outputs) is just starting ([Interpreting the Effects of Quantization on LLMs, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.16785v1)). The intellectual ancestor is Hooker et al.'s [What Do Compressed Deep Neural Networks Forget?](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05248) (finding: compression disproportionately damages long-tail/underrepresented examples — the "Pruning Identified Exemplars" that a compressed model newly gets wrong). Community-side, indie KL-divergence audits (KL divergence = a statistical distance between two probability distributions, here the full vs. quantized model's next-token predictions) of GGUF quants (GGUF = the llama.cpp file format for quantized models people download and run locally) are now a genre ([localbench Gemma GGUF study](https://localbench.substack.com/p/gemma-4-31b-gguf-kl-divergence)).
2. **Small-vs-large capability divergence.** The "emergence is a metric artifact" line — the claim that abilities appearing suddenly at scale are an illusion created by the scoring metric, not a real jump ([Schaeffer et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004) — finding: of the 39 preferred BIG-Bench metrics at most 5 show emergence, and just 2 discontinuous/nonlinear metrics — Multiple Choice Grade and Exact String Match — account for >92% of all claimed emergent abilities; verified primary, deepening pass 2); the **small-model learnability gap** — models ≤3B get *worse* when distilled from long-CoT (long chain-of-thought = lengthy step-by-step reasoning traces) strong reasoners and need shorter chains ([Small Models Struggle to Learn from Strong Reasoners, Li et al., Findings of ACL 2025, pp. 25366–25394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12143) — 8 authors, UW/CMU/WWU; verified primary + venue (deepening pass 2): small students learn better from shorter reasoning; e.g. Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct gains >8 pts on MATH/AMC from Mix-Long vs training on long CoT; offers "mix distillation" as a fix); and existence proofs that tiny task-specific models beat LLMs ([Tiny Recursion Model, 7M params, 45% ARC-AGI-1, solo author](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871) — a 7-million-parameter recursive network scores 45% on the ARC-AGI-1 abstract-reasoning benchmark, beating most large models).
3. **Fine-tuning science** — what SFT/LoRA/DPO (SFT = supervised fine-tuning on input→output examples; LoRA = low-rank adaptation, training small add-on weight matrices instead of the whole model; DPO = direct preference optimization, tuning on "this answer beats that one" pairs) actually do behaviorally. Pillars: fine-tuning (even benign) erodes safety alignment ([Qi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03693) — finding: a few fine-tuning steps, even on harmless data, can strip a model's safety guardrails, with the follow-up "shallow alignment" mechanism — alignment lives in the first few output tokens, so it's easy to dislodge); LoRA is a weaker learner but a weaker forgetter than full FT (full fine-tuning) ([LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673) — finding: LoRA absorbs less new-domain skill but also retains more of the original model's abilities); narrow fine-tuning can produce *broad* misalignment ([Emergent Misalignment, Betley et al.](https://truthful.ai/papers/emergent-misalignment/) — finding: tuning a model only on insecure code makes it behave badly across unrelated topics; strongest in GPT-4o and open Qwen2.5-Coder-32B); refusal is a single residual-stream direction (one linear direction in the model's internal activations controls whether it refuses) ([Arditi et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://x.com/maximelabonne/status/1803108202784997672) — finding: erase that one direction and the model stops refusing), operationalized by indie "abliteration" work (a community technique that removes the refusal direction to "uncensor" a model) ([Labonne's HF tutorial](https://huggingface.co/blog/mlabonne/abliteration)). The interpretability wing is **model diffing** (comparing the internals of two model versions to see what changed): crosscoders (a sparse-feature tool trained across both models to name shared and new concepts) that identify concepts introduced by chat-tuning ([Overcoming Sparsity Artifacts in Crosscoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02922) — finding: naive crosscoders falsely attribute concepts as "new" due to sparsity artifacts, and fixes them; [Delta-Crosscoder for narrow fine-tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04426v1) — a crosscoder variant adapted to the narrow-fine-tuning case).
4. **Distillation effects.** Distilled models lose things headline metrics don't capture — calibration (whether the model's confidence matches its accuracy), abstention (knowing when to say "I don't know"), diversity, safety ([position paper: Knowledge Distillation Must Account for What It Loses — single author](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25110) — argues students can match top-line scores while quietly losing these properties, and calls for "what was lost" reporting); injected adversarial bias propagates 5.7×–29.2× into students on unseen/OOD tasks under untargeted propagation (a teacher deliberately poisoned with bias passes it to the student amplified many-fold; teacher ARR ~69% → student ARR ~77%) ([Cascading Adversarial Bias, Chaudhari et al., CCS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24842) — Northeastern + Google DeepMind; verified primary, deepening pass 2); distilled reasoning models have distinct representational/repetition signatures ([Towards Understanding Distilled Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03730) — finding: distilled reasoners show identifiable internal and repetition patterns setting them apart from the teacher).
5. **Open-weights ecosystem tracking.** Radboud's 14-dimension openness grid (rating each model on 14 facets — code, data, weights, license, docs — rather than a single "open" yes/no) over 189 systems, naming "open-washing" clusters (models marketed as "open" while withholding key pieces like training data) ([European Open Source AI Index](https://osai-index.eu/about/); [Rethinking open source generative AI, MPI page](https://www.mpi.nl/publications/item3588217/rethinking-open-source-generative-ai-open-washing-and-eu-ai-act)); software-repository-mining (borrowing the empirical methods of GitHub-mining research to study a model hub as a software repository) of the HF (Hugging Face) Hub (380k+ models — [Castaño et al.](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ef1de85ebe1828a17bdf48d4bffa57f5cd245e37) — large-scale mining of Hub models; [Ait et al. on Hub suitability for empirical studies](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.14841) — assesses whether Hub metadata is clean enough for research; [Lessons Learned from Mining the Hugging Face Repository](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323) — methodological pitfalls of Hub-mining, e.g. sampling and causality); qualitative adoption studies ([Open AI in the Wild: r/LocalLLaMA, UT Austin](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22211v1) — interview/forum study of why hobbyists adopt open models).
6. **On-device model behavior.** Mostly systems-flavored today: engine choice (which inference runtime you use) changes latency ~5x for the same model (MNN vs llama.cpp, two competing local-inference engines), and there's a widening accuracy-vs-on-device-throughput gap across model generations ([Understanding LLMs in Your Pockets, COTS mobile study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03613) — benchmarks LLMs on commercial off-the-shelf phones; [lm-Meter](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06126v1) — an on-device measurement tool for latency/energy). Behavioral (not just latency) divergence across engines/quant pipelines is thinly studied — see gaps.

**What the day-to-day work looks like:** download checkpoints (often a base/instruct/quantized triple — the raw pretrained model, its chat-tuned version, and a compressed version of the same), build a behavioral eval battery (a suite of tests probing behavior, not just accuracy), run controlled comparisons on 1–4 GPUs, do statistics on divergence (KL, flips, refusal rates, calibration), and publish with full artifacts. Pythia exists precisely to enable this kind of controlled science ([EleutherAI/pythia](https://github.com/EleutherAI/pythia): 16 models, 70M–12B, released with reproducible training order so you can study exactly how behavior forms during training).

## Published exemplars (small-team-weighted)

1. **[Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871)** — Jolicoeur-Martineau, **solo author**, Samsung SAIL Montréal (verified primary, pass 2). 7M-param recursive net hits 45% ARC-AGI-1, beating most LLMs; the paper states ARC-AGI runs took "~3 days with 4 H100" and gives **no dollar cost** — the "<$500" is journalist-computed (⚠️ secondary-only), though a few-hundred-dollar order of magnitude follows from the primary hardware/time statement. Landed because it's a crisp existence proof that tiny-model science can headline against frontier LLMs — and it is literally within this researcher's budget envelope.
2. **[Emergent Misalignment](https://truthful.ai/papers/emergent-misalignment/)** (Betley et al., ICML 2025) — ~8 authors, independent org + collaborators (Truthful AI). Fine-tune on insecure code → broad misalignment; a pre-registered expert survey (experts predicted the outcome in advance, on record) failed to predict it. Landed because it revealed a genuine unknown in fine-tuning science; replicable on open Qwen2.5-Coder-32B; spawned a follow-up literature ([model organisms](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11613) — small controlled setups built to study the phenomenon; [linear representations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.11618) — locates the misalignment as a direction in activation space) within months.
3. **[Accuracy is Not All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09141)** — 4 authors, MSR India. Introduced "flips" (correct↔incorrect answer churn) as a cheap distance metric showing compressed models behave differently even at matched accuracy. Landed because it gave the quantization-behavior field its core measurement critique.
4. **Refusal-direction → abliteration chain** — [Arditi et al., "Refusal in LMs Is Mediated by a Single Direction," NeurIPS 2024](https://x.com/maximelabonne/status/1803108202784997672) (small, largely early-career/independent team) plus [Labonne's solo tutorial](https://huggingface.co/blog/mlabonne/abliteration). Landed because a single-direction interp finding became a widely used community technique within weeks — the archetype of open-weights behavioral science translating to practice.
5. **[LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09673)** (Biderman et al., Databricks Mosaic + Columbia) — the reference point for "what does LoRA actually do": less target-domain learning, less source-domain forgetting, stronger regularization than full FT. Industry team, but the experiments are replicable at small scale.
6. **[Open AI in the Wild: Adoption and Adaptation of Open Models on r/LocalLLaMA](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22211v1)** — 4 authors, UT Austin. Purist-vs-pragmatist openness framing; documented adoption drivers (privacy, cost, "uncensored" variants) and named missing infrastructure — including **standardized quantization benchmarks** — as the thing keeping the community stuck in "remedial maintenance." Landed as the first serious qualitative study of this exact community.
7. **[localbench: Gemma 4 31B GGUF quality benchmark](https://localbench.substack.com/p/gemma-4-31b-gguf-kl-divergence)** — indie, non-peer-reviewed; author verified as **oobabooga** (deepening pass 2). KL divergence over ~250k tokens across 53 quants from 4 uploaders (all figures verified against the source page); found unsloth/bartowski split the Pareto frontier ("Neither dominates the other across all sizes") and that **non-Latin scripts and long documents degrade fastest at every quant level** (KL 0.222 / 0.466 even at Q8_0 — the least-lossy 8-bit quant, where degradation should be minimal). Landed (in-community) because nobody else measures the artifacts people actually download; also illustrates the genre's rigor gap (no confidence intervals — no error bars on the numbers, confirmed from the page).
8. **[Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004)** (Schaeffer, Miranda, Koyejo — 3 authors, Stanford; NeurIPS 2023 outstanding-paper attention) — metric choice, not scale, manufactures apparent emergence. Verified primary (deepening pass 2): of 39 preferred BIG-Bench metrics at most 5 show emergence; >92% of claimed emergence is produced by just 2 metrics (Multiple Choice Grade, Exact String Match); the effect disappears under continuous metrics (Brier Score). Landed because it reframed the small-vs-large divergence question as an evaluation-methodology question — directly adjacent to this researcher's published specialty.

*Honorable mentions:* [Pythia](https://github.com/EleutherAI/pythia) (the infrastructure exemplar); [What Do Compressed DNNs Forget?](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05248) (the founding compression-behavior paper); [Small Models Struggle to Learn from Strong Reasoners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12143) (learnability gap); [Overcoming Sparsity Artifacts in Crosscoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02922) (model-diffing methodology).

## Where the gaps are

Grounded in limitation sections, position papers, and community discourse actually read for this brief:

1. **No standardized quantization-behavior benchmark.** Named explicitly as missing infrastructure by the [r/LocalLLaMA study](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22211v1) (producers should invest in "standardized quantization benchmarks"). The indie KL audits ([localbench](https://localbench.substack.com/p/gemma-4-31b-gguf-kl-divergence)) have no statistical treatment, and academic proposals (flips, KL) haven't been packaged into an adopted harness. This is an *evaluation-methodology* gap more than a systems gap.
2. **PTQ-safety results are inconclusive and fragmented.** The [Critical Weight Protection paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.12033v1) states prior PTQ safety findings "remain inconclusive," and that *smaller models diverge more across quantization methods* — small models × quantization × safety is specifically under-characterized. [Q-resafe](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20251v1) adds that quantization can be *deliberately exploited* to induce malicious behavior — an attack surface with little follow-up.
3. **Distillation has no "what-was-lost" reporting norm.** The [Wang position paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.25110) argues students can preserve headline scores while losing calibration, abstention, boundary behavior, safety, and diversity — and calls for model-card-style loss reporting (a standardized disclosure sheet, like the "model cards" that document a model's intended use and limits) that doesn't exist yet. With distilled reasoning models (R1-distills = smaller models distilled from DeepSeek-R1's reasoning traces, etc.) everywhere, this gap is live.
4. **Behavioral divergence across on-device inference stacks is nearly unstudied.** The mobile literature measures latency/energy ([lm-Meter](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06126v1), [COTS phones study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03613)); whether the *same checkpoint* answers differently across MNN/llama.cpp/MLC/ExecuTorch (four competing on-device inference engines) quant pipelines (and whether safety behavior survives) is an open empirical question. ⚠️ inferred absence — no paper found in this sweep, which is itself the point.
5. **Non-English degradation under compression.** Both localbench (non-Latin scripts degrade fastest) and the r/LocalLLaMA study (multilinguality deficits as adoption barrier) point at compression × multilingual behavior as measurable, harm-relevant, and mostly ignored — it directly echoes Hooker's long-tail result at the language level.
6. **Model diffing methods are young and artifact-prone.** Crosscoder diffing misattributes concepts due to "Complete Shrinkage"/"Latent Decoupling" ([sparsity artifacts paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02922)); narrow-fine-tuning regimes needed a new variant within a year ([Delta-Crosscoder](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04426v1)). Method-validation studies are cheap on Pythia-scale models.
7. **Ecosystem tracking is snapshot-heavy, longitudinally thin.** The [OSAI Index](https://osai-index.eu/about/) grades openness at points in time; HF-mining papers themselves flag methodological issues (stratified sampling, causality — [Lessons Learned](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07323)). Continuous *license-drift* monitoring (models relicensed, gated, or deleted over time) wasn't found as a maintained dataset in this sweep. ⚠️ inferred absence.
8. **Small-model learnability gap mechanisms.** [Small Models Struggle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12143) shows ≤3B models are hurt by long-CoT distillation and offers a mitigation (mix distillation) but not a mechanism — connects directly to metric-artifact questions from the mirage paper.
