# Evaluation Methodology & the Standards Layer (mid-2026)

*Researched July 2026. Every claim below comes from a page opened during research unless marked "⚠️ unverified" or "(inferred)". Inline links point at the source actually read.*

## The shape of this space — what the work actually consists of

This is the meta-layer of AI evaluation: not building benchmarks, but making evaluation itself trustworthy — and turning that trustworthiness into standards regulators can lean on. Five distinguishable sub-areas:

**1. The "science of evals" agenda (measurement + construct validity).**
The founding text is Apollo Research's [We Need a Science of Evals](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/science/we-need-a-science-of-evals/) (Jan 2024): evals are used for high-stakes decisions (RSPs, policy) but lack replicability, statistical guarantees, and coverage — e.g. "simple formatting changes... can lead to a ~5 percentage point change in accuracy." A widely-read [response by Hernández-Orallo & Martínez-Plumed](https://aievaluation.substack.com/p/a-response-to-we-need-a-science-of) countered that the post reinvents decades of psychometrics/measurement theory (construct validity, reliability) and ignores software testing and NIST's decades of evaluation practice. The academic follow-through:
- [Wallach et al., "Evaluating Generative AI Systems Is a Social Science Measurement Challenge"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00561) (ICML 2025, Microsoft Research–led, 20 authors) — a four-level measurement framework (background concept → systematized concept → instrument → instance measurements); argues ML "jumps straight from background concepts to measurement instruments."
- [Weidinger, Raji et al., "Toward an Evaluation Science for Generative AI Systems"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05336) (Mar 2025; DeepMind/Berkeley/MSR/Stanford authors) — three lessons from safety engineering in aviation/pharma: metrics must connect to real-world performance; metrics must be iteratively refined; evaluation *institutions* and norms must be built.
- [Bean et al., "Measuring what Matters: Construct Validity in LLM Benchmarks"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04703) (NeurIPS 2025 D&B; Oxford Internet Institute–led) — systematic review of **445 benchmarks** by ~29 expert reviewers; finds vague phenomenon definitions and near-absence of statistical tests; ships an operational checklist.
- [BetterBench (Reuel et al., NeurIPS 2024 spotlight, Stanford)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990) — 46-criteria benchmark-quality assessment applied to 24 benchmarks; "most benchmarks do not report statistical significance... nor allow easy replication"; living repository at [betterbench.stanford.edu](https://betterbench.stanford.edu/).

**2. Statistics of evals.**
Anchor: [Miller (Anthropic), "Adding Error Bars to Evals"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640) ([blog](https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals)) — treats an eval as an experiment; gives formulas for standard errors (incl. clustered SEs, which can be **>3× larger than naive SEs**), paired-difference tests between models, and power analysis for planning eval sizes. Notably a **single-author** paper. The construct-validity and BetterBench reviews (above) show these practices are still rarely adopted — that adoption gap *is* the research area.

**3. Contamination detection.**
- The [CONDA workshop](https://conda-workshop.github.io/) (ACL 2024–) institutionalized the topic; its [shared task report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21530) built a public, PR-driven evidence database: 566 contamination entries over 91 sources from **23 community contributors** — explicitly open to solo contributions via GitHub.
- [Cheng, Chang & Wu (Jilin University), "A Survey on Data Contamination for LLMs"](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14425v1) taxonomizes detection by access level: white-box (n-gram overlap, embedding similarity, activations), gray-box (Min-K% token-probability outliers, perplexity), black-box (paraphrased multiple-choice probing, output-distribution tests); mitigations = data updating (dynamic/live test sets), data rewriting, and prevention (encryption, access control, inference-time decontamination).

**4. Saturation analysis.**
MMLU-class benchmarks are functionally saturated for frontier models (⚠️ specific % claims come from secondary blogs, e.g. [Kili's 2026 benchmarks guide](https://kili-technology.com/blog/ai-benchmarks-guide-the-top-evaluations-in-2026-and-why-theyre-not-enough)). Responses: harder benchmarks ([Humanity's Last Exam — 2,500 questions from ~1,000 experts, published in Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09962-4), with a [verified subset run independently by Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/hle)) and — newer, and more this brief's territory — *meta-analyses of saturation itself*: ["When AI Benchmarks Plateau: A Systematic Study of Benchmark Saturation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16763) (190 benchmarks across 61 lab reports, 2022–2025), ["Benchmark Health Index"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11674) (scores 106 benchmarks on discrimination/anti-saturation/impact), ["The Ouroboros of Benchmarking"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01365) (nearly all pre-2025 benchmarks surpassed). Saturation/lifecycle analysis is a young, data-analysis-shaped niche.

**5. The standards & regulatory layer.**
- **NIST / CAISI (US).** The AI Safety Institute was reorganized as the [Center for AI Standards and Innovation](https://www.nist.gov/caisi), "industry's primary point of contact" for testing and collaborative research; runs unclassified evaluations focused on cyber/biosecurity, develops guidance, represents the US in international standards. ⚠️ Secondary sources ([ExecutiveGov](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/nists-caisi-openmined-ai-evaluation-privacy), [Cybersecurity Dive](https://www.cybersecuritydive.com/news/nist-ai-model-testing-caisi-google-microsoft/819452/), [Microsoft blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2026/05/05/advancing-ai-evaluation-with-the-center-for-ai-standards-us-and-innovation-and-the-ai-security-institute-uk/)) report 40+ model evaluations and pre-deployment agreements with five frontier developers by mid-2026 — not confirmed on nist.gov directly. The **ARIA** program ([launch](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2024/05/nist-launches-aria-new-program-advance-sociotechnical-testing-and), [pilot report NIST AI 700-2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.700-2.pdf)) is NIST's sociotechnical field-testing effort: pilot ran 3 scenarios (TV spoilers, meal planning, travel), 3 testing layers (fixed prompts, red-teaming, realistic field use), 1,500+ annotations by 7 trained staff over 508 sessions, producing a "Contextual Robustness Index (CoRIx)". The [International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/02/international-network-advanced-ai-measurement-evaluation-and-science) (10 members: AU, CA, EU, FR, JP, KE, KR, SG, UK, US) published **consensus areas on practices for automated evaluations** (Feb 2026), out of a Dec 2025 CAISI workshop at NeurIPS — the clearest signal that "eval best practices" are becoming intergovernmental standards material.
- **EU AI Act + GPAI Code of Practice.** The [Code of Practice](https://code-of-practice.ai/) (published 10 July 2025; [Commission page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai)) is a voluntary compliance tool written by independent experts. Its Safety & Security chapter (systemic-risk models only) requires a Safety & Security Framework with evaluation triggers; per-risk analysis = model-independent info → **model evaluations** → risk modeling → risk estimation → post-market monitoring; documentation retained 10 years; enforcement (Art. 101) begins **2 Aug 2026** ([Latham & Watkins summary](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/eu-ai-act-gpai-model-obligations-in-force-and-final-gpai-code-of-practice-in-place)). [CSET's analysis](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/eu-ai-code-safety/): evaluations must cover four named systemic risks (CBRN, loss of control, cyber offense, **harmful manipulation**) via "benchmarks, red teaming, and human uplift studies"; OpenAI/Google/Mistral/Anthropic supported, Meta refused; and — key gap — acceptable risk thresholds are "almost universally undefined" and fewer than half of reviewed firms substantively test dangerous capabilities. Meanwhile the *harmonized standards* for high-risk systems from [CEN-CENELEC JTC 21](https://jtc21.eu/) are badly delayed: original deadline Apr 2025, [acceleration package adopted Oct 2025](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2025/brief-news/2025-10-23-ai-standardization/) targeting deliverables by Q4 2026 — leaving providers months, not years, to comply.
- **ISO/IEC.** [ISO/IEC 42001:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001) (AI management systems) is written by [JTC 1/SC 42](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui/en/#!iso:std:81230:en), which maintains 50+ AI standards; participation is via national-body working groups (individual experts join through their national committee); certification is done by accredited third-party bodies, not ISO. 42001 is a *process/management* standard — it does not tell you which evals to run (inferred from scope).
- **MLCommons AILuminate.** [v1.0 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05731) (~102 authors — industry consortium scale): 12 content-hazard categories, five-tier grades (Poor→Excellent) computed *relative to reference models*, ensemble-of-tuned-open-safety-evaluators as grader. Self-acknowledged limits: single-turn only, content hazards only, evaluator uncertainty. [Current state](https://mlcommons.org/ailuminate/): English/French/Chinese safety suites, English jailbreak suite, agentic-reliability and multimodal workstreams underway. The [AI Risk & Reliability working group](https://mlcommons.org/working-groups/ai-risk-reliability/ai-risk-reliability/) says "We welcome others to join us" — 100+ orgs listed; ⚠️ unverified whether individual (non-member-org) participation is fully free.

**6. Tooling ecosystems (the de-facto standards).**
- **[Inspect](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/)** (UK AISI): open-sourced May 2024, created by **JJ Allaire** (RStudio/Posit founder) during a sabbatical then as AISI staff engineer ([Parlance Labs interview](https://parlance-labs.com/education/evals/allaire.html)); [Inspect Evals](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/inspect-evals) is the community benchmark repo (dozens of evals; maintained with **Arcadia Impact** and the **Vector Institute**); 50+ contributors; used by frontier labs and safety orgs — [Apollo publicly adopted it](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/apollo-is-adopting-inspect/). The closest thing to a de-facto standard for safety-institute-grade evals; also has a [sandboxing toolkit for agent evals](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/the-inspect-sandboxing-toolkit-scalable-and-secure-ai-agent-evaluations).
- **[lm-eval-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness)** (EleutherAI): backbone of the (former) HF Open LLM Leaderboard era; its paper ["Lessons from the Trenches on Reproducible Evaluation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782) is the canonical practitioner account of why eval numbers don't reproduce.
- **[HELM](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm)** (Stanford CRFM): the "holistic, multi-metric" framework with domain leaderboards (MedHELM, VHELM, HEIM…). Per its README, HELM **entered maintenance mode June 1, 2026** — a live example of academic eval-infrastructure fragility.
- **[OpenAI Evals](https://github.com/openai/evals)**: the hosted Evals platform is being deprecated (read-only Oct 31 2026, shutdown Nov 30 2026 per [OpenAI docs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/evals)); [simple-evals](https://github.com/openai/simple-evals) frozen since July 2025. ⚠️ platform-vs-repo details partially unverified.
- **Commercial observability/eval layer**: [Langfuse](https://laminar.sh/article/langfuse-alternatives-2026) (OSS, MIT, ~28k stars), LangSmith (LangChain-native, commercial), [Braintrust](https://www.braintrust.dev/articles), W&B Weave, Arize Phoenix, Confident AI/DeepEval. ⚠️ One source ([Firecrawl roundup](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/best-llm-observability-tools)) reports OpenAI acquired **promptfoo** in March 2026 with the core staying MIT-licensed — unverified elsewhere. This layer is where "evals" meets ordinary product engineering; it is commercially crowded but methodologically thin (inferred — vendors compete on tracing/UX, not measurement validity).

## Published exemplars

1. **[Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)** — Evan Miller, **solo author**, Anthropic (Nov 2024). Landed because it converted a vague complaint ("evals lack rigor") into usable formulas (clustered SEs, paired tests, power analysis) with a headline result — naive SEs understate uncertainty ~3× on clustered eval data. Proof that a single statistically-literate person can move the whole field's norms.
2. **[We Need a Science of Evals](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/science/we-need-a-science-of-evals/)** — Apollo Research (Jan 2024), small-org blog post, not even a paper. Named the agenda; still the citation anchor for the field, and triggered substantive responses ([Hernández-Orallo's rebuttal](https://aievaluation.substack.com/p/a-response-to-we-need-a-science-of)) that mapped the intellectual terrain.
3. **[BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990)** — Reuel + 5 coauthors, Stanford (NeurIPS 2024 spotlight). A grad-student-led meta-evaluation: 46 criteria, 24 benchmarks scored, checklist + living website. Landed because it made "benchmark quality" measurable and public.
4. **[Measuring what Matters: Construct Validity in LLM Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04703)** — Oxford Internet Institute–led, NeurIPS 2025 D&B. Bigger team (distributed expert review of 445 benchmarks) but PhD-student-led; landed because it quantified the validity crisis (vague constructs, no statistics) and shipped an actionable checklist — got [CACM coverage](https://cacm.acm.org/news/measuring-what-matters-in-large-language-model-performance/).
5. **[Lessons from the Trenches on Reproducible Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782)** — EleutherAI collective (Biderman et al.). Landed because it documented, from maintaining lm-eval-harness for 3 years, exactly where eval numbers diverge (setup sensitivity, prompt formatting, scoring choices) — practitioner ground truth the position papers cite.
6. **[CONDA 2024 Shared Task report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21530)** — 23 contributors, community compilation: 566 contamination-evidence entries across 91 sources. Landed as infrastructure: a standing public database anyone can extend one PR at a time — the lowest-friction on-ramp in this whole space.
7. **[Evaluating Generative AI Systems Is a Social Science Measurement Challenge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00561)** — Wallach et al., ICML 2025 position paper. Landed because it imported a mature discipline's vocabulary (systematization, validity, reliability) and gave the "science of evals" crowd theoretical footing.
8. **[Black-Box Access is Insufficient for Rigorous AI Audits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14446)** — Casper et al., FAccT 2024, grad-student-led. Landed because it structured the auditor-access debate (black/white/outside-the-box) that now shapes third-party evaluation policy (e.g., CAISI–OpenMined privacy-preserving eval work ⚠️ per [secondary reporting](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/nists-caisi-openmined-ai-evaluation-privacy)).

## Where the gaps are

- **Statistics exist but aren't adopted.** Miller's formulas are two years old, yet the two big meta-reviews ([construct validity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04703), [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990)) both find most benchmarks report no significance tests or uncertainty. Gap: making error bars the *default output* of the standard harnesses (Inspect, lm-eval-harness) and auditing published leaderboards for statistical validity. (Whether the harnesses have since integrated these methods is unchecked — see unknowns.)
- **Regulators now require evals that barely exist.** The EU Code of Practice mandates evaluation of four systemic risks including **harmful manipulation** and **loss of control** ([CSET](https://cset.georgetown.edu/article/eu-ai-code-safety/)); CSET reads current practice as far below this bar — risk thresholds "almost universally undefined," under half of firms substantively test dangerous capabilities. Persuasion/manipulation measurement in particular has no AILuminate-style standard benchmark (inferred from the CoP requirement vs. the benchmark landscape read here).
- **Multi-turn and agentic safety evaluation is officially a hole.** AILuminate's own paper limits itself to single-turn, content-only hazards and names multiturn/multimodal/multilingual as future work ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05731)); MLCommons' agentic-reliability standard is still a "workstream" ([AILuminate site](https://mlcommons.org/ailuminate/)).
- **Harmonized-standards vacuum under the AI Act.** JTC 21 deliverables slipped from Apr 2025 toward Q4 2026 ([CEN-CENELEC](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2025/brief-news/2025-10-23-ai-standardization/)), leaving providers ~6–8 months to implement whatever finally publishes ([analysis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13600834.2025.2570966)). Anything that translates eval methodology into standard-ready test procedures lands in a vacuum right now.
- **Contamination detection for closed models is weak.** The [Jilin survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14425v1)'s black-box methods are the least developed tier, and there is no reporting *requirement* anywhere in the standards layer connecting contamination evidence to benchmark score claims (inferred — no such requirement surfaced in NIST/EU/MLCommons materials read here).
- **Saturation/lifecycle science is brand new.** The systematic saturation studies only appeared in late 2025–2026 ([plateau study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16763), [Benchmark Health Index](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11674)) — measurement of benchmark decay, discrimination power over time, and retirement criteria is wide open and data-analysis-shaped.
- **Real-world/field validity.** ARIA's pilot ([NIST AI 700-2](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.700-2.pdf)) is the first government attempt at field-testing with real users; Weidinger et al.'s first lesson (metrics ↔ real-world performance) remains mostly unmet by benchmark practice.
- **Infrastructure fragility.** HELM went into maintenance mode (June 2026, [README](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm)); OpenAI's Evals platform is shutting down (Nov 2026). De-facto standardization is consolidating on Inspect + community repos — sustainability and governance of shared eval infrastructure is itself an unsolved standards question.
- **Ecosystem fragmentation / no metadata standard.** EvalEval's [Every Eval Ever shared task](https://evalevalai.com/events/shared-task-every-eval-ever/) and [Evaluation Cards](https://huggingface.co/blog/evaleval/evaluation-cards-launch) exist precisely because eval results are scattered, undocumented, and non-comparable — the schema work is early and open to contributors.

## Fit notes

The researcher's published work (LLM-as-a-judge for RAG hallucination detection) is itself evaluation-methodology research; judge validity, agreement statistics, and uncertainty quantification map directly onto the field's documented gaps (evaluator uncertainty is a named AILuminate limitation; absence of statistics is the top finding of both meta-reviews). The compute profile of this space is laptop + API: the exemplar works above are literature reviews, statistical re-analyses, checklists, community databases, and single-author statistics papers — none required training runs. Concrete open on-ramps that exist today: EvalEval working groups and shared tasks, CONDA's PR-based evidence database, Inspect Evals contributions, MLCommons' AI Risk & Reliability working group, and national-body routes into SC 42/JTC 21. XGBoost/classical-ML and pipeline skills transfer directly to the newest sub-niche (benchmark saturation/health meta-analysis), which is currently done with exactly those tools.
