# Problem 07 — Bias & Fairness: Model Bias Measurement, Deployed-System Audits, and the Compliance Layer

*Brief date: July 2026. Grounding: internal map doc [`fairness-bias-and-sociotechnical-ai.md`](../../ai-research-map/13-fairness-ethics-and-human-factors/fairness-bias-and-sociotechnical-ai.md) (verified against primary sources), plus fresh web research. Claims marked "map" are read from the verified internal doc; everything else links to a page opened during this research. ⚠️ = unverified or inferred.*

## The problem, concretely

AI now sorts résumés, prices loans, allocates clinical care, and flags welfare recipients — and biased systems deny opportunities at population scale, quietly:

- **Hiring, at legal-system scale.** In *Mobley v. Workday*, a federal judge granted conditional certification (May 16, 2025) of a **nationwide ADEA collective action** over Workday's AI applicant-screening; Workday represented that **1.1 billion applications were rejected** through its tools in the relevant period, ~14,000 people opted in by the March 2026 deadline, and the court rejected Workday's motion to dismiss on March 6, 2026 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/federal-court-allows-collective-action-lawsuit-over-alleged), [Clearinghouse case page](https://clearinghouse.net/case/44074/), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sheilacallaham/2026/05/29/a-federal-judge-a-1967-law-and-a-billion-rejected-job-applications/)).
- **Healthcare.** A widely used care-management algorithm used **cost as a proxy for need**; because less is spent on Black patients at equal sickness, the bias cut the number of Black patients flagged for extra care **by more than half**. Fixing the label removed the bias ([Obermeyer et al., *Science* 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aax2342)).
- **Welfare.** Rotterdam's fraud-risk model (a gradient-boosting machine built by Accenture, trained on 12,707 past investigations) scored **young mothers and poor Dutch speakers as high-risk**; thousands were investigated and hundreds lost benefits ([Lighthouse Reports, "Suspicion Machines"](https://www.lighthousereports.com/investigation/suspicion-machines/), [methodology](https://www.lighthousereports.com/suspicion-machines-methodology/)).
- **Lending.** Risk-equivalent minority mortgage borrowers paid **+7.9 basis points (~$450M/yr)**; fintech algorithms discriminated less than face-to-face lenders in pricing and not at all in approval — algorithmic decisioning can *reduce* bias relative to humans, which is part of why measurement matters ([Bartlett, Morse, Stanton & Wallace, JFE 2022 / NBER w25943](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25943)).
- **Model-level bias hides under alignment.** Leading LLMs make harsher decisions about African American English speakers (jobs, criminal sentencing) even when overt race prompts look positive; RLHF and scale **mask overt bias but leave covert dialect bias intact** (map; [Hofmann et al., *Nature* 633:147, 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07856-5)). Assigning innocuous demographic **personas** silently degrades reasoning: 80% of tested personas show bias, with drops of 70%+ on some datasets ([Gupta et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04892)).

**Measurable sub-problems:** (1) covert/dialect/persona bias in LLMs that survives safety training; (2) outcome bias in deployed screeners (hiring, lending, clinical, welfare); (3) **fairness drift** after deployment — a VA surgical-outcomes study (2013–2023) shows gaps emerge over time and population-level retraining can *worsen* subgroup disparities (map; [JAMIA 2025](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/32/5/845/8074959)); (4) **the measurement protocol itself injecting bias** — side-by-side comparison amplifies covert dialect bias ~51.8% vs. independent rating (map; [Kondapally et al., FAccT '26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24384)), and LLM judges flip >50% of verdicts under position perturbation (map; [CALM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736)); (5) **compliance verification** — of 391 NYC employers checked, only 18 posted the legally required bias audit ([Wright et al., FAccT 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01399)); (6) **outcome homogenization** — shared components make the *same individuals* get rejected everywhere (map; [Bommasani et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13972)).

## The research field around it

Organized around **ACM FAccT** (next: [Montréal, June 25–28, 2026](https://facctconference.org/2026/index.html)) and **AIES**; heavily interdisciplinary (CS + law + social science). Sub-areas and maturity:

| Sub-area | State |
|---|---|
| **Benchmark-based NLP bias measurement** (WEAT-descendants, stereotype benchmarks) | Mature but *internally discredited in part*: the field's own critiques show benchmarks lack construct validity ([Blodgett et al., ACL 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.81/)), intrinsic metrics don't predict downstream bias ([Goldfarb-Tarrant et al., ACL 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.150/)), and decontextualized "trick tests" match realistic-use evals only at **chance level** ([Lum, Anthis, Nagpal & D'Amour, ACL 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12649)) |
| **Correspondence-style audits of deployed/consumer systems** (name-swap résumés, matched-guise dialect pairs) | Active and productive; small teams; e.g. [Wilson & Caliskan, AIES 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20371) (85.1% White-name preference in LM résumé retrieval), [Webster 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11548) |
| **Formal fairness metrics & impossibility** | Theoretically mature; separation vs. sufficiency impossibility makes metric choice normative; "substantive fairness" proposed as the escape (map; [Green 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.04642)) |
| **Less-discriminatory-alternatives (LDA) / model multiplicity** | Emerging legal-technical hybrid: equally accurate models with less disparate impact almost always exist, and firms arguably have a **duty to search** for them ([Black, Koepke, Kim, Barocas & Hsu](https://openscholarship.wustl.edu/law_scholarship/946/); [legal-duty companion](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06817v1); statistical-guarantee methods just appearing — [arXiv:2512.23943](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23943) ⚠️ abstract-level only) |
| **Sociotechnical evaluation frameworks** | Framework-rich, measurement-poor: three-layer (capability → interaction → systemic) framing with layers 2–3 mostly unmeasured (map; [Weidinger et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11986)) |
| **Audit-ecosystem & compliance studies** | Young; studies of the auditors and laws themselves: [Raji et al., "Outsider Oversight," AIES 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04737); [Birhane et al., "AI auditing: The Broken Bus," 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14462); [Null Compliance, FAccT 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01399) |
| **Clinical fairness drift / continuous monitoring** | Promising but unproven (map; [JAMIA 2025](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/32/5/845/8074959)) |

**The compliance layer (as of July 2026)** is a moving target: **NYC LL144** mandates annual independent bias audits + public posting for automated employment decision tools (map; [NYC DCWP](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/automated-employment-decision-tools.page)). **Colorado**'s SB 24-205 was delayed to June 30, 2026, then **replaced** by SB 26-189 (signed May 14, 2026; effective Jan 1, 2027), which drops the duty-of-care/impact-assessment model for a disclosure/transparency model ([Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/colorado-enacts-artificial-intelligence-replacement-law.html), [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/colorados-ai-law-delayed-until-june-2026-what-the-latest-setback-means-for-businesses/)). **EU AI Act**: high-risk Annex III obligations (employment, credit scoring) were due Aug 2, 2026, but the **Digital Omnibus** agreement would push them to Dec 2, 2027 — pending formal adoption, Aug 2, 2026 remains an active date ([Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/eu-agrees-to-delay-key-ai-act-compliance-deadlines/), [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/), [official timeline](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/)). **EEOC** removed its AI technical-assistance guidance on Jan 27, 2025, though Title VII/ADA/ADEA still apply and its FY2024–28 enforcement plan keeps algorithmic discrimination as a priority ([K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/The-Changing-Landscape-of-AI-Federal-Guidance-for-Employers-Reverses-Course-with-New-Administration-1-31-2025), [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-02-21-gone-but-not-forgotten-federal-laws-still-apply-despite-guidance-disappearance-act)). **Illinois HB 3773** (effective Jan 1, 2026) prohibits discriminatory AI use in employment but mandates no statistical audit (map).

## Who does this work

- **Academic groups** — Univ. of Washington (Caliskan & students: résumé-retrieval audits), Stanford (Jurafsky/Bommasani: dialect bias, homogenization; Ho's RegLab co-authored Outsider Oversight), UC Berkeley (Deborah Raji: audit ecosystems), Chicago Booth / UC Berkeley (Obermeyer, Mullainathan: clinical + lending), WashU Law (Pauline Kim: LDA doctrine), Cornell **Citizens and Technology Lab** (crowdsourced LL144 compliance study with 155 student investigators — [CAT Lab](https://citizensandtech.org/research/2024-algorithm-transparency-law/)). Funding: standard academia + foundation grants.
- **Nonprofit research institutes** — **DAIR** (Timnit Gebru; launched Dec 2021 with **$3.7M from Ford, MacArthur, Kapor, Open Society, Rockefeller**; Gebru has said two-year foundation grants make the model precarious — [MacArthur](https://www.macfound.org/press/grantee-news/new-institute-to-focus-on-community-rooted-ai-research), [UC Berkeley CDSS](https://cdss.berkeley.edu/news/dair-creating-new-incentive-structure-ai-research)); **Ada Lovelace Institute** (UK; "technical methods for regulatory inspection," first healthcare algorithmic impact assessment for the NHS AI Lab — [report](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/report/algorithmic-accountability-public-sector/), [Examining the Black Box](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/report/examining-the-black-box-tools-for-assessing-algorithmic-systems/)); Algorithmic Justice League (Buolamwini, grew out of Gender Shades ⚠️ current funding/activity not verified this session).
- **Investigative journalism as auditing** — **Lighthouse Reports** (+WIRED, Pulitzer Center support) obtained Rotterdam's actual model and ran a peer-reviewed audit, publishing [code and data on GitHub](https://github.com/Lighthouse-Reports/suspicion_machine); The Markup does adjacent work ([example](https://themarkup.org/hello-world/2023/04/01/it-takes-a-small-miracle-to-learn-basic-facts-about-government-algorithms)). ProPublica's COMPAS investigation is the genre's origin ⚠️ well known but not re-verified this session.
- **Commercial audit firms (LL144/EU-driven market)** — **BABL AI** (independent third-party audits + an auditor certification program — [babl.ai](https://babl.ai/)), **ORCAA** (Cathy O'Neil; bias + regulatory compliance), **Eticas** (functionality + unfair-practice detection); documented clients include HireVue, Airbnb, the states of Illinois and Colorado, and the cities of Barcelona and Amsterdam (per [Birhane et al.'s survey of the audit ecosystem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.14462)).
- **Agencies** — NYC DCWP (LL144 enforcement), Colorado AG (delayed enforcement — [Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/colorado-attorney-general-delays-enforcement-of-colorado-ai-act/)), EU AI Office, EEOC (enforcement priority retained despite guidance removal).
- **How outsiders contribute** — FAccT/AIES accept correspondence audits needing **no inside access** (public APIs + synthetic résumés/tweets); CAT Lab's citizen-science model recruits volunteers; Lighthouse publishes replicable methods; BABL sells auditor certification; several canonical works below were 1–2 authors with no lab.

## Published exemplars — small-team/solo works that moved the field

1. **Gender Shades** — Buolamwini & Gebru, FAT* 2018 (**2 authors**; a grad student + one researcher). Intersectional error-rate audit of 3 commercial gender classifiers: darker-skinned women misclassified up to **34.7%** vs. **0.8%** for lighter-skinned men. Invented the intersectional-benchmark audit template and forced vendor responses ([MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/gender-shades-intersectional-accuracy-disparities-in-commercial-gender-classification/), [IBM's response](http://gendershades.org/docs/ibm.pdf)).
2. **Dissecting racial bias in a health algorithm** — Obermeyer, Powers, Vogeli & Mullainathan, *Science* 2019 (**4 authors**). Showed the bias mechanism is the *label* (cost as proxy for need), not the model — the single most-cited demonstration that proxy choice is where deployed bias lives ([Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aax2342)).
3. **Dialect prejudice predicts AI decisions** — Hofmann, Kalluri, Jurafsky & King, *Nature* 2024 (**4 authors**). Matched-guise probing found covert AAE bias more negative than any recorded human stereotype since the 1930s; RLHF masks only the surface (map; [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07856-5)).
4. **Résumé retrieval bias** — Wilson & Caliskan, AIES 2024 (**2 authors**). 550+ résumés × 9 occupations; White-associated names preferred **85.1%** of the time, Black men worst-off intersectionally; code public ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20371), [GitHub](https://github.com/kyrawilson/Resume-Screening-Bias), [Brookings summary](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/gender-race-and-intersectional-bias-in-ai-resume-screening-via-language-model-retrieval/)).
5. **"Fairness is Not Enough" / Illusion of Neutrality** — Webster 2025 (**solo author**). Audited 8 consumer AI platforms with fictitious résumés and showed apparent non-bias can be *incompetence* (models scoring keyword-stuffed junk at ω²≈0.99) — bias audits must include competence checks (map; [arXiv:2507.11548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11548)).
6. **Null Compliance** — Wright et al., FAccT 2024 (CAT Lab + 155 volunteer investigators). Of 391 NYC employers, **18** posted the mandated audit; named the "null compliance" loophole (employer discretion over scope makes silence uninterpretable) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01399), [FAccT PDF](https://facctconference.org/static/papers24/facct24-113.pdf)).
7. **Suspicion Machines** — Lighthouse Reports + WIRED, 2023 (journalist-sized team). First outside audit of a *live government welfare model* using the actual code/data; found ethnicity/gender discrimination and near-random accuracy; methods and model artifacts published ([investigation](https://www.lighthousereports.com/investigation/suspicion-machines/), [GitHub](https://github.com/Lighthouse-Reports/suspicion_machine)).
8. **Beyond Trick Tests (RUTEd)** — Lum, Anthis, Nagpal & D'Amour, ACL 2025 (**4 authors**). Decontextualized bias tests picked the "least biased" model no better than chance vs. realistic-use evaluations — a direct methodological indictment of the standard benchmark stack ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12649), [ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.7/)).

## Where the gaps are (named by the field itself)

- **Construct validity of bias benchmarks** — benchmarks "frequently lack clear articulations of what is being measured," conflate race/culture/nationality, and contain logical/grammatical faults ([Blodgett et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.81/)). Still not fixed for the LLM era.
- **Measurement doesn't transfer** — intrinsic embedding-bias metrics show no reliable correlation with application bias ([Goldfarb-Tarrant et al. 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.150/); a later re-analysis aligning constructs found a more nuanced picture — [arXiv:2409.09260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.09260)); trick tests ↔ realistic-use evals correspond at chance ([Lum et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12649)).
- **The protocol is a confounder** — side-by-side (ranking-like) evaluation *amplifies* covert dialect bias ~51.8% vs. absolute rating; overt bias persists after safety alignment (map; [Kondapally et al., FAccT '26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24384)). LLM judges themselves fail position/self-enhancement/verbosity robustness (map; [CALM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736)).
- **Bias vs. incompetence is unidentified** — an unbiased-looking screener may just be noise; audits rarely test discernment (map; [Webster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11548)).
- **The audit ecosystem lacks standards, access, and teeth** — audits are ad hoc, method-opaque, and unconnected to consequences ([Birhane et al., "Broken Bus"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14462)); audits without institutional design (accreditation, data access, complaint channels) won't produce accountability ([Raji et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04737)); LL144's scoping discretion produces null compliance ([Wright et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01399)); quantitative auditors report systematic data-access barriers ([Access Denied, arXiv:2502.00428](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00428) ⚠️ found via search, not read in full).
- **One-shot audits vs. drift** — fairness gaps emerge post-deployment and retraining can worsen them; continuous fairness-drift monitoring is proposed but immature (map; [JAMIA 2025](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/32/5/845/8074959)).
- **Group metrics miss individual systemic failure** — individual-level homogenization consistently exceeds group-level; the same people are rejected everywhere and group audits can't see it (map; [Bommasani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13972)).
- **Interaction/systemic layers unmeasured** — most evals stop at capability; context and multimodal gaps are explicit (map; [Weidinger et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11986)).
- **LDA search is doctrine ahead of method** — the legal argument for searching for less-discriminatory equivalent models is developed ([Black et al.](https://openscholarship.wustl.edu/law_scholarship/946/)), but practical, statistically-grounded search procedures are only now appearing ([arXiv:2512.23943](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23943) ⚠️ abstract-level).

## What a solo researcher could do here (study shapes, not recommendations)

1. **Protocol-sensitivity audit of a standard bias benchmark.** Re-run one established bias eval (e.g., persona or dialect) under absolute vs. pairwise vs. k-way ranking protocols across 4–6 frontier APIs; quantify protocol-induced effect-size variance. Direct extension of the contrastive-amplification finding ([Kondapally FAccT '26](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24384)) and CALM's judge-robustness method; API-only cost; pure eval-methodology work.
2. **Competence-adjusted bias auditing.** Generalize Webster's solo two-experiment design (bias test + discernment test with matched/mismatched/keyword-stuffed inputs) to a new domain (loan-application triage or clinical-note summarization); precedent shows one author can do this on consumer platforms ([Webster](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11548)).
3. **LL144 audit-report corpus study.** Scrape/collect the audit reports that *were* posted (Null Compliance found 18 among 391 employers; more exist by 2026), and analyze metric choices, impact-ratio distributions, auditor identity, and scoping language — the compliance layer as data. Extends [Wright et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01399); data-pipeline skills, zero GPU.
4. **Fairness-drift replication on public tabular data.** Use ACS/folktables-style yearly data with XGBoost to test when subgroup gaps drift and whether population-level retraining worsens them, mirroring the VA clinical finding outside medicine (map; [JAMIA 2025](https://academic.oup.com/jamia/article/32/5/845/8074959)); also a natural home for an LDA/model-multiplicity search experiment ([Black et al.](https://openscholarship.wustl.edu/law_scholarship/946/)).
5. **LLM-judge fairness under demographic content.** Combine a published LLM-as-a-judge background with CALM-style perturbation testing: do judge models score identical content differently when author dialect/persona varies, and does judge choice change audit conclusions? Sits at the intersection of [CALM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736), [Hofmann et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07856-5), and [Gupta et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04892).
6. **RUTEd-style task-grounded eval for one deployed use case.** Build a realistic-use bias eval (e.g., résumé summarization or reference-letter drafting) and test whether trick-test rankings predict it — replicating/extending the chance-level correspondence result ([Lum et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12649)) on tasks it didn't cover.

## What we still don't know (program ledger)

- **Does mandated auditing change outcomes?** No causal evidence found that LL144 audits reduced hiring disparities; compliance itself is near-null ([Wright et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01399)). ⚠️ absence-of-evidence from this session's searches, not a verified negative.
- **Do mitigations generalize?** Counterfactual-fairness fine-tuning helps ~half of traits and doesn't generalize (map); prompt-level debiasing over-corrects non-monotonically (map).
- **Is the intrinsic/extrinsic disconnect real or a construct-alignment artifact?** The re-analysis ([arXiv:2409.09260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.09260)) complicates the original result. ⚠️ read at abstract level.
- **Final regulatory targets** — EU omnibus deferral (Dec 2, 2027 for employment/credit) awaits formal adoption; Colorado's SB 26-189 requirements (eff. Jan 1, 2027) are disclosure-focused but untested.
- **Auditor quality** — no public evaluation of whether commercial LL144 auditors' methods detect known-biased systems was found (inferred gap; consistent with the "Broken Bus" critique).
- **Scale of covert bias across languages/dialects beyond AAE** — the covert-bias result is AAE-centric; ⚠️ cross-dialect generality unverified.
- **Whether *Mobley* produces the first merits ruling on AI-vendor disparate-impact liability** — case ongoing as of mid-2026.

## Fit notes

The field's most-cited open wounds are *evaluation-methodology* wounds — construct validity, protocol confounds, judge bias, benchmark–reality gaps — which map directly onto a profile with a published LLM-as-a-judge study on hallucination detection. Canonical works here were 1–4 authors (Gender Shades: 2; Wilson & Caliskan: 2; Webster: solo), and correspondence-style audits need only public APIs and synthetic inputs, fitting a laptop + ≤$500/study budget. The deployed-system side (Rotterdam's model was a GBM; drift studies and LDA searches run on tabular data) matches XGBoost/classical-ML and data-pipeline strengths. The compliance layer (LL144 reports, EU/Colorado timelines) is analyzable as public-document corpora with no institutional access required.
