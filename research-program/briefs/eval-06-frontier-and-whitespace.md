# The Frontier and Whitespace of Evaluation Research (mid-2026)

**What this brief is:** a map of what the evaluation field's own leaders say is *missing* — mined from position papers, lab posts, coalition agendas, and community discourse, each gap grounded in a citable source complaining about it. Compiled 2026-07-08. Everything cited was opened during research; secondary-source-only items are flagged ⚠️.

---

## The shape of this space

"Eval research" in 2026 is no longer "build a benchmark." The field's self-critique literature has converged on the framing that evaluation must become a **measurement science**, and the work now splits into recognizable sub-areas:

1. **Science of evals / measurement theory** — construct validity (does the test measure the concept it claims to), reliability (same test, same result), statistical rigor, and predictive validity (do scores forecast real behavior). Anchored by Apollo Research's ["We Need a Science of Evals"](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/science/we-need-a-science-of-evals/) (Jan 2024; argues evals drive high-stakes decisions yet lack the basics of a science — replicability, error bars, coverage) and Weidinger et al.'s ["Toward an Evaluation Science for Generative AI Systems"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05336) (2025, DeepMind + Raji, Wallach, Mitchell, Koyejo, Bommasani et al.; argues static benchmarks "face validity challenges" and case-by-case audits "rarely scale," drawing on aviation/pharma safety-testing history).
2. **Meta-evaluation (auditing the benchmarks themselves)** — [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990) (scored 24 benchmarks against 46 best-practice criteria; MMLU scored worst), the Oxford-led ["Measuring what Matters"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04703) (29 reviewers systematically reviewed 445 benchmarks for construct-validity failures — tests measuring something other than what they claim; NeurIPS 2025), and the [Agentic Benchmark Checklist](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825) (a checklist of validity requirements for agent benchmarks).
3. **Agent evaluation** — testing models that take multi-step actions (tools, browsing, code): long-horizon tasks ([METR's time-horizon metric](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/); how long a task a model can complete), cost-aware evaluation ([AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502); scoring accuracy against dollars spent), failure diagnosis ([HORIZON](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11978)), and safety/safeguard evals for agents ([UK AISI agenda](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/research-agenda)).
4. **Safety & dangerous-capability evals** — testing for high-harm capabilities: lab evals (DeepMind's [dangerous-capabilities program](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13793)), scheming/situational-awareness evals (Apollo; whether a model deceives or knows it is being tested), and the elicitation problem — how hard you tried to make the model perform (["The Evals Gap"](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/gJJEjJpKiddoYGZKk/the-evals-gap)).
5. **Evaluation infrastructure & economics** — harnesses (the software that runs models against benchmarks: EleutherAI's lm-eval, ["Lessons from the Trenches"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782)), shared artifact schemas — common formats for storing eval results ([Every Eval Ever](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14516)), and cost analysis ([EvalEval: "AI evals are becoming the new compute bottleneck"](https://evalevalai.com/research/2026/04/29/eval-costs-bottleneck/)).
6. **Ecosystem & access politics** — third-party testing regimes — independent outside evaluators of frontier models ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/third-party-testing)), legal safe harbors for independent evaluators — protection from terms-of-service enforcement ([Longpre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04893)), leaderboard governance ([The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879)), and funder conflicts ([FrontierMath/OpenAI](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/)).
7. **Deployment/practitioner evals** — real-work benchmarks — tasks from actual jobs rather than exams ([GDPval](https://openai.com/index/gdpval/)), vertical expert-rubric benchmarks — domain tests graded on practitioner-written rubrics ([Vals AI](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks)), and the applied evals discourse (error analysis + LLM-judge validation — using one model to grade another and checking that grader against humans: [Hamel Husain](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/), [Who Validates the Validators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272)).

The through-line in every leader statement: **scores are cheap, valid inferences are not.** The [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026) (Bengio-chaired; the flagship multi-government synthesis on AI risk) now names an explicit "evaluation gap": *pre-deployment test results do not reliably predict real-world performance*, and models increasingly distinguish test settings from deployment.

## Published exemplars (skewed toward small teams)

1. **[Adding Error Bars to Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)** — Evan Miller, **solo author** (Anthropic, Nov 2024; also a [blog post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals)). Applies clustered standard errors (honest error bars when questions come in correlated groups), power analysis (how many questions you need to see a real difference), and paired-difference inference (compare two models on the *same* items to cancel question-difficulty noise) to evals; shows naive SEs (standard errors, the ± on a score) can be >3× too small. Landed because nobody had written down the basic statistics of the field's core activity. Proof that a single person with stats training can produce a field-reference paper.
2. **[AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502)** — Kapoor, Stroebl et al., ~5-person Princeton team (NeurIPS 2024). Named four failures of agent benchmarking (cost-blind accuracy, conflated developer needs, missing holdouts, irreproducibility). Became the standard citation for "agent evals are broken" and spawned the Holistic Agent Leaderboard.
3. **[Who Validates the Validators?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272)** — Shankar, Zamfirescu-Pereira, Hartmann, Parameswaran, Arawjo (Berkeley/Montréal, UIST 2024, 5 authors). Discovered **criteria drift**: humans can't fix judge criteria before seeing outputs, undermining any static LLM-as-judge pipeline. Small HCI study, outsized influence on industry eval tooling (EvalGen concepts now in vendor products).
4. **[BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990)** — Reuel + 5 coauthors (Stanford, NeurIPS 2024 spotlight). Scored 24 benchmarks against 46 lifecycle practices (design→documentation→maintenance); MMLU worst-in-class; most benchmarks report no statistical significance. A checklist + [living repository](https://betterbench.stanford.edu/) — a maintainable-by-few artifact.
5. **[Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499)** — METR (Mar 2025). Invented the 50%-task-completion **time horizon** (the task length a model finishes half the time); "doubling every ~7 months" became the most-quoted capability trend of 2025. Shows a *metric construction* contribution (inventing a new way to measure) can beat a benchmark contribution; follow-up on [domain variation](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-14-how-does-time-horizon-vary-across-domains/) admits generalization limits.
6. **[The Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879)** — Singh et al. (Cohere Labs + AI2/Princeton/Stanford/UW, Apr 2025). Documented private-variant testing (labs quietly testing many model versions and reporting only the best), unequal sampling, and overfitting on Chatbot Arena (112% ArenaHard win-rate gain with no MMLU gain — i.e. gaming the leaderboard without real capability gain). Forced a [public response from LMArena](https://arena.ai/blog/our-response/) and changed disclosure norms; see [Simon Willison's digest](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Apr/30/criticism-of-the-chatbot-arena/) for the discourse.
7. **[The SWE-Bench Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286)** — Microsoft Research (NeurIPS 2025). Two cheap diagnostic probes showed SOTA (state-of-the-art) models "solve" SWE-Bench (a software-bug-fixing benchmark) partly by memorization (76% buggy-file identification from issue text alone on SWE-Bench repos vs 53% elsewhere — the model recognizes the repo, it hasn't reasoned). Exemplar of low-compute **benchmark forensics** — auditing a flagship benchmark without training anything.
8. **[Establishing Best Practices for Building Rigorous Agentic Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825)** — Zhu et al. (2025). Audited 10 agent benchmarks: task-validity (is the task actually doable/well-posed) and outcome-validity (does the grader truly detect success) violations cause up to ~100% relative mis-estimation (SWE-bench Verified's weak tests; τ-bench crediting empty responses). Produced the ABC (Agentic Benchmark Checklist) now cited by benchmark builders.

## Where the gaps are (named, with the source that complains)

**A. Measurement science**
1. **No consensus methods or language; immature measurement protocols.** UK AISI's own [research agenda](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/research-agenda) says evals suffer "a lack of consensus around methods and language, immature measurement tools and protocols." Apollo's [Science of Evals](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/science/we-need-a-science-of-evals/) says current evals "are not yet resistant to scrutiny."
2. **Construct validity failures are the norm, not the exception** (construct validity = the test measures the concept it claims to, not something incidental). [Measuring what Matters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04703) (445 benchmarks, 29 reviewers) finds systemic patterns in phenomena/tasks/metrics that "undermine the validity of resulting claims"; the [medical-benchmark position paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10694) shows licensing-exam benchmarks fail construct validity against real clinical data (passing the exam ≠ handling real patients).
3. **Statistical rigor absent.** [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990): most benchmarks report no significance and aren't replicable. [Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640): clustered SEs (grouped-data error bars) 3× naive. **And the fix is off by default even where implemented** (repo-verified, deepening pass 2): UK AISI's `inspect_ai` defaults `stderr()` to an iid CLT SE and offers Miller-style clustered SEs only opt-in via a `cluster=` argument; EleutherAI's `lm-evaluation-harness` ships iid `mean_stderr` + item-level `bootstrap_stderr` with **no clustered-SE option at all**. The International Network consensus does ask evaluators to "include uncertainty estimates" (practice 7) but names no method — leaving the *default-behavior* gap wide open. UK AISI also wants "statistical frameworks for low-data regimes" (rigor when you only have a handful of test items) — still open.
4. **Predictive validity untested** (predictive validity = do eval scores forecast real downstream behavior). Apollo's own [research note](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/research-note-our-scheming-precursor-evals-had-limited-predictive-power-for-our-in-context-scheming-evals/) concedes their scheming *precursor* evals (early-warning tests meant to foreshadow deception) "had limited predictive power" for the actual scheming evals — i.e., even the flagship safety-eval shop can't yet predict downstream behavior from upstream measurements. UK AISI names "capability prediction models" (forecasting what a model can do before you fully test it) as open.
5. **Eval incentives shape model pathologies.** OpenAI's [Why Language Models Hallucinate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04664): binary 0-1 grading structurally rewards guessing over calibrated uncertainty; "mainstream benchmarks" need re-scoring to reward IDK ("I don't know" answers). Almost no benchmarks have adopted this yet (⚠️ adoption level is my inference from reading, not a measured fact).

**B. Real-world / external validity**
6. **The "evaluation gap": scores don't predict deployment.** The [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026) states results from pre-deployment tests "do not reliably predict real-world performance"; its 2025 edition called existing safety evals "spot checks." [Weidinger et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05336) lesson #1: metrics must connect to real-world performance.
7. **Real-work benchmarks are tiny and lab-owned.** [GDPval](https://openai.com/index/gdpval/) (44 occupations, tasks graded on expert deliverables from real jobs) is the flagship attempt, but only a 220-task gold subset (the human-verified reference answers) is public and grading runs through OpenAI's own service — external-validity measurement is itself concentrated in a frontier lab.
8. **Vertical/regulated-domain evals show huge headroom and almost no independent coverage.** Vals AI's [Finance Agent Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.00828): best model 46.8% at $3.79/query; their [Legal Research Bench](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks) leader is at ~44% all-pass (passes every graded criterion). Practitioner-rubric private benchmarks (domain-expert-written, held secret to avoid contamination) exist for a handful of verticals only.
9. **Research-practice gap.** The [EvalEval ACL 2026 workshop](https://evalevalai.com/events/2026-acl-workshop/) names the tensions outright: "speed vs. rigor, benchmarks vs. deployment needs, generality vs. task-specificity," and a persistent "gap between research and practice." Practitioner discourse ([Hamel Husain](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/), [LLM-judge guide](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/)) runs on error analysis + judge-human agreement (checking that a model-grader agrees with human raters), largely disconnected from academic benchmark literature. [Criteria drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272) — graders change their standards once they see outputs — means static judge rubrics are theoretically unsound, and no one has a settled fix.

**C. Agents**
10. **Agent benchmark rigor.** [ABC audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825): task/outcome-validity violations mis-estimate performance by up to 100% relative. [AI Agents That Matter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502): no holdouts (hidden test items to catch overfitting), cost ignored.
11. **Long-horizon failure modes uncharacterized** (long-horizon = tasks needing many sequential steps). [HORIZON](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11978): "long-horizon failures remain poorly characterized, hindering principled diagnosis"; METR notes [time horizons vary by domain](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-14-how-does-time-horizon-vary-across-domains/) and its metric rests on ~170 timed tasks — coverage is thin.
12. **Agent reliability under repetition.** [EvalEval cost post](https://evalevalai.com/research/2026/04/29/eval-costs-bottleneck/): 60% single-run → 25% under 8-run consistency (must succeed on all 8 attempts); almost no benchmarks report multi-run reliability.
13. **Agent safety/safeguard evals in realistic environments.** [UK AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/research-agenda): agents comply with malicious requests without jailbreaks (adversarial prompts that bypass safety training); "simple universal jailbreak templates can be adapted to effectively jailbreak agents"; realistic safeguard-evaluation environments are named as a build-target, i.e., missing.

**D. Ecosystem / access**
14. **Eval cost is the new access barrier.** [EvalEval](https://evalevalai.com/research/2026/04/29/eval-costs-bottleneck/): $40k for one agent-leaderboard run; single GAIA runs (GAIA is a general-assistant agent benchmark) to $2,829; "academic groups, AI Safety Institutes, and journalists now hit the budget constraint before the technical one."
15. **No legal safe harbor for independent testing** (safe harbor = a legal guarantee that good-faith evaluators won't be sued or banned). [Longpre et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04893) (+ [Knight Institute essay](https://knightcolumbia.org/blog/a-safe-harbor-for-ai-evaluation-and-red-teaming)): ToS (terms-of-service) enforcement chills good-faith research; researcher-access programs "lack independence from corporate incentives." Anthropic's [third-party-testing post](https://www.anthropic.com/news/third-party-testing) concedes the needed regime (trusted tests + trusted administrators) doesn't exist yet. ⚠️ Secondary reporting ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-ceo-calls-for-faa-style-regulation-of-powerful-ai-models-what-enterprises-should-know)) says Amodei's June 2026 essay escalates this to *mandatory* third-party testing — I did not read the essay itself.
16. **Benchmark-funder conflicts of interest.** [FrontierMath/OpenAI disclosure failure](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/) — Epoch took OpenAI funding for FrontierMath and OpenAI had visibility into most problems/solutions; Epoch retained a **separate holdout set** + a **verbal no-train agreement**, but the funding was only disclosed ~**Dec 20 2024** at o3's launch, with many contributing mathematicians unaware until then (primary-verified, deepening pass 2; Besiroglu: "We made a mistake in not being more transparent") ([community lessons](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8ZgLYwBmB3vLavjKE/some-lessons-from-the-openai-frontiermath-debacle)). The credibility hit compounded when Epoch later [corrected errors in 42% of problems (June 12 2026)](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2). [Leaderboard Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20879) shows leaderboard operators can structurally favor big providers. Governance norms for eval independence are unwritten — and the [International Network's Feb-2026 consensus](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/international-ai-network-consensus-and-open-questions) leaves funder-independence/disclosure *out* of its 8 practices entirely (verified pass 2), so this remains genuine whitespace.
17. **Results don't accumulate.** [Every Eval Ever](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14516) (48 authors): results "scattered across leaderboards, papers, blog posts, harness logs" in 31 incompatible formats; frameworks give divergent scores for nominally identical evals.
18. **Evaluation awareness breaks the whole paradigm** (evaluation awareness = the model detects it is being tested and behaves differently). [IAISR 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026): models increasingly distinguish tests from deployment and "exploit loopholes in evaluations"; see also [IAPS on evaluation awareness](https://www.iaps.ai/research/evaluation-awareness-why-frontier-ai-models-are-getting-harder-to-test) and Apollo/OpenAI collaboration findings. Also **elicitation upper bounds** (how do you prove you got the model's *best* effort, so a low score isn't just under-testing): ["The Evals Gap"](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/gJJEjJpKiddoYGZKk/the-evals-gap) — "we neither have a principled way to quantify 'how hard we tried' nor a way to upper-bound the model's capabilities."
19. **Contamination forensics under-supplied** (contamination = test items leaked into training data, so a high score is memorization not skill). [SWE-Bench Illusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286) shows a top-3 benchmark partially measures memorization; nobody systematically audits the others.
20. **Sociotechnical impacts underreported.** [EvalEval workshop](https://evalevalai.com/events/2026-acl-workshop/): developers "underreport or deprioritize key impacts such as environmental costs, data provenance (where the training/test data came from), and labor practices."

## Fit notes

The researcher's published LLM-as-a-judge work on RAG (retrieval-augmented generation — grounding model answers in fetched documents) hallucination detection sits exactly on gaps 5, 9, and B-generally: judge validation (criteria drift is unresolved), deployment-vs-benchmark divergence, and incentive-aware scoring. The most-cited exemplars here are solo or ≤6-author works requiring statistics, careful data handling, and API access rather than training compute — Miller's error-bars paper, the SWE-Bench Illusion probes, and BetterBench-style audits are all executable within a laptop + ≤$500 API budget; the EvalEval cost post explicitly frames cheap-but-rigorous evaluation as a field-level need. EvalEval's shared tasks and the ACL 2026 workshop provide open, co-authorship-granting entry points; METR lists a research-collaborators track and Apollo hires evals scientists. XGBoost/classical-ML skills map directly onto the open "capability prediction from model attributes and historical data" problem named in the UK AISI agenda.

**RESOLVED**
- **FrontierMath/OpenAI conflict (gap #16) — primary-verified**, upgraded from "took lab money without disclosing it" to the precise account: OpenAI funded it + had visibility into most problems/solutions; Epoch kept a separate holdout + verbal no-train agreement; disclosed ~Dec 20 2024 at o3 launch with contributors largely unaware; and Epoch later corrected **42% of problems** (Jun 12 2026). Sources: [TechCrunch (Jan 2025)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai/), [Epoch frontiermath-tier-4-v2](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2).
