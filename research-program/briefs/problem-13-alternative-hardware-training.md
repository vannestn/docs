## The problem, concretely

Almost every load-bearing assumption in the 2026 AI economy — trillion-dollar lab valuations, the durability of NVIDIA's moat, the theory that US export controls throttle Chinese AI, and the compute-threshold logic behind regulation — rests on a belief about *hardware substitutability* (whether you can swap in non-NVIDIA chips without breaking the training): that frontier-scale training is hard to do on anything but NVIDIA GPUs (and the two proven exceptions, Google TPU and AWS Trainium — both custom AI chips built by the cloud provider itself). If that belief is wrong — if 1T+ parameter models can be reliably pretrained on domestic Chinese ASICs (application-specific chips, i.e. custom silicon for one job), AMD GPUs, or wafer-scale engines (a single giant chip the size of a whole silicon wafer) — several of those bets weaken at once. And here is the research gap: **the belief is being tested in public, but almost nobody is independently verifying the results.** Vendors announce full-stack non-NVIDIA training runs; the claims get repeated; no third party audits the cluster, the token count, or the stability record.

The concrete, measurable sub-problems:
1. **Which non-NVIDIA platforms have actually *completed* a frontier-scale (≳10²⁴–10²⁶ FLOP total compute, ≳100B-param) pretraining run — as opposed to announcing one, demoing inference (running a finished model), or "supporting" a model post-hoc?**
2. **Is the "training runs die late on alt hardware" folklore true?** What is the real evidence on loss-spike (sudden training-loss jumps) / rollback (reverting to an earlier checkpoint) rates as a function of silicon and numerics (the number-format arithmetic details)?
3. **Is a model's training hardware detectable after the fact** (numerical/behavioral provenance — telltale fingerprints in the weights or outputs)? If yes, vendor hardware claims become auditable.
4. **What efficiency (MFU — Model FLOPs Utilization, the fraction of a chip's peak compute actually used) penalty does alternative silicon actually pay**, and is it closing?

## The landscape: who has actually trained at scale off NVIDIA

Ordered by strength of evidence — from *proven, independently corroborated* to *marketing*.

### Tier 1 — proven at the absolute frontier (independently corroborated)
- **Google TPU** (Tensor Processing Unit — Google's in-house AI accelerator, in production since ~2016) — the giant existing proof. Gemini 3 "was trained entirely on TPUs" and tops LMArena (a crowd-voted model leaderboard); Anthropic's Claude and Google's Gemini "have the majority of their training and inference infrastructure on Google's TPUs and Amazon's Trainium" ([SemiAnalysis, read](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/tpuv7-google-takes-a-swing-at-the) — a chip-industry analysis newsletter that calls TPU "neck and neck with … Nvidia"). Ironwood (TPU v7): 4,614 TFLOPs/chip (trillion floating-point ops/sec), 192 GB HBM (high-bandwidth memory stacked on-package), 42.5 Exaflops at 9,216-chip pod scale (one interconnected training unit) ([Google, read](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/ironwood-tpu-age-of-inference/)). This is the existence proof that frontier-scale training does *not* require NVIDIA.
- **AWS Trainium** (Amazon's in-house training accelerator) — Anthropic housed **~500,000 H100-equivalents in Trainium2** (measured in NVIDIA-H100 compute units) at Project Rainier by end-2025, and Amazon/Anthropic have built two frontier-scale data centers totaling **~1.3M Trainium2 chips** as of Jan 2026 ([Epoch, read](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/frontier-labs-dont-use-most-ai-compute) — Epoch AI is a nonprofit that tracks AI compute; this piece argues frontier labs run on a minority of total AI compute). Trainium is the second independently-corroborated non-NVIDIA frontier platform.

Epoch's framing: NVIDIA is "over 60% of total compute, with Google and Amazon making up much of the remainder" ([Epoch search snippet](https://epoch.ai/trends) ⚠️; the per-lab Trainium figures above are from the read page). So non-NVIDIA is already a **large minority of frontier compute** — but concentrated in two Western silicon lines, both vertically integrated (chip and cloud owned by the same company), both proven.

### Tier 2 — credible completed runs, single-source / lightly audited
- **Huawei Ascend (Pangu)** — the strongest *published* Chinese-hardware-only case. **Pangu Ultra MoE, 718B params, trained on ~6,000 Ascend NPUs** (Huawei's neural-processing-unit AI chips), with a real arXiv report ([2505.04519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04519) — what it did: documents a full 718B-param MoE training run on Ascend hardware; finding: **30.0% MFU** and 1.46M tokens/s on 6K NPUs, with DeepSeek-R1-class benchmark results) ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2025/05/10/huawei-introduces-pangu-ultra-moe-a-718b-parameter-sparse-language-model-trained-efficiently-on-ascend-npus-using-simulation-driven-architecture-and-system-level-optimization/)). Ascend is on China's government-approved procurement list while NVIDIA is excluded; 910C output targeting ~600K units in 2026 (⚠️ SemiAnalysis/search snippets). Export-control context: BIS (US Bureau of Industry and Security) guidance treats using Ascend 910B/C/D as an export-rule violation ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/department-of-commerce-formally-rescinds-ai-diffusion-rule-says-deploying-huawei-ascend-chips-violates-us-export-rules/) ⚠️ snippet). This is the cleanest counter-example to "China can't train at scale," and it *has* a methods paper — unlike LongCat-2.0.
- **MetaX (SpikingBrain)** — hundreds of **non-NVIDIA MetaX C550 GPUs** trained SpikingBrain-7B/76B, stable >2 weeks, **23.4% MFU vs 25.8% on an NVIDIA A800 cluster** (A800 = the export-throttled China version of the A100) — a real, small efficiency gap, in a real arXiv report ([2509.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276), internal map, verified — what it did: trained a spiking/brain-inspired LLM on Chinese MetaX GPUs and logged the stability run; finding: multi-week stable training at only a ~2-point MFU deficit vs NVIDIA). Sub-frontier scale but a rare *documented* stability record on Chinese silicon.

### Tier 3 — claimed frontier run, no independent methods paper
- **Meituan LongCat-2.0** — the trigger (above). Full 1.6T run claimed on unnamed domestic ASICs, **[vendor]-only, tech blog not arXiv, ASIC undisclosed.** Larger param count than Pangu but *weaker* evidence.

### Tier 4 — mostly inference, or "supports" not "trained"
- **Cambricon (MLU590/Siyuan)** — largely **inference**-targeted (A100-class performance); targeting 500K accelerator shipments in 2026 vs ~116K in 2025; customers ByteDance/Alibaba (⚠️ [Presenc](https://presenc.ai/research/chinese-ai-chips-landscape-2026), search snippets). No public frontier *training* run.
- **Baidu Kunlun** — runs a ~30,000-chip cluster training Qianfan-VL (Baidu's vision-language model) on domestic silicon (⚠️ search snippet [the-substrate](https://www.the-substrate.net/p/where-chinas-ai-chip-supply-chain)); Samsung reportedly halted supply (of the HBM memory) early 2025 under export tightening ⚠️.
- **Alibaba T-Head (PPU/Zhenwu)** — "several hundred thousand" units shipped ⚠️ (snippet); role in training its own Qwen models (Alibaba's open LLM family) unclear/undisclosed.
- **Moore Threads (MTT S5000)** — runs DeepSeek-V4 natively (~4,000 tok/s prefill — the input-processing phase; inference framing); "Day-0 adaptation of DeepSeek-V4 in 2026" (getting a just-released model running immediately) is a **deployment/support** claim, not a from-scratch training run (⚠️ snippets).
- **AMD Instinct (MI300X/MI350)** — real and growing on **MLPerf training** (the industry-standard training-speed benchmark suite): MI355X trained Llama-2-70B LoRA (a lightweight fine-tune, not full pretraining) in ~10 min; broad OEM (hardware-vendor) participation; Oracle 16,384-GPU MI300X superclusters ([AMD, search](https://www.amd.com/en/blogs/2025/accelerating-ai-training.html) ⚠️), and MI400 is pitched as rack-scale competitive with NVIDIA VR200 in H2 2026 — but **no publicly-disclosed frontier-flagship pretraining run** is yet attributable to AMD as the sole silicon. AMD is closest among Western challengers to Tier 1 for *training*, gated by ecosystem (software stack), not the chip.
- **Cerebras (WSE)** — wafer-scale (WSE = Wafer-Scale Engine, one chip spanning a full wafer); Cerebras-GPT (up to 13B) and customer runs are real and completed ([Cerebras](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/cerebras-gpt-a-family-of-open-compute-efficient-large-language-models) ⚠️ snippet), but public runs are **sub-frontier**; 2026 momentum is inference. Similar story for Groq/SambaNova (inference ASICs).

**The pattern that matters for research:** move down the tiers and *evidence quality collapses faster than capability claims do.* Tier 1 has independent corroboration; Tiers 2–4 rest increasingly on vendor blogs, "our hardware supports it" statements, and community speculation. The gap between "announced" and "audited" is the whole opportunity.

## The "training dies late on alt hardware" folklore — what evidence exists

The folklore: large runs on non-NVIDIA silicon are prone to catastrophic late-stage loss spikes / rollbacks (numerics, kernel bugs, interconnect faults), making full frontier runs impractical. What the literature actually shows:

- **Loss spikes and rollbacks are a general frontier-training phenomenon, not specific to alt silicon.** The canonical example is on *NVIDIA/TPU*: **PaLM 540B** (Google's 2022 flagship LLM) **saw ~20 spikes and recovered by rolling back ~100 steps and skipping the triggering batch** ([search: Rohan Paul / Spike No More](https://www.rohan-paul.com/p/stabilizing-llm-training-techniques) ⚠️; corroborated by alphaXiv summary of [2312.16903](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2312.16903v4) — a study of what causes loss spikes and how to prevent them). Causes are excessive LR (learning rate — the step size of each weight update), exploding gradients, and **numerical issues in large-scale distributed setups** — and *"loss spikes can arise from hardware or transient computational issues"* (⚠️ search synthesis). So instability is real and partly hardware-linked, but it is a *shared* problem, and the mitigation toolkit (logit softcapping and z-loss — curbing runaway output magnitudes; QK-norm — normalizing attention scores; gradient clipping — capping update size; precision policy — choosing number formats carefully; narrow-vs-wide-spike rollback — how far back to revert) is well established.
- **The strongest *pro*-stability claims on alt silicon are exactly the vendor claims we can't yet verify.** Moonshot's Kimi K2 reported **zero loss spikes** over 15.5T tokens with MuonClip (a training-stabilizing optimizer variant) (internal map, verified — but on NVIDIA H800); Pangu Ultra MoE's stability is documented in a real paper on Ascend; SpikingBrain ran stably >2 weeks on MetaX. LongCat-2.0's "no rollbacks or irrecoverable loss spikes" fits this genre but is the *least* documented of the set.
- **Verdict:** The folklore is **overstated as a categorical claim** — Google (TPU), Huawei (Ascend, with a paper), and Meituan (ASIC, claimed) all report completing large runs — but **under-measured as an empirical question**: there is no public, cross-silicon dataset of spike/rollback rates. That absence is a research opening, not a settled answer.

## Why it matters

- **NVIDIA-durability assumptions in valuations.** A large share of the AI capital stack is priced on NVIDIA's training moat. Every credibly-audited non-NVIDIA frontier run (TPU/Trainium already; Ascend/ASIC if verified) is direct evidence on how durable that moat is.
- **Export-control efficacy.** Controls assume compute scarcity throttles Chinese frontier training. If domestic ASICs can complete 1T+ runs, the theory of change weakens — but *only if the claims are real.* This is precisely where independent verification is decision-relevant (cross-ref [Problem 03](problem-03-supply-chain.md); Chris Miller — author of *Chip War* — reads it as: controls hurt Chinese chip *production* and global share, not model *capability*).
- **Supply-chain resilience.** Hardware substitutability is the flip side of chokepoint concentration ([Problem 03](problem-03-supply-chain.md): ASML/TSMC/HBM). Verified alt-silicon runs tell you how fast the ecosystem could route around a shock — or around NVIDIA.
- **Regulation keyed to FLOP.** Compute-threshold rules implicitly assume you can attribute a run to hardware. Provenance detectability (below) would make hardware claims auditable, strengthening both governance and this brief's own verification agenda.

Independent tracking/verification of hardware-migration claims is thin, and that is the headline finding.

| Press | media | SCMP/Caixin/the-decoder/MarkTechPost repeat claims and (usefully) flag the unnamed-ASIC gap | reporting, not verification |

**The gap, stated plainly: almost no one independently verifies "trained on hardware X" claims at the run level.** Epoch verifies *data centers* (satellite/permits) but not *training-hardware attribution*; academics have *methods* (HSPI, numerical fingerprinting) not yet pointed at live vendor claims; press flags gaps but can't close them. This is an unusually open niche.

## What a solo researcher could do here

Profile fit: data pipelines, LLM tuning, eval methodology; laptop + free tiers + ≤$500/study + frontier APIs; Claude-assisted; impact-first.

1. **Alt-silicon training-run observatory with verification gradings (flagship, low-cost, high-impact).** A public, versioned ledger of *claimed* non-NVIDIA frontier/near-frontier training runs (LongCat-2.0, Pangu, SpikingBrain, Qianfan-VL, AMD/Cerebras runs…), each graded on an evidence rubric: *ASIC named? methods paper vs blog? MFU disclosed? stability record disclosed? independently corroborated? weights released?* This is desk research + a schema — directly in the wheelhouse (mirrors Problem 03's Epoch/OSINT model). It fills the exact gap above and would be citable immediately. **Budget: ~$0.**
2. **Behavioral/numerical provenance fingerprinting — "is training silicon detectable?"** Operationalize HSPI ([2411.05197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05197)) against *open-weight* models with *known-different* training hardware (LongCat-2.0 [claimed ASIC] vs LongCat-Flash [NVIDIA H800]; Pangu [Ascend] vs a comparable NVIDIA-trained MoE; SpikingBrain [MetaX]). Question: do low-level numerical signatures (reduction order — the sequence in which parallel sums combine; FMA behavior — fused multiply-add rounding quirks; quantization artifacts — traces of low-precision math) survive into open weights and cluster by silicon? If yes, vendor hardware claims become **auditable** — a genuinely new capability. Feasible on laptop + a few hundred $ of frontier-API/inference calls; squarely eval-methodology work.
3. **Nano-scale stability replication on rentable non-NVIDIA cloud.** Rent **AMD MI300X (~$1–2/hr, Azure/Vultr/DigitalOcean)** or **Google TPU** on demand ([MI300X pricing](https://getdeploying.com/gpus/amd-mi300x) ⚠️ snippet; [TPU pricing](https://cloud.google.com/tpu/pricing)) and run small (≤1B) pretraining under deliberately spike-prone regimes (high LR, aggressive batch scaling), logging spike/rollback rates vs a matched NVIDIA baseline. Tests the "dies late on alt hardware" folklore *empirically* at a scale a ≤$500 budget can reach. Won't reach frontier, but produces the first *reproducible* cross-silicon stability micro-benchmark.
4. **Re-eval of LongCat-2.0's near-frontier benchmark claims.** The SWE-bench-Pro (a hard software-engineering benchmark; ~59.5 vs GPT-5.5 ~58.6) and OpenRouter "Owl Alpha" leaderboard claims are ⚠️ **vendor-framed / independent confirmation pending.** A clean independent re-eval of an open-weight model where the *hardware story is the headline* is a tidy eval-methodology contribution and a natural companion to (1).
5. **MFU-gap meta-analysis.** Collate every *disclosed* MFU number by silicon (Ascend 30.0% [Pangu], MetaX 23.4% vs A800 25.8% [SpikingBrain], H100 34%→54% software-driven [SemiAnalysis]) into one normalized comparison, with explicit caveats that MFU is a *joint config* property (it depends on model, software, and setup together), not a chip property. Small, desk-based, and clarifies the "how big is the penalty, is it closing" question that valuations turn on.

## What we still don't know (ledger)

- **Which ASIC trained LongCat-2.0.** Genuinely undisclosed; Huawei/MetaX/Moore Threads issued only "we support it" statements. Do not treat "Ascend" as fact.
- **Whether LongCat-2.0's zero-rollback / 35T-token / ASIC-only claims survive audit** — no arXiv paper, no third-party corroboration as of 2026-07-08.
- **Actual cross-silicon spike/rollback rates.** No public dataset; the folklore is untested at scale.
- **Whether training hardware is detectable from open weights / model behavior.** HSPI works on served models under some settings; unknown whether the signal survives into distributed frontier training and released checkpoints.
- **The true MFU penalty curve for each alt platform and whether it's converging** to NVIDIA.
- **How much of "China trains without NVIDIA" is production reality vs. announcement** — the Tier-3/4 runs are mostly inference or support claims, not audited pretraining.
- **What share of *global frontier training* is now non-NVIDIA** — Epoch gives compute *stocks* (>60% NVIDIA) but not a run-completion breakdown.
