## The problem, concretely

Almost every load-bearing assumption in the 2026 AI economy — trillion-dollar lab valuations, the durability of NVIDIA's moat, the theory that US export controls throttle Chinese AI, and the compute-threshold logic behind regulation — rests on a belief about *hardware substitutability* (whether you can swap in non-NVIDIA chips without breaking the training): that frontier-scale training is hard to do on anything but NVIDIA GPUs (and the two proven exceptions, Google TPU and AWS Trainium — both custom AI chips built by the cloud provider itself). If that belief is wrong — if 1T+ parameter models can be reliably pretrained on domestic Chinese ASICs (application-specific chips, i.e. custom silicon for one job), AMD GPUs, or wafer-scale engines (a single giant chip the size of a whole silicon wafer) — several of those bets weaken at once. And here is the research gap: **the belief is being tested in public, but almost nobody is independently verifying the results.** Vendors announce full-stack non-NVIDIA training runs; the claims get repeated; no third party audits the cluster, the token count, or the stability record.

The concrete, measurable sub-problems:
1. **Which non-NVIDIA platforms have actually *completed* a frontier-scale (≳10²⁴–10²⁶ FLOP total compute, ≳100B-param) pretraining run — as opposed to announcing one, demoing inference (running a finished model), or "supporting" a model post-hoc?**
2. **Is the "training runs die late on alt hardware" folklore true?** What is the real evidence on loss-spike (sudden training-loss jumps) / rollback (reverting to an earlier checkpoint) rates as a function of silicon and numerics (the number-format arithmetic details)?
3. **Is a model's training hardware detectable after the fact** (numerical/behavioral provenance — telltale fingerprints in the weights or outputs)? If yes, vendor hardware claims become auditable.
4. **What efficiency (MFU — Model FLOPs Utilization, the fraction of a chip's peak compute actually used) penalty does alternative silicon actually pay**, and is it closing?

## The landscape: who has actually trained at scale off NVIDIA

Ordered by strength of evidence — from *proven, independently corroborated* to *marketing*.

### Tier 1 — proven at the absolute frontier (independently corroborated by ≥2 first-party primaries each)
- **Google TPU** (Tensor Processing Unit — Google's in-house AI accelerator, in production since ~2016) — the giant existing proof. **PRIMARY-VERIFIED 2026-07-08 (deepening pass 2):** the official **Google DeepMind Gemini 3 Pro Model Card** states *"Gemini 3 Pro was trained on Google's custom TPUs (Tensor Processing Units)"* using *"JAX and Google's Pathways architecture,"* with **no mention of NVIDIA** ([model-card PDF, read + downloaded](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Model-Cards/Gemini-3-Pro-Model-Card.pdf)) — a first-party disclosure, not a secondary claim. Independently corroborated by SemiAnalysis (TPU "neck and neck with … Nvidia," [read](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/tpuv7-google-takes-a-swing-at-the)) and by **Anthropic's own newsroom**, which lists *"three chip platforms — Google's TPUs, Amazon's Trainium, and NVIDIA's GPUs"* ([Anthropic, read](https://www.anthropic.com/news/expanding-our-use-of-google-cloud-tpus-and-services)). Ironwood (TPU v7): 4,614 TFLOPs/chip, 192 GB HBM, 7.37 TB/s HBM bandwidth, **42.5 Exaflops at 9,216-chip pod scale** — **all four figures re-verified verbatim against the Google blog 2026-07-08** ([Google, read](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/ironwood-tpu-age-of-inference/)). This is the existence proof that frontier-scale training does *not* require NVIDIA.
- **AWS Trainium** (Amazon's in-house training accelerator) — **PRIMARY-VERIFIED 2026-07-08 (deepening pass 2) from two first-party sources.** (1) **Amazon's own Q4-2025 earnings report:** *"Trainium2 is fully subscribed with 1.4 million chips landed,"* and *"Project Rainier, the world's largest operational AI compute cluster with 500,000+ Trainium2 chips, which Anthropic is using to train its industry-leading AI model, Claude"* ([aboutamazon Q4-2025, read](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-earnings-q4-2025-report)). (2) **Anthropic's own newsroom:** *"we currently use over one million Trainium2 chips to train and serve Claude"* ([Anthropic, read](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute)). (Also corroborated: AWS CEO Matt Garman, re:Invent 2025 — "more than 1 million Trainium processors" deployed.) **Figure updated: ~1.3M → 1.4M Trainium2 chips "landed"** (Amazon primary). Trainium is thus the second non-NVIDIA frontier platform whose *at-scale Claude training* is attested by the chip vendor **and** the model lab independently — not a single-source claim.

Epoch's framing: NVIDIA is "over 60% of total compute, with Google and Amazon making up much of the remainder" ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/trends) ⚠️); and the dedicated **Epoch data-insight page** (read 2026-07-08) states verbatim *"Google is the largest single owner of AI compute, holding about one quarter of global cumulative capacity as of Q4 2025"* — "primarily from its own custom TPU chips rather than NVIDIA's GPUs" ([Epoch, read](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/google-custom-tpus-ai-compute)). So non-NVIDIA is already a **large minority of frontier compute** — but concentrated in two Western silicon lines, both vertically integrated (chip and cloud owned by the same company), both proven. **This is a genuine *independent* primary for "TPU trains at frontier scale": Epoch (nonprofit, Open Phil) reaching the same conclusion as Google's own model card, via a different dataset (compute-stock accounting), not a restatement of it.**

### Tier 2 — credible completed runs, single-source / lightly audited
- **Huawei Ascend (Pangu)** — the strongest *published* Chinese-hardware-only case. **Pangu Ultra MoE, 718B params, trained on 6K Ascend NPUs** (Huawei's neural-processing-unit AI chips), with a real arXiv report ([2505.04519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04519), **primary read cover-to-cover 2026-07-08** — what it did: documents a 718B-param MoE training system on Ascend; finding: **30.0% MFU** and **1.46M tokens/s on 6K NPUs** (and 18.9% MFU / 0.61M TPS on 4K NPUs), DeepSeek-R1-class benchmarks). **Hardware named in-paper: the Ascend 910B platform** (§2.2 explicitly models "the Ascend 910B platform"; frameworks = MindSpeed + Megatron) — a *harder* attribution than most Chinese runs. **Two corrections from the primary read:** (1) the MFU 30.0% is a *cumulative* result of four engineering strategies (baseline→+58.7%; Table 5), not a raw chip number — reinforcing that "MFU is a joint config property"; (2) the paper documents the *system and MFU* but contains **no explicit spike/rollback log for the 718B run** — the loss curves are for 20B ablations. So "Pangu's *stability* is documented in a paper" is **overstated**: its *efficiency and system design* are documented; its stability record is not. Ascend is on China's government-approved procurement list while NVIDIA is excluded; 910C output targeting ~600K units in 2026 (⚠️ SemiAnalysis/search snippets). Export-control context: BIS guidance treats using Ascend 910B/C/D as an export-rule violation ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/department-of-commerce-formally-rescinds-ai-diffusion-rule-says-deploying-huawei-ascend-chips-violates-us-export-rules/) ⚠️ snippet). **⚠️ Authenticity caveat (added 2026-07-08):** in July 2025 the "HonestAGI" group alleged Huawei's *sibling* model **Pangu Pro MoE (72B)** was an "upcycled" copy of Alibaba **Qwen 2.5** (near-identical QKV-bias and attention-LayerNorm patterns; a Qwen license file was found in Pangu's GitCode repo); a self-described Huawei whistleblower separately alleged a Pangu Ultra MoE variant "very similar to DeepSeek-V3." Huawei's Noah's Ark Lab denied it, affirming the model was **"developed and trained on Huawei's Ascend hardware platform … not incremental training on any models."** The dispute is **about architectural originality, not the hardware claim**, and it targeted the **72B Pro**, not the **718B Ultra** whose training report we read; the accusers' credibility is also contested (repo deleted). Net: still the cleanest *documented* Chinese-hardware training case, but "cleanest counter-example" should carry this originality asterisk.
- **MetaX (SpikingBrain)** — hundreds of **non-NVIDIA MetaX C550 GPUs** ran SpikingBrain-7B and SpikingBrain-76B-A12B (12B active), stable >2 weeks, **23.4% MFU (7B) vs 25.8% on an NVIDIA A800 cluster** (A800 = the export-throttled China version of the A100) — a real, small efficiency gap, in a real arXiv report ([2509.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276), primary read 2026-07-08 — what it did: trained a spiking/brain-inspired LLM on Chinese MetaX GPUs and logged the stability run; finding: multi-week stable training at only a ~2-point MFU deficit vs NVIDIA). **Correction (2026-07-08):** this is **not from-scratch pretraining** — the paper's own abstract/§1 say it starts from an *open-source Qwen2.5-7B-base checkpoint* and does **continual pre-training (CPT)** with **"less than 2% of the compute required for training from scratch"** (~150B tokens). So it is a rare *documented multi-week stability record on Chinese silicon*, but a **conversion/CPT run**, not a frontier-token pretraining — a weaker existence proof than "trained SpikingBrain from scratch on MetaX" would be.

### Tier 3 — claimed frontier run, no independent methods paper
- **Meituan LongCat-2.0** — the trigger (above). Full 1.6T run claimed on unnamed domestic ASICs, **[vendor]-only, tech blog not arXiv, ASIC undisclosed.** Larger param count than Pangu but *weaker* evidence.

### Tier 4 — mostly inference, or "supports" not "trained"
- **Cambricon (MLU590/Siyuan)** — largely **inference**-targeted (A100-class performance); targeting 500K accelerator shipments in 2026 vs ~116K in 2025; customers ByteDance/Alibaba (⚠️ [Presenc](https://presenc.ai/research/chinese-ai-chips-landscape-2026), search snippets). No public frontier *training* run.
- **Baidu Kunlun** — runs a ~30,000-chip cluster training Qianfan-VL (Baidu's vision-language model) on domestic silicon (⚠️ search snippet [the-substrate](https://www.the-substrate.net/p/where-chinas-ai-chip-supply-chain)); Samsung reportedly halted supply (of the HBM memory) early 2025 under export tightening ⚠️.
- **Alibaba T-Head (PPU/Zhenwu)** — "several hundred thousand" units shipped ⚠️ (snippet); role in training its own Qwen models (Alibaba's open LLM family) unclear/undisclosed.
- **Moore Threads (MTT S5000)** — runs DeepSeek-V4 natively (~4,000 tok/s prefill — the input-processing phase; inference framing); "Day-0 adaptation of DeepSeek-V4 in 2026" (getting a just-released model running immediately) is a **deployment/support** claim, not a from-scratch training run (⚠️ snippets).
- **AMD Instinct (MI300X/MI350)** — real and growing on **MLPerf training** (the industry-standard training-speed benchmark suite). **Updated 2026-07-08 with MLPerf Training v6.0:** MI355X (MXFP4) came **within ~6% of NVIDIA B200 (NVFP4) on Llama-3.1-8B *pre-training*** and within ~5% on Llama-2-70B fine-tuning; AMD reported **multi-node MLPerf results for the first time**, and Oracle submitted **512× MI300X** ([AMD blog](https://www.amd.com/en/blogs/2026/amd-delivers-breakthrough-mlperf-training-6-0-results.html)). **This one is *structurally* multi-source: MLPerf is a neutral consortium benchmark (results published by MLCommons 2026-06-16, [MLCommons](https://mlcommons.org/2026/05/gpt-oss-moe-training6/)), and the ~5–6% gap is independently restated by [igor'sLAB](https://www.igorslab.de/en/amd-mlperf-training-6-0-instinct-mi355x-approaches-blackwell-scales-multiple-servers/) (read 2026-07-08 — verbatim "within five percent of the B200" on 70B FT, "about six percent" on 8B pretrain), a non-AMD outlet, which adds the independent caveat that "companies should not automatically infer their own training costs" and that generalization "to other models and software environments cannot be derived from this."** So the AMD-vs-B200 *benchmark* claim rests on vendor + neutral-consortium + independent-analyst — the strongest-corroborated non-NVIDIA claim in the brief after Tier-1. Still: these are *benchmark* workloads (8B pretrain / 70B fine-tune), and **no publicly-disclosed frontier-flagship pretraining run is yet attributable to AMD as the sole silicon.** AMD remains the Western challenger closest to Tier 1 for *training*, gated by ecosystem (ROCm software stack) and multi-node maturity, not the raw chip — and the per-chip gap to NVIDIA on a real pretraining benchmark is now single-digit percent.
- **Cerebras (WSE)** — wafer-scale (WSE = Wafer-Scale Engine, one chip spanning a full wafer); Cerebras-GPT (up to 13B) and customer runs are real and completed ([Cerebras](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/cerebras-gpt-a-family-of-open-compute-efficient-large-language-models) ⚠️ snippet), but public runs are **sub-frontier**; 2026 momentum is inference. Similar story for Groq/SambaNova (inference ASICs).

**The pattern that matters for research:** move down the tiers and *evidence quality collapses faster than capability claims do.* Tier 1 has independent corroboration; Tiers 2–4 rest increasingly on vendor blogs, "our hardware supports it" statements, and community speculation. The gap between "announced" and "audited" is the whole opportunity.

## The "training dies late on alt hardware" folklore — what evidence exists

The folklore: large runs on non-NVIDIA silicon are prone to catastrophic late-stage loss spikes / rollbacks (numerics, kernel bugs, interconnect faults), making full frontier runs impractical. What the literature actually shows:

- **Loss spikes and rollbacks are a general frontier-training phenomenon, not specific to alt silicon.** The canonical example is on *NVIDIA/TPU*: **PaLM 540B** (Google's 2022 flagship LLM) **saw ~20 spikes and recovered by rolling back ~100 steps and skipping the triggering batch** ([search: Rohan Paul / Spike No More](https://www.rohan-paul.com/p/stabilizing-llm-training-techniques) ⚠️; corroborated by alphaXiv summary of [2312.16903](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2312.16903v4) — a study of what causes loss spikes and how to prevent them). Causes are excessive LR (learning rate — the step size of each weight update), exploding gradients, and **numerical issues in large-scale distributed setups** — and *"loss spikes can arise from hardware or transient computational issues"* (⚠️ search synthesis). So instability is real and partly hardware-linked, but it is a *shared* problem, and the mitigation toolkit (logit softcapping and z-loss — curbing runaway output magnitudes; QK-norm — normalizing attention scores; gradient clipping — capping update size; precision policy — choosing number formats carefully; narrow-vs-wide-spike rollback — how far back to revert) is well established.
- **The strongest *pro*-stability claims on alt silicon are exactly the vendor claims we can't yet verify.** Moonshot's Kimi K2 reported **zero loss spikes** over 15.5T tokens with MuonClip (a training-stabilizing optimizer variant) (internal map, verified — but on NVIDIA H800); Pangu Ultra MoE's stability is documented in a real paper on Ascend; SpikingBrain ran stably >2 weeks on MetaX. LongCat-2.0's "no rollbacks or irrecoverable loss spikes" fits this genre but is the *least* documented of the set.
- **Verdict:** The folklore is **overstated as a categorical claim** — Google (TPU), Huawei (Ascend, with a paper), and Meituan (ASIC, claimed) all report completing large runs — but **under-measured as an empirical question**: there is no public, cross-silicon dataset of spike/rollback rates. That absence is a research opening, not a settled answer.

## Why it matters

- **NVIDIA-durability assumptions in valuations.** A large share of the AI capital stack is priced on NVIDIA's training moat. Every credibly-audited non-NVIDIA frontier run (TPU/Trainium already; Ascend/ASIC if verified) is direct evidence on how durable that moat is.
- **Export-control efficacy.** Controls assume compute scarcity throttles Chinese frontier training. If domestic ASICs can complete 1T+ runs, the theory of change weakens — but *only if the claims are real.* This is precisely where independent verification is decision-relevant (cross-ref [Problem 03](problem-03-supply-chain.md); Chris Miller — author of *Chip War* — reads it as: controls hurt Chinese chip *production* and global share, not model *capability*).
- **Supply-chain resilience.** Hardware substitutability is the flip side of chokepoint concentration ([Problem 03](problem-03-supply-chain.md): ASML/TSMC/HBM). Verified alt-silicon runs tell you how fast the ecosystem could route around a shock — or around NVIDIA.
- **Regulation keyed to FLOP.** Compute-threshold rules implicitly assume you can attribute a run to hardware. Provenance detectability (below) would make hardware claims auditable, strengthening both governance and this brief's own verification agenda.

**The gap, stated plainly: almost no one independently verifies "trained on hardware X" claims at the run level.** Epoch verifies *data centers* (satellite/permits) but not *training-hardware attribution*; academics have *methods* (HSPI, numerical fingerprinting) not yet pointed at live vendor claims; press flags gaps but can't close them. This is an unusually open niche.

## What a solo researcher could do here

Profile fit: data pipelines, LLM tuning, eval methodology; laptop + free tiers + ≤$500/study + frontier APIs; Claude-assisted; impact-first.

1. **Alt-silicon training-run observatory with verification gradings (flagship, low-cost, high-impact).** A public, versioned ledger of *claimed* non-NVIDIA frontier/near-frontier training runs (LongCat-2.0, Pangu, SpikingBrain, Qianfan-VL, AMD/Cerebras runs…), each graded on an evidence rubric: *ASIC named? methods paper vs blog? MFU disclosed? stability record disclosed? independently corroborated? weights released?* This is desk research + a schema — directly in the wheelhouse (mirrors Problem 03's Epoch/OSINT model). It fills the exact gap above and would be citable immediately. **Budget: ~$0.**
2. **Behavioral/numerical provenance fingerprinting — "is *training* silicon detectable from released weights?" (reframed 2026-07-08 — this is a HYPOTHESIS, not an application of an existing method).** Deepening changed this study's status materially. HSPI ([2411.05197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05197)) and the two allied methods ([2602.09369](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.09369), [2411.00442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00442)) all detect **inference/execution** hardware by *running the model and probing its outputs* — **none shows that training silicon leaves a recoverable signature in a static released checkpoint.** So the honest framing is: *does a training-hardware signature exist in open weights at all?* — an **open, higher-risk research question**, not "operationalize HSPI." A tractable first experiment: take open-weight pairs trained on *known-different* silicon (LongCat-2.0 [claimed ASIC] vs LongCat-Flash [NVIDIA H800]; a Pangu [Ascend] vs a comparable NVIDIA-trained MoE; SpikingBrain [MetaX, though note it's CPT-from-Qwen, a confound]) and test whether *weight-statistics* features (per-layer distributions, reduction-order artifacts, low-precision quantization traces) cluster by training silicon **when architecture/data are controlled for** — the hard confound, since these models differ in architecture *and* data *and* silicon. **Expected honest outcome: likely a null or weak result** (training optimization tends to wash out low-level hardware arithmetic differences, and weights don't carry the runtime reduction order the way live inference does). A rigorous *negative* result — "training silicon is NOT detectable from released weights, so vendor training-hardware claims are NOT auditable this way" — is itself a valuable, publishable finding that *bounds* the whole verification agenda. Feasible on laptop + a few hundred $ of inference calls; squarely eval-methodology work, now correctly scoped as exploratory.
3. **Nano-scale stability replication on rentable non-NVIDIA cloud.** Rent **AMD MI300X (~$1–2/hr, Azure/Vultr/DigitalOcean)** or **Google TPU** on demand ([MI300X pricing](https://getdeploying.com/gpus/amd-mi300x) ⚠️ snippet; [TPU pricing](https://cloud.google.com/tpu/pricing)) and run small (≤1B) pretraining under deliberately spike-prone regimes (high LR, aggressive batch scaling), logging spike/rollback rates vs a matched NVIDIA baseline. Tests the "dies late on alt hardware" folklore *empirically* at a scale a ≤$500 budget can reach. Won't reach frontier, but produces the first *reproducible* cross-silicon stability micro-benchmark.
4. **Re-eval of LongCat-2.0's near-frontier benchmark claims.** The SWE-bench-Pro (a hard software-engineering benchmark; ~59.5 vs GPT-5.5 ~58.6) and OpenRouter "Owl Alpha" leaderboard claims are ⚠️ **vendor-framed / independent confirmation pending.** A clean independent re-eval of an open-weight model where the *hardware story is the headline* is a tidy eval-methodology contribution and a natural companion to (1).
5. **MFU-gap meta-analysis.** Collate every *disclosed* MFU number by silicon (Ascend 30.0% [Pangu], MetaX 23.4% vs A800 25.8% [SpikingBrain], H100 34%→54% software-driven [SemiAnalysis]) into one normalized comparison, with explicit caveats that MFU is a *joint config* property (it depends on model, software, and setup together), not a chip property. Small, desk-based, and clarifies the "how big is the penalty, is it closing" question that valuations turn on.

## What we still don't know (ledger)

- **Which ASIC trained LongCat-2.0.** Genuinely undisclosed; Huawei/MetaX/Moore Threads issued only "we support it" statements. Do not treat "Ascend" as fact.
- **Whether LongCat-2.0's zero-rollback / 35T-token / ASIC-only claims survive audit** — no arXiv paper, no third-party corroboration as of 2026-07-08.
- **Actual cross-silicon spike/rollback rates.** No public dataset; the folklore is untested at scale.
- **Whether training hardware is detectable from open weights / model behavior.** **Sharpened 2026-07-08:** the three cited fingerprinting methods (HSPI [2411.05197], GPU telemetry [2602.09369], FPRev [2411.00442]) *all* detect **inference/execution** hardware by running-and-probing — **none is evidence that a *training*-silicon signature survives into released weights.** So this is not "HSPI might extend"; it is a genuinely *open and probably-hard* question whose likely answer is *no*. This directly gates study-shape #2 and part of the verification agenda: if training hardware is undetectable from weights, run-level attribution must rely on process evidence (clusters, power, disclosures), not forensics on the checkpoint.
- **The true MFU penalty curve for each alt platform and whether it's converging** to NVIDIA.
- **How much of "China trains without NVIDIA" is production reality vs. announcement** — the Tier-3/4 runs are mostly inference or support claims, not audited pretraining.
- **What share of *global frontier training* is now non-NVIDIA** — Epoch gives compute *stocks* (>60% NVIDIA) but not a run-completion breakdown.

## Deepening pass (2026-07-08)

Primary sources read in full this pass: **HSPI** ([2411.05197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05197), all 14 pp.), **Pangu Ultra MoE** ([2505.04519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04519), all 18 pp.), **SpikingBrain** ([2509.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276), front matter). Both new PDFs downloaded legal-first from arXiv into `papers/program-pdfs/`. The pass changed the brief in one *load-bearing* way (the fingerprinting/gating assumption), added one *material caveat* (Pangu authenticity), and firmed several figures.

### 1. The gating assumption is NOT supported — the single most important finding
The row's study-shape #2 rested on "training-silicon signature survives into released weights → vendor hardware claims become auditable." **Reading HSPI cover-to-cover shows this is not what HSPI (or its two allied papers) establishes.**
- **HSPI is an *inference/serving*-hardware detector.** Its entire threat model (Fig. 1, §4.1) is: *you buy inference from a provider — is it really on an H100?* The signal it exploits is generated **at inference** (quantization format, kernel choice — FlashAttn/Triton/FlashInfer, CUDA version, sharding, FMA/reduction-order **during the forward pass**). Its "white-box 83.9–100%" numbers come from **running the known model on candidate GPUs and comparing logits** (Tables 1–2, 9–11), not from inspecting a static checkpoint. HSPI-BI *fails* for LLMs; only HSPI-LD works, and it still requires running the model on the hardware. **Nowhere does the paper link released weight values to the silicon that produced them in training.**
- The brief's second cited method, **[2602.09369], is actually titled *"Timing and Memory Telemetry on GPUs for AI Governance"*** (title verified) — an **inference-time GPU-*class* fingerprint** for policing *where compute runs*, explicitly "to prevent outsourcing of computations to unapproved GPU platforms." Again execution-time, not training-provenance.
- **FPRev [2411.00442]** (the accumulation-order tool) reveals FP summation order **by probing a live implementation with designed inputs** — runtime, not recoverable from static weights.
- **Verdict:** *No primary source demonstrates a recoverable training-silicon signature in released weights.* Study-shape #2 has been **reframed from "operationalize HSPI" to "test whether such a signature exists at all"** — an open, higher-risk question whose likely answer is a (still-publishable) **null result**. This is the biggest single correction of the pass and the main reason confidence rose only modestly: the domain is better-mapped, but a shape I'd flagged as a clean win is now correctly labeled speculative.

### 2. Pangu Ultra MoE — confirmed on specs, but two corrections + an authenticity asterisk (see inline edits, Tier 2)
- **CONFIRMED from primary:** 718B params; **30.0% MFU, 1.46M tok/s on 6K Ascend NPUs** (18.9%/0.61M on 4K); DeepSeek-R1-class benchmarks; **hardware named in-paper = Ascend 910B**; frameworks MindSpeed+Megatron.
- **Correction A:** the 30.0% MFU is a *cumulative* result of four engineering strategies (Table 5: baseline→+58.7%), not a raw chip figure — supports the "MFU is a joint config property" caveat in study-shape #5.
- **Correction B:** the paper documents the **system + MFU**, but has **no explicit spike/rollback log for the 718B run** (loss curves shown are 20B ablations). "Pangu's stability is documented in a paper" was **overstated**; its *efficiency/design* are documented, its *stability record* is not. This trims one of the anti-folklore data points.
- **Authenticity asterisk (new):** July 2025 "HonestAGI" alleged the *sibling* Pangu Pro MoE (72B) was upcycled from **Qwen 2.5** (QKV-bias/LayerNorm match + a Qwen license file in the repo); a whistleblower alleged a Pangu-Ultra variant close to **DeepSeek-V3**. Huawei denied it and *affirmed the Ascend-training claim*. The dispute is about **architectural originality, not hardware**, and targeted the **72B** not the **718B** report we read; accusers' credibility is contested. Net: Pangu is still the cleanest *documented Chinese-hardware training* case, but the "counter-example" now carries an originality caveat the brief previously omitted.

### 3. SpikingBrain — weaker existence-proof than stated (see inline edit, Tier 2)
Primary read: SpikingBrain is **continual pre-training from an open Qwen2.5-7B-base checkpoint** with **"<2% of the compute required for training from scratch"** (~150B tokens), 76B variant = **76B-A12B** (12B active), on hundreds of MetaX C550. So it is a genuine **multi-week stability record on Chinese silicon** but a **conversion/CPT run, not frontier-token pretraining** — a real but *lighter* data point for "China can train off NVIDIA." (Also a confound for study-shape #2: its weights inherit Qwen, not a clean silicon signal.)

### 4. LongCat-2.0 — status unchanged on the hard points, "which ASIC" narrowed (see inline edits)
- **Still no arXiv methods paper** (verified: only LongCat-Flash [2509.01322] and LongCat-Flash-Thinking [2509.18883] have arXiv reports). The single biggest reason to hold the zero-rollback/ASIC-only claims as unverified **still holds**.
- **Still no independent benchmark confirmation** of the SWE-bench-Pro ~59.5 / leaderboard claims.
- **"Which ASIC" narrowed:** secondary reporting now converges on **Huawei — Atlas-950 SuperPods + HCCL** (Nation Press, AI Weekly, both read; count via BeInCrypto), but every source flags this as *inference, not Meituan disclosure*. Upgraded from "pure speculation" to "convergent secondary reporting"; still **not primary-confirmed**, and the specifics point at **Atlas-950 (Ascend 950-class, 2026)** rather than the 910C earlier speculation assumed.

### 5. Tier-1 firmed with primary/quasi-primary figures
- **TPU/Trainium are more solidly Tier-1 than before.** Anthropic's own newsroom confirms **Claude Opus 4.5 trained on a mix of Google TPUs + AWS Trainium**, and it is expanding to **up to 1M TPUs / >1 GW in 2026**; **Gemini 3 trained entirely on TPU** (SemiAnalysis + Google). **Epoch (2026): Google owns ~1/4 of global cumulative AI compute, mostly its own TPUs; Amazon ~2.5M H100e, mostly Trainium.** **Amazon Q4 2025 earnings (primary): "Trainium2 … fully subscribed with 1.4M chips landed"** — updates the brief's ~1.3M figure to **~1.4M**.

### Dimension-score corrections warranted by this pass
