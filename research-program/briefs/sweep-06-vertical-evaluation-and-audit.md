# Domain-Vertical Evaluation & Third-Party Audit

*Sweep 06 — researched July 2026. Every claim below is sourced to a page actually opened during this sweep unless flagged ⚠️ unverified. "READ" = stated in the source; "INFER" = my synthesis.*

## The shape of this space

Three overlapping practices, with different institutions, methods, and publication venues:

**1. Domain-vertical evaluation** — building and running benchmarks/evals of AI in high-stakes verticals where generic leaderboards say nothing useful.
- **Medicine**: [MedHELM](https://medhelm.org/) (Stanford CRFM-led, multi-institution; 121 clinical tasks in a clinician-validated taxonomy of 5 categories/22 subcategories, 35 benchmarks — 14 private, 7 gated, 14 public; [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23802v2), [Stanford HAI writeup](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/holistic-evaluation-of-large-language-models-for-medical-applications)). OpenAI's [HealthBench](https://pacific.ai/evaluating-frontier-llms-for-healthcare-with-medhelm-40-clinical-scenarios-and-a-new-q2-2026-leaderboard/) (5,000 conversations scored against physician-written rubrics, May 2025; "HealthBench Professional" extension April 2026 — READ from the Pacific AI overview page). Notable methodology detail: MedHELM scores open-ended outputs with an **ensemble of three LLM judges whose agreement with expert clinicians (ICC = 0.47) exceeded clinician-to-clinician agreement** (READ, Pacific AI/MedHELM page).
- **Law**: [LegalBench](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/legalbench/) ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.11462), [GitHub](https://github.com/HazyResearch/legalbench)) — 162 tasks hand-crafted by 40 contributors, an open collaborative model. Stanford RegLab's ["Hallucination-Free?"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362) audit of commercial legal RAG tools (below).
- **Finance**: [FinanceBench](https://www.patronus.ai/announcements/patronus-ai-launches-financebench-the-industrys-first-benchmark-for-llm-performance-on-financial-questions) (Patronus AI, Nov 2023, [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.11944), [HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PatronusAI/financebench)); [FinBen](https://www.bprigent.com/article/fpna-ai-benchmarks) (open-source, 36 datasets / 24 tasks / 7 capability aspects — READ from Prigent's overview). Newer agentic finance evals exist ([BigFinanceBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03829), [FinTrace](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.10015) — ⚠️ only titles/abstract-level read).
- **Education**: strikingly benchmark-thin; the strongest published work is *field-trial* evaluation, e.g. [Tutor CoPilot RCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017) (below). A benchmark-style framework ([TEACH-AI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.04107)) only appeared late 2025 (⚠️ title-level read only).
- **Government/public sector**: emerging and fragmented — [CitizenQuery](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.04064) (UK citizen queries), [MÖVE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13111) (German public sector), [POLIS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04705) (bilingual policy tasks), [CPSDBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07234) (Chinese public security). A 2026 meta-analysis, ["Agent Benchmarks Fail Public Sector Requirements"](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20617), reviewed 1,300+ benchmark papers and found **no single benchmark meets public-sector criteria**; ~half of recent benchmarks pad small real-world cores with LLM-generated examples, and only ~30% evaluate under realistic/field-like conditions (READ from search-result summary of the paper — ⚠️ exact figures not re-verified against the PDF).

**2. Algorithmic auditing as a practice** — examining *deployed* systems (often classical ML, not LLMs) for bias/validity, increasingly under legal mandates:
- **NYC Local Law 144** (bias audits of Automated Employment Decision Tools; [DCWP page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/about/automated-employment-decision-tools.page)) is the template now being copied — a 2026 law-firm analysis says California, New Jersey, and Illinois are following NYC's blueprint ([Bochner PLLC](https://www.bochner.law/news/publications/2026-04-30-algorithmic-bias-audit-compliance-navigating-the-2026-frontier)).
- **Insurance**: Colorado [SB 21-169](https://doi.colorado.gov/for-consumers/sb21-169-protecting-consumers-from-unfair-discrimination-in-insurance-practices) + Regulation 10-1-1 require life insurers using external consumer data (ECDIS) to run **annual quantitative testing for unfairly discriminatory outcomes** with annual attestations to the Division of Insurance (READ across [Credo AI](https://www.credo.ai/blog/colorado-sb21-169-8-things-you-need-to-know-about-colorados-new-ai-insurance-regulation), [Pinnacle Actuarial](https://www.pinnacleactuaries.com/article/colorados-division-insurance-proposes-draft-testing-fairness-algorithms-and-predictive), [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/the-future-of-insurance-colorados-new-ecdis-and-ai-model-regulations-1.html)).
- **Adversarial/second-party audits without cooperation**: Eticas' [Adversarial Algorithmic Auditing Guide](https://eticasfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/ETICAS-Adversarial-Algorithmic-Auditing-Guide-2023.pdf); journalism-led audits (Lighthouse Reports, below; ProPublica's 2016 COMPAS "Machine Bias" analysis, which still anchors the fairness literature — READ via [COMPAS Data Revisited](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.04711) and [HDSR](https://hdsr.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/7z10o269)).
- **Frontier-model safety auditing** is professionalizing separately: a 48-author position paper, ["Frontier AI Auditing"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11699) (Brundage first author, A. Feder Cooper corresponding), proposes tiered **AI Assurance Levels (AAL-1/AAL-2)** and notes dedicated third-party safety-evaluation capacity worldwide is "likely only a few hundred full-time employees" concentrated in a handful of nonprofits and government institutes.

**3. Incident analysis** — post-deployment failure cataloging and epistemics:
- [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/) (AIID), run by the Responsible AI Collaborative; [OECD AI Incidents Monitor](https://ethicai.net/ai-incidents); [AIAAIC Repository](https://oecd.ai/en/catalogue/tools/aiaaic-repository) (independent, started June 2019 by Charlie Pownall as a private project, now open public-interest resource); [MIT AI Risk Repository / AI Incident Tracker](https://airisk.mit.edu/blog/ai-incident-tracker-june-2026-update) (classifies incidents against risk taxonomies and rates severity).
- The craft has its own methods literature: ["Lessons for Editors of AI Incidents from the AIID"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16425) (Paeth, Atherton, Pittaras, Frase, McGregor — 5 authors, 750+ incidents analyzed) on structural ambiguities in cause/harm/severity determination.

**INFER — the connective tissue:** all three practices share one core skill: *constructing valid measurements of AI behavior under domain constraints, then defending them*. The benchmark people, the auditors, and the incident editors cite each other (e.g., ["Who Audits the Auditors"](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3531146.3533213) catalogs benchmark builders and journalists alike as auditors).

## Published exemplars (small-team-first)

1. **Gender Shades** — Buolamwini & Gebru, **2 authors** (MIT Media Lab / Microsoft Research), FAT* 2018 ([paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/buolamwini18a.html)). Audited three commercial face APIs; darker-skinned women misclassified up to 34.7% vs ≤0.8% for lighter-skinned men. Landed because: intersectional disaggregation was novel, targets were named vendors, and it triggered product changes and the modern audit movement.
2. **Dissecting racial bias in a health algorithm** — Obermeyer, Powers, Vogeli, Mullainathan, **4 authors**, *Science* 2019 ([paper](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aax2342)). Found a widely used care-management algorithm used *cost as proxy for need*, systematically under-serving Black patients. Landed because: one crisp mechanistic finding on a deployed system affecting millions; became the canonical proxy-bias case.
3. **External validation of the Epic Sepsis Model** — Wong et al., University of Michigan, *JAMA Internal Medicine* 2021 ([paper](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2781307), [PubMed editorial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34152360/)). Single-site retrospective validation (27,697 patients): AUC 0.63, sensitivity 33% — vs vendor-claimed performance; Epic later overhauled the model (⚠️ the "withdrawn by 180 customers" figure seen only in a [secondary blog](https://rewardhealth.com/archives/3526)). Landed because: it showed a *proprietary, hospital-deployed* model could be independently audited with routine EHR data and standard ML-validation statistics.
4. **Hallucination-Free? Assessing the Reliability of Leading AI Legal Research Tools** — Magesh, Surani, Dahl, Suzgun, Manning, Ho, **6 authors** (Stanford RegLab/HAI + collaborators), 2024–25 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362), [PDF](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Legal_RAG_Hallucinations.pdf), [HAI writeup](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-or-more-benchmarking-queries)). First *preregistered* adversarial eval of commercial legal RAG (Lexis+ AI 17%, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research 33% hallucination). Landed because: directly falsified vendor "hallucination-free" marketing with rigorous methodology — the archetype of a domain RAG audit.
5. **FinanceBench** — Patronus AI (first author Pranab Islam) + 15 domain experts, Nov 2023 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.11944), [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PatronusAI/financebench)). 10k QA pairs over SEC filings; headline: GPT-4-Turbo + retrieval failed/refused **81%** of questions. Landed because: first credible finance-vertical benchmark, startup-sized team, one shocking number.
6. **Tutor CoPilot RCT** — Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb, Demszky, **5 authors** (Stanford), 2024 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017), [project](https://edunlp.stanford.edu/projects/tutor-copilot)). First RCT of a human-AI tutoring system: 900 tutors / 1,800 students; +4pp mastery overall, +9pp for weakest tutors; $20/student/year. Landed because: it evaluated *outcomes*, not benchmark scores — the model for education-vertical evaluation.
7. **Suspicion Machines** — Lighthouse Reports + WIRED + Vers Beton, 2023 ([investigation](https://www.lighthousereports.com/investigation/suspicion-machines/), [methods](https://pulitzercenter.org/how-we-did-it-unlocking-europes-welfare-fraud-algorithms), [code/data](https://github.com/Lighthouse-Reports/suspicion_machine)). Obtained Rotterdam's Accenture-built gradient-boosting welfare-fraud model (trained on 12,707 investigations) via FOI negotiation; demonstrated ethnicity/gender discrimination by re-running the model. Landed because: unprecedented *technical* access for journalism — a full second-party audit of a live government model, reproducible from their repo.
8. **Null Compliance** (LL144) — Wright et al., FAccT 2024 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01399), [PDF](https://facctconference.org/static/papers24/facct24-113.pdf)). 155 student investigators checked 391 NYC employers: only **18 posted bias-audit reports, 13 posted notices**; the law's scope discretion makes non-posting uninterpretable ("null compliance") and incentivizes deliberate ignorance. Landed because: it's the definitive empirical verdict on the first US algorithmic-audit mandate — and a template for cheap distributed compliance auditing.

## Where the gaps are (named, sourced)

1. **No audit standards; "audit" is undefined.** [Who Audits the Auditors](https://facctconference.org/static/pdfs_2022/facct22-3533213.pdf) (Costanza-Chock, Raji, Buolamwini; field scan of 438 individuals/189 orgs): without standards, "claims that systems have been audited are difficult to verify and may exacerbate, rather than mitigate, bias and harm." Ada Lovelace (2024–25 project pages): "no standardised or consistent practices for algorithm auditors to follow."
2. **Audits rarely convert to accountability.** [The Broken Bus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14462) (Birhane, Steed, Ojewale, Vecchione, Raji; SaTML 2024): "only a subset of AI audit studies translate to desired accountability outcomes"; ecosystem "muddled and imprecise." The open research problem they name: which combinations of audit design, methodology, and institutional context actually produce consequences.
3. **Mandates without enforcement.** The [NY State Comptroller's Dec 2025 audit of DCWP](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools): **two** AEDT complaints in two years; DCWP found 1 non-compliance in 32 companies where the Comptroller's team found ≥17 potential violations; no technical consultation with OTI. Combined with Null Compliance: the first-generation audit law is measurably not working — INFER: methods for *verifying* compliance ecosystems are themselves an open research vertical.
4. **Auditor independence / scope capture.** The ORCAA-HireVue episode ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90597594/ai-algorithm-auditing-hirevue)): auditee controlled scope and publicity. IAAA's founding principle (auditors answer to public/regulators, not vendors) exists precisely because current practice doesn't guarantee it ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/a-new-association-wants-to-professionalize-the-ai-auditing-industry)).
5. **Data/model access is the binding constraint on quantitative audits.** [Access Denied: Meaningful Data Access for Quantitative Algorithm Audits](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00428) (⚠️ abstract-level read); Suspicion Machines required six months of FOI negotiation for *one* municipal model; The Markup: ["It takes a small miracle to learn basic facts about government algorithms"](https://themarkup.org/hello-world/2023/04/01/it-takes-a-small-miracle-to-learn-basic-facts-about-government-algorithms).
6. **Benchmark quality itself is poor and unmeasured.** [BetterBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990) (NeurIPS 2024 spotlight): of 24 assessed benchmarks against 46 lifecycle best practices, most don't report statistical significance or support replication. The [public-sector meta-review](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20617): no benchmark meets public-sector requirements; heavy synthetic-data padding risks inflated scores.
7. **Vertical coverage is uneven.** Medicine now has industrial-scale infrastructure (MedHELM, HealthBench); law and finance have credible first-generation benchmarks; **education and government are thin** — education's best evidence is one RCT, and public-sector benchmarks are mostly non-English or brand-new (CitizenQuery, MÖVE, POLIS-Bench, all 2025–26). Insurance auditing under Colorado's regulation is mandated but has no visible public methods literature (INFER — I found regulatory/compliance-vendor commentary, no published audits).
8. **Incident analysis has unresolved epistemics.** [Paeth et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16425): cause, harm-extent, and severity determinations are structurally ambiguous; "epistemic uncertainty in AI incident reporting is unavoidable." Multiple redundant databases (AIID, AIAAIC, OECD AIM, MIT tracker) with no shared taxonomy — the [collaborative harms taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01294v2) is a first attempt.
9. **Frontier-audit capacity shortage.** [Frontier AI Auditing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11699): third-party safety-evaluation capacity ≈ a few hundred FTEs globally; named challenges include expanding the provider ecosystem without quality collapse and technical readiness for deeper assurance levels.

## Fit notes

The published exemplars here were produced with exactly the researcher's stack: public documents/FOI data + classical ML statistics + frontier-API calls — none required training runs (Rotterdam's audited model was literally a gradient-boosting machine, the researcher's home tooling; LL144 impact-ratio math is basic disparate-impact statistics). The researcher's LLM-as-a-judge/RAG-hallucination publication is methodologically adjacent to the two highest-impact recent works in this space: RegLab's preregistered legal-RAG hallucination audit and MedHELM's LLM-jury validation (ICC vs clinicians) — both are evaluation-methodology contributions of the kind already demonstrated. Solo viability is empirically established at every tier: 2–6-author audits in *Science*/JAMA/FAT*, a one-founder incident repository (AIAAIC) that became OECD-cited infrastructure, and a professionalizing auditor track (IAAA/BABL certification) that runs on credentials rather than institutional affiliation.
