# AI Development & Research Tooling as a Research Domain

The tooling layer everyone in AI depends on — eval harnesses (software that runs a model against a benchmark and scores it), observability/tracing (logging what an AI system does step-by-step so you can inspect it), agent frameworks (libraries for building multi-step tool-using AI agents), fine-tuning infrastructure, dataset tooling, reproducibility tooling — treated two ways: as a **research object** (what's measurably broken) and as a **contribution surface** (small teams whose tools became infrastructure). Grounded first in the internal map docs [ai-for-software-engineering.md](../../ai-research-map/11-emerging-application-subfields/ai-for-software-engineering.md) and [building-agents-and-rl-environments.md](../../ai-research-map/15-research-methods-and-how-systems-are-built/building-agents-and-rl-environments.md), then extended by web research (all links opened).

---

## The problem, concretely

The measured behavior of AI systems is heavily determined by the tooling around them, and that tooling is unreliable in quantifiable ways:

- **The harness moves scores more than the model.** Scaffold choice (the code wrapping the model — prompts, retries, tool plumbing) swings SWE-bench (a coding benchmark of real GitHub bug fixes) results 10–20 points; three agent systems running the *same* Claude Opus 4.5 on SWE-bench Pro scored 50.2%–55.4% ([analysis](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/swe-bench-verified-june-2026-benchmark-vs-scaffolding-analysis), via internal map — read). The load-bearing generalization — that scaffold choice is a first-order determinant of measured accuracy — now rests on **three mutually independent peer-reviewable primaries** from four unrelated institutions (pass 3): (1) the Holistic Agent Leaderboard (HAL, [arXiv:2510.11977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977), 30 authors incl. Narayanan/Liang/Song, Princeton/Stanford, read pass 2) states "scaffolds dramatically impact both accuracy and cost, yet comparisons across scaffolds are rare," across 21,730 rollouts × 9 models × 9 benchmarks; (2) "Stop Comparing LLM Agents Without Disclosing the Harness" ([arXiv:2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23950), Tulane/Rutgers/Virginia Tech, read locally `tooling-harness-disclosure-2605.23950.pdf`) formalizes a "Binding Constraint Thesis" — for comparable-capability models the harness "is often a stronger determinant of agent performance than the model it wraps" — and cites a fixed-model harness-only change lifting Terminal-Bench 2 pass@1 from 69.7%→77.0% and "up to 15 percentage points of scaffold-only variation on SWE-bench Verified"; (3) Harness-Bench ([arXiv:2605.27922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27922), Peking Univ./Qiyuan Tech, read locally `tooling-harness-bench-2605.27922.pdf`) measures a **23.8-point aggregate gap** between the best and worst configurable harness (NanoBot 76.2 vs OpenClaw 52.4) under an identical task set + model-backend pool across 5,194 trajectories. Different implementations of the *same benchmark* (MMLU, a 57-subject multiple-choice knowledge test) in different harnesses "give widely different numbers and even change the ranking order of models" ([Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-llm-leaderboard-mmlu)).
- **Eval infrastructure lacks basic statistics.** BetterBench (what it did: scored 24 widely-used benchmarks against 46 best-practice criteria) found "most benchmarks not reporting statistical significance of their results nor allowing for their results to be easily replicated" ([arXiv:2411.12990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990), NeurIPS 2024 D&B spotlight). Evan Miller's error-bars paper (what it argues: eval scores need proper uncertainty math) says the eval literature "largely ignored the literature from other sciences on experiment analysis and planning" ([arXiv:2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)).
- **Benchmarks themselves rot.** UTBoost (what it did: re-checked SWE-bench test cases) found 176 mislabeled patches in SWE-bench Lite / 169 in Verified; correcting them affected 40.9% of Lite leaderboard entries (18 ranking changes) and 24.4% of Verified (11 ranking changes) ([arXiv:2506.09289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09289), via map — read; headline numbers verified verbatim pass 3). Epoch's audit found ~10% of OSWorld (a computer-use agent benchmark) tasks broken/invalid ([Epoch](https://epoch.ai/blog/what-does-osworld-tell-us-about-ais-ability-to-use-computers), via map). The **generalization — label/validity errors in test sets reshuffle model rankings — is independently established across four disjoint domains** (pass 3): coding (UTBoost), computer-use (Epoch OSWorld), image/text/audio classification (Northcutt/Athalye/Mueller, "Pervasive Label Errors in Test Sets Destabilize ML Benchmarks," [arXiv:2103.14749](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14749), NeurIPS 2021 D&B — 3.3% avg error across 10 datasets; ResNet-18 beats ResNet-50 on ImageNet once mislabeled-example prevalence rises just 6%, VGG-11 beats VGG-19 on CIFAR-10 at 5%), and knowledge-QA (Gema et al., "Are We Done with MMLU?", [arXiv:2406.04127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04127) — 6.49% of MMLU questions erroneous, 57% in the Virology subset, "significant discrepancies with … originally reported" performance, motivating MMLU-Redux). These four author groups share no overlap and reach the same conclusion, so "benchmarks rot and the rot moves rankings" is a multi-source primary-verified claim, not a SWE-bench artifact.
- **Inference is not reproducible even at temperature 0** (the "always pick the most likely token" setting that should be deterministic). Thinking Machines showed the same prompt sent 1,000 times under identical settings produced 80 distinct outputs; root cause is lack of *batch invariance* (results changing depending on how requests are grouped together on the GPU) in inference kernels, and their fix costs ~60% throughput ([blog](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/)). This silently undermines every eval run through a serving stack.
- **Observability outruns evaluation.** LangChain's State of Agent Engineering survey (N=1,340, Nov–Dec 2025): 57% have agents in production; quality is the #1 barrier (32%); ~89% have observability but only 52.4% run offline evals (batch tests on saved cases) and 37.3% online (live monitoring of production traffic) — traces are collected but not converted into systematic quality measurement ([survey](https://www.langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering)).
- **Training-environment tooling has a verifier-quality crisis** (verifier = the automated test that decides whether an RL agent's attempt was right, i.e. the reward signal). R2E-Gym's own audit: <20% of auto-generated tests distinguish correct from incorrect patches, and some are *toxic* (pass wrong patches, fail correct ones, up to ~10% on some problems) — weak rewards "teach the wrong behaviors at scale" ([arXiv:2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164), via map — read).
- **The plumbing is an attack surface.** 5.5% of 1,899 audited open-source MCP servers (MCP = Model Context Protocol, the standard for connecting tools/data to LLMs) had tool-poisoning vulnerabilities (malicious tool descriptions that hijack the model) ([arXiv:2506.13538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13538), via map).
- **Even well-funded tooling is fragile.** Meta's torchtune — 150+ contributors, 21 recipes — announced it is "no longer actively maintained" and wound down during 2025 ([GitHub issue #2883](https://github.com/meta-pytorch/torchtune/issues/2883)); maintenance longevity is itself an unsolved problem.

## The research field around it

Sub-areas, with maturity:

- **Evaluation-harness science** (mature methods, active): the lm-eval-harness team's retrospective (what it did: distilled 3 years of maintaining the standard harness) documents sensitivity to eval setup, cross-method comparison difficulty, and irreproducibility ([arXiv:2405.14782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782)); statistics for evals ([arXiv:2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)); benchmark meta-assessment (BetterBench, [arXiv:2411.12990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990)); benchmark auditing (UTBoost [arXiv:2506.09289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09289), Epoch's OSWorld audit).
- **Agent/LLM observability standards** (early, standards-formation phase): OpenTelemetry (OTel — the dominant open standard for software tracing/metrics) is actively defining GenAI *semantic conventions* (an agreed vocabulary for what to log about an LLM call), with an agent-application convention only recently drafted; the OTel blog explicitly frames current agent observability as a "fragmented landscape" across frameworks ([OTel blog 2025](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2025/ai-agent-observability/), [OTel GenAI observability 2026](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2026/genai-observability/)). Two competing vendor conventions predate it: Arize's [OpenInference](https://github.com/Arize-ai/openinference/) and Traceloop's [OpenLLMetry](https://github.com/traceloop/openllmetry) (whose conventions were partially upstreamed into — merged back into — OTel).
- **Developer-pain empirical studies** (small but real SE-research niche): taxonomy of LLM-app developer challenges (what it did: mined 29,057 OpenAI-forum questions, hand-coded 2,364), published at TOSEM ([arXiv:2408.05002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.05002)); Stack Overflow + GitHub study of agent-framework challenges (finding: five problem families, with retrieval/orchestration issues persisting as long-lived maintenance burdens while install/prompting issues resolve fast) ([arXiv:2510.25423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25423)); MAST, the 14-mode multi-agent failure taxonomy (a catalog of how agent teams break; κ=0.88 = high inter-rater agreement) ([arXiv:2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657), via map — read).
- **RL-environment and verifier engineering** (fast-moving, verifier quality is the frontier): backtranslated executable gyms (R2E-Gym — training environments auto-built by turning real code into runnable tasks), adaptive procedural environments (RLVE — environments whose difficulty auto-tunes to the model, [arXiv:2511.07317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07317)); per the internal map, "the frontier of progress is now reward/verifier quality and eval validity, not raw capability or environment cost" (read).
- **Reproducibility & reporting standards** (consensus-building stage): REFORMS — a 32-item, 8-module reporting checklist for ML-based science, built by 19-researcher consensus, published in Science Advances ([arXiv:2308.07832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07832), [reforms.cs.princeton.edu](https://reforms.cs.princeton.edu/)); a leakage taxonomy (a catalog of ways test data contaminates training/evaluation) across 17 fields ([Kapoor & Narayanan](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.07048)); deterministic inference (Thinking Machines above; follow-on academic work e.g. [LLM-42](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17768)).
- **Dataset tooling & metadata** (standardizing): MLCommons Croissant (a standard metadata format describing an ML dataset — its fields, splits, license — so tools can load it uniformly) ([arXiv:2403.19546](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.19546), NeurIPS 2024) — now supported by Kaggle and Hugging Face and required/recommended for NeurIPS dataset-track submissions ([MLCommons](https://mlcommons.org/2025/02/croissant-qa-community/), [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/croissant-a-metadata-format-for-ml-ready-datasets/)).
- **Agent-framework design critique** (practitioner discourse, pre-academic): 12-Factor Agents (~23.5k stars) argues most frameworks aren't used in production agents and offers a design rubric instead of code ([GitHub](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents)).

Publication venues observed: NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks (Croissant, BetterBench), TOSEM/ICSE-adjacent SE (software-engineering research) venues (developer-challenge studies), Science Advances (REFORMS), SOSP/MLSys (systems conferences — vLLM's PagedAttention, its memory-management technique for LLM serving), plus heavyweight non-venue artifacts (Thinking Machines blog, HF blog, 12-factor).

## Published exemplars (small-team/solo weighted)

1. **lm-evaluation-harness + "Lessons from the Trenches"** ([arXiv:2405.14782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782); [repo](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness)) — volunteer-collective tool that became the de-facto standard harness and Open LLM Leaderboard backend; the paper turned 3 years of maintenance experience into evaluation-methodology findings. The template for "maintenance → research standing."
2. **Inspect (UK AISI + Meridian Labs)** ([GitHub](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_ai); [Inspect Evals](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/inspect-evals)) — government-built harness that became shared safety-eval infrastructure (METR, Apollo, other AISIs); Anthropic's Petri is built on it ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/petri-open-source-auditing)). Shows tooling as soft power and a live contribution surface.
3. **vLLM / PagedAttention** ([blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2023-06-20-vllm); [Sky Lab](https://sky.cs.berkeley.edu/project/vllm/)) — a handful of Berkeley grad students reframed LLM serving as an OS virtual-memory problem (managing the model's attention cache in pages, like an operating system manages RAM); now 2,000+ contributors. One good systems idea + open code = field-wide infrastructure.
4. **Unsloth** ([GitHub](https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth); [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/unsloth-ai)) — two brothers hand-optimizing fine-tuning kernels (2–5x faster, ~70% less memory); 10M+ monthly model downloads. Proof that a two-person team can own a whole layer (accessible fine-tuning).
5. **Aider + polyglot leaderboard** ([leaderboard](https://aider.chat/docs/leaderboards/); [Epoch listing](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/aider-polyglot)) — solo developer Paul Gauthier's 225-exercise, 6-language benchmark became a community reference tracked by Epoch AI and cited on model launches. A solo-maintained eval can become an industry yardstick.
6. **Docent (Transluce)** ([intro](https://transluce.org/introducing-docent); [open-sourcing](https://transluce.org/docent/blog/open-source)) — nonprofit-built agent-transcript analysis tool used by 25+ orgs (Anthropic, DeepMind, METR, Apollo, academia) and in Claude 4 pre-deployment safety analysis; integrated with SWE-bench. The clearest recent case of *analysis tooling* conferring standing.
7. **"Adding Error Bars to Evals"** ([arXiv:2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)) — one author, no GPUs, just statistics applied to eval reporting; widely cited as the reference for eval error analysis. Existence proof that solo methodology work lands.
8. **12-Factor Agents** ([GitHub](https://github.com/humanlayer/12-factor-agents)) — a *document* (23.5k stars) distilling why agent frameworks fail in production; influenced practitioner discourse without shipping a framework. Standing via synthesis, not code.

## Where the gaps are

- **No cross-harness metrology** (a systematic, standardized way to measure how much different harnesses disagree). The HF MMLU incident ([blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-llm-leaderboard-mmlu)) and Lessons-from-the-Trenches document that harness implementation changes scores and rankings, but there is no systematic, maintained study quantifying lm-eval vs Inspect vs HELM disagreement across benchmarks/models, nor tooling that certifies two harness implementations as equivalent. **Hostile search (pass 2) confirms this stands:** the closest adjacent artifacts do *not* fill it — [EvalSense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18823) does domain-specific *meta*-evaluation *within a single framework* (not cross-harness); HAL ([arXiv:2510.11977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977)) standardizes *agent* eval and even names non-standardized infrastructure as Challenge #1–3 but does not measure lm-eval-vs-Inspect-vs-HELM divergence on shared items; the surviving discourse ("divergence traces to prompt template + output parsing, not the model") is folk knowledge across blogs, un-quantified. **Boundary sharpened (pass 3):** two 2026 primaries now do cross-harness metrology *for agent scaffolds* — Harness-Bench ([arXiv:2605.27922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27922)) reports a fixed-protocol 23.8-pt best-vs-worst scaffold gap and "Stop Comparing…" ([arXiv:2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23950)) proposes a locked-harness/factorial disclosure protocol — so the *agent-scaffold* half of this gap is now partly occupied. The *static-eval-harness* half (lm-eval-vs-Inspect-vs-HELM divergence on shared items, plus an equivalence-certification tool) remains unbuilt: neither paper touches classic QA/knowledge harnesses. ✅ absence corroborated by third-pass search for the eval-harness variant; the agent-scaffold variant is now a moving target, not empty.
- **Statistical rigor exists on paper, not in tooling defaults.** Error-bars methodology ([arXiv:2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)) and BetterBench's finding that most benchmarks skip significance reporting ([arXiv:2411.12990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990)) haven't translated into harnesses emitting paired-difference tests (comparing two models on the same items) / clustered SEs (error bars corrected for questions that come in related groups) by default. ⚠️ inferred from docs/repo browsing at survey level, not an exhaustive feature audit.
- **The trace→eval pipeline is missing** (no *portable/standard* tool turns collected traces into reusable regression test cases). 89% observability adoption vs 52.4%/37.3% eval adoption (N=1,340, Nov 18–Dec 2 2025 — all figures ✅ verified against the [LangChain survey](https://www.langchain.com/state-of-agent-engineering) primary page, pass 2) — the survey reads this as "the market has adopted tracing but hasn't linked traces to systematic quality improvement." Standards fragmentation (OTel GenAI vs OpenInference vs OpenLLMetry; agent conventions still draft — [OTel](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2025/ai-agent-observability/)) blocks portable tooling here. **Hostile search (pass 2) confirms the gap with a sharpened boundary:** the one library that names both sides — [eval2otel](https://github.com/evalops/eval2otel) — runs the *opposite* direction (eval results → OTel spans, for dashboarding), not traces → eval datasets; the "bootstrap eval sets from real traces" pattern is discussed only inside *vendor-locked* platforms (Braintrust, Databricks/MLflow), i.e. no standard-conformant, portable OTel-GenAI→regression-eval converter exists. ✅ absence corroborated, not just inferred.
- **Verifier auditing has no reusable, standalone tool** (stands with a caveat, pass 2). R2E-Gym measured distinguishing/toxic test rates for its own dataset ([arXiv:2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164)); the internal map calls verifier quality the field's binding constraint (read). **Hostile search (pass 2):** verifier-quality *concern* is now active — SWE-RM ([arXiv:2512.21919](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21919v1), execution-free SE feedback), R2E-Gym's hybrid verifiers, and ASG-SI ([arXiv:2512.23760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23760), decomposed auditable rewards with schema/argument/output-utilization validity metrics) all build *better verifiers or per-project audits*, but each is embedded in its own training pipeline. **No** benchmark-agnostic OSS tool that ingests an arbitrary SWE-Gym-style environment and reports its reward channel's distinguishing/toxic rates surfaced. ✅ absence corroborated; the reusable-standalone framing is what remains unclaimed (the *technique* is increasingly published).
- **Reproducible inference isn't productized for evals** (partially narrowing, pass 2). Batch-invariant kernels exist ([Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference/)) at ~60% slowdown (SGLang later cut overhead to ~34% by combining them with CUDA graphs), but no eval harness or serving stack offers a documented "deterministic eval mode." The companion claim that "nobody has quantified how much serving nondeterminism moves published benchmark scores" **has weakened**: a 2026 wave now attacks this — [LLM-42](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17768) (determinism via verified speculation), [DiFR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.20621) (inference verification despite nondeterminism), [DASH deterministic attention](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21824) (ICLR 2026), and Hydari & Iqbal's stochasticity-of-agents study ([arXiv:2606.08998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08998)), with reporting of single-digit-percent accuracy drift across hardware/batch conditions. So the *productized deterministic-eval-mode* gap is real, but the *unquantified* framing is now stale — the open piece is a systematic end-to-end study across published leaderboards, not the absence of any quantification.
- **Benchmark rot detection is one-off, not continuous** (stands, pass 2). UTBoost and Epoch's OSWorld audit were single studies; there's no CI-style rot monitoring for live leaderboards. **Hostile search (pass 2):** the field's *response* to rot is to replace benchmarks (MMLU→MMLU-Pro/HLE, HumanEval→LiveCodeBench) or harden them (SWE-bench→Verified), and "CI/CD eval" discourse means teams gating *their own* deployments against synthetic tests — not continuous label-validity monitoring of *public leaderboards*. Generic label-error frameworks exist in other domains (e.g. REC✓D / object-detection label correction, [arXiv:2508.06556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06556)) but aren't wired to live AI leaderboards. ✅ absence corroborated; a continuous rot-CI for leaderboards remains unbuilt.
- **Maintenance sustainability is unstudied *for AI research tooling specifically*** (refined pass 2). torchtune wound down despite Meta backing ([issue](https://github.com/meta-pytorch/torchtune/issues/2883)); grant programs (a16z, GitHub Accelerator) are one-shot. A general OSS-sustainability literature *does* exist and adjoins this — the Astropy sustainability case study ([arXiv:2402.15081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15081)), PyPI donation-URL/ecosystem-impact studies ([arXiv:2601.15139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15139), [arXiv:2605.06164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06164)), and the Tidelift maintainer surveys (burnout/unpaid-work findings). But hostile search surfaced **no** study that measures what keeps *AI eval/agent/fine-tuning tooling* maintained (funding model → longevity), i.e. the torchtune-vs-lm-eval-harness divergence is uncharacterized. This is a genuine open empirical question, not merely unfound. GitHub Accelerator's post-2024 status is also unconfirmed (no 2025/2026 cohort located — [program page](https://accelerator.github.com/)).

## What a solo researcher could do here

Concrete study shapes matching the profile (eval methodology + data pipelines + LLM tuning; laptop + ≤$500 GPU + APIs). Facts and precedents only — no ranking:

1. **Cross-harness equivalence study.** Run 3–5 open models on the same benchmarks through lm-eval, Inspect, and HELM; decompose score divergence into prompt formatting, answer extraction, and scoring differences; ship fixes upstream. Direct precedents: [HF MMLU blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-llm-leaderboard-mmlu), [arXiv:2405.14782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782). Cost: small open models on a rented GPU + API models; mostly engineering.
2. **Statistical-rigor audit + upstream PRs.** Re-analyze published leaderboard deltas with Miller's estimators ([arXiv:2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)) to flag statistically-insignificant claimed wins; contribute paired-test/SE reporting to lm-eval or Inspect. BetterBench ([arXiv:2411.12990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12990)) shows the audit genre publishes at NeurIPS D&B. Near-zero compute.
