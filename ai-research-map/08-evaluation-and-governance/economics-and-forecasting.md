# Economics & Forecasting

Quantifying AI's trajectory and its economic impact — the numbers that anchor policy and
investment.

## Key directions & work

### Forecasting capability & autonomy
- **METR time-horizons** — the task length a model completes at 50% reliability has been
  doubling ~every 7 months historically, *accelerating to ~4 months* in 2024–25; the latest
  frontier model reaches a ~14.5-hour horizon (Feb 2026). The most-cited autonomy trend.
  [METR](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)
- **Epoch trends** — training compute ~5×/yr; algorithmic efficiency ~3×/yr; inference cost
  at fixed performance halving ~every 2 months. [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/trends)
- **Compute thresholds** — models above 10²⁶ FLOP rising from ~10 (2026) to >200 (2030);
  largest datacenter ~800k H100-equivalents, Meta Hyperion targeting ~3.7M by 2028. [arXiv:2504.16138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.16138)

### Economic impact
- **Anthropic Economic Index** — AI usage skews toward *augmentation* over automation; more-
  exposed industries saw *greater* productivity/employment/wage growth, with no economywide
  job loss yet detectable. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/labor-market-impacts) ·
  [Stanford HAI AI Index](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report/economy)

### Scenario forecasting
- **AI 2027** (Kokotajlo et al.) — influential scenario; in 2026 the lead author *walked back*
  the median for transformative AI from ~2028 to ~2029, with breakthroughs more likely early
  2030s. [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/) · [review](https://futuresearch.ai/ai-2027-6-months-later/)

## State of research

**Best-performing now:** METR time-horizons and Epoch's compute/efficiency trends are the
canonical, widely-trusted quantitative anchors. Anthropic's Economic Index is the leading
empirical labor-impact dataset.

**Promising but unproven:** Translating capability trends into *economic* outcomes,
task-level automation forecasting, and reconciling fast time-horizon growth with modest
measured labor impact.

**Open problems & weaknesses:** Forecasts are contested and have been revised *longer*
(AI 2027). Time-horizon metrics may not capture reliability/robustness needed for real
automation. Labor data is early and augmentation-vs-displacement is unresolved. Scaling laws
predict loss, not capabilities or economic value. Selection/measurement effects abound.
