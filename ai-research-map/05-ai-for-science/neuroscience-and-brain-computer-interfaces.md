# Neuroscience & Brain-Computer Interfaces

AI decoding neural activity — restoring speech to paralyzed patients and building "brain
foundation models."

## Key directions & work

- **Real-time voice synthesis from brain activity** (UC Davis) — an ALS patient's intended
  speech synthesized with tone/melody; 9.1% WER on a 50-word vocabulary. [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/06/250612081317.htm)
- **Inner-speech decoding** (Stanford) — decoded *imagined* speech from motor cortex across 4
  paralyzed participants (up to 74% on a 125K-word vocabulary), with a "password" mental-privacy
  safeguard. [Cell 2025](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(25)00681-6)
- **Cross-species neural foundation model (BIT)** — pretrains on animal data, transfers to human
  speech decoding, cutting WER from 24.69% to 10.22%. [arXiv:2511.21740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21740)
- **"Brain foundation models"** emerged as a defined 2025 paradigm spanning EEG/MEG/fMRI/fNIRS.
  [Survey arXiv:2503.00580](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00580v1)

## State of research

**Best-performing now:** invasive (intracortical) speech BCIs are **proven in-patient** —
real-time text and even voice synthesis for people with paralysis is a landmark result.

**Promising but unproven:** cross-species pretraining transfer; non-invasive decoding; general
"brain foundation models."

**Open problems & weaknesses:** results are **single-subject and invasive** (surgical implant);
**non-invasive** decoding is far weaker; and the work raises serious **mental-privacy** concerns
that are now an active research/ethics topic. Generalization across people and tasks is open.
