# 13 · Fairness, Ethics & Human Factors

The *sociotechnical* side of AI research — bias and fairness, the human-AI interaction
that determines real-world outcomes, and the nascent question of whether models themselves
warrant moral consideration. This is the axis the frontier-capability map systematically
under-weights.

## The shape of this area

The field shifted from in-lab "fairness metrics" toward **deployed-system auditing under new
law** (EU AI Act, NYC LL144, Illinois HB 3773, EEOC requirements all live by 2026),
**representational harms** in generative media, and a sharper **structural critique** (is
"responsible AI" substantive or performative?). The HCI evidence is sobering: human-AI teams
often *underperform* the better of human-or-AI alone, explanations frequently *fail* to fix
over-reliance, and heavy chatbot use can *worsen* well-being. And at the speculative edge, a
small but institutionalizing community asks whether models deserve moral consideration at all.

> **📦 Why this is its own branch** — capability research asks "can the model do X?" This
> branch asks "for whom does it work, what does it do to the people using it, and who is
> accountable?" Those questions have their own methods, communities, and failure modes.
