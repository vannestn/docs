# Venture Capital & Funding

## In brief
- **What it is** — A map of where private investment is flowing in AI, and what those bets reveal about which research directions investors think will pay off. Venture capital funds early-stage companies in exchange for equity, hoping a few big winners cover many losses; where that money lands is a live signal of what the market believes is coming next.
- **Why it's pursued** — Frontier AI research is extraordinarily expensive (talent, compute, data), so who gets funded — and at what scale — largely decides which directions get seriously explored. Tracking the flows shows which theses investors are underwriting, sometimes years ahead of any shipped product.
- **Potential impact** — Funding concentration steers the whole field: well-capitalized directions attract talent and compute and crowd out others, and a handful of firms can define the near-term agenda. The upside is fast progress on backed ideas; the key risk is that valuations run far ahead of results, so a correction could strand the least-proven bets.

## The "post-scaling research era" bet
The most striking 2025–26 pattern: **enormous rounds for labs with little or no public
output** — betting on talent and a research thesis rather than shipped products.
- **Safe Superintelligence (SSI)** — ~$6B raised, ~$32B valuation, **zero papers/products**.
  Sutskever frames 2026 as a return to a "research era" ("a different mountain to climb").
- **Thinking Machines Lab** (Mira Murati) — multi-billion valuation pre-product.
- **Reflection AI** — $2B at $8B to be a Western open-weight frontier lab.
- **Recursive Superintelligence** — $650M at $4.65B for recursive self-improvement.

## Where else capital concentrated
- **World models / spatial intelligence** — World Labs ($1B), Decart (~$300M).
- **Robotics foundation models** — Skild AI (~$1.4B at >$14B), Physical Intelligence.
- **Autonomous science labs** — Periodic Labs (~$300M seed), Lila Sciences ($350M Series A).
- **Interpretability** — Goodfire (~$1.25B valuation; Anthropic's first outside investment).
- **Alternative architectures** — Inception (diffusion LMs), Liquid AI, Cartesia.

## What the money signals about research
- Investors believe **algorithms, not just scale**, drive the next leap (the SSI thesis).
- **World models and robotics** are seen as the next platform after LLMs.
- **AI-for-science** is fundable as a direct value-creation engine (drugs, materials).
- **Safety/interpretability** is now venture-backable, not just academic.

## State of the dynamic

**Clearest signal:** AI-for-science and robotics funding tracks concrete, evaluable value
(trials, materials, working robots). Interpretability becoming venture-backable is a genuine
shift.

**Most speculative:** The mega-funded stealth labs — valuations vastly exceed demonstrated
results, and the "research era" thesis is unproven.

**Risks & weaknesses:** Valuations may be disconnected from near-term revenue; much funding
depends on continued capability progress and cheap-ish compute. A correction would hit the
pre-product labs hardest. Capital concentration also shapes *which* research gets done —
well-funded directions crowd out others.
